# The player's run: legs, then arms and hands

2026-10-15. Question: the developer twice said the jog looked like the man "needs to poop". What on our bodies makes Kimodo's jog read that way, and what fixes it without remaking the take?

## Legs: the body's rest stance carried into the clip

Each body's clips are the mannequin's copied bone by bone, which is exact only where the two skeletons stand alike at rest. Tripo places a body's joints in its skin: the base's rest legs splay 6 degrees out at the hip and 5 at the knee, its feet turn out 15 and its forearms bend 16 forward, where the mannequin's run straight along their bones. Copied by rotation, the jog kept all of that and came down on feet 30 cm apart, toes out, the knees passing 27 cm apart: a waddle.

Fixed as Unreal's IK Retargeter and Unity's Humanoid retarget, onto a reference pose (`tools/characters/aim.py`): on each body, each limb segment of a stride is turned every frame to point as the mannequin's does, and each leg turned in at the hip by the one angle that lands the feet the clip's `step` apart. On the base the feet now land 8 cm apart (5 to 10 is a runner's), the knees pass 15 cm apart, toes forward. The developer: "the legs are great, the hands are kinda weird", and after the forest film, that the jog itself was right and only the hands were bad.

## Arms and hands: what Kimodo's take does

Measured on the base body through the jog (a throwaway Blender script reading the clip's joints; the trunk's line is pelvis to neck, swing is the upper arm's angle from it, forward positive):

| | Take as made | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Upper arm swing, left | 19 forward to 42 back | about 35 forward to 40 back |
| Upper arm swing, right | 13 forward to 7 back | as the left, half a stride later |
| Elbow bend | 84 to 105, most bent at the back of the swing | 80 to 100, most bent in front |
| Hand height in front, of pelvis to neck | 0.25 (the belly) | about 0.6 (the chest) |
| Hand at the back of the swing | 1 cm in front of the hip | behind the hip |
| Forearm across the body | 39 typical, 65 at worst | under about 20 |
| Wrist bend off the forearm's line | 17 typical, 23 at worst | under about 10 |
| Hand roll from thumb-side-up | 71 typical (palm down) | under about 25 |
| Finger curl, mean per finger | 64 (straight) | about 180 (a loose fist) |

So the hands hovered at the belly and met near the middle, palms down and fingers straight; the right arm hardly swung; and the elbow closed behind, not in front, the reverse of a runner's.

## The fix: an arm pass layered on the take

Engines layer an arm pose over a locomotion clip rather than remake it, and so does `made_clips.py` (`swing_arms`, the entry's `"arms"` in `art/animation/made.json`), on the mannequin's clip before any body is made, its legs and spine untouched (every leg and spine bone's world turn measured identical, frame by frame, to a thousandth of a degree):

- the upper arms' swing mapped from the take's range onto the target's, both timed by the left arm, the right half a stride later;
- the elbow bent from 80 behind to 100 in front, smoothly with the swing;
- the forearm turned out about the upper arm until it points at most 20 degrees across the body;
- the wrist brought to within 8 degrees of the forearm's line;
- the hand rolled to within 25 degrees of neutral, a neutral that turns with the forearm as it sweeps, so the hand does not wring;
- the fingers closed 0.8 of the way into the library's clenched fist (`"fist"`).

`aim.py` now also aims the hand, wrist to knuckles, so a body whose rest hand droops still carries the fist in line. The sprint shared the hands' faults (fingers straight, wrists bent 30 to 48 degrees, hands crossing in front) but not the swing, so its pass keeps its swing and sets only its elbow, forearm, wrist, roll and fist.

The numbers after, on the base, are in the table in the decision's log (D360) and the commit; the side, front and back loops were judged against the run references (RDR2, The Last of Us, Assassin's Creed) by an adversarial review before it went to the developer.

## What it costs

Nothing at runtime: the pass is baked into the clip. Forearm roll now sweeps further through the stride (the hinge plane turned out), which `twist.py` spreads; `clip_check.py`'s worst step is 49 degrees against a 135 limit.
