# Building against the developers' goals: the fourth review

2026-10-15. Question: does what landed in building since the third review ([goals-review-2026-10-15.md](goals-review-2026-10-15.md), at 2511e39) match the baseline the developers set? In their words: "keep this minimal set of scopes, the wood, the rocks, the construction, the destroy, and make sure everything works flawlessly and performantly because thats our baseline"; "just the core 'can we construct it with infinite pretend resources' stuff"; "dont scope creep"; materials "pretty straight forward.. and simple, to add … 100s of them … it also has to be performant"; kits "cleanly designed so each one doesnt have to be customized by the game"; pieces with "dimensions/center points/etc"; destruction that will "'explode' into voxels … fade", "performant AND look sick"; and "easily repeatable QA things that will capture videos … per set". In scope: wood (logs, hewn, shakes) and stone (stone, slate). Earth, lime-wash, wattle and thatch are frozen; brick is removed. And is it still Enshrouded's voxel approach, with the cube as the truth (D192, D211)?

The developers' standing rule: "you should always have an agent review your work against our original goals because i know we've gotten off track sometimes and moved away from the enshrouded-style voxel based approach".

How it was done: the 155 commits from 2511e39 to 7a39b5a touching `terra_build`, `terra_game/src/build`, the kits, `art/kits`, `docs`, `research`, `tools` and `xtask` (D290, D303, D305 and D193 as amended, D306, D308, D320, D278, D281, D284, D287, D319, doors filling their openings). The code was read, not only the docs: the no-per-kit test (`kit.rs`), the contract checker (`checker.rs`), `modules::lay` and `lay_cut`, the members in `draw.rs`, the refusals in `ops.rs`, `support::unjoined`, `build/loss.rs`, `inspect::cases`, `tools/kits/pack.ts`, and every material, pattern, pack and pieces file. Run fresh at 7a39b5a on the RTX 5090: `cargo xtask kits`, `cargo xtask contract`, `cargo xtask qa build --set logs` (its first trace failed, finding 11; run again, the machine at 46% load) and `cargo xtask qa destroy --set stone` (first pass, film and trace, the machine at 34% load). Also judged: the newest shots in the developers' worktree (the kit sheets of 02:20, `damage-depth.jpg`, `destroy-fragments.mp4` and `destroy-trace.txt`, whose castle and earlier build traces are quoted as such). A material of each pattern in scope was added with `cargo xtask pack` and taken out again, uncommitted. 0e9f2a7 landed during the review and is judged from its code and message. Ranks: **drift** (away from a goal, or a developers' call taken by an agent), **defect** (meant to work, does not), **polish**.

## The short answer

The core holds and is closer to the developers' ask than at any earlier review. The cube is the truth everywhere: damage, collapse, fragments, decks and doors all read the cubes, and fragments touch nothing. A wood or stone material is a ten-line file over its pattern, and the pack tool makes one from a CC0 scan in seconds. No game or layout code names a material, and a test holds that. Destruction now does what the developers described: things burst into cubes of their material that fall, settle and shrink away. The build and destroy scenarios are filmed the same every time, per set.

It is not yet "flawless and performant". Against D25's 75 fps (13.3 ms at the 99th percentile), building a cabin in logs sits right at the bar. Destroying in stone, the castle and the town are over it, by the main thread and not the GPU. The QA tools still judge against 16.7 ms. The build film ends with a hole in the cabin: a knocked-out corner cannot be built again under the wall that stands over it. The dormer's ridge splays into loose boards, and the mesh checker, which reports wood and stone at zero faults, never looks at a dormer. Destroyed cubes leave their plans floating as blueprints. A new roof material, or a log material without `--under`, cannot be made in one command. Finally, the baseline itself is in no decision row. So frozen kits still ship in the settlement and the sets, and the town (D330) and town life (D324) were built on D321, which is only Leaning.

## Answers to the questions asked

1. **Is the baseline flawless and performant?** No. Defects seen: findings 3, 4, 5 and 8, and finding 10's looks. Every frame over budget is in finding 2. Under the old 16.7 ms bar, destroy in stone has 59 frames over and 1 hitch, all in the cracks stage, and build in logs has 17 over and none. Under D25's 13.3 ms they have 139 and 68 over. In the build, the worst are a roof laid in the storey stage (25.0 ms), the material change (22.8 ms) and the carve (22.4 ms). The GPU is never the cause (2.5 to 3.6 ms); the main thread is, through re-laying on a blow, an edit or a damage redraw. What works: every stage of both films does what it says, apart from the rebuilt corner. Changes are never shown torn in either fresh run. A wall's change from logs to hewn is an instant repaint (D298; the material stage's fill is empty). Fragments are made in 0.2 ms a fall at most.
2. **Is adding a material simple?** For stone, yes: one command, two files of about ten lines, a credit and a texture set, made in 2 s, with no code and no per-kit data. Wood needs `--under pine_logs`, which is one extra flag, but finding 6 says why it is needed and where it is not documented. A roof is not simple: it needs its own pieces file. No game code was needed in any case. Filmed with `cargo xtask qa shapes --set <it>`, no refusals: the stone drew every shape and a carved wall in its scan, with the block pattern's copings, quoins and plinth. Those stay the pattern's pale dressed stone whatever the wall, so a dark stone gets pale quoins (a material may set its own `Dressed`). The willow logs drew their courses in the new bark, with sawn ends and the pine logs' daub.
3. **Is anything customised per kit?** Not in code: the test in `kit.rs` fails on any material's, pack's or piece's name, or a kind by number, in `terra_build` and `terra_game`, and it passes. In data, the per-material packs carry shape (hewn's `profile: Hewn`, `notch: Flush` and its dress roles), which is the material instance D290 allows. Shakes and slate each carrying their own rafter and batten is the one place where the data does what the pattern says it should not (finding 6). `tools/kits/pack.ts` names a default roof material per pattern, which is tooling, not the game. The contract holds for every modelled module, and not for corner stones or the two stretches (finding 7).
4. **Scope creep and drift?** In building, the frozen kits still carry work and ship in the settlement (finding 1). Storage paying for building (D295, 4fa864f) landed before "infinite pretend resources" and should not grow. Outside building but in its crate, the town and town life rest on a Leaning decision (finding 9). The climate, body warmth, biome and character work in the same window is other tracks and is not judged here. From Enshrouded's voxel approach there is no drift: fragments are cubes of the material, as asked, and decks, doors, damage depth and collapse are all worked out from the cubes. The one place a plan outlives its cubes is finding 5.
5. **Decisions and docs.** Stale or contradictory: finding 12, and D303 against what the code stretches (finding 7). Calls that belong to the developers: the baseline's scope (finding 1), D321 under D324 and D330 (finding 9), and the looks of D305 and D320 (finding 13).

## What is on track, and should stay

- The cube as the only truth: damage, collapse (lowest-first flood), subsidence, decks framed on their posts (D306) and doors filling their holes are all from the cubes. Fragments are visuals that touch no one.
- A material as a small file over four patterns and a pack, merged field by field. The no-per-kit test. One draw per pattern per section from texture arrays. A base asks only its own materials. A hundred materials measured on both GPUs at no cost to the frame (D290).
- Repainting within a pattern at once (D298), seen in the build film.
- Fragments as the developers described them, at 0.2 ms a fall. Pipelines are compiled ahead, so the first fall is not black. The cap fades the oldest.
- QA films per set, the same every run, with a real-time trace, edits timed to the screen and torn changes counted (D308). They found findings 3, 5 and 11 within one run each.
- The junk ratchet in `cargo xtask check` (D287), and the model contract checked for props, doors and modelled pieces (D303).

## Limits

The second build trace ran while `cargo xtask check` ran on the same machine, so its times may be high. The castle and town300 numbers are quoted from the record and the shots, not run fresh. Both fresh runs and the kit sheet stills predate 0e9f2a7, whose fixes (the daub's black web, holes on their stones' outline) are judged from its code and message. The new materials were judged in the shapes film only, not under a roof or in a mix.
