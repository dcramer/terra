# Climbing in shipped games and engines: what is climbable, what it costs, how the body follows the face

2026-10-25. Question: D477 makes climbing free climbing on stamina, "as in Enshrouded and Breath of the Wild", and leaves its detail to be designed. How do shipped games answer what can be climbed, what a climb costs and what happens at zero, how a climb starts and ends at a ledge, overhangs, speed and a climb jump; and how do engines move the capsule along the face and animate it? It feeds the Climbing section of [docs/controller.md](../docs/controller.md#climbing).

## Answer

Every shipped free climber is one pattern: a movement mode of its own, entered against a face too steep to walk, in which gravity is off, the input moves the body along the face's plane at about a walk's quarter, the capsule is held at a gap from the face and turned to it each tick, stamina drains while it moves, and at zero the body lets go and falls (the fall harming as any fall does). At the top a probe at the hands finds no face and a floor above, and a pull-up clip carries the body onto it. What differs is what can be held (everything, or marked surfaces), whether holding still costs, and whether the face may lean over. No engine whose code we may take ships a climber: Jolt, Godot and O3DE have none, Unreal has the custom movement mode framework and no climbing of its own, so ours follows Unreal's framework from its docs and the BotW-recreation write-ups (D196), on our mover's existing queries.

Notable: Enshrouded does not free climb. A body climbs only walls fitted with iron lattices, latching on by walking up to one, on stamina, and falls at zero; climbing anything is a third-party mod. D477's "as in Enshrouded" holds for the stamina, not for "any surface".

## How engines and write-ups build it

**Unreal's framework** (`UCharacterMovementComponent`): a climb is `MOVE_Custom` with a custom mode number, its physics in `PhysCustom`, replicated and predicted like walking when the character derives from the engine's classes. Unreal itself has no climber; the Game Animation Sample (5.4) ships traversal (vault, hurdle, 1 m and 2 m mantles) chosen by obstacle height and fitted by Motion Warping, under the Unreal EULA, so docs only for us.

**Vitor Cantão's BotW climber on Unreal** (blog, the clearest full account):

- *Finding the face*: every tick a capsule sweep 20 cm ahead gathers the wall's hits; a line at eye height confirms there is face where the hands would be; the face's normal and point are the average of the hits (refined by a small sphere cast toward each, because overlapping geometry skews the average).
- *Starting*: a hit within 25 deg of facing, not a ceiling, with face at eye height.
- *A tick of climbing*, in order: refresh the face; stop if it is gone or a ceiling, or land if a walkable floor is within 1 m under the feet while moving down; advance a climb dash; velocity by acceleration (380 cm/s²) and braking (550) toward the input on the face, max 1.2 m/s; move along the face, turning toward `-normal` (rate 6); try the ledge; snap the capsule back to 45 cm from the face (rate 4, swept). The capsule is shortened by 30 cm while climbing.
- *The ledge*: moving up at a third of the climb speed or more, a ray at eye height finds no face within 2.5 radii, and the point 1.2 m ahead and 1.6 m up is walkable floor reached by an unblocked capsule sweep: a root-motion pull-up clip plays.
- *The climb dash*: a speed curve to 9.5 m/s at 0.4 s, ended at 1.15 s, along the input on the face's plane.
- *Animation*: a 2D blend space by velocity in the face's frame (up and right); ground foot IK off; hands and feet traced to the face and offset additively, then full-body IK (Control Rig) holds the body to the wall.

**Root-motion mantles** (Laura's Unreal notes): the mantle clip's own height curve drives the body; it starts partway in, at the time whose remaining rise equals the ledge's height (a bounded binary search), so one clip serves many heights; the capsule's collision is off during it and the end checked beforehand.

**Other write-ups** (nbertoa's UE5 climber, the Udemy UE5 climbing course): the same shape; average the sweep's hits and normals, interpolate the rotation to `-normal`, and correct toward the face every tick or the body drifts off it. Mantles are traced forward then down, refusing a top that is not walkable.

## What it means for ours

- **No new query.** The mover's query trait already answers contacts near a capsule with a normal. A probe at hand height and one at the feet give the face, and on the voxel ground the SDF's gradient is the face's normal at once, already blended across a dug voxel face's steps (what Unreal averages hits to get). Buildings, props and flora answer by their shapes as now.
- **A mover state beside swimming.** Climbing is a ground state (`Climbing`) and a module (`mover::climb`) as swimming is (`mover::swim`); collide-and-slide and the sweep are reused unchanged, gravity off, the stick-to-floor step turned toward the face.
- **The capsule stays upright** (D53). Shipped climbers turn the mesh to the face and keep the capsule's collision simple; Unreal's write-ups shorten it.
- **The mantle is the mover's path, the clip fitted to it** (the server never waits on animation): rise then forward over a fixed time, the clip started at the height that matches (Laura's search) or warped (Motion Warping).
- **Base defence follows from the rules.** With a thousand players, climbable built walls mean raiders climb them (Conan). An overhang limit, materials that cannot be held, and a blow knocking a climber off are each a defence a player can reason about (D122).
