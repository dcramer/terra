# Cameras for planning

2026-10-12. Question: which games give building, and planning in particular, a camera or controller other than the character's own, and what does each choice cost in a game you play as a character?

This feeds D170 (Plan mode, Leaning) and the developers' idea, noted with it, that Plan mode might get "a camera that is not your character's, perhaps from above" ([tribe.md](../docs/tribe.md#plan-and-build-handing-work-to-the-tribe)). It does not decide anything.

## How this was found

From patch notes, official pages, wikis, mod pages, reviews and forum threads, read on 2026-10-12. Nothing was checked in a game. Nexus Mods and the Fandom wikis would not load, so what they say comes from search summaries and is marked. Games I could not confirm anything for are in [Limits](#limits), not below.

## Four patterns

1. **The character's camera only.** You build from your own eyes or over your shoulder, perhaps with a first-person toggle or a longer reach.
2. **A camera that leaves the body, tied to a range.** The view flies off while the character stays where it is, bounded by the base or a distance.
3. **A separate view from above.** A Sims-like camera over the plan, either as a mode or as the whole game.
4. **A separate design space.** A screen, station or world where designs are made and saved, then placed in the real world.

## The trade-offs

| Pattern | Good | Bad | For a persistent multiplayer survival game |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Character camera | Most immersive; no second control scheme; you see only what you could see (D93) | Your body blocks the view; roofs, upper storeys and large footprints are hard; the commonest complaint, and the commonest mod | Safe and simple, but the drafting the developers find unfun stays unfun |
| 2. Detached, tied to range | Fixes the view where the work is; one camera for drafting and checking; the character is still in the world | The body is left standing, vulnerable or frozen; a camera through walls is a scouting tool, so servers turn it off (Space Engineers); terrain clipping (Starfield) | Workable if bounded to your own claim and kept out of solids |
| 3. View from above | Best for drawing walls on a grid and seeing a whole layout (The Sims, Kenshi); familiar from builders | Least immersive; needs a cursor-driven control scheme; strongest when time stops, and ours does not | Fits drafting, which costs nothing and changes nothing, if the world goes on around the body |
| 4. Separate design space | Freedom to try anything at no cost; designs saved and shared; no risk | Splits the game in two; a design made away from the site ignores its ground; content made out of context | Good for saved designs (D170's copy and save), not for drafting where you stand |

Three things recur:

- **Drafting and building want different cameras.** The games that feel most physical keep building in the body (The Forest, Bellwright, Medieval Dynasty, Space Engineers welding a projection). Where a game gives a free camera, it is for laying out, not for doing the work. Starfield and Animal Crossing switch to a view from above only for arranging.
- **Every free camera is bounded.** By the base (Valheim's workbench, Starfield's outpost, Animal Crossing's room), by a distance (Runic's 100 m), or by permission (Space Engineers' admins, Conan's creative mode).
- **What happens to the body is always an answer someone had to choose:** paused (The Sims), frozen in place (My Build Camera), left vulnerable (Runic Build Camera), or flown (Conan).

## What it means for us

Suggestions for the developers; how Plan mode is entered and looks is a design question, and D170 is Leaning.

**The split matches D139 and D170.** A draft is paperwork: it costs nothing and touches nothing. A plan is built by hands carrying logs. So drafting can leave the body without breaking "realistic but fun" (D122), while building, the immersive part, stays in it. Build mode keeps the character camera; Plan mode is where a different camera belongs.

**Recommended: Plan mode lifts the camera into a surveyor's view over the claim.**

- **Trigger.** Switching the hammer to Plan (T, as in tribe.md) lifts the camera; switching back drops it into the character. The character stands and studies a sheet of plans, so others see what they are doing.
- **Camera.** Above the site, panned, turned and zoomed like The Sims', tilting from straight down for drawing walls on the 0.5 m grid (D57, D154) to a low angle for seeing roofs. Walls cut away and storeys chosen one at a time, so upper floors and interiors can be drafted. It follows the ground, so slopes never swallow it (Starfield's fault).
- **Bounds.** Inside your own claim, and no further than some distance from the character. Inside the claim you built everything, so seeing it from above tells you nothing D93 would hide. It does not pass through closed walls into places you have no right to, and outside a claim it is the character's camera as now.
- **The body.** Stays in the world, standing, vulnerable, as in Runic's mod; time does not pause. Damage, or something hostile coming near, drops you back into the character, with a cue first. That is the honest answer for a persistent world, and it makes drafting something you do somewhere safe, which is realistic.
- **What you do there.** Everything D170 lists for drafts: draw walls, floors, roofs and openings; copy, mirror, move; save a design; see the whole cost and the stores' share; hold T to hand a building over. Not building itself.
- **Multiplayer.** Drafts stay as D170 says: seen by those with the hammer in hand within the claim. Two people can draft at once, each seeing the other's cursor on the plan.

**A drafting table later, as pattern 4.** A placed table at the outpost could open the same view for the whole claim with saved designs, as No Man's Sky's workshop and Satisfactory's designer do. It would make planning a place you go, which some may prefer for immersion; it is not needed first.

**Risks.**

- **Two control schemes.** D144's controls are written for a crosshair; the view from above needs a cursor version of drawing, the build menu and the wheel. This is the largest cost, and controllers make it larger.
- **Scouting.** A camera that leaves the body sees what the character cannot. Bounding it to your own claim and keeping it out of solids answers most of it; whether that is enough for PvP is open.
- **Leaving the body in danger.** Players may hate being hit while looking away, or learn to draft only in safety. The auto-return is the tested answer; it needs trying.
- **Plans drawn from above may not suit the ground.** The Sims' lots are flat; ours are not. The view should show the ground's shape and where it is too steep before a draft is handed over.
- **Building in Build mode stays as it is**, so the complaints the mods answer (roofs, the body in the way) remain there. Plan mode can take the big layout work off it, and Enshrouded's first-person toggle is the cheap help for what is left.

## Limits

- Nothing was played. Each row rests on a patch note, a wiki or one or two guides or threads; download counts are snapshots.
- Could not confirm anything about a building camera in Soulmask, Once Human (a "projection camera" and a "fly mode" are mentioned in one thread, unexplained), Palworld, LEGO Fortnite, Grounded 2, Sons of the Forest or Timberborn, so they are left out. Fallout 4's own workshop camera was not checked, only the mod.
- Starfield's top-down view: not found whether the character walks under it or it pans on its own, or how far it goes.
- World of Warcraft's housing (2025) showed a bird's-eye view of a house's rooms in Blizzard's preview, but how its camera works was not found.
- Nexus Mods and the Fandom wikis would not load; their facts come from search summaries. The Enshrouded feedback board request could not be read.
- How it feels to switch between a body and a view from above in a world that keeps going is the real question, and no source answers it. A spike in the sandbox would: lift the camera over a claim and draft a room from above.
