# 75 fps: where the frame goes

2026-10-15. **Question:** with D25's new budget, a frame within 13.3 ms at its 99th percentile (75 fps) at 1080p on the developers' RTX 3080 at the high preset, which of main's benchmark scenes hold to it, where is the frame lost, and what would win it back, at what cost to the look?

## How it was measured

- `cargo xtask bench`, every scene, three runs, on 58fb275, offscreen at 1920x1080, high preset. The machine was shared: load 5 to 49 on 24 cores while other agents compiled, and runs queued for the GPU behind theirs (D319). Three scenes (`village`, `town300-bare`, `town300-fly`) and two of `walk`'s runs were lost, ended as stuck while waiting for the GPU (fixed on main since).
- Then A/B runs of `walk` and `forest`, one run each, with a throwaway switch read from the environment and removed afterwards (development.md): each thing turned off or changed alone.
- **Two faults in the measuring were found and fixed first.** The GPU's time added every pass's *last* time, so the sky light's filter, which runs a few frames after the sky is painted, was counted in every frame: 0.6 ms that was not there (`lightprobe_irradiance_map` 0.34, `lightprobe_radiance_map` 0.24). And **the sun's shadow maps are missing from the passes' times**: Bevy gives `per_view_shadow_pass` 0.03 ms, but turning shadows off saves 3.5 ms of frame. A new line says how long the render world works on a frame and how long it then waits for the GPU, which shows what the passes miss.
- GPU pass medians repeat within 1 to 2% from run to run whatever the load; frame medians within about 0.5 ms; 99th percentiles of a walking scene follow the machine's load (`walk` 15.7 ms at load 10, 44 at 38), so they are read only where the load was low.

Two kinds of miss. The forest scenes are **GPU-bound**: `forest` and `walk` spend 12.3 to 12.5 ms a frame on the GPU and the render world waits about 6 ms of each frame for it. The building scenes and `world` are **CPU-bound at their worst frames**: their medians fit with room, and their 99th percentiles are bursts of the game's own update, a separate question per scene.

## Where the forest's frame goes

`walk` at 17:00, the heaviest view (b9f44296, render world busy 5.7 ms, then waiting 5.8 ms for the GPU, so the frame is the GPU's):

| Part | ms | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| The sun's shadow maps, four cascades of 4096 to 300 m | about 2.4, untimed | frame 12.27 to 8.70 with shadows off, the timed passes 9.53 to 8.34 |
| `main_opaque_pass_3d`, 8 million triangles | 3.74 | |
| `early prepass`, the same 8 million (depth, normals, motion) | 1.91 | |
| `volumetric_lighting`, the haze and its shafts, 48 steps a pixel | 1.74 | 0.72 of it is sampling the shadow map; frame 12.27 to 10.56 with no haze |
| `uniform_allocation` and `bin_unpacking`, per view | 0.78 | 0.30 with no shadow cascades to prepare |
| `ssao` | 0.37 | |
| `atmosphere_luts` | 0.33 | |
| `bloom`, `taa`, clustering, sky, exposure, tonemapping | 0.5 | |

All told the sun's shadows cost **3.5 ms** of the 12.3: drawing them, the haze looking them up 48 times a pixel, and preparing four more views. `forest`, standing still, is the same within half a millisecond.

## What would win it back, ranked

A/B runs, frame medians, `walk` | `forest` (base 12.27 | 12.54):

| Change | Frame | Its cost to the look |
|---|---|---|
| Shadows off (to measure only) | 8.70 \| 9.03 | everything |
| No haze (to measure only) | 10.56 \| 11.07 | the forest's depth |
| Three cascades over the same 300 m | 11.34 \| 11.84 | coarser shadows from about 20 to 100 m |
| A 2048 shadow map | 11.73 \| 11.85 | every shadow half as sharp |
| Occlusion culling, the camera's | 12.06 \| 11.95 | none; the render world 1.9 ms busier on the CPU |
| Occlusion culling, the sun's cascades | 13.14 \| 12.69 | none; no gain |
| Both | 14.55 \| 14.65 | none; the render world CPU-bound at 9.7 to 11 ms |

1. **Shadows, 3.5 ms.** Shipped open worlds rarely redraw every cascade every frame: Unreal's virtual shadow maps keep pages and redraw only those whose casters moved, and cascaded maps elsewhere are commonly refreshed in turn, the near one every frame and the far ones every second to fourth. Here the far cascades hold slowly swaying crowns, so redrawing cascades 2 and 3 every other or fourth frame would save about 1 to 1.5 ms for a far shadow that steps at 20 to 40 Hz, near invisible at that distance. Bevy redraws all four every frame, so it is our own cascade scheduling (D184, D196). Cheaper still and measured: three cascades, about 0.8 ms, or a 2048 map, about 0.6 ms, each softer. And the forest's casters: its crowns cast through alpha-tested cards in the first cascade; lumps or shells sooner would cut fill (the lush forest's species, being changed by another agent, so not measured here).
2. **The haze, 1.7 ms.** Bevy marches every pixel 48 steps. Shipped volumetrics march at reduced resolution or in a froxel grid: Assassin's Creed IV (Wronski, SIGGRAPH 2014) and Frostbite (Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2015) light a camera-aligned grid of cells a few screen pixels across and some 64 slices deep, once, and look it up per pixel. Half resolution with a depth-aware upsample would save about 1.2 ms, its shafts a little softer at trunks' edges unless the upsample respects them; 32 steps instead of 48 about 0.5 ms, more noise for TAA to hide. Its 0.7 ms of shadow lookups also shrink with any shadow saving above.
3. **Triangles, 5.6 ms between the prepass and the main pass.** Eight million a pass, mostly grass and leaf cards. The prepass draws alpha-tested foliage so the main pass shades only what is in front (D288). Fewer triangles is a look decision: grass density or reach, cards per crown, crowns' detail distances. Bevy's two-phase occlusion culling (the camera's) culls a fifth of them (7.95 to 6.45 million) and saves 0.45 to 0.85 ms of GPU, but costs the render world 1.9 ms of CPU: on this machine a frame 0.2 to 0.6 ms quicker, on D25's 4-core CPU likely slower. Not taken.
4. **The air's tables, 0.33 ms.** Bevy works out all four of Hillaire's tables every frame; the transmittance and multiple-scattering ones depend only on the air's make-up and, as his paper does, need working out only when it changes. Perhaps 0.15 ms, no change to the look, but a change inside Bevy.
5. **Per-view preparation, 0.8 ms**, scales with views and meshes: fewer cascades take some of it (0.30 with none).
6. **SSAO 0.37, bloom 0.14, TAA 0.10**: small; not worth the look.

No change found that saves time with no cost to the look and no cost elsewhere, so none was made. The cheapest real steps to a forest that holds 13.3 ms with room are the shadow ones in 1, then the haze's resolution in 2: together some 2.5 to 3 ms, for the developers to weigh against what they change. The CPU-bound scenes' bursts are for their own owners: `world`, `going-up`, `town300` and `castle` each need a `--frame-times` run to say which systems they are.

## Limits

- One shared machine, busy throughout; one run per A/B. GPU medians are reliable, CPU medians and every walking 99th percentile less so.
- The shadows' cost is inferred from A/B runs, not timed: Bevy's span round the shadow passes reads 0.03 ms, and why it misses them is not found.
- The integrated GPU was not measured this round.
- Nothing here measured the look; the costs to it are what each change does, not pictures compared.
