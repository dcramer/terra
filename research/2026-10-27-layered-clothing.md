# Layered clothing: how shipped games put a shirt with trousers, tucked or not

2026-10-27, characters. Question: our smith's shirt is made tucked (`chest_long_sleeves_tucked`, its hem at the hips joint, `--over=trousers` in `tools/characters/build_bodies.sh`). Worn alone, skin shows at the hip: 2.4% of the watched skin at rest and 2-5% in every clip, against gear_check.py's 2% bar; over trousers it is hidden. `assets/data/townsfolk.ron` always pairs it with trousers, but a player can wear it alone. How do great games, our inspirations first (docs/vision.md), do shirts and trousers, and which way fits ours (one look per item, made on the base and carried to every body, D128; a new kind is data, D127)?

## Answer

No game we found makes a shirt that only works over trousers. Each does one of three things: the piece has **one look worn the same everywhere** (Kenshi, Enshrouded, Soulmask, Medieval Dynasty, Dune: Awakening, Rust, Skyrim), the **outer layer is drawn over the inner by a fixed order of layers** (Rust's two layers a slot, Cyberpunk 2077's garment score, Kingdom Come's four layers), or the item has **a look per state** chosen by what is worn over it (Cyberpunk's `&Part` shirt under a jacket) or by the player (RDR2's wardrobe styles). The order of layers is the fit for us: a shirt made untucked, hanging to its hem alone, and the trousers made over it, so worn together it goes into the waistband. We already do it for the woman's shift under the skirt (`skirt ... --over=shift,bodice`).

## The techniques

| Technique | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cyberpunk 2077 garment support | Each garment carries garment-support data (shape keys in Blender). ## Ours

- `shell_garment.py`'s COVERAGE: `chest_long_sleeves` ends at a hem 0.25 of the way from the hips joint to the knee; `chest_long_sleeves_tucked` is the same at 0.0, the hips joint. That one number is all "tucked" changes.
- `--over=ITEM` makes a piece stand at least `OVER_M` (4 mm) outside one made before it; `--under=ITEM` keeps it to the skin where a piece from the same dressed model covers it. The order is fixed at build time: boots over trousers, the apron over trousers, the skirt over the shift and bodice.
- docs/characters.md: one piece per slot (D64), pieces of different slots stacking by heights off the skin (chest within 1.8 cm above the waist, a hem free below it); the body hides the patches a piece wholly covers.

So we already have the layer order, baked at build time, which is Cyberpunk's garment score without the runtime squash. A shirt made untucked (hem 0.25) and the trousers made `--over=shirt` reads as tucked when both are worn and hangs to its hem alone, with one look per item carried to every body. The cost: the trousers stand about 4 mm plus the shirt's offset further out round the hips in every outfit, under the coat too (the coat then made `--over=trousers`), and the shirt's lower band is baked from a dressed model whose trousers cover it (`--plain` fills it, as the shift's under the skirt). A runtime squash (Cyberpunk, Mutable's Clip Morph) or a look per state (Cyberpunk's `&Part`, RDR2's styles) is for when the order changes with what is worn or the player chooses; neither is needed for one shirt.
