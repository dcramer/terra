# Building against the developers' goals

2026-10-13. Question: does the building system as built on `main` (3da73e0) match what the developers asked for: an Enshrouded-style voxel approach (D211), building by hand (D226), the cube as the truth (D192), the art guide (D14), performance at many buildings, real materials and authored pieces (D177 and its refinements), and the original ask, "complete the construction tech implementation fully for our demo, with the two core materials (log-based and stone-based), and the varying builds"? Whether it works is secondary.

The developers asked for this review: "you should always have an agent review your work against our original goals because i know we've gotten off track sometimes and moved away from the enshrouded-style voxel based approach".

How it was done: the building docs, the research notes behind them and every Building decision read; `git log 2a119ad..3da73e0` for `terra_build` and `terra_game/src/build` (34 commits); then the code itself (`terra_build`'s `lib.rs`, `cubes`, `rooms`, `layout/*`, `sections`, `walk`, `save`, `village`; `terra_game`'s `build.rs`, `build/layout.rs`, `build/store.rs`, `build/plans.rs`, `play/tool.rs`, `assets/data/builds.ron`), and the latest screenshots (the demo route, roofs as cubes, the earth and wattle kits, the hand tools, the role pieces) beside Enshrouded's wiki galleries and store shots, which are Keen's and stay out of the repository. No new pictures were taken; the demo route's pictures predate roofs as cubes (D241) and the earth and wattle houses in the village (D236), so the village's roofs were judged from the kit sheet's and the bench village's. Severity: **drift** (away from a goal), **defect** (meant to work, does not), **polish**.

## The short answer

The core is on the goal and should not be undone: a cube store that is the truth, roofs now cubes too, every exposed edge dressed by a role worked out from the cubes and never stored, shapes, a material picker, brushes and carving, ghosts walked through. Where it has drifted is around that core: every edit lays the whole base again, the village and the demo are put up by the Sims-era shortcuts on a storey-and-panel model the cubes were meant to replace, rooms and door steps still read storeys and panels, hand building a cube a click runs into a 255-part ceiling, door steps collide outside any cube, and 300 buildings were never measured. The docs lag the code badly, and D177, D181 and D160 contradict what is built.

## Answers to the questions asked

1. **Where it departs from the Enshrouded-style voxel approach.** Not in the store, nor in the look's method, which are Enshrouded's (cubes, faces culled and filled in world space, a role for every exposed edge). It departs in the whole-base relayout (1), the village built from room and wall-tile shortcuts (2), storey tiles and panels under rooms, steps and frames (5), door steps outside the cubes (4), and timber masses that do not read as one structure (6). Timber laid along whole rows is D192's "long members on runs" and fine.
2. **The cube is the truth.** Upheld in saves (cubes, parts and their terms, sampled ground and props; old roof sections turned into cubes), support (`support.rs`, with ramps for roof slopes) and collision (`walk.rs`: cubes to their fill and shape, posts at their drawn width), except door steps (4). Walls, rooms and columns are cached in `Base` but worked out from the cubes and never saved, as they should be; the cache is rebuilt over the whole base on every operation (`work_out`), another whole-base cost.
3. **Blending and roles.** Stone, earth, wattle and daub and roofs change by role and neighbours; stone's corners, tops, feet and openings reached the bar ([role-pieces.md](role-pieces.md)). Timber takes a plate, a sill and posts by role but decides crest and foot per run, not per cube, and thick masses fail (6). Kinds meet by each kit's `meets` table (D232).
4. **Building by hand.** Possible in full; primary in neither the menu nor the demo (8, 2); capped by the part palette (3).
5. **The look.** Mostly at the bar; earth, the change of material and the village's props read cheap (9); the demo's pictures are stale.
6. **Performance.** Tracked for 50 buildings on both GPUs, with a bench that fails on budget; not for 300, not since D241, and not for edits (7, 1).
7. **Docs and decisions.** Above.

## What is on track, and should stay

- The 32-bit cube in sections of 16, operations checked by one function with inverses, property tests over random operations (D199, D231).
- Roofs as cubes built by hand, "add a roof" as a plan of the same cubes, and old saves converted (D226, D241): the largest single step back toward Enshrouded.
- Roles worked out from the cubes and the ground, never stored, each edge owned by one cube, so walls part built or carved look finished (D215); kits as data, with a checker (D210).
- Generated logs (D200) and the displaced stone surface (D197), each chosen by the developers from side-by-side spikes.
- The material picker, change in place, brushes, carving and taking out the cube aimed at (D232, D233); 32 shapes (D231).
- Ghosts as blueprints, walked through; only what is built collides (D192, D213).
- Detail by distance with baked far meshes; fifty buildings cost about 0.1 ms on the fast GPU ([building-drawing.md](building-drawing.md)).
- The original ask's two core materials and varying builds: logs and stone, with about three hundred variants by plan, scheme, roof and width (D229).

## Limits

Judged from code and stills by an agent; nothing was played. The demo's pictures are from before roofs became cubes. Finding 6's walls were not traced to the cubes that made them, and finding 9's change of material may be a stage under way rather than the finished one. Performance figures are the notes' own, not measured again.
