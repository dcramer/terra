# How shipped isometric ARPGs make their art, and where a Tripo and Bevy pipeline departs

Dated 2026-11-05 (experiment iso-art, job j1). The developer asked for "a replicable art pipeline" for the 2.5D isometric RPG experiment ([the spike](2026-11-05-isometric-party-spike.md)): Tripo models, Tripo's auto-rig and preset clips, effects and ambient, judged adversarially. This note gathers what is known of how Diablo IV, Path of Exile 2, Last Epoch, V Rising and Hades II make theirs. **Primary** marks the studio's own words; **inferred** marks what is read off their store screenshots (14 kept with their numbers outside the repository, `~/.cache/terra-review/iso-art/refs/`) or general practice. Little is published beyond art-direction talk; nothing here gives a polygon or texture budget from a studio.

## What each game does

**Diablo IV** (Blizzard, own engine). Primary: "all things Diablo are in service to our isometric point of view" (John Mueller, art director); armour is made of "bigger shapes", and what hurts readability is cut; every asset is checked at two cameras, the game's and a closer full-body one; a detail map ("a small, repetitive texture applied on top of the material") keeps materials sharp; PBR so characters "look as good in daylight as they do in a dark dungeon" (Blizzard quarterly update, June 2021). Other cameras were tried and dropped; the camera zooms in in towns and out for world bosses (Matt McDaid via VG247). Spell effects grow with the skill's rank (Daniel Briggs). A Diablo IV VFX artist's own pipeline is Substance Designer textures, Maya meshes, shaders on particles and ribbons. Water had its own GDC talk ("H2O in H3LL", 2024). Diablo III's VFX (Julian Love, GDC 2013) are the lineage: meshes carrying one noise texture scrolled at several scales under a soft mask, fast tempo for aggression. Inferred from the frames: a perspective camera, steep (about 55 to 60° down), narrow field of view; the hero about 0.09 to 0.13 of the frame's height; dungeons nearly black (p50 luma 0.005 to 0.008) with candle and brazier pools; ground decals (poison, ripples, sigils) carry much of the effect.

**Path of Exile 2** (Grinding Gear, own engine). Primary: screen-space global illumination by radiance cascades (Alexander Sannikov, ExileCon 2023), so every effect and torch lights and bounces off the ground; the animators check poses with a game camera in Maya because legs vanish under the torso from above, exaggerate or "break" limbs to read, keep arcs "readable from all angles" (a slash lined up with the camera "loses all readability and power"), and say one or two frames change the feel (GGG's animation interview); a player has 40 to 80 basic attack clips; NPC idles are 20 s loops of small actions; PoE2 adds additive hit reactions, light and heavy stun told apart (interview, 2024); enemies telegraph (a shoulder drop before a lunge). Inferred: the hero at 0.13 to 0.16 of the frame; the highest fine detail of the set (0.23 to 0.28): sharp, dense ground (sticks, bones, rocks) with no blur; effects are emissive flipbooks and ember sprites over strong bloom, and dark decals (a shock-wave scorch) ground them.

**Last Epoch** (Eleventh Hour Games, Unity). Primary: a lighting pass over zones made early chapters darker and less "idyllic" (patch 0.7.7); skill effects now "contain a number of lights" so spells light the surfaces near them (forum, "graphical improvements for skills"). Inferred: the closest camera of the set (the hero up to 0.20 of the frame), saturated (0.38 to 0.65), ribbon slash trails and beams, real water with sun glints, dense ferns.

**V Rising** (Stunlock, Unity HDRP and DOTS). Primary: "the lighting works the same on both backgrounds and characters"; characters simplified for readability, the vampire's silhouette turned against the world's, natural animation "to make them more believable" (Johan Wahlbäck, dev update 3); volumetric fog is the effect that "influenced the game the most", used for mood, for spells and as line-of-sight; HDRP's physically based sky drives day and night with volume profiles per area and hour; a custom post pass darkens what is near the camera; 2,700 particle systems (Unity case study, Rasmus Höök, Filippa Arvidsson). Inferred: the hero 0.11 to 0.16 of the frame; night is blue moonlight with warm lamps; slashes are bright red arcs, saturation the highest of the set (0.72 to 0.75).

**Hades II** (Supergiant, own engine, 2D). Primary for Hades: characters are sculpted in ZBrush, retopologised in Maya, coloured in Substance over baked occlusion and curvature, then given hand-painted ink lines (Paige Carter); modelled and animated in Maya and rendered to 2D sprites (Supergiant); backgrounds are painted. Hades II's own pipeline is not published. What carries over: a 3D model and clip judged as a still at the game's size, line weight and value doing what lighting does in 3D, effects as bright, saturated additive shapes (magenta and cyan arcs) over a dark ground, the hero small (0.07 to 0.08) and readable by colour and outline alone.

## What they share

- **Camera**: steep perspective, narrow field of view, 15 to 25 m off; the hero 0.07 to 0.20 of the frame (median 0.12); the follow camera locked on the hero, zoomed by context, never free-rotated in combat. Every asset is checked at that camera (Diablo IV, GGG).
- **Characters and creatures**: big shapes, few small ones; silhouette and value against the ground first; detail maps or sharp textures so materials read at 100 to 200 px; many clips (dozens of attacks) because a clip's key pose is what reads; anticipation and follow-through exaggerated; additive hit reactions; telegraphs. Rim light and the fight's own lights lift characters off dark ground.
- **Effects**: an emissive core over bloom, flipbook fire and embers, ribbons for slashes, meshes with scrolling noise for streams and novas, decals for scorch, poison and sigils, and a light in every big effect.
- **Ambient**: fog (V Rising's volumetric, Diablo IV's haze), embers and dust in light pools, candles, braziers and lamps, wind in grass and reeds, water with specular glints.
- **Light and post**: dark frames with true blacks (night and dungeon p5 0.0002 to 0.007), several local lights per screen, strong contrast, sharp output (fine detail 0.13 to 0.28 in 10 of 14 frames; the four under are fog, snow and a bright torch bloom), saturated effects over a muted world.

## Where we depart

- **Models from Tripo, not artists.** Tripo's mesh and textures stand in for ZBrush, retopology and Substance. Its tells (melted small detail, mush hands, seams) must be judged at the game camera, which is forgiving: at 0.12 of the frame a hero is about 130 px tall. Each asset is still checked at two cameras, as Diablo IV does. A detail map in the material (Bevy has none built in; a small shader extension) is the cheap way to Diablo IV's sharpness.
- **Tripo's auto-rig and presets, not 40 to 80 bespoke clips.** One mesh a character with its gear, Tripo's skeleton and its preset clips (idle, walk, run, attack, hit, death) played as they are. Presets are made for eye level, so the judge must read each key pose from above; what cannot be re-posed is helped by what the ARPGs use besides the clip: hit-stop, a hit flash and an additive flinch, rim light, a telegraph effect, and an attack arc turned off the camera's axis by the attacker's facing.
- **Bevy, not a shipped renderer.** Bevy has the parts: PBR, point and spot lights with shadows, bloom, TAA, SSAO, volumetric fog, decals (clustered decals in recent Bevy) and particle crates (Hanabi). It has no radiance-cascade GI, so a spell lights the ground with a point light, not a bounce; and no sharpening after TAA by default (contrast-adaptive sharpening exists as a post pass), which the spike found its biggest gap.
- **Effects from shaders, not a VFX artist's library.** Flipbooks, ribbons and scrolling-noise meshes are made in code and free textures; judged on shape, core, light and fade.
- **Numbers, not taste.** Every still is measured against the references by the same script and judged by the rubric in `~/.cache/terra-review/iso-art/judge.md`, with the blind pairwise step the spike found more reliable than an absolute score.
- **Open question**: an ARPG follow camera (these five games) or the spike's fixed Baldur's Gate II view is a design choice for the developers; this experiment uses the follow camera because the references do, and decides nothing.

## Is it good enough, at what cost (2026-11-06, after jobs j1 to j49)

**Verdict: the pipeline is replicable and cheap per asset, and it holds a vertical-slice look (5 of 10 against shipped ARPGs' store frames) in three biomes; it is not near shippable (7, the approval bar), and the last ten jobs bought tenths, not points.** The limit is no longer the assets but the scene's surface density, colour and effect finish, which are an artist's hours in the shipped games and a job each here.

Scores, by a harsh judge against two store frames per biome (rubric and blind pairwise step in the skill's `judging.md`; a judge's absolute score drifts 0.3 between runs, so only a clear pairwise win counts):

| Biome | First | Best | What won the last clear round | Gaps the judge still names |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ruin courtyard, night (PoE2, V Rising) | 3.5 (r1) | 5.4 (r15) | the floor strewn with rubble, skulls and bones (fine detail 0.082 to 0.110) | scatter of one size without intent; surfaces flat (fine 0.11 against 0.14 to 0.23, saturation 0.53 against 0.70); embers and smoke landed but read only slightly |
| Forest glade, day (Last Epoch, PoE2) | 4.5 (r2) | 5.4 (r11) | lawn clumps over the flat lime lawns | the pond's glints a confetti of specks, no sun path; ribbon effects without a hot core; muddy mid-tones (p50 0.068 against 0.09 to 0.105) |
| Marsh, night (V Rising) | 3.5 (r1) | 4.9 (r11) | moonlit water and banks (rounds r4 to r9) | flat empty grass, no reed beds; water one milky blue; blacks lifted (p5 0.006 against 0.0002 to 0.004); mist lost twice and was taken out |

Assets, judged one by one at the game camera (assets-r1): a prop 6 to 7 (barrel 7, wagon 6, dead trees 6.5, sword 6.5), the knight 6, the creatures 4.5 to 5 (ogre, treants, tree monster, moose), small props 3 to 4 (torch, bone pile). What holds: Tripo's mesh and texture at 15 to 20 m, its biped rig and presets played as they are once `export` puts them in place and closes the loops, and the cut to the 24k creature cap under the same skeleton (`decimate`, j42). What does not: non-humans on the human preset (the treants crawl to a run), every non-biped rig gets a walk only (no idle, attack or death without paid motions), Tripo's glossy materials read plastic under a rim, and creatures share the ground's value until tinted.

The biomes' creatures: from the pipeline, the knight, ogre, tree monster, two treants and the moose (all from rigged projects already in the account, 0 credits); beside them, the repository's animated animals, not from this pipeline (deer, boar, fox, squirrel, finches, mallard, heron, owl, crows, hares, spiders, pigeons, fireflies and wisps).

Cost per asset (tools/iso-art/README.md has the table): a new figure is 85 to 95 Studio credits (concept 10 to 20, model 55, rig 20; 55 to 65 with a standard model), a new prop 65 to 75 (35 to 45), an asset adopted from a rigged project 0. At the Professional plan's $20 for 3,000 credits that is about $0.60 a figure and $0.45 a prop (Tripo's API sells credits at a cent each: $0.90 and $0.70). Minutes: animate 0.7 to 1.7, export 0.1 to 0.9, check under a second, the decimate splice a few minutes of Blender on the worker; model and rig untimed (none were affordable). No credits were spent: all night the account held 5 and its free images were used up.

The real cost is agent time: 52 jobs and leads, 326M tokens summed (mostly the same context read again each turn), 2.4M written, about 6 to 8M a job; a biome to its 5 took 7 to 13 jobs, and each judge round about 80k. A job lands one gap; a lead fits about seven rounds.

What a credit top-up would buy, at 85 to 95 a figure: 1,000 credits ($7 on the plan) is ten new rigged creatures with their presets, the one thing the night could not do. Cheapest first: the account's unrigged wolf, giant spider and skeleton at 20 each for the rig alone (forest and ruin), then new ones the biomes lack, a marsh hag or bog troll; then prompted motions (20 each) for an idle, attack and death on the quadrupeds. It would not move a biome's score: the judge's gaps are the ground, the water and the effects, which credits do not touch.
