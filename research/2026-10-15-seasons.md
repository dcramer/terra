# The lush forest through a year

2026-10-15. Question: drawn from the climate's fields and the past they leave (docs/climate.md, D327), does the lush forest read as the same wood in each season, and what does each look like, measured?

## How it was looked at

The vantage sheet (`cargo xtask vantage --time 13 --date <d>`) and the walk film (`cargo xtask film --time 13 --date <d>`) at four dates, all at 13:00, measured with `tools/sandbox/look.ts` against Sons of the Forest's summer forest (`sotf-forest-day`) and its snowy forest (`sotf-forest-snow`, made for this from the store's multiplayer trailer, D327). There is no autumn reference: autumn was judged against what a mixed Scots pine and oak wood looks like in temperate Europe. An adversarial review judged the first pass (4.5 of 10, not approved to show) and its gaps were worked on.

What the climate says at the camera on each date (the log's `climate:` line), at the start's 40° north near the sea:

## What each looks like

- **25 August**, as tuned: green oaks among Scots pines and spruces, dry ground. Unchanged by the seasons' code: the six views' look numbers match the day before's to three decimals.
- **15 October**: the summer wood just turning. Bracken goes gold first, the oaks olive at their edges; the floor still green. Mean brightness 0.098 against August's 0.120, the October sun being 17° lower at noon.
- **25 November**: the oaks tawny and half bare, the early ones bare, hazel and bilberry gone to twigs, bracken rust; fallen oak leaves warm brown on the floor under the broadleaves; the ground wet and dark. Red over green 1.13 against August's 0.94. Sky seen straight up from eye height 10% against 2.8%.
- **15 January**: half a metre of snow on the ground, on needles and the tops of limbs; bare oaks, the grass buried to its tips and straw where it shows, litter gone under it; the pines and spruces dark green. Sky seen straight up 23%. Against Sons of the Forest's snowy forest: mean 0.31 (0.37), darkest twentieth 0.052 (0.051), below 0.08 17% (16%); bluer and flatter there, under an overcast sky, where ours is sunny.

## Gaps left

- Rocks and fallen logs carry no snow: their moss material (`mossy.wesl`, the lush floor's) does not read the climate maps yet.
- The far forest's billboards and the far crowns' lumps keep their summer green; from the overlook the far wood does not turn.
- Bracken stands upright through deep snow, where real bracken is flattened by its first fall.
- Winter's air is summer's: no colder haze, and the far land carries no snow.
- The forest's crowns are planted as of their summer leaves and thinned for the sky's light by kind (`lush/seasons.rs`), found by the tree nearest under each crown.
