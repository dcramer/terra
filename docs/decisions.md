# Decisions

The source of truth for what is settled. Other docs must agree with this one.

**Who decides:** design decisions (gameplay, art, scope, what the game is) belong to the developers. Technical decisions (engine, architecture, tooling, workflow) are made by the agent, recorded here with the reason, and can be overruled. The Owner column says which is which.

**Everything here is provisional.** This is pre-production: the whole record is our current thinking, written down so we can keep working from it, and any of it may change. The statuses say how firm an item is relative to the others, not that anything is locked.

**Decided**: our current answer; don't reopen without a reason. **Leaning**: working assumption, untested or unconfirmed. **Open**: not chosen. **Rejected**: considered and dropped.

| D11 | Engine selection criteria | Design | Decided | Everything in Rust. | D12 | Engine | Tech | Decided | Bevy 0.19 as the framework, with our own voxel terrain ported from the parts of Voxel Tools we need. | D13 | Planet scale | Design | Open | Recommendation: about 2 km radius. Prototypes take radius as a parameter and default to 2 km. | Rendering cost was the same at 2 km and 20 km, so this is a feel and content choice. Physics at the larger size is unverified and may need a double-precision build. |
| D14 | Art direction | Design | Leaning | Forever Ago is the visual inspiration: simple finely-cut shapes, flat colour, warm light and cool shadow, haze, a small palette per place. It must not look chunky or Minecraft-like. | Named by the developers on 2026-10-10, who were unsure it could be achieved. The environments and props are what to take from it; its characters are a particular taste and ours may differ. The sandbox's outpost scene is the closest attempt so far. See [art.md](art.md). Earlier, No Man's Sky was the benchmark; it stays as the model for generating variety. |
| D15 | Astroneer | Design | Decided | Not a model for gameplay, and not the art target. It is only evidence that this tech is achievable by a small team. | Its gameplay isn't fun; its art is fine but not the bar. |
| D16 | Order of hostile life | Design | Leaning | Plants, then insect swarms, then animals. | Matches the fiction and the cost order. Not yet confirmed. |
| D17 | Second planet | Design | Leaning | Architect for it, don't build it until one planet is a complete game. | Scope. |
| D18 | Fail state | Design | Leaning | None. Setbacks only. | Follows from D3. |
| D20 | Does terraforming reshape solid ground? | Design | Decided | No. The solid ground only changes when players dig. | Decided by the developers, and confirmed on 2026-10-10. It also avoids replaying every edit when terrain changes. |
| D21 | How terrain edits are stored and synced | Tech | Decided | As operations (shape, position, size) applied in the server's order by every player, never as voxel data. | Passed the co-op spike test. Add snapshots of edited blocks for late joins once worlds have very many edits. |
| D22 | Languages | Tech | Decided | Rust for everything. No scripting language. | Follows from D11. The simulation was already Rust and measured about 90 times faster than GDScript. |
| D23 | Development workflow | Tech | Decided | One Cargo workspace. `cargo test` runs everything with no GPU. Screenshots come from the Linux build under WSL's software renderer; performance is measured on the Windows build, cross-compiled from WSL, and natively on macOS. Toolchain, Bevy and all dependencies are pinned. Debug builds keep line numbers only. | Each step was exercised in the toolchain check except macOS. Full debug information made a Windows build too large to load. |
| D24 | Water in the first version | Design | Decided | Sea level as a global value that rises as ice melts. Nothing more complicated: no flowing voxel water. | Decided by the developers. The engine offers no fluid simulation for smooth terrain. |
| D25 | Performance target | Tech | Decided | 60 fps at 1080p on a mid-range discrete GPU and a 4-core CPU, while hosting three other players. Every prototype reports frame times on the integrated GPU as a low-end check, measured in the real play loop and not only in scripted runs. Nothing ships on an untested performance assumption. The world must be able to show very many objects and let the player interact with any of them, and we look for ways to make that cheap everywhere. | Performance is a requirement. The developers can change the hardware target; it is a default until they do. |
| D26 | Where the simulation runs | Tech | Decided | On its own thread, at a requested rate, never inside the render frame. The game takes a short lock to read or change it. | Running it inside the frame dropped the demo to 7 fps at x100. On a thread, x100 (6,000 ticks a second) costs the frame nothing. |
| D27 | Build in Rust instead of Godot | Design | Decided | Yes. | Decided by the developers on 2026-10-06, accepting that it costs weeks before the game is back to where the Godot demo is. |
| D28 | How the code is split | Tech | Decided | Anything that must be the same for every player lives in plain Rust crates that do not depend on Bevy (`terra_gen`, `terra_sim`, `terra_voxel`, `terra_core`, `terra_net`). Only the applications, `terra_game` and the `terra_sandbox` test environment, use Bevy. | Fast tests with no GPU, a headless host, and Bevy's frequent releases touching one crate. The simulation crate already moved engines unchanged. |
| D29 | How state is shared | Tech | Decided | World changes are operations applied in the server's order (D21). The simulation runs only on the server and is sent as snapshots. Moving things are sent as positions and smoothed. | The simulation is small enough to send, which removes any need for players to compute identical floating-point results. |
| D30 | Maths in shared state | Tech | Decided | The `libm` crate for sine and similar functions, never the system maths library. A lint rejects the system functions. | Measured: the system library made Windows meshing six times slower; `libm` matched Linux. It also gives the same answer on every platform. |
| D31 | Bevy version policy | Tech | Decided | Pinned. Upgrade once the crates we depend on support the new release, not before. | Bevy changes its API every few months; 0.20 is in release candidate while most of the ecosystem is on 0.19. |
| D32 | Terrain meshing library | Tech | Leaning | The `transvoxel` crate to start; port Voxel Tools' mesher if re-meshing on detail changes proves costly. | The crate bakes seam stitching into each mesh. Closed by the terrain milestone. |
| D33 | Physics | Tech | Leaning | Avian for rigid bodies. The player and creatures query voxel data directly instead of using collision meshes. | Avian supports Bevy 0.19 and is active, but is untested here. The voxel-query approach worked in the Godot demo. |
| D34 | Networking library | Tech | Leaning | Our own messages over `renet`, with a Steam transport added later. No replication framework. | Our model needs only a reliable ordered channel and an unreliable one. Untested here. |
| D35 | UI | Tech | Leaning | Bevy's own UI for the game; `bevy_egui` for developer tools. | Untested here. Bevy's UI is younger than Godot's; reassess when the game needs real menus. |
| D36 | Solar-system coordinates | Tech | Leaning | One coordinate frame per planet. Adopt `big_space` when a second planet exists. | A 2 km planet needs nothing special. `big_space` currently trails Bevy by one release. |
| D37 | Repository tooling | Tech | Decided | One Cargo workspace at the root with a pinned compiler. `cargo xtask check` is the gate for every change, and CI runs it on Linux, macOS and Windows. `cargo xtask doctor` checks a machine, and `cargo xtask win` runs a Windows build on the GPU from WSL. `AGENTS.md` holds the instructions for every coding agent; `CLAUDE.md` only points to it. | Two developers on different platforms, and agents writing the code, need one command that says whether a change is acceptable. Tasks are written in Rust so they run the same everywhere. |
| D38 | Licence | Design | Decided | MIT, for everything in this repository. | Decided by the developers on 2026-10-10. The repository is public, and without a licence nobody else could legally use it. It also matches Voxel Tools, which we port from. |
| D39 | What the engine must do regardless of gameplay | Design | Leaning | Voxel planets; a seamless transition between ground and space; resources harvested in an organic way; procedural generation almost everywhere. | What the developers currently expect to want, stated on 2026-10-10 so engine work has something to aim at. They have since said it is all still to be determined. |
| D40 | Materials in voxels | Tech | Leaning | Voxels carry a material as well as a shape, from the first version of the terrain. Removing terrain reports the volume of each material taken. | Provisional, because D39 is. The technical reading of "organic harvesting" in D39: resources are in the ground and digging yields them. Adding materials to a shape-only terrain later would mean redoing its storage, meshing and edits. Voxel Tools already does this, so there is a design to port. |
| D41 | Generation toolkit | Tech | Leaning | One shared crate for seeds, noise and repeatable random streams, giving the same result on every machine. Built first. Everything generated is a function of the seed and a position; only changes are stored or sent. | Provisional, because D39 is. Follows from D8 and D39. Terrain, materials, scattering, plants and sky all need it, and D21 and D29 depend on every machine generating the same world. |
| D42 | Engine build order | Tech | Leaning | A small foundation (shared contracts, then the generation toolkit), then four tracks in parallel: terrain; a walkable stand-in world; things on the ground; networking. See [engine.md](engine.md). | A working plan, not a commitment. A stand-in planet computed from the terrain formula lets everything except the terrain itself be built before voxels exist. Separate crates keep parallel work from colliding. |
| D43 | What can be harvested | Design | Leaning | Possibly rock and ore by mining, trees by chopping, grass by cutting. The developers are not sure yet. Also open: whether things regrow, and whether everything can be harvested. | The engine supports all three kinds (ground, objects, cover) through one contract, so this can stay open. See [engine.md](engine.md). |
| D44 | Melting into pools of water | Design | Open | The developers may want things to melt into pools. Recommendation: allow it by treating ice as a layer on the ground and water as a level that differs by place, with no flow. | Would extend D24, which has one global sea. Compatible with D20 because the ground itself never changes. The engine asks for the water level by position either way (D45). |
| D45 | Contracts for harvest and water | Tech | Leaning | Every harvest is an operation that returns a yield. Water is asked for by position, not read as one global number. | Provisional. Keeps D43 and D44 open at no cost: one sea is a constant answer to the same question pools would answer. |
| D46 | A sandbox for experiments | Tech | Leaning | `terra_sandbox` is the test environment for graphics and utility work: a window on the stand-in planet, a free camera, live tweaks, screenshots and frame timing, and experiments switched on from the command line. It is not the game. | The developers wanted somewhere to experiment that is neither gameplay nor terrain. It also gives the Rust build its first window and its measuring harness. Whether its parts later become the game's shell is undecided. |
| D47 | Numerous world objects | Tech | Leaning | Objects such as trees and rocks are computed from the seed and their location, not stored. Only changes are remembered, as operations. Only nearby ones are drawn, baked together by area, and interaction is by lookup. | Tried in the sandbox: about a million choppable trees on a 2 km planet for 216 KB of worst-case state and 3.4 ms a frame on the RTX 5090. Not yet tried on voxels, with heavier objects, or in motion. See [research/forest-scale-test.md](../research/forest-scale-test.md). |
| D48 | Construction | Design | Leaning | Subnautica 2's base building is the inspiration: rooms you shape and resize, with freedom over windows, instead of fixed modules snapped together. | Named by the developers on 2026-10-10. A first experiment exists. After playing it the developers called it a good start but clearly not the thing: there is no choosing a part to build and no cycling through a part's variants, among other gaps. It is also boxy, which they have said they do not want. At their request the tech demo then gained a dial of parts with variants, furniture as placeable items, controls after Subnautica 2's, and an optional grid that items snap to. They want new rooms to snap to existing ones, which the tile model gives; a room placed across another to extend it; and interior walls as a part, indoors only. On terrain their leaning is that a foundation may go into the ground and anything else that would clip is shown red and refused. What the parts are and what they cost are still theirs to settle. See [research/subnautica-2-building.md](../research/subnautica-2-building.md). |
| D49 | How we work from here | Design | Leaning | Larger design docs come before implementation. Experiments stay small and are not a substitute for a design. | The developers said on 2026-10-10 that it would probably be better to work on larger design docs than to implement things one after another. |
| D50 | Style of play | Design | Leaning | Like No Man's Sky or Subnautica, broadly: a player model, walking around with tools to do tasks, collecting resources, taking actions such as building. | Stated by the developers on 2026-10-10 as the rough style. The choices this leaves open are listed in [player.md](player.md). |
| D51 | Server-first multiplayer | Design | Decided | The game always talks to a server, which decides everything and sends each player what is near them. For friends playing together the server runs inside one player's game; for a persistent world it runs alone. Both are the same code. Make a handful of players work first, then measure larger worlds before promising them. | Agreed by the developers on 2026-10-10 as the safest way. It keeps a large persistent world possible without building all of it now. See [multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md). |
| D52 | How many players a world holds | Design | Open | The developers would ideally like 100 concurrent players on a persistent server. Whether that is a target or a ceiling to leave room for is not settled. | Raised on 2026-10-10. It would replace the limit of four in D2. The questions it raises about planet size, resources and ownership are in [multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md). |
| D53 | Collision | Tech | Open | Required: collision shapes that work with voxel terrain, bases and objects, for players, creatures and aiming. The developers want collision boxes on everything, used for placing things as well as for walking. How is not designed. The leaning in D33 (a physics engine for shapes, with players asking voxel data directly) is untested. | Asked for by the developers on 2026-10-10. The sandbox's player uses stand-in arithmetic: circles for trees, boxes for placed items, a grid for walls. See [engine.md](engine.md). |
| D54 | Models, for now | Tech | Leaning | Free CC0 low-polygon packs (Quaternius), read for their shapes only and given our own colours in code, so every model shares one palette and one shader. | Made the outpost scene possible in a day with no artist, and keeps the door open to replacing any model without touching the look. Untested: whether stock shapes can carry the game's own identity, and what a dead planet is built from. Sources and licences are in [THIRD_PARTY.md](../THIRD_PARTY.md). |
| D55 | Carrying and cost | Design | Leaning | The player carries raw items, and those let them build anything they are capable of building. There is an inventory, and an action bar that items are put on to be used. | Said by the developers on 2026-10-10 with "I think". The sandbox has a stand-in: five raw items, a cost for every buildable, a full refund, and a cutter tool to gather more. What the raw items are, what "capable" means and what taking apart gives back are open. See [construction.md](construction.md). |
| D56 | How construction is put together | Design | Leaning | Buildables are entries in a list; where one may go is a named rule; a piece's look comes from what it is next to, so that rounding at corners and the like apply to anything of that kind; material is a property. Rooms are plain boxes. | The developers asked on 2026-10-10 for a system that is really easy to extend, with details that morph by what a piece is connected to. The shape is a proposal in [construction.md](construction.md) for them to correct. |
| D19 | Three.js as primary stack | Tech | Rejected | | It is a renderer, not an engine; the CPU-heavy core would need WASM anyway; the browser is a poor co-op host; no precedent at this scope. |

## Log

- **2026-10-04**: Initial set. D1 to D8 confirmed in conversation. Three.js rejected after a second look at scaling.
- **2026-10-04**: Browser demoted to nice-to-have (D10). Engine criteria set (D11). With the browser no longer a constraint, the engine lean moved from Bevy to Godot with Voxel Tools (D12). Planet scale deferred to the spike (D13). No Man's Sky named the art benchmark, two treatments to be tried (D14). Astroneer dropped as a model (D15).
- **2026-10-04**: Spike tests for co-op digging and terrain change run. Co-op digging by operations passed (D21). Reshaping ground under edits is costly, which opened D20.
- **2026-10-04**: The developers delegated technical decisions to the agent and kept design decisions. On the spike's evidence, D9, D12 and D21 were closed and D22 and D23 added. D13, D20 and D24 remain open design decisions with recommendations.
- **2026-10-04**: Performance made a requirement (D25). Measured rendering on the integrated GPU and the simulation tick in both languages; D22 reversed from GDScript to Rust on the result.
- **2026-10-04**: The developers decided D20 (terraforming does not reshape the ground) and D24 (water is sea level plus melting, nothing complicated).
- **2026-10-04**: A developer played the demo and found it laggy. Cause: the simulation ran inside the frame and my instructions said to run it at x100. Moved it to its own thread (D26). The developers asked whether to build a custom Rust engine; recorded as D27.
- **2026-10-06**: The developers decided to build everything in Rust (D11, D27). Engine changed from Godot to Bevy with our own terrain (D12). D22 and D23 rewritten to match. Architecture decisions D28 to D31 made; library picks D32 to D36 recorded as Leaning until exercised. Research moved to `research/`.
- **2026-10-10**: Repository scaffolding added (D37): root workspace, pinned compiler, `cargo xtask check` and `doctor`, CI, `AGENTS.md`. `terra_sim` moved to `crates/` and switched to `libm` (D30).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers chose the MIT licence (D38).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers named what the engine must do regardless of gameplay (D39). From that: materials in voxels (D40), a generation toolkit built first (D41), and the engine split into a foundation and four parallel tracks (D42).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers confirmed the ground is not reshaped (D20), floated melting into pools of water (D44) and sketched what might be harvested (D43). The engine's contracts are shaped to keep both open (D45).
- **2026-10-10**: Foundation built: `terra_gen` (seeds, random streams, noise, with outputs pinned and checked on three platforms) and `terra_core` (the shared contracts and a stand-in planet).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers said the engine plan and everything around it is still to be determined. D39 to D42 and D45, which I had recorded as Decided, are now Leaning, and the record as a whole is marked provisional. The foundation crates built on them are small and general, and stay as a starting point.
- **2026-10-10**: Sandbox built (D46): the first Rust window, with the stand-in planet, a hot-reloading ground shader, live tweaks, scripted screenshots and frame timing, and one experiment that scatters and harvests rocks.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers named Forever Ago as the visual inspiration, and ruled out a chunky or Minecraft-like look (D14). Art direction rewritten around it, with a first look test from the sandbox.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers asked that performance with very many interactive objects be a baseline concern, and that we look for ways to make things cheap everywhere (D25). A forest test in the sandbox tried one such pattern (D47).
- **2026-10-10**: Construction experiment built after the developers named Subnautica 2 as the inspiration (D48). They then asked that larger design docs come before more implementation (D49), and noted that nothing can really be tested until a player can walk around the world.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers described the rough style of play (D50). Drafted [player.md](player.md) around it, as the first of the larger design docs, with sixteen open questions and no code.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers raised supporting 100 concurrent players on a persistent server (D51). Drafted [multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md) on what that changes. D2's player count is under review until they decide.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers agreed the game is server-first (D51). The target number of players is split out as its own open question (D52). "Host" became "server" throughout D21, D29 and the tech plan.
- **2026-10-10**: At the developers' request, built a really simple tech demo: a player figure with a builder tool, walking on the stand-in planet and through the base (the sandbox's `play` experiment). They also asked for real collision against voxels and everything else, recorded as an open requirement (D53).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers said the simple demo looked bad and asked for a proof of concept that could impress someone. The `play` experiment became an outpost scene: fine ground, water, free models in our colours (D54), grass and wind, a base with trim and lit rooms, an animated figure, and on-screen prompts for the builder. They also said that what to take from Forever Ago is its environments and props, not its characters (D14).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers played the outpost scene and called it a good start. On construction (D48) they said it is clearly not Subnautica 2's system yet: a player should select the part to build and be able to cycle its variants, among other things. Recorded; no design doc for construction exists yet.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers asked for the building menu to be implemented in the tech demo directly: a dial of parts as in Rust or Subnautica, with the example base's furniture as buildable items, and items that are solid. Built in the `play` experiment. The catalogue of parts is a stand-in, and items live in the sandbox, not yet in `terra_build`'s operations (D21).
- **2026-10-10**: The developers asked for controls that feel like Subnautica 2's, collision boxes on everything including for placement (D53), and an optional grid that things snap to, with rooms snapping to rooms (D48). Built in the `play` experiment. Which of Subnautica 2's controls could be confirmed is in [research/subnautica-2-building.md](../research/subnautica-2-building.md). Whether rooms may clip into terrain is open.
- **2026-10-10**: After trying it the developers asked for a visible grid and the small comforts of good building games, a room placed across another to extend it, interior walls as a simple indoor-only part, and said only a foundation should clip into terrain (D48). `terra_build` gained interior walls and merging rooms; the rest is in the `play` experiment. A fault where the mouse escaped the window was fixed.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers corrected the interior walls: no interior rooms, just plain walls, with windows and doors as their own parts cut into interior and outside walls alike. The grid toggle moved to X.
- **2026-10-10**: The developers asked for rooms to be plain boxes with no lights, for details that morph by what a piece is next to (rounded corners, flat where segments continue), for the whole system to be really easy to extend, and for an inventory of raw items that building draws on (D55, D56). Rooms made plain. Drafted [construction.md](construction.md) with what they have said, a proposed shape, the gap and eight open questions.
- **2026-10-10**: Built the stand-in for D55 in the `play` experiment: an inventory of raw items, an action bar of tools, a cost for every buildable with a refund on taking apart, and a cutter that gathers from trees, rocks and bushes.
