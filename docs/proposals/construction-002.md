# Construction 002: one way to draw every construction material

Status: **Decided** (D290): the goal is the developers' (2026-10-14), the method below the agent's. It recommends one way, and says how to get there from the four ways the game draws materials today. It changes nothing in [construction-001](construction-001.md)'s rules: the cube stays the truth, and only how a material is drawn is in question. The research is [research/uniform-materials.md](../../research/uniform-materials.md). What it must meet to scale to hundreds of materials is [its last section](#hundreds-of-materials).

## What it answers

The developers, 2026-10-14: "again, my goal is to have uniformity. one way we do these construction materials (or maybe one per type, floors roof, etc). so if we generate them everywhere, we have to know that will solve all problems. same with models. and they need to be efficient when placing materials to quickly swap to the right visual etc". Earlier, D251: "i think we want hand made models? enshrouded does this, we are doing what enshrouded does. ideally the same system used for roof materials … is whats also the wall material (and floor materials…)"; D211: "ON PAR WITH ENSHROUDED … when two cubes touch, it somehow blends it"; and of the modelled log beside the generated ones: "those lm ones look way better imo", "tho it seems its mostly a texture thing?"

**Today it is four ways**: block kinds (stone, brick, earth, lime-wash) as their cubes' surface dressed by role with pieces a script makes (D197, D215); logs and hewn timber generated by code (D200, D262), with decks, stairs and boarded shapes also made by code; roofs as their cubes' sloped surface with modelled ridge, verge and eave pieces (D258); wattle and daub as a surface with a frame of modelled members (D236).

## The candidates

| | How it draws everything | A swap of material |
|---|---|---|
| **A: the surface only** | One mesher for every kind: the cubes' surface, every material a texture set (colour, normal, height, in texture arrays, picked by a material ID per vertex); log courses, shingles, bricks and thatch in the textures and their parallax; edges by neighbour rules in the mesher or shader. No geometry per material | Writes an ID: the same mesh, another layer |
| **B: the surface and a kit of placed pieces** | A as the base, and each material a kit of hand-made pieces, one or more for each role (a log along a course and its end, a quoin, a lintel, a coping, a ridge, a verge, an eave), placed by one rule engine from the cubes; a kind with no face of its own (logs) leaves its surface empty | IDs for the surface; the pieces round the change looked up again |
| **C: pieces for everything** | Valheim's and Fortnite's way: a modelled piece per material, shape and neighbourhood, walls and roofs as panels | A piece per piece |
| **D: one way per type** | A for walls, floors and roof coverings; modelled modules only for long members (logs, frames, decks); no role pieces | As A, and as B for members |
| **E: a hand-made reference, baked onto what the rules lay** | Each material modelled once by hand (a log, a patch of wall, a run of shingles), its look baked into texture sets; the rules lay all geometry, the surface and swept members and profiles, wearing the bake. Nothing placed | As A: the same geometry wears another bake, where the two kinds' shapes agree |

**What each cannot do.** A cannot draw whatever a material's look is in its edges or members: logs, frames, framed openings, quoins, copings, a ridge's capping. C cannot carve, take any shape or thickness, or hide its grid. D is A with members and keeps A's failures on stone and roofs. E draws every case, but only as well as a swept shape can: a log's outline, a branch stub, a ridge's capping and a quoin's broken stone are not in it. B draws every case, at the price of a kit of pieces per material and more triangles.

## Recommendation: B and E as one system

B and E are not rivals: they are the two things a role can ask of one kit. **Every material is one kind of data, drawn by one engine:**

1. **A material is a set of hand-made reference models**: a log, a patch of its wall, a run of its shingles, its quoin, its ridge, modelled in Tripo or by hand and cleaned up in Blender (D251). **Its texture sets are baked from them**, so its surface, its swept members and its placed pieces share one look and one grade (D14's "one kit, one grade").
2. **One rule engine lays every role from the cubes.** For each role, the kit's data says one of two things: **wear** the bake on geometry the rules shape (the surface over any shape; a log, plank, rail or coping along any run, cut to its length), or **place** a reference piece whole, where its outline is the look (a log's sawn end and stubs, a ridge's capping, a verge, a quoin, a lintel's stone). Walls, floors and roofs differ only in their roles.
3. **One mesher for every surface**, in texture arrays with a material ID per vertex: a section's surfaces are one draw, its placed pieces one draw per kit's atlas.
4. **A swap is a lookup.** Where no cube's solidity changes, the roles stay; only the changed cubes' layers and the pieces round them are looked up again, so a change is on screen in a frame or two, not after the base is laid again.

Why: it is the only arrangement that meets every case; it is what every good-looking builder found does (Enshrouded's fill and trims, Space Engineers' panels and edge models, The Sims' roofs and trims); and it answers the log. The developers asked whether the modelled log's lead is "mostly a texture thing": the bake test says the texture is most of it, since generated logs in its bark come most of the way; the spike says the geometry is the rest, since the same texture on a flat surface loses every end. Wearing the bake keeps what the rules give (any length, ends, corners, carving); placing pieces adds what only a model has. "One way" in the developers' sense: a new material is reference models, their bake and a kit file, never code.

**Theirs to decide.** Wearing a bake on geometry the rules shape is code-made geometry, which D177 and D251 ask to avoid and D200 allowed for logs. Here every look comes from a hand-made model; only lengths and cuts come from the rules. Whether that meets "hand made models" is the developers' call (question 2). And how long a change of material takes the player: today it is a construction job, taken out and built again step by step (D232, D139), 5 to 10 held steps a box, where Satisfactory's Customizer and V Rising's wallpaper repaint at once (question 3). The drawing serves either.

## From four ways to one

Each step lands checked by the kit sheet, `cargo xtask kits`, `sections` and the artifacts ratchet (D287), and is measured before the next.

1. **One kit format**: `pattern` becomes rules over roles, each role `wear` or `place`, and `timber`'s numbers become a member's section and its bake. Today's kits rewritten as data, drawing nothing differently.
2. **A bake from reference models in `tools/kits`**: the log agent's bake (the Tripo log onto a cylinder, tiled, its normal steepened, height from its lumps) as the pipeline's general step, for a member, a patch of wall and a run of covering.
3. **Logs first**: the generated logs wear the modelled log's bake (the test's middle column); then, if the developers want its outline, its ends and stubs placed as pieces (D200's comparison, now shown).
4. **Every other kit's bake**: hewn timber, planks and decks, wattle's oak, stone's and earth's dressings, roof boards; their placed pieces modelled where the outline is the look (quoins, lintels, copings' ends, ridges), as D258 began.
5. **Surfaces in texture arrays**: one surface material with a layer per texture set, its parallax and masks, after the ground's arrays (`ground.wesl`).
6. **The swap as a lookup**: roles kept per section and laid again only where solidity changes; measured from the hammer to the screen in `cargo xtask rules`.
7. **Code deleted** once nothing uses it: the separate drawing of decks, stairs and boarded shapes, which become members wearing their bake, and the numbers-to-mesh path of `tools/kits`.

Steps 1, 5 and 6 are technical and go first; steps 2 to 4 change looks, and each waits on the developers' approval of its kit.

## What the developers settled, and what is still theirs

On 2026-10-14 they set the goal: "what im aiming for, again, is it being pretty straight forward.. and simple, to add new materials, that way we could have 100s of them. models are fine, generated is fine, whatever is going to make them visually and technically work the best. it also has to be performant"; and asked "can each material be abstracted into like effectively data files or are we having to micro manage a bunch of complex things?". So:

1. **B and E as one system** is adopted (D290), the method the agent's, judged on visual and technical merit.
2. **Rule-shaped geometry wearing a hand-made model's bake** is allowed ("models are fine, generated is fine"), which amends D177's and D251's "not geometry generated by code" for materials.
3. **How long a change of material takes the player**, left to the agent with the rest ("Like get it done"): between two materials of one pattern, a repaint at once, the old given back and the new taken in one act, as Satisfactory's Customizer and V Rising's wallpaper repaint; across patterns, where the structure itself changes, the step-by-step job of D232 (D298).
4. **Logs** wear the modelled log's bake on the generated logs, as the developers liked the modelled log "mostly for its texture"; the modelled log placed whole stays a per-material override (`timber.module`). With it, two fixes to how a log weathers: its bark lost toward its ends a corner at a time (per-vertex weathering, as it now wears one bake end to end), and the side the weather beats on worked out per wall from which face is outside and which inside (rooms in three dimensions, D261), not one way for a whole base.
5. **Everything else left open was left to the agent** ("Like get it done", 2026-10-14): the nine modelled pieces on the approval sheet are accepted as the shared reference pieces (D258), and the terrain shares this material library (D252), its packs and arrays made so the terrain can read them.

## Hundreds of materials

Hard requirements, so that a new material is minutes of work and data only, and a base of a hundred materials draws as cheaply as one of ten. Shipped games that carry hundreds of materials do it the same few ways: a **material ID** per voxel or vertex into **texture arrays** (Teardown's palettes, 7 Days to Die's 255 paints from one array, Enshrouded's building materials as voxel material indices, inferred from its galleries); **material instances** that override a few parameters of a parent (Unreal's material instances, Minecraft's block models naming a `parent`); shared **trim sheets** and modular pieces retextured per material (Space Engineers' skins, The Sims' swatches); **bindless** materials so many materials batch into one draw (Bevy's own `StandardMaterial` since 0.16); and **texture streaming** or **virtual texturing** so only what is near is resident (Unreal's streaming pool, Far Cry's adaptive virtual textures).

1. **A small fixed set of patterns.** Four: **block** (a block kind's cubes as one surface: stone, brick, earth, plaster), **coursed** (runs of members: logs, hewn timber), **roof** (a covering over roof cubes) and **frame** (a block surface with a frame of members laid over it: wattle and daub). Each owns all the hard logic once: corners, joins, openings, gables, ghosts, part-built, detail by distance and swaps. Its defaults are a file, `assets/data/kits/patterns/<pattern>.ron`: its rules' numbers, its roles and its shared pieces.
2. **No code is customised by material** (the developers, 2026-10-14: "thats why its important that these kits are cleanly designed so each one doesnt have to be customized by the game"): no game or layout code names a material or a kit, branches on a kind's number, or holds a per-material or per-piece offset, scale or fudge; a test fails on any material's or pack's name or a kind by number in `terra_build` and `terra_game`. A settlement's builders' roles name their materials in data (`assets/data/settlement.ron`).
3. **A material is a small file**, `assets/data/kits/<name>.ron`, on one screen, 10 to 20 lines: its id (what its cubes carry), its pattern, its reference pack, its cost, its weight against other materials, and only what it overrides. It is a material instance: its pattern's file, then its pack, then its own lines, merged field by field and entry by entry (a role's `Profile(extend: 0.22)` changes only that number). A mistake names the file.
4. **Pieces are shared per pattern** and wear the material's bake: one coping, plinth, quoin, lintel and member set for the block pattern, drawn in the material's `Dressed` and `Hewn` texture sets; one member set for the coursed pattern; one rafter and batten set for roofs. So a hundred materials do not need a hundred sets of models. A material's own pieces (thatch's sedge ridge, slate's ridge tiles) are an optional override in its pack. Every placed piece keeps the model contract (D303): its real size stated and held, its origin at its anchor (where the layout seats it: a roof's deck or covering, a wall's face, its grid place), glTF's axes (+X along, +Y up, +Z across), and its `start` and `end` sockets; so one piece lands right on any material with no offset per piece or per material, and the kit checker fails a model made to two sizes.
5. **One command makes a reference pack** from a source, a CC0 scan, a Tripo reference model or a generator (`cargo xtask pack`): it fetches or bakes the texture set at the budget, checks it (sizes, maps, the kit checker), credits it in `assets/materials/README.md`, and writes the pack (`assets/data/packs/<name>.ron`: its texture sets, what its colour is multiplied by, its mean colours for far off, its relief) and the material's file with the next free id, which is all the hammer's picker and the kit sheet need to show it.
6. **Performance with hundreds**:
   - **One draw per pattern per section**, whatever the number of materials: every texture set is a layer of four texture arrays (colour, normal, occlusion-roughness-metal, height), each corner carries its layer, and one material draws them, with parallax per layer. A pack's own modelled-piece atlas, an override, is a draw of its own.
   - **A texture budget per material**: each texture set a layer of 1024 by 1024 with mipmaps, compressed (BC1 colour and ARM, BC5 normal, BC4 height) where the GPU can, about 3.4 MB a set.
   - **Residency**: a layer is on the GPU only while a laid section uses it, written into a slot of the arrays as it comes and the slot freed when it goes, a table from layer to slot read by the shader.
   - **A swap is a lookup** where no cube's solidity changes and the two materials' patterns lay the same shapes: the changed cubes' corners take the new layer in the meshes already drawn, on screen within one or two frames; otherwise the sections are laid again as now.
   - **Measured**: draws and frame time before and after on both GPUs; a village of a hundred materials against the same village in ten.
7. **Wear or place, per role, by visual merit:**

| Pattern | Wears the bake (geometry the rules shape) | Places a reference piece whole | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block | Faces, floors, copings, plinths, sills, lintels, fascias, treads (profiles swept to any run) | Quoins and jamb stones | The surface alone lost every lintel, quoin and plinth ([candidates](../../research/images/uniform-candidates-2026-10-14.jpg), stone A); swept profiles take any length and corner; a corner stone's broken outline is its look |
| Coursed | Every log, beam, plank and deck along its run, its sawn ends | Nothing by default; the modelled whole log as an override | The generated logs in the modelled log's bark came most of the way and kept ends and corners ([logs](../../research/images/logs-texture-test-2026-10-14.jpg), middle column); the developers liked the modelled log "mostly for its texture" |
| Roof | The covering, rafters, battens | Ridge, hip, valley, eave, verge, flashing | Without them a ridge is a crease and a verge reads thin (candidates, roofs A); the modelled ridges and verges read (the approval sheet) |
| Frame | The infill's face; posts, plates, rails and braces (a member module scaled to each) | Nothing | A member is long and straight; its look is its texture (the approval sheet's oak member) |
