# Construction 002: one way to draw every construction material

Status: **Open** (D290), for the developers. It recommends one way, and says how to get there from the four ways the game draws materials today. It changes nothing in [construction-001](construction-001.md)'s rules: the cube stays the truth, and only how a material is drawn is in question. The research is [research/uniform-materials.md](../../research/uniform-materials.md).

## What it answers

The developers, 2026-10-14: "again, my goal is to have uniformity. one way we do these construction materials (or maybe one per type, floors roof, etc). so if we generate them everywhere, we have to know that will solve all problems. same with models. and they need to be efficient when placing materials to quickly swap to the right visual etc". Earlier, D251: "i think we want hand made models? enshrouded does this, we are doing what enshrouded does. ideally the same system used for roof materials … is whats also the wall material (and floor materials…)"; D211: "ON PAR WITH ENSHROUDED … when two cubes touch, it somehow blends it"; and of the modelled log beside the generated ones: "those lm ones look way better imo", "tho it seems its mostly a texture thing?"

**Today it is four ways**: block kinds (stone, brick, earth, lime-wash) as their cubes' surface dressed by role with pieces a script makes (D197, D215); logs and hewn timber generated by code (D200, D262), with decks, stairs and boarded shapes also made by code; roofs as their cubes' sloped surface with modelled ridge, verge and eave pieces (D258); wattle and daub as a surface with a frame of modelled members (D236).

## The candidates

| | How it draws everything | A swap of material |
|---|---|---|
| **A: the surface only** | One mesher for every kind: the cubes' surface, every material a texture set (colour, normal, height, in texture arrays, picked by a material ID per vertex); log courses, shingles, bricks and thatch in the textures and their parallax; edges by neighbour rules in the mesher or shader. No geometry per material | Writes an ID: the same mesh, another layer |
| **B: the surface and a kit of placed pieces** | A as the base, and each material a kit of hand-made pieces, one or more for each role (a log along a course and its end, a quoin, a lintel, a coping, a ridge, a verge, an eave), placed by one rule engine from the cubes; a kind with no face of its own (logs) leaves its surface empty | IDs for the surface; the pieces round the change looked up again |
| **C: pieces for everything** | Valheim's and Fortnite's way: a modelled piece per material, shape and neighbourhood, walls and roofs as panels | A piece per piece |
| **D: one way per type** | A for walls, floors and roof coverings; modelled modules only for long members (logs, frames, decks); no role pieces | As A, and as B for members |
| **E: a hand-made reference, baked onto what the rules lay** | Each material modelled once by hand (a log, a patch of wall, a run of shingles), its look baked into texture sets; the rules lay all geometry, the surface and swept members and profiles, wearing the bake. Nothing placed | As A: the same geometry wears another bake, where the two kinds' shapes agree |

**What each cannot do.** A cannot draw whatever a material's look is in its edges or members: logs, frames, framed openings, quoins, copings, a ridge's capping. C cannot carve, take any shape or thickness, or hide its grid. D is A with members and keeps A's failures on stone and roofs. E draws every case, but only as well as a swept shape can: a log's outline, a branch stub, a ridge's capping and a quoin's broken stone are not in it. B draws every case, at the price of a kit of pieces per material and more triangles.

## Recommendation: B and E as one system

B and E are not rivals: they are the two things a role can ask of one kit. **Every material is one kind of data, drawn by one engine:**

1. **A material is a set of hand-made reference models**: a log, a patch of its wall, a run of its shingles, its quoin, its ridge, modelled in Tripo or by hand and cleaned up in Blender (D251). **Its texture sets are baked from them**, so its surface, its swept members and its placed pieces share one look and one grade (D14's "one kit, one grade").
2. **One rule engine lays every role from the cubes.** For each role, the kit's data says one of two things: **wear** the bake on geometry the rules shape (the surface over any shape; a log, plank, rail or coping along any run, cut to its length), or **place** a reference piece whole, where its outline is the look (a log's sawn end and stubs, a ridge's capping, a verge, a quoin, a lintel's stone). Walls, floors and roofs differ only in their roles.
3. **One mesher for every surface**, in texture arrays with a material ID per vertex: a section's surfaces are one draw, its placed pieces one draw per kit's atlas.
4. **A swap is a lookup.** Where no cube's solidity changes, the roles stay; only the changed cubes' layers and the pieces round them are looked up again, so a change is on screen in a frame or two, not after the base is laid again.

Why: it is the only arrangement that meets every case; it is what every good-looking builder found does (Enshrouded's fill and trims, Space Engineers' panels and edge models, The Sims' roofs and trims); and it answers the log. The developers asked whether the modelled log's lead is "mostly a texture thing": the bake test says the texture is most of it, since generated logs in its bark come most of the way; the spike says the geometry is the rest, since the same texture on a flat surface loses every end. Wearing the bake keeps what the rules give (any length, ends, corners, carving); placing pieces adds what only a model has. "One way" in the developers' sense: a new material is reference models, their bake and a kit file, never code.

**Theirs to decide.** Wearing a bake on geometry the rules shape is code-made geometry, which D177 and D251 ask to avoid and D200 allowed for logs. Here every look comes from a hand-made model; only lengths and cuts come from the rules. Whether that meets "hand made models" is the developers' call (question 2). And how long a change of material takes the player: today it is a construction job, taken out and built again step by step (D232, D139), 5 to 10 held steps a box, where Satisfactory's Customizer and V Rising's wallpaper repaint at once (question 3). The drawing serves either.

## From four ways to one

Each step lands checked by the kit sheet, `cargo xtask kits`, `sections` and the artifacts ratchet (D287), and is measured before the next.

1. **One kit format**: `pattern` becomes rules over roles, each role `wear` or `place`, and `timber`'s numbers become a member's section and its bake. Today's kits rewritten as data, drawing nothing differently.
2. **A bake from reference models in `tools/kits`**: the log agent's bake (the Tripo log onto a cylinder, tiled, its normal steepened, height from its lumps) as the pipeline's general step, for a member, a patch of wall and a run of covering.
3. **Logs first**: the generated logs wear the modelled log's bake (the test's middle column); then, if the developers want its outline, its ends and stubs placed as pieces (D200's comparison, now shown).
4. **Every other kit's bake**: hewn timber, planks and decks, wattle's oak, stone's and earth's dressings, roof boards; their placed pieces modelled where the outline is the look (quoins, lintels, copings' ends, ridges), as D258 began.
5. **Surfaces in texture arrays**: one surface material with a layer per texture set, its parallax and masks, after the ground's arrays (`ground.wesl`).
6. **The swap as a lookup**: roles kept per section and laid again only where solidity changes; measured from the hammer to the screen in `cargo xtask rules`.
7. **Code deleted** once nothing uses it: the separate drawing of decks, stairs and boarded shapes, which become members wearing their bake, and the numbers-to-mesh path of `tools/kits`.

Steps 1, 5 and 6 are technical and go first; steps 2 to 4 change looks, and each waits on the developers' approval of its kit.

## Open for the developers

1. **Adopt B and E as one system** (D290), with this migration.
2. **Whether rule-shaped geometry wearing a hand-made model's bake counts as hand-made** (D177, D251): if not, B alone, every member a placed piece.
3. **How long a change of material takes the player**: a construction job as now (D232), or a repaint at once.
4. **Logs**: the generated logs in the modelled log's bark, or the modelled log placed (D200), as the two sheets compare.
