# Settings: how any theme works

Part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89): one way for any realm's theme to work, medieval, machine, wild, gothic or another, so that each feels like itself, none is better than the others, a new one is data rather than new code, and any number of generated realms (D17) still fit one lore. This page owns realms' themes and cultures; their physical ground, water and size are in [worlds.md](worlds.md). The developers called it roughly on point on 2026-10-11 (D116, Leaning); their answers are folded in below.

## What the developers have said

- Realms may differ in setting, "like fantasy or primitive or futuristic", shown in their cultures, which may teach different things and from which you may recruit (D116, D161).
- The art style is one guide for every setting, direction D ([art-style.md](art-style.md), D14): a realm brings its parts, palette and light, never a style of its own. The four settings below come from an earlier mood board of four worlds ([research](../research/art-explorations.md)).
- On 2026-10-11, about a technological realm: no machine guns; advanced but on theme with magic, and not superior. Whether its makers are gone or still at work could be either. What matters is **ways to make different kinds of themes work, as the visual design goals do.**
- Also on 2026-10-11: there will be **many generated realms**, with no fixed number (D17), so what is needed is **techniques that make each one fit the lore**, not a handful of hand-made worlds.
- Later the same day, reacting to this proposal: **roughly on point.** Some realms have **more magic or less**. A theme's **weapons should differ, as swords differ from staves: not better or worse, different ways to play.** **Loot**, procedural loot included, may become part of the game, though not a large part. **Mixing settings** within a realm, yes, carefully, as an advanced feature for later. The cosmology's premise and an ancient people who built everywhere are left to the generator: "this is procedural". Each setting's speciality is undecided.
- Standing: one art style (D14), your gear is your gear in every realm (D86), no science fiction toolsets.

## The idea: roles, as the art has palette roles

The art makes many realms from the same models by giving every part a **palette role** (wood, stone, cloth, metal, accent, glow) that each realm fills with its own colours, under one material and one light ([research](../research/ai-models-for-art.md#the-bake-off-second-round-style)). The same model is a medieval cart, a gothic hearse or a brass machine by what fills its roles.

Proposed: **design works the same way.** The game defines a fixed set of **gameplay roles**: a way of using magic, a place where it pools, a culture, materials, stations, weapons, a speciality. A setting is data that fills each role with its own thing. Systems are written once against the roles (D59: a new world is a new theme, not new code).

Two rules keep settings equal:

1. **Rank is the only power axis.** Every realm has iron to diamond ranks (D159, [progression.md](progression.md#skills-on-the-rank-ladder)). A setting changes how things look and work, never how strong they are: a bronze-rank crossbow and a bronze-rank bow hit about as hard.
2. **A setting is a way of using magic.** Nothing in any realm runs on anything else. "Advanced" is a style of using magic, not more of it: engineered in one realm, wild in another, corrupted in a third.

## The roles

| Role | What every setting must say | The rule that keeps it fair |
|---|---|---|
| **Magic** | How its people and land use magic | The same magic and mana underneath (D102) |
| **Pooling** | Where magic gathers, and what forms there (D103) | Monsters of every setting follow the same rank rules |
| **Wildlife** | What lives there that is not a monster | Food, hide and other materials in the same roles |
| **Cultures** | Who lives there, how they build and what they want (D86, D95) | Built from the same building parts (D108); recruits work the same way (D90) |
| **Materials** | What fills the material roles: a soft, a hard, a metal, a cloth, a rare | Same roles, same counts in recipes; a realm's rare material is what draws you there |
| **Stations** | Its look for each station role: making, smelting, cooking, a special one | Same station kinds and timing (D109) |
| **Weapons** | Its own kinds of weapon, each a way to fight: a sword, a staff, a bolt-thrower | Each kind plays differently; none is stronger at a rank than another |
| **Speciality** | What it teaches that no other setting does | One field each, all needed, none the top |
| **Look** | Its palette, its kit of models, its light | One art style, the same material and light (D14) |

## Weapons: different ways to play

From the developers: a theme's weapons differ the way a sword differs from a staff, not better or worse but a different way to play. Proposed, so that holds across many themes:

- **Every weapon kind has a way it plays**: close and quick, slow and heavy, at range, through abilities, defensive. A theme brings its own kinds, or its own take on one, such as a gothic scythe that reaps several at once or a machine realm's bolt-thrower that fires a few shots from its core and then must be recharged.
- **Rank sets the budget, the kind spends it.** At a rank, every kind is worth about the same over a fight; one hits hard and slow, another light and often, another from afar with less. That is what keeps a theme from outclassing another.
- **Skills follow the kind** (D88): you get better with what you use, so trying a new realm's weapons is starting a new skill, not trading up. Archery is one such skill, and a style of bow probably another within it (D117, [combat.md](combat.md)).
- **Gear goes with you** (D86), so a player can carry a staff from one realm and a sword from another, and fight their own way.

## Loot

From the developers: loot, procedural loot included, may become part of the game, though not a large part. Proposed, through the same roles: a found item is **a theme's weapon or gear kind, at a rank, with a few rolled properties drawn from the realm's affinity**, such as a death realm's blade that drains, or a storm realm's that arcs. The rank caps what it can roll, so loot is a sidegrade or a way to play, not a ladder above crafting. Crafting stays the steady way to equip (D109); loot is the surprise.

## Making generated realms fit the lore

Four hand-made settings do not reach any number of generated realms. Proposed: a generator that **starts from a cause and derives the rest**, so every realm is explained by the same few ideas and none is a random pile of themes. After Dwarf Fortress and Caves of Qud, which generate histories that read as lore, and No Man's Sky, whose fiction explains why its worlds repeat.

**1. One cosmology that expects variety.** Left to the generator by the developers. Proposed as the working premise: realms are many places in one cosmos, joined by portals, and **each is shaped by the magic that dominates it**. That one premise explains why realms differ so much, why their peoples use magic in different ways, and why portals lead from one to another, without a story written for each.

**2. A few seeds, everything else derived.** A realm is generated from a handful of choices, and every role is worked out from them by rules:

| Seed | Chooses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Affinity** | The kind of magic that dominates: growth, death, fire, iron, storm, crystal, and so on | Death: graves pool, the undead form, gothic cultures, bone-silver |
| **Climate** | Its land and weather, from the planet's theme (D59) | Cold and misty suits death; a hot one gives a desert of the dead |
| **Age** | Where its people are in their history: rising, thriving, declining, fallen, returning | Fallen: ruins and guardians; rising: settlements still building |
| **Rank** | How strong it is ([arc.md](arc.md)) | Iron for a starting realm, gold for a far one |
| **Magic** | How much magic there is, from the developers: some realms have more, some less | Little: few pooling places, few monsters, more wildlife and plain craft, cores scarce. Much: magic pools everywhere, monsters form fast, the land itself is strange |

How much magic a realm has is apart from its rank: a low-magic realm can still be dangerous, through its wildlife, its people or its land. Each affinity names a **setting template**: how its people use magic, what pools and forms, its material and station roles, its palette. Climate and age bend the template; the seed makes each place on the realm unlike the others. The four mood-board worlds become four templates among many, and a new template is data.

**3. A generated history, left in the land.** Each realm gets a short history of a few events, made by rule from its seeds: a people rose, used the magic in their way, and something happened, such as overreach, war, a plague or a portal opening. The history is never shown as text. It is **built into the world**: ruins where the old works stood, magic pooling where it was overdrawn, the culture that survived and what it believes, names on old stones. Players read a realm's past from its places and people (D92), as Enshrouded's and Kenshi's are read.

**4. Threads that cross realms.** Realms feel like one cosmos when some things recur:

- **Peoples who travel**: traders, pilgrims or refugees found in many realms, carrying news and goods between them.
- **An old people who built everywhere**: the same kind of ruin in many realms, from one ancient culture, a mystery that spans the game.
- **The organisation across realms** (D172, [lore.md](lore.md#threats)): a dangerous faction with a cell in each realm, generated from the same parts, so its trail leads from realm to realm.
- **Your own mark**: claims, recruits and what you carry go with you between realms (D86).

**5. Rules a generator must keep.** What makes a realm fit, checked whatever the seeds: everything runs on magic; nothing outclasses its rank; no science fiction toolsets; one art style; a culture builds with the same parts as everyone (D108); and a few combinations are refused where they would contradict each other, such as a fire affinity in a frozen climate without a reason in its history.

**6. Hand-made anchors.** Open: whether the starting realm, and perhaps a few landmark ones, are made by hand to set the tone. That pulls against "no handcrafted maps" ([vision.md](vision.md#non-goals)); a middle way is a starting realm generated like any other under tighter rules, as [magic.md](magic.md#essences-in-the-starting-realm) does for its essences. Either way, generated realms are built from hand-written templates, names and history events, so what is generated is the combination, never the content from nothing. Names come from per-culture sound sets, so a gothic people and a growth people do not name things alike.

## A worked example: the techno realm

The techno-punk street above, worked through as a proposal: how a realm can feel technologically advanced without guns, without science fiction, and without being better than magic. Open, for the developers to react to; nothing here is built or decided.

| Based on | What to take |
|---|---|
| *He Who Fights With Monsters* | Force without magic does not scale against rank, and magic items are already technology made of magic ([research/hwfwm-magic.md](../research/hwfwm-magic.md)). |
| Eberron (Dungeons & Dragons) | Magic as industry: trains on lines of lightning, airships driven by bound elementals, built soldiers. Machines that are plainly magic. |
| *Arcane* | Hextech: engineering shaped around a magic crystal. Precise, powerful, dangerous, never replacing the people who use it. |
| Kenshi, Soulmask, Subnautica | An advanced people who are gone, known from their ruins, their guardians and what still runs. Technology you dig up and learn, not something you are behind. |

Proposed: **a civilisation of magitech whose machines run on magic, cannot grow, and are best at work that repeats.** Its devices are tools for holding ground; your own power is still what gets you anywhere new.

**1. Everything runs on magic.** A device is powered by a **core**: the crystallised magic monsters leave, of the monster's rank (D165, [magic.md](magic.md#raising-powers)). There is no other power, so monsters, pooling places and machines become one economy.

**2. A machine is as strong as its core, and stays so.** A bronze-rank turret is a bronze-rank turret for as long as it stands. It does not learn, rank up or improve by use. You do (D88, D164, [progression.md](progression.md)). So machines are strong early and in numbers, and are outgrown by a person who keeps climbing.

| | Machines | Your own skills and essences |
|---|---|---|
| Grows | No: as strong as its core's rank | Yes, by use and through ranks |
| Works without you | Yes, while powered | No |
| Needs | Cores, repair (D99), and a claim to stand in (D94) | You |
| Good at | Work that repeats: making, carrying, guarding, lighting | Fighting, exploring, anything new |

**3. Machines hold and run a place.** Stations that work on their own (D109), faster or for longer, such as a core-fired smelter that runs all night. Infrastructure in a claim: lifts, pumps, lamps, doors, carts on rails, chutes between stations, all parts of a build or props from the catalogue (D108). Wards and sentries that defend an outpost while you are away, such as a turret firing bolts of its core's element. Constructs, perhaps: built workers and guards that do simple work tirelessly but do not trade, teach, talk or run a station that needs skill, so rescued people (D90) stay the heart of a hold.

**4. What they are not.** No guns, no lasers, no vehicles that outrun everything, no machine that out-fights a person of its rank. A hand-held device is at most a charged tool: a bolt-thrower with a few shots from its core, slower than a bow in skilled hands, useful to someone without the skill.

**5. Its makers, gone or still at work.** The developers said it could be either. If gone, what is left is ruins where the old works drew magic in, so magic pools there now and monsters form among the machines; guardians still keeping their posts at their cores' rank; perhaps descendants who keep some of the old knowledge and might teach it or join you (D86, D90). You learn a device by salvaging one or from people who know it, not from a research tree (D68). Its machines are the past, and something went wrong with them, which keeps the realm from feeling ahead of the others; what went wrong is the realm's generated history (D172). If the makers are still at work, a living culture of tinkers, the same rules hold. A realm could have both.

**6. Its speciality is holding a place**: automation, defence and logistics, beside the other settings' in the table above. A hold that draws on several realms' specialities is stronger than one that uses one, which is a reason to go everywhere (D91) and to trade.

**7. Its look** is one art style (D14): brass, bronze, carved stone and crystal, not chrome or plastic; machines built by hand and fitted together; runes and glow showing where the power runs.

Combat is unchanged at its centre (melee, a bow, abilities), with turrets and constructs fighting beside you at their cores' rank. Machines you carry work in every realm, as all gear does; machines you build stay in the claim where they stand.

The risks: **automation crowding out play**, as factory games show, which keeping machines in claims, on cores you must hunt, at their cores' rank, should hold back; **constructs crowding out recruits**, unless they are limited to simple work; and **looking like science fiction**, which the look and the rule that everything runs on magic keep away.

## Why roles

- **A new setting is a template**, not new systems: it fills the roles, picks a palette and a kit, and the generator and the game run it. That is what makes any number of realms (D17) possible.
- **No setting outclasses another**, because power lives in rank and roles share numbers. A player moves between realms for what they teach and what they hold, not to trade up.
- **It keeps themes on theme.** Every role is filled with a way of using magic, so a machine realm cannot drift into science fiction and a gothic one cannot drift into horror without rules.
- **It matches how the art is being made**, so a setting's look and its play are one piece of data.

## How it would be made

A setting is part of a realm's theme (D59): a list of what fills each role, with its palette and its kit of models. Recipes, stations, weapons and creatures name roles, and the setting says what each is called, looks like and is made of. The sandbox does a little of this already: the stations' screens and the build menu read their lists from data, and everything built uses one catalogue (D108, D109).

## Mixing settings

From the developers: settings may mix within a realm, such as a gothic quarter in a medieval town, but carefully, and as an advanced feature for later. A first version keeps one setting to a realm; when mixing comes, it is a second template given a place within the first, with a reason in the realm's history.
