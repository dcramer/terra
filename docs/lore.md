# Lore: how the universe works

The foundation every system stands on: what magic is, where monsters come from, what makes a person powerful, and how the realms hang together. Written 2026-10-12 at the developers' request (D125), as part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89).

## Where it comes from, and where it doesn't

**The foundational lore is taken from *He Who Fights With Monsters*, as world-building.** We use the books' fundamental systems, the rules of how their universe works, and their names for those systems: ranks, essences, confluences, awakening stones, spirit coins, quintessence, auras, the astral. We do not use their content: the actual lists of essences, abilities, stones, confluences, monsters, peoples, places and gods are ours (D125). And we don't copy their text. Everything below is in our own words, and is ours to change.

| Taken from the books: the fundamentals | Ours: everything specific |
|---|---|
| Magic everywhere, gathering in places | Which magics there are, and what each realm's is like ([settings.md](settings.md)) |
| Monsters formed from gathered magic | Every kind of monster ([creatures.md](creatures.md)) |
| Essences that give a person powers, a few to a person | Every essence and every ability ([essences.md](essences.md)) |
| Ranks that everything climbs | What each rank means in play, and how it is crossed |
| An aura that betrays your power | How it is shown ([your-character.md](your-character.md)) |
| Many worlds, and ways between them | Every realm, generated ([worlds.md](worlds.md), [settings.md](settings.md)) |
| Magic that can be studied as well as held | Every discipline and style |
| People whose work is keeping monsters down | Every people, faction, culture and settlement ([people.md](people.md)) |
| Gods who are the greatest users of magic | Every god, each belonging to one realm |
| | The history; the long threat; every name, by the rules in [naming.md](naming.md) |

**Not taken at all:**
- the books' characters and their story;
- their places, peoples, organisations and gods;
- their lists: which essences, abilities, awakening stones and confluences exist.

A reader who knows the books should recognise how our universe works and what its parts are called, and find none of its particular essences, powers, people or places.

## Magic

- **Magic is everywhere.** It runs through the land, the water, the air and every living thing, as a quantity that flows. It is not a fuel some people own. It is the medium the world is made in.
- **It flows and it gathers.** It drifts like water finds low ground. It collects in some places more than others: deep forest, old ruins, caverns, ground where much has died, the land around a way between realms. Some places drain it, too. How much a realm has overall differs: some are thick with it, some thin (D116).
- **Every realm has its own kind.** In one, magic leans to growth, in another to death, fire, storm or metal. That leaning shapes everything that forms there: the land, the monsters, how the people use magic. This is why realms differ so much ([settings.md](settings.md)).
- **What it means for play:** magic is a field over the world that rises and falls by place and time, the same shape of thing as temperature in [climate.md](climate.md). Systems read it where they are.

## Monsters

- **Where magic gathers too thickly, it takes shape.** Monsters are not born. They form out of the magic of a place, in its character: a bog's magic makes bog things, a battlefield's makes what haunts it. The thicker the magic, the stronger what forms (D103).
- **Left alone, they multiply and spill over.** A place where magic keeps pooling keeps making monsters. Unculled, they build until they burst out into the land around, onto roads and into settlements: a surge.
- **When they die, the magic falls back into things.** A monster leaves behind what it was made of: materials found nowhere else, and sometimes, rarely, an essence. This is why people hunt them, and what much of the economy runs on.
- **Animals are something else.** Ordinary wildlife is born and lives and dies like any animal. It lives alongside the magic, and some of it is changed by it, but it is not made of it ([creatures.md](creatures.md)).
- **What it means for play:** the world makes its own trouble, everywhere and without a writer. Keeping a place's magic culled is work that never ends, and the reason settlements need help and holds need defending ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#what-makes-it-go-magic-as-the-worlds-weather)).

## Essences and powers

- **An essence is magic condensed into one idea:** fire, swiftness, blood, iron, shadow. It can be held in the hand as a found thing.
- **A person can take an essence into themselves,** and it becomes part of who they are. It grants powers in its idea, which wake over time and grow by use. A person has room for only a few. Holding several, their ideas join into one more that is theirs alone, made from the combination.
- **Powers are who you are, not what you know.** No one chooses a class. What you become is what you have absorbed and how you have used it.
- **Using powers draws on a reserve of one's own,** refilled by rest (D102: mana).
- **What it means for play:** essences are rare, found by hunting and by exploring strong places, and they are a player's identity ([essences.md](essences.md)).

## Ranks

- **Everything that holds magic has a rank:** a person, a monster, an essence, a power, a material, a place, a way between realms.
- **Each rank is a different order of being,** not a little more of the same. One rank up is stronger by far more than numbers would suggest. Crossing a rank changes what a power does, and changes the person: they become harder to kill and slower to age.
- **Strength comes from rank first.** A skilled fighter of a low rank loses to a clumsy one of a higher.
- **Ranks are climbed by using one's powers,** and by taking in magic to feed them. Crossing to the next often needs more than practice ([essences.md](essences.md#crossing-to-the-next-rank)).
- **What it means for play:** rank is the one power axis, in every realm (D116). It orders the world without a story: low-rank land and portals lead to higher ones ([arc.md](arc.md)).

## Aura

- **Power shows.** Everyone with magic gives off an aura that others can feel: a pressure, a weight in the air.
- **The stronger press the weaker.** A much higher rank's aura can make the weak struggle to stand. The skilled can hold theirs in, hide it, or turn it on others.
- **What it means for play:** this is how you judge a monster or a stranger without a number over their head (D93). You feel it, and learn to read it. It is also an ability of its own to train.

## Studied magic

- **Not all magic is held.** Some is worked by knowledge: drawing on the magic of a place through circles, marks, wards and rites, without powers of one's own. Anyone can learn it, slowly, from teachers and texts.
- **Fighting has its studied side too:** styles passed down by teachers and books.
- **What it means for play:** the second way to grow, beside skills and essences ([essences.md](essences.md#three-layers), D124). It is where a realm's learned knowledge lives.

## Realms and the ways between

- **There are many realms,** each a whole world (D17, D87). Between them lies the astral, a space that is no place: the gap that separates them, where the magic that underlies everything runs without form.
- **Ways open between realms** where the separation wears thin, often where magic gathers. Some are natural, some were made, and the knowledge of making them can be learned (D72).
- **A way has a rank,** and leads to a place of about its rank. That is how the realms are ordered for whoever travels them.
- **The realms are one cosmos** with one set of rules. That is why the same magic, the same ranks and the same kinds of essence are found in every one, however strange the realm ([settings.md](settings.md#making-generated-realms-fit-the-lore)).
- **What it means for play:** realms are reached by portals, generated with no fixed number, and each is shaped by the magic that dominates it.

## Gods

Decided that they exist (D156), because they enrich the world.

- **A god is a superior user of magic:** one of a realm who went deeper into its magic than anyone, far past the highest rank a person reaches. It is the same magic and the same ladder, at its far end, not a different order of thing.
- **Gods belong to a realm.** Each realm has its own, and their natures follow its magic: a death realm's gods are of death and endings, a growth realm's of seasons and life. No god is worshipped everywhere.
- **Peoples believe things about them,** not always the same things, and not always true. Shrines, priests, rites and offerings are part of a culture ([people.md](people.md)).
- **What it means for play:** gods are made by the generator with their realm, from its magic and its history ([naming.md](naming.md#lore-by-the-same-rules)). How far they act in play (blessings, a priest's powers, a god's anger at what you do) is open.

## Beings of the gap

Proposed, Leaning (D157): **in the lore only.** Something lives in the formless gap between realms, older and larger than any god, and perhaps keeps the cosmos in order. It is known from old carvings, from what the deepest scholars of the gap claim, and from what the gods themselves seem to fear. It is never met in play, for now.

## People and monsters

- **Wherever magic makes monsters, people organise to keep them down:** hunters, wardens, sworn bands, whatever a culture calls them. The work is dangerous and well rewarded, and the people who do it are respected and needed.
- **Settlements live by it.** A village near a pooling place is safe for as long as someone culls it ([people.md](people.md)).
- **What it means for play:** the player's place in the world is ready-made. You are one of the people who go out into the wild, and every culture has a role for you.

## Open: for the developers

1. Answered: **gods** exist, as realm-bound superior users of magic (D156). **Beings of the gap** are in the lore only, for now (D157).
2. **Why the player comes back from death** (D18). Proposed: something in the lore, such as a bond to the place you rest, rather than an unexplained respawn.
3. **The long threat** (premise B in [arc.md](arc.md)), not yet chosen. Three that fit the rules, to react to:
   - **a drought:** magic draining out of realms, leaving them dead;
   - **a rot in the water:** magic turning foul and making worse things wherever it pools;
   - **something rising from the gap,** pressing through where realms wear thin.
4. **Names.** Our own words, made by one pattern: proposed in [naming.md](naming.md) (D158).
