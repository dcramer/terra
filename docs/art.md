# Art direction

Status: Leaning (D14). **The target is now the four-world mood board** on [Terra Style Directions](https://claude.ai/artifact/AWgMLGJmS5GR7hvjJMbKq4): a medieval town, a techno-punk street, fantasy wilds and a gothic graveyard, in direction C's painted look and rich light, at life size (D110). The developers accepted it on 2026-10-11. The sections below were written for the earlier direction, [Forever Ago](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1215940/Forever_Ago/)'s flat-coloured facets, and are kept until this page is rewritten around the board; where they disagree with it, the board wins. The firm constraint stands: it must not look chunky or Minecraft-like.

## The essence

Simple shapes, flat colour, and light doing nearly all the work. The world looks like a carefully lit model: clean enough to read at a glance, soft enough to feel warm.

1. **Simple shapes, finely cut.** Things are built from flat planes you can see, but many small ones, with graceful outlines. A boulder is dozens of facets, not six.
2. **Flat colour, no texture.** A surface is one colour. Detail comes from its shape and from how light falls across it, never from a painted-on pattern.
3. **Warm light, cool shadow.** A warm sun against shadows lit by a cool sky. This contrast is most of the mood.
4. **Distance becomes layers.** Haze fades far things toward the colour of the sky, so hills turn into soft pastel bands instead of sharp detail.
5. **A small palette per place.** A handful of colours that belong together, saturated but soft. A place is recognisable by its colours alone.
6. **Small, dense life.** Where things grow, they grow as many small flat-coloured pieces: ferns, grass, flowers. Lushness comes from quantity, not detail.
7. **A lens.** A little bloom, gentle colour grading, and sometimes blur in the distance. It should feel photographed, not rendered.

## Life-sized

The world is at real-world scale (D110, Decided). A broadleaf tree stands 15 to 25 metres, a pine taller; boulders range from knee-high to bigger than a house; hills rise tens of metres and mountains hundreds. The player should feel small against the land, which is also what makes haze and layered distance work. Stylised shapes do not mean small things: a model is simplified, never shrunk.

## What it is not

- **Not chunky.** No cubes, no visible grid, no oversized facets on small things. If a shape reads as "blocks", it is wrong.
- **Not photoreal.** No surface textures, no noise, no grit.
- **Not outlined.** No cartoon ink lines; edges come from light.
- **Not pixelated.**

## Mood boards, 2026-10-11

Four directions drawn by an image model, each with the same wolf, villager, workbench, tree and outpost: [Terra Style Directions](https://claude.ai/artifact/AWgMLGJmS5GR7hvjJMbKq4) (private to the developers). A is papercraft facets (this page's direction), B soft rounded shapes, C painted textures, D graphic layers: bold simplified shapes, big flat areas of colour, crisp shadow shapes. The developers' first reaction: D is "pretty dang attractive" and "feels more ambitious"; B is cartoony; A is nice and may sit better with voxels. Later the same day they said C's lighting and look were better, if it can be made to work with the game's goals, and that a mood board of four worlds (a medieval town, a techno-punk street, fantasy wilds, a gothic graveyard) is the mood board they want. Not a decision; D14 stands until they choose. C as concept art paints colour into each model, which many realms cannot use; split into palette roles for colour and shared painted materials and light for the look, it can (see [research](../research/ai-models-for-art.md#the-bake-off-second-round-style)). D was shown to work as a shader on our models.

## Where we are

The closest we have come is the outpost scene, the sandbox's `play` experiment, made on 2026-10-10. Run it with `cargo xtask win -p terra_sandbox -- --experiment play`, or `cargo run` anywhere but WSL.

![The outpost: a base in a meadow, pines behind it, a fire in front](images/outpost-2026-10-10.png)

![The same place from above, with the lake beyond](images/outpost-from-above-2026-10-10.png)

![Indoors, lit by its own lamps and by the sun through the windows](images/outpost-indoors-2026-10-10.png)

What it has that the earlier tests did not: designed shapes, from free model packs recoloured to one palette; grass, flowers and bushes in quantity, moving in a wind; fine smooth ground with grass, soil and sand; water that is clear at the shore and mirrors the sky further out; a base with trim, a foundation and lit rooms; furniture; and shading in corners and under things.

What it still lacks against the reference:

- **Other planets.** This is close to the starter planet (D62). The dead and strange planets have not been attempted.
- **Our own shapes.** The trees and furniture are a stock pack's. They carry our colours, not our design.
- **Layered distance.** The planet is small, so there are no far mountains to fade into bands.
- **Blur and framing.** No depth of field, and the camera is simply behind the player.
- **A character.** The figure is a recoloured mannequin.

### Earlier tests

Made the same day, before any models. Run them with `cargo run -p terra_sandbox -- --experiment look --experiment forest`.

![Look test: a faceted desert with boulders, shrubs, haze and a soft sky](images/look-test-2026-10-10.png)

![Forest test: low-polygon pines over the same desert](images/forest-test-2026-10-10.png)

The second image adds the `forest` experiment: generated pines, each built from a few flat-faced cones.

What they show: flat-coloured faceted ground, generated boulders and shrubs with flat faces, a warm sun with cool shadows, haze toward the horizon, a gradient sky with a soft cloud, and bloom.

What it does not have, and the reference does:

- **Authored shapes.** Every model in the reference was designed by an artist. Ours are lumpy spheres.
- **Foliage.** One kind of tree, and no grass, ferns or flowers. This is the biggest gap wherever the planet is alive.
- **Composition.** The reference frames each scene. We have a free camera on a generated world.
- **Layered distance.** Our planet is small, so the horizon is a few hundred metres away and there are no far mountains to turn into bands.
- **Water, interiors, people.**

## How the look is made

| Ingredient | Where it comes from | State |
|---|---|---|
| Faceted, flat-shaded surfaces | A shader lights each triangle as one plane | Working on the ground and on generated shapes |
| Warm sun, cool shadow | Light colours and a sky-coloured fill light | Working |
| Haze and layered distance | Distance fog tinted toward the horizon | Working |
| Sky | A gradient shader with soft clouds | Working, with day and night: the sun crosses it, then stars and a moon; the light, shade and haze follow how high the sun is |
| Bloom and grading | Camera effects built into Bevy | Working |
| Blur in the distance | A camera effect built into Bevy | Not tried; needs care in first person |
| Elegant shapes | Free and bought low-polygon packs, read for shape and given our colours in code; generators for rocks and plants | Working with free packs in the outpost scene (D54); generators started |
| Dense foliage | Instancing models; grass as many single-triangle blades merged into one mesh per square of ground | Working near the outpost: 13,000 plants and rocks and 650,000 blades |
| Wind | One vertex shader for every plant, used for shadows too | Working |
| Ground cover | Each vertex of the ground says what grows there; the shader paints it and roughens the edges | Working on the stand-in planet |
| Water | A see-through surface that mirrors the sky at a low angle, over ground that darkens with depth | Working; no waves, no swimming |
| Shade in corners | Screen-space ambient occlusion, built into Bevy | Working |
| A palette per place | Generated from the simulation's values | Not started |

## Why this direction suits us

- **It is the most available style.** Flat-coloured low-polygon packs are plentiful and consistent with each other, which matters with no artists (D1).
- **It is the easiest style to generate.** A believable low-polygon rock or fern is far simpler to produce from rules than a realistic one.
- **It hides what we are weak at.** No textures means no mismatched textures.
- **Voxel terrain can wear it.** Smooth voxel meshes lit one triangle at a time give faceted ground for free. The risk is facet size: coarse terrain reads as chunky, so close-up terrain has to be fine.

## Principles that still hold

- **The art is the simulation rendered.** Sky colour from air pressure, ground tint from moisture and life, plant density from the simulation.
- **One material pipeline for everything.** Every asset goes through the same palette and shader, so the game reads as one thing.
- **Effort goes into light and atmosphere**, which is shader code.

## Open questions

- **What does a dead planet look like in this style?** The references are lush or warm. Some of our planets are airless and lifeless, and have to be beautiful too. With any number of worlds, each needs a palette of its own, made by rule (D59, D17).
- **How does the look change through the phases?** A palette per biome and phase is the idea; nobody has drawn one.
- **Night.**
- **First person or third?** Blur and framing matter much more to this look in third person. First person removes a large animation cost.
- **Machines and bases.** The shop interior hints at an answer; nothing is designed.
