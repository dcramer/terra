# World instances: loading worlds in and out

Status: Leaning, technical (D468 to D471, by the agent); nothing built yet. The developers' answers on dungeons (D497 to D502) are at the end: a dungeon is a place in its realm, as in Valheim and Enshrouded, so the unit loaded in and out is a realm, crossed to through a portal. [worlds.md](worlds.md) says what the realms are as places; this doc says how a world is loaded, run, left and freed.

Asked for by the developers: "when we talk about a dungeon, you could imagine you going to that world unloads the current world on the client and loads that dungeon - we need a system for that and we need it well compartmentalized." Then, asked whether a dungeon is its own world: "hm i think its more like valheim/enshrouded actually" (D497).

## Constraints

- Many realms, each its own round world with its own coordinate frame (D4, D17, D36); a world nobody is in costs nothing beyond its seed and what players changed (D47).
- A thousand players on one server (D52), so many worlds run at once on the server while each client is in one.
- No traditional loading screen when crossing; the crossing itself may cover loading (D72). The loading screen's tracking of what is still coming (D135) is reused under that cover.
- ECS throughout (D58); rules in engine-free crates (D28); the layers (D198) unchanged.
- A module (terrain, fire, wildlife, weather) must neither leak into the next world nor need to know that worlds come and go.

## How shipped games do it

| Game or engine | What a world is | Crossing | What we take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unreal: level streaming, World Partition | A `UWorld` with a persistent level; sublevels or grid cells streamed in by distance | Seamless travel keeps a short list of actors (player controller and state, game mode) and the `GameInstance`; everything else belongs to the old world and goes with it | Belonging to the world is the default, persisting is opt-in; streaming within a world stays the terrain's job |
| Minecraft | A dimension is its own `ServerLevel` in one server: chunks, entities, ticking and time of its own | An entity changing dimension is removed and made again in the other level from its saved data; the client throws its level away and shows "Loading terrain" | One world object per world on the server; a hand-off is a save and a load |
| Valheim | Dungeons are rooms generated by a `DungeonGenerator` high in the sky above their entrance, in the same world | A fade and a teleport | A dungeon in its realm's world (D497); a door that moves you takes a short fade (D501) |
| Enshrouded | Vaults are part of the one voxel world | None, seamless | A dungeon dug into the realm's ground at its site, walked into, as `terra_dungeon`'s stamp already lays one |
| WoW | Instance dungeons are copies of a map per group, with an id, lockouts and an idle timer before they are freed | Portal and loading screen | Instance ids, an idle grace before freeing, shared or private as a rule of the kind |
| Destiny | Activities and patrol spaces are "bubbles" on servers | Corridors and doors that hide the streaming | A cover the player walks through rather than a screen |

Bevy gives the pieces, in the pinned 0.20: States with `OnEnter`/`OnExit` and computed states; state-scoped despawning (`DespawnOnExit`, which calls `World::despawn_all_where`); required components registered at run time (`App::register_required_components`); component hooks; `App`/`SubApp`, each with its own `World` and schedules; reference-counted asset handles (the last strong `Handle` dropped sends `AssetEvent::Unused` and frees the asset, on the GPU too); entity disabling (`Disabled`); reflected snapshots (`DynamicWorld` in `bevy_world_serialization`).

## A world instance (D468)

A **world instance** is one running copy of a realm, the world a portal leads to. Every realm is the same unit, differing by its field (what is solid) and its theme (what lives, what light, which sky), not by the code that runs it. A dungeon is a place inside its realm's instance, dug into its ground (D497), shared by the whole server (D498) and kept cleared in the realm's record (D499); going into one unloads nothing. **A Bevy `World` holds at most one instance**, so a module written against "the world" in its `World` works unchanged on the client and the server.

| An instance owns | Global, outside every instance |
|---|---|
| Its spec (`WorldSpec`: id, kind, seed, theme, planet spec) | The player's character: body, vitals, skills, what they carry and wear |
| The ground: voxel field, edits, chunks and their meshing tasks | The camera, the controls, UI and screens, settings and options |
| Sites, dungeons, bases, claims, props, piles, storage | Kinds read from the data files (D136): species, items, flora kinds, fire kinds |
| Creatures, townsfolk, fires, smoke, wakes | The network session and the game's tick (`Time<Fixed>`) |
| Its clock and day, climate, weather, wind | The asset files every world uses (the player's figure, icons, UI) |
| Sun, sky and air, the light and grading on the camera | Music, if the developers want it to carry across (open) |
| Its audio bed | The remote probe and the census |
| Its asset set (below) | |
| Navigation and spatial lookups | |

**Lifecycle on the server.** Cold (a seed and, if anyone changed it, a save) → Warming (made when a player nears its door: assets and the ground round the arrival) → Active (ticking, with players) → Idle (empty, still ticking for a grace, so a quick return or a straggler costs nothing) → Frozen (in memory, not ticked, its clock stopped, D500) → Saved (its record written) → Freed (memory returned; Cold again). The grace and freeze times are tuning. Many instances are Active at once.

**On the server each instance is a headless `App`** of its own (its `World` and schedules, the same plugins less drawing), held by an instance host that ticks every Active one on the task pool with `World::run_schedule(FixedMain)`. Separate `World`s tick in parallel without locks, and an instance is the unit that can move to another process when one process is not enough (D52, [multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md)). The cost to measure first: an empty instance's tick, times the realms a busy server holds. Within an Active realm, places no player is near are not simulated, and what follows from time catches up when one comes near (D500).

**On the client the instance is in the main `World`**, since Bevy's renderer extracts from it. The client is in one instance at a time; crossing replaces it.

Rejected: one `World` with an instance id on every entity and every resource turned into a per-instance component. Every query of every module would group by instance, the game's 243 resource types would all change, and a leak between worlds would be a logic bug rather than impossible.

## Compartmentalization in the ECS (D469)

One pattern for entities, one for resources, one for components a world puts on global entities. Modules keep spawning and inserting as today.

1. **Entities belong to the world by default.** `WorldScoped` is registered as a required component of `Transform` (`App::register_required_components::<Transform, WorldScoped>`), so every placed thing (a chunk, a prop, a creature, a fire, the sun) is the world's at spawn, with no extra archetype move. Registration happens first, in the shell, before any entity with a `Transform` exists; Bevy refuses it later (`ArchetypeExists`), a loud failure at start. **`Global` opts out**: its hook removes `WorldScoped`; it goes on the few roots that outlive a world (the player's body, the camera). Leaving despawns every root that carries `WorldScoped` with its descendants, through `World::despawn_all_where` as Bevy's `DespawnOnExit` does, `Disabled` entities included. A child of a `Global` root (the figure's bones, what the hands hold) is not a root and stays. A world entity with no `Transform` (an ambient sound bed, a weather emitter) adds `WorldScoped` itself; the census catches one that forgets.
2. **Resources a world owns are declared once:** `app.world_resource::<Terrain>()`. Leaving removes each of them. A module builds them on entering, from `WorldSpec`, in `OnEnter(InWorld)`, never in `Startup` or at plugin build; its systems run only while they exist (`run_if(resource_exists::<_>)`, the layers' existing rule for what may be missing, D198). A kind without a weather or a sky simply never inserts it. Dropping a resource drops its `Task`s, which cancels work in flight (meshing, choosing chunks).
3. **Components a world puts on a global entity are declared too:** `app.world_component::<Atmosphere>()`. Leaving removes them from every entity. This is the sky, haze, exposure and grading on the global camera.
4. **Caches that outlive a world hold `AssetId`s, not strong handles**, or are world resources themselves; a global resource holding strong handles to a world's assets is a leak by construction.

The states: `Travel` is `Entering`, `Playing` or `Leaving`, and a computed state `InWorld` is on while entering and playing, so content spawned while entering is scoped too. The first world of a run is entered the same way as any other: one path.

**The census** (`terra_world::instance::Census`) counts entities by archetype (sorted component names), by `WorldScoped`, `Global` and neither; `Assets<T>` length per registered type; the resources present; and, on screen, the GPU memory by label (`gpu_memory`). It is read as `world.census` through the remote probe, so `r snap` and `r diff` show what a crossing changed.

What each part changes:

| Part | Change |
|---|---|
| `terra_world::loading` (D135) | Entered again for a crossing, with the crossing's cover instead of the start's screen; `Progress` starts over |
| `planet`, `start`, `terrain` | `Planet`, `Start`, `Sited`, `Terrain`, `GroundLook` and the land's textures become world resources built from `WorldSpec`; `Planet` holds the realm's field, its dungeons dug into it |
| `sky`, `air`, `daylight`, `climate`, `weather`, `wind`, `grading` | Their state and clock are world resources; what they put on the camera is a world component |
| `flora`, `woods`, `cover`, `grown`, `scatter`, `lakes`, `shore` | World resources, so their instance buffers go with them |
| `fire`, `smoke`, `drift`, `rises`, `wakes` | Scoped by default; pools tied to a world's kinds become world resources |
| `audio` and the game's soundscape | The bed is the world's and adds `WorldScoped`; music open |
| Walker | The body and camera are `Global`; `ArriveAt` places the body at the arrival |
| Survival | What a player carries stays on the player; props, piles, storage and claims are the world's and saved with it |
| Creatures, folk | Scoped by default; their zones and spawn state are world resources; species stay global data |
| Building, bases | Bases and the base store are the world's, saved with it (`terra_build::save`) |
| `play::save` | Split into the character's record and the instance's (below) |
| Experiments | Their world setup moves from `Startup` to `OnEnter(InWorld)` |

## The crossing and the way back (D470)

A portal is an entity in the world with `Door { to: InstanceKey, arrival }`. The server decides every crossing (D51). A dungeon's entrance is not a crossing: the dungeon is in the same instance (D497), walked into; a door that moves the player to another place in the realm takes the same short fade, with no unloading.

1. **Approach.** In reach of a portal, the server warms the target and tells the client its spec; the client starts loading the target's asset set (D72's proposal: load the world beyond as you approach).
2. **Cross.** The player steps through; the server moves the character from the old instance to the new one (a save of the character out of one `World` and a load into the other, as Minecraft does) and sends `Cross { to, spec, arrival, epoch }`.
3. **Cover.** The client fades out, a short fade under 3 s whatever the entrance (D501, D72), and holds the game clock, as the loading screen does (D135).
4. **Leave.** `Travel::Leaving`: the instance's record is kept (frozen, below); scoped roots are despawned; world resources and world components removed; then the old asset set is dropped, after the new one's handles are taken, so an asset both use stays loaded.
5. **Enter.** `WorldSpec` inserted, `Travel::Entering`: modules build their resources and spawn their content; the record, if there is one, is read back; `Progress` (D135) tracks ground meshing, files, shaders and what is drawable.
6. **Play.** When ready, the body is placed at the arrival, the cover lifts and `Travel::Playing` begins.

**The way back.** The character carries a return stack: each crossing pushes where it came from (instance key, place, facing); a portal back pops it, so it leads to the spot in the realm the player left. Dying is the survival rules' business, not the stack's.

**A quick crossing** is the same sequence with the target already warm; the aim is a cover under a second for a warmed realm, measured. Keeping the old world on the client as `Disabled` entities, for an instant return, is rejected for now: two worlds' GPU memory, and two sets of world resources cannot share one `World`.

## Asset sets (D471)

Each theme names its **asset set** in its data (`assets/data/sets/<theme>.ron`): the models, textures, sounds and shaders its world draws on. Entering an instance loads its realm's set as a world resource of strong handles, tracked by `Progress`; leaving drops it. A dungeon's theme has a set too, held while a player is near the dungeon and dropped after, as the terrain streams chunks. Bevy's handles are the reference count: an asset held by the next world's set, by a global (the player's gear) or by another world on the server stays; the rest is freed, from the GPU too. What a world makes (chunk meshes, instance buffers) is its own and goes with its resources and entities. The new set is taken before the old is dropped; meshes and buffers, the bulk of a world, go before either.

## Saving and networking (D471)

- **Two records.** The character's (body, vitals, skills, items, return stack, which instance they are in) and each instance's: its spec, the operations in the server's order (D21), and a section per module that has state beyond its rules (bases, props and storage, claims, creatures alive, fires, its clock and weather, and which of its dungeons are cleared, D499). A module registers its section (`app.save_section::<FireRecord>()`, write from and read into the `World`). RON through serde, as today (D353); the world itself is computed, not stored (D47), so Bevy's `DynamicWorld`, which would store what is computed, is not used.
- **Frozen is saved.** Leaving a world writes its record (in memory, and to disk on the save's schedule); returning reads it. A crossing, a save, a load and the server's hand-off are one code path, so a round trip tests saving.
- **Replication by instance.** A connection is subscribed to one instance at a time, and within it by area ([multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md)); world messages carry the instance's epoch, and the client drops any of an epoch it has left. The character, inventory and the session travel on the player's own channel, outside any instance.

## The first slice

In the sandbox, with no server: a portal in the forest that unloads the forest and loads a second realm, and a portal back that restores the forest as it was. A dungeon needs no crossing (D497): `terra_dungeon`'s stamp already digs one into the forest's ground ([dungeons.md](dungeons.md)), and keeping it cleared and undiggable (D499, D502) is that doc's work.

- **`terra_world::instance`**: `WorldSpec`, `Travel` and `InWorld`, `WorldScoped` and `Global`, `world_resource` and `world_component`, leaving and entering, `Census` and `world.census`.
- **The modules the forest walk uses** moved onto it, as in the table above.
- **A second realm**: a small world of another seed and theme from those the sandbox already has (the tundra's), with an arrival beside the portal back; its seed from the forest's seed and the portal's place (`terra_gen::hash`).
- **The portal**: in the forest near the start, crossing on interact under the short fade (D501).
- **An experiment, `portal`**, standing for the forest walk with the portal (D467's way), with keys to cross for scripted runs.

**The test that proves nothing leaks.**

1. *A unit test in `terra_world`, no GPU*: a stand-in module spawns placed and unplaced entities, a world resource, a component on a `Global`, and assets, one also held by a `Global`. After a round trip: the census equals the one before; the shared asset is loaded and the world's others are freed; and a stand-in that spawns an unscoped entity without a `Transform` is reported by the census, so the test is known to catch a leak.
2. *A scripted run of `portal` on the worker*: five round trips, the census read in the forest before the first and after each return, and in the other realm. Pass: after trips 2 to 5, entity counts by archetype, asset counts by type and the resources present equal trip 1's exactly, and GPU memory and the process's resident memory are within 2% of trip 1's (the first trip may fill caches such as pipelines); in the other realm, no forest resource is present and none of the forest's set is loaded; `r diff` of the forest's state from before the first trip to after the last shows nothing changed but the player's place. Each crossing's time is reported.

## The developers' answers (2026-10-25)

Asked on the review desk; the developers' words are in each decision.

1. **A dungeon is a place in its realm**, as in Valheim and Enshrouded, not its own instance (D497, Decided). This made the realm the unit loaded in and out and the portal the only crossing (D468, D470, D471 amended).
2. **One copy of each dungeon for the whole server**; anyone may follow you in (D498, Decided).
3. **A cleared dungeon stays cleared**, the default to build on (D499, Decided).
4. **A world nobody is in freezes**, clock included; within an occupied realm, places nobody is near stand still, and what follows from time catches up when a player comes near, as Minecraft and Valheim do (D500, Leaning, the agent's on the developers' note).
5. **A short fade, whatever the entrance** (D501, Decided).
6. **No building in a dungeon, and its rock cannot be dug**, as Valheim and Enshrouded keep theirs (D502, Leaning, the agent's on the developers' note).
