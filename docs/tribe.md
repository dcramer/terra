# Your tribe: the people of your claim

Part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89). How recruits are found, and the rest of the world's people, are in [people.md](people.md). This page covers the tribe they join: what recruits do once they are home, and who else belongs. Each item is as settled as the decision it cites; the rest is a proposal for the developers to react to. What other games do is in [research/recruits-at-the-base.md](../research/recruits-at-the-base.md).

"Tribe" is the developers' word, used here as a working name.

## What the developers have said

From 2026-10-12:

- **The intent is Kenshi-based to some degree** (D161). You recruit people, bring them back safely, and they live in your tribe.
- **They take part on their own, however the simulation makes sense** (D161, D162): working crafting stations and helping haul resources. You do not assign them.
- **It is not a colony sim** (D161). You do not micromanage them. "A little automatically managed tribe simulator."
- **They have stats they level up, as you do** (D88, D123), so they get better at the work they do.
- **Building help is useful for carrying things to a building** (D161). They must not start building a plan while you are still designing it.
- **They take their gear on their own** (D161). Systems may be needed to help you decide what they can and cannot use; not settled.
- **They help defend you in some situations** (D90, Leaning): if you are attacked near your claim, or in raids, if raids come later.
- **They travel and fight beside you**, later (D161): a future to-do.
- **They can die** (D161).
- **No ageing, for now** (D161). Families, as in Medieval Dynasty, would be neat but are wishful thinking.
- **A tribe belongs to a claim** (D166). Everyone in it, players as well as recruits, is part of it, since you can recruit other players too.
- **Medieval Dynasty is a source of inspiration** alongside Kenshi.

## Based on

- **Kenshi**: people who join you, work, fight and grow by doing (D95).
- **Medieval Dynasty**: a village that grows around you. Villagers who get better at their trade, live in houses you build, and keep working while you are away.
- Two more, suggested by the research for the part neither of those has, work without managing:
  - **Palworld**: everyone at the base takes whatever work they are suited to, and drops it when the base is attacked.
  - **Dragon Quest Builders 2**: you say what you want by putting things in the world, such as a blueprint, a labelled field or a chest, and residents get on with it.

## Not a colony sim

The line between the two, proposed: **in a colony sim the player is the scheduler; in ours the player builds the place and the people fill it.**

| You do | You never do |
|---|---|
| Find people, recruit them and bring them home safely ([people.md](people.md#recruits)) | Set work priorities, schedules or zones |
| Build what the tribe needs: beds, stations, storage, fields | Assign each recruit to a job before they will work |
| Draw the plans for what is to be built (D152, D154), and say when one is ready | Equip each recruit by hand |
| Stock the stores, and sometimes ask one person for something | Put the injured to bed, or remind anyone to eat |

**Building a forge is how you say you want smithing.** Someone who can smith takes it up. A field is how you say you want farming. A finished plan is how you say you want a wall. The world is the interface, as in Dragon Quest Builders 2.

## Who does what

From the developers (D162): recruits take part on their own, in whatever way the simulation makes sense. How the simulation decides is proposed here; the player never sees the mechanism, only people getting on with things.

- **The claim has work to do**: every station wants a worker, and so do fields, animals, the woodpile, the stores and the watch. What counts as work is data, as stations are (D136).
- **The best fit takes each piece of work**: whoever is most skilled at it. Then whoever **likes** it best: each recruit likes a kind of work or two, from what they did before and from their culture (D86). Bren was a smith before you rescued her, so she goes to the forge even when someone else is as good. Then whoever is nearest.
- **People keep their work.** Who does what is worked out again only when something changes: someone joins, leaves or dies; a station is built or taken down; someone gets better. Not every moment. So people stay at their work and get good at it, rather than wandering off to whatever is nearest.
- **Liking pulls toward specialising.** Skills grow by doing (D123), so whoever starts at a job gets better at it. Without a pull toward what each person likes, whoever took the nearest work at the start would be stuck in it. With it, a born smith drifts back to the forge when a second one is built.
- **Asking, the one override.** Talk to a recruit and ask them to work the kiln. They keep at it until you ask otherwise. It is a word in the world, not a screen of switches.
- **With nothing to do at their own work**, a recruit helps wherever help is wanted: hauling, carrying to a build, fetching. More recruits than work means a faster tribe, never an idle one.

## What comes first

Proposed: one order of priorities for everyone, the same in every claim, so the tribe does the obvious thing without being told. It is data, not anyone's setting.

1. **Danger**: fight it if they can, keep away from it if they cannot (see [Defence](#defence)).
2. **Their own needs**: eat, sleep, warm up, heal.
3. **Their own work.**
4. **Help**: carry to a build, haul to the stores, fetch what their work is missing.
5. **Their own time**: talk, sit by the fire, wander the claim, which is what makes the place look lived in.

A build never pulls a smith off the forge. Help comes from those with nothing to do at their own work. That rule avoids Palworld's failure, where a new plan empties the mines.

## Hauling and the stores

Proposed:

- **A claim's stores are one store to its tribe.** A recruit who needs iron knows there is iron in the claim, and goes to whichever chest holds it. Nothing is ever "in the wrong chest", which is the most common complaint about Medieval Dynasty and Soulmask.
- **Hauling is still seen.** A recruit walks to the chest and carries the iron to the forge. A hunter carries the deer home. A log goes over a shoulder (D140). Realistic, so you see the tribe working, but never the reason work stops.
- **Output goes to the nearest store** with room, carried by whoever made it or by someone helping.
- **Between claims**, recruits who carry goods from an outpost to home are for later, after the first claim works. The idea is roads, carts and caravans of your own ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#why-explore-and-why-settle-in-more-than-one-place)).

## Building

From the developers (D161): help is useful for carrying things to a building, and **no one starts on a plan you are still designing**. Proposed:

- **A plan is yours until you say it is ready.** While you draw, a plan is a draft, which only you change and the tribe leaves alone. When you are happy with it, you say so: one action on the plan, such as "build this", or one for every draft in the claim. Only then does the tribe touch it. Palworld starts building the moment a plan is laid, which is the failure the developers named.
- **Then they carry.** Recruits with nothing else to do bring what the plan needs from the stores and lay it by the build: logs over the shoulder (D140), stone, beams. This is the help the developers asked for. You put the parts in, and the materials are already there.
- **Putting parts in themselves** is open. Dragon Quest Builders 2, Palworld and Sons of the Forest let them, and [construction.md](construction.md#planned-then-constructed) allows it. Proposed: later, and only for a recruit who is a skilled builder (D88), so a big build goes up around you while you work on it too.
- **Recruits never draw plans.** What the claim looks like is yours.
- **When materials run out**, the build waits, and the claim's list of what its plans still need tells you what to fetch ([construction.md](construction.md#planned-then-constructed)).

## Equipment

From the developers (D161): they take their gear on their own. Systems may be needed to help you decide what they can and cannot use. Proposed:

- **They arrive with what they had**, and it is theirs.
- **They take what their work needs from the stores**: a woodcutter the best axe they can use, a guard armour and a weapon. They put it back when it is not needed. No one is equipped by hand, which is the chore players complain of in Kenshi and Soulmask.
- **Hand it over yourself** if you want someone to have a thing. What you give a recruit is theirs to keep.
- **Gear wears** (D99), and they mend it themselves at the station that mends it, quicker if they know the craft.
- **What they wear shows** on them (D128), so a guard looks like a guard.
- **Deciding what they may use** is open. The simplest is a chest of your own, which the tribe leaves alone, so your best sword and last good food stay yours. Finer rules, such as what each recruit may take or what is kept for guards, may be needed once tribes are big; to be seen in play.

## Needs

Proposed, light, and never a death spiral:

- **A bed in a building**, indoors (D155). A new bed goes to the nearest recruit without one, as in Enshrouded.
- **Food from the stores**, better if someone cooks. **Warmth** (D153).
- **Comfort makes work better**: a good bed, a roof, a fire, good food, and the work they like. Without them, a recruit works slower and says so. Pushed too far, they leave. They never starve in front of you.
- **No mood meter to tend.** How someone is doing shows in how they look and what they say (D93).

## Growing

Recruits grow as you do (D123). Their work's skills rise by doing it, on the same rank ladder as yours ([essences.md](essences.md#skills-on-the-rank-ladder)). What they can make follows their rank: an iron-rank smith cannot work silver-rank metal.

Crossing to the next rank may need a key (D126): a book, a teacher or a feat. You, or another recruit who already stands at that rank, can be the teacher, so a master in the tribe raises the others.

Their skills, ranks and likings read on their own sheet, like yours (D93).

**No ageing, for now** (D161). Recruits do not grow older, and no one is born into the tribe. Families, where recruits pair up and have children who grow into it, as in Medieval Dynasty, are wishful thinking for later.

## Defence

From the developers (D90, Leaning): they help defend you if you are attacked near your claim, and in raids if those come later. Proposed:

- **When danger comes into the claim, or you are attacked near it, everyone who can fight drops their work and fights.** The rest take shelter indoors. Afterwards, everyone goes back to what they were doing. This is the one switch every game in the research uses.
- **The watch is work like any other**: a guard or two who keep at it, patrol the claim, and ring a bell or shout when something comes.
- **How well they fight is their skill and rank**, as for you ([combat.md](combat.md)). A bronze-rank monster walks through iron-rank farmers.
- **Raids are not a concern for now** ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#questions-for-the-developers), question 2). When they come, they come to claims. A raid should be something you can answer, not a summons that nags, as Fallout 4's settlement quests do.

## Coming with you

From the developers (D161): recruits can travel and fight beside you, as a future to-do. Nothing is designed for it yet. When it comes, the questions are how many can come, how you ask them, and how they keep up on big round realms ([your-character.md](your-character.md)).

## When a recruit dies

From the developers (D161): **recruits can die.** Proposed for how:

- **Downed before dead.** A recruit beaten in a fight goes down first, as in Kenshi. Help them up, or carry them home, and they mend in bed. Left down while the fight goes on, they can die.
- **Dead is dead.** What they carried lies where they fell, as yours does (D84).
- **Never from the system's own failings**: a pathing bug, a recruit who wandered out of the claim into a monster. The research found these the losses players resent most in Kenshi and Soulmask. Deaths should follow from danger the player could see and answer.

## How many

Proposed: as many as your claims can house and feed ([people.md](people.md#recruits)). Since recruits come by rescue and by helping settlements (D90), the supply is a limit of its own, as in Enshrouded.

There will also be a cap per claim, technical and mine to set, since every recruit costs the server something (D52).

## Seeing what they do

Proposed:

- **A roster, to read, not to run**: who is in the tribe, what they are doing now, and their skills.
- **Above all, why someone is not working**: "Bren is waiting: no charcoal in the stores." An idle worker with no visible reason is the failure every game in the research shares, and their guides are troubleshooting lists.
- **Said in the world first** (D93): Bren tells you the forge is out of charcoal when you pass. The roster only gathers what you could have heard.

## Whose tribe

From the developers (D166): **a tribe belongs to a claim, and everyone in it is part of it, players as well as recruits**, since you can recruit other players too. So the tribe is the group that holds the claim: whoever is in it may build there, use its stores and stations, and ask its recruits for something ([construction.md](construction.md#claims)).

Proposed:

- **Recruiting a player** is asking them to join, and their accepting. Unlike a recruit, a player can leave whenever they like.
- **Recruits are the tribe's, not one player's.** One who joined because you rescued them stays with the tribe if you leave.

Open: how this meets outposts, since a tribe may hold more than one claim (D91); whether a player can be in more than one tribe; and who in a tribe may do what, such as take down its buildings or bring someone in. [multiplayer.md](multiplayer.md) holds the ownership questions.

## What it asks of the engine

Technical, mine:

- **Detail by distance.** With a player nearby, recruits walk, carry and work in full. With no one near, a claim's work runs as a summary: so much made, eaten and mended an hour, as Medieval Dynasty's output appears in storage. A thousand players' tribes (D52) cannot all be walking.
- **One list of work per claim.** Each claim keeps a list of what wants doing. Recruits take from it, rather than each searching the world. The list changes only when something changes (D127).
- **Pathing in what players build** is the hardest part, and the top complaint in Palworld and Kenshi: storeys, stairs, doors and clutter. Our parts come from known kits on a grid (D48), so where a walker can go can be worked out from the grid as each part goes in, the same way buildings and rooms are (D155).
- **Kinds of work, likings, priorities and needs are data** (D136), so a realm's culture can bring recruits who like different work.

## Questions for the developers

1. **Plans are drafts until you say they are ready**, and the tribe then carries what they need: is that the building help you want?
2. **Recruits putting parts in**, beyond carrying: later and only for skilled builders, or never?
3. **What recruits may use**: is a chest of your own enough to start with?
4. **Needs**: as light as proposed (comfort makes work better; pushed too far, they leave), or more?
5. **Downed before dead**: is that how recruits should die?
6. **Tribes and outposts**: one tribe across all your claims, or one per claim?
7. **Recruiting players**: can a player be in more than one tribe, and who in a tribe may do what?
