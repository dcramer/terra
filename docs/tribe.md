# Your tribe: its people and outposts

Part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89). How recruits are found, and the rest of the world's people, are in [people.md](people.md). This page covers the tribe they join: what recruits do once they are home at an outpost, and who else belongs. Each item is as settled as the decision it cites; the rest is a proposal for the developers to react to. What other games do is in [research/recruits-at-the-base.md](../research/recruits-at-the-base.md).

"Tribe" is the developers' word, used here as a working name.

## What the developers have said

From 2026-10-12:

- **The intent is Kenshi-based to some degree** (D161). You recruit people, bring them back safely, and they live in your tribe.
- **They take part on their own, however the simulation makes sense** (D161, D162): working crafting stations and helping haul resources. You do not assign them.
- **It is not a colony sim** (D161). You do not micromanage them. "A little automatically managed tribe simulator."
- **They have stats they level up, as you do** (D88, D123), so they get better at the work they do.
- **Building help is useful for carrying things to a building** (D161). They must not start building a plan while you are still designing it. Recruits should potentially help build, too (Leaning). A plan is handed to them by switching from blueprint to build: Plan and Build modes, agreed for now, perhaps to revisit (D170, Leaning).
- **They take their gear on their own** (D161). A container is either personal or the tribe's, and that is enough.
- **Recruits are much like players** (D169): health, stamina and mana, hunger, thirst and body temperature. Cold or heat slows their work. They sleep at night, and need a bed: without one, their stamina stays very low, so they work very slowly all the time. Potentially they sleep on the ground, badly rested, getting a little stamina back.
- **They help defend you in some situations** (D90, Leaning): if you are attacked near your claim, or in raids, if raids come later. Monsters will likely not form inside an outpost, but danger comes close to home, and protecting the outpost, with walls, guards or otherwise, is part of having one (D169).
- **They travel and fight beside you**, later (D161): a future to-do.
- **They die when their health runs out** (D161, D169). They also die of hunger and thirst: an empty gauge weakens them and wears health away over a few days, not fast, the same as for players. A way to revive them may come later. For now they drop what they had; graves may come later.
- **No ageing, for now** (D161). Families, as in Medieval Dynasty, would be neat but are wishful thinking.
- **A tribe holds its claims, and each claim is an outpost** (D166). Everyone in the tribe, players as well as recruits, is part of it, since you can recruit other players too. All of a tribe's outposts are shared within it, but **each recruit belongs to one outpost**. Moving one is likely done at a station, for now the claim stone: you take them with you, or let them walk on their own and risk death (Leaning).
- **A player is in one tribe at a time** (D166). Tribes are like clans or guilds, with two ranks, member and leader. A player on their own is a tribe of one.
- **Medieval Dynasty is a source of inspiration** alongside Kenshi.

## Based on

- **Kenshi**: people who join you, work, fight and grow by doing (D95).
- **Medieval Dynasty**: a village that grows around you. Villagers who get better at their trade, live in houses you build, and keep working while you are away.
- Two more, suggested by the research for the part neither of those has, work without managing:
  - **Palworld**: everyone at the base takes whatever work they are suited to, and drops it when the base is attacked.
  - **Dragon Quest Builders 2**: you say what you want by putting things in the world, such as a blueprint, a labelled field or a chest, and residents get on with it.

## Not a colony sim

The line between the two, proposed: **in a colony sim the player is the scheduler; in ours the player builds the place and the people fill it.**

| You do | You never do |
|---|---|
| Find people, recruit them and bring them home safely ([people.md](people.md#recruits)) | Set work priorities, schedules or zones |
| Build what the tribe needs: beds, stations, storage, fields | Assign each recruit to a job before they will work |
| Draw the plans for what is to be built (D152, D154), drafting the big ones first if you like ([Building](#building)) | Equip each recruit by hand |
| Stock the stores, and sometimes ask one person for something | Put the injured to bed, or remind anyone to eat |

**Building a forge is how you say you want smithing.** Someone who can smith takes it up. A field is how you say you want farming. A plan is how you say you want a wall. The world is the interface, as in Dragon Quest Builders 2.

## Who does what

From the developers (D162): recruits take part on their own, in whatever way the simulation makes sense. How the simulation decides is proposed here; the player never sees the mechanism, only people getting on with things.

- **Each outpost has work to do**: every station wants a worker, and so do fields, animals, the woodpile, the stores and the watch. What counts as work is data, as stations are (D136).
- **The best fit takes each piece of work**: whoever is most skilled at it. Then whoever **likes** it best: each recruit likes a kind of work or two, from what they did before and from their culture (D86). Bren was a smith before you rescued her, so she goes to the forge even when someone else is as good. Then whoever is nearest.
- **People keep their work.** Who does what is worked out again only when something changes: someone joins, leaves or dies; a station is built or taken down; someone gets better. Not every moment. So people stay at their work and get good at it, rather than wandering off to whatever is nearest.
- **Liking pulls toward specialising.** Skills grow by doing (D123), so whoever starts at a job gets better at it. Without a pull toward what each person likes, whoever took the nearest work at the start would be stuck in it. With it, a born smith drifts back to the forge when a second one is built.
- **Asking, the one override.** Talk to a recruit and ask them to work the kiln. They keep at it until you ask otherwise. It is a word in the world, not a screen of switches.
- **With nothing to do at their own work**, a recruit helps wherever help is wanted: hauling, carrying to a build, fetching. More recruits than work means a faster tribe, never an idle one.

## What comes first

Proposed: one order of priorities for everyone, the same at every outpost, so the tribe does the obvious thing without being told. It is data, not anyone's setting.

1. **Danger**: fight it if they can, keep away from it if they cannot (see [Defence](#defence)).
2. **Their own needs**: eat, sleep, warm up, heal.
3. **Their own work.**
4. **Help**: carry to a build, haul to the stores, fetch what their work is missing.
5. **Their own time**: talk, sit by the fire, wander the outpost, which is what makes the place look lived in.

A build never pulls a smith off the forge. Help comes from those with nothing to do at their own work. That rule avoids Palworld's failure, where a new plan empties the mines.

## Hauling and the stores

Proposed:

- **An outpost's stores are one store to its recruits.** A recruit who needs iron knows there is iron in the outpost, and goes to whichever chest holds it. Nothing is ever "in the wrong chest", which is the most common complaint about Medieval Dynasty and Soulmask.
- **Hauling is still seen.** A recruit walks to the chest and carries the iron to the forge. A hunter carries the deer home. A log goes over a shoulder (D140). Realistic, so you see the tribe working, but never the reason work stops.
- **Output goes to the nearest store** with room, carried by whoever made it or by someone helping.
- **Each outpost's stores are its own.** Recruits stay at their outpost (D166) and there is no fast travel between claims (D72), so goods move between outposts only when someone carries them. Recruits who carry them, by roads, carts and caravans of your own, are for later ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#why-explore-and-why-settle-in-more-than-one-place)).

## Building

From the developers (D161): help is useful for carrying things to a building, and no one starts on a plan you are still designing. Recruits should potentially help build, too (Leaning). How a plan passes from you to the tribe: Plan and Build modes, agreed for now and perhaps to be revisited (D170, Leaning).

### Plan and Build: handing work to the tribe

The problem: plans cost nothing to lay and are meant to be tried, moved and redrawn (D139). Recruits must not start on one you are still drawing. But adding a single wall inside a house should not take an extra step. How other games do it, from memory and unchecked:

- **Palworld** starts building the moment a plan is laid, which is the failure the developers named.
- **Dragon Quest Builders 2** designs a blueprint first, then hands it to residents.
- **Manor Lords** has you draw a plot, then confirm it.
- **RimWorld** lets you forbid a plan until it is wanted.
- **The Sims** separates its build mode from living.

The first proposal, staking out each building before the tribe touches it, suits one big construction. It is an extra step for every small one, as the developers pointed out. They suggested switching from blueprint to build instead. Proposed, after that: **the hammer has two modes, and the common one needs no extra step.**

| Mode | What you lay | The tribe |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** (the default) | A plan, as today: the pale ghost, meant to be built | Carries for it at once and, later, builds it |
| **Plan** | A draft: drawn in blue lines like a drawing on paper, with your name on it, seen only by those building (see below) | Leaves it alone |

**How it plays**:

- **A wall inside the house**: in Build mode, as always, lay it. A recruit brings the logs, and you or a builder put it in. Nothing extra.
- **A new hall**: tap T to switch to Plan, and draw. Try three layouts, move the doors, leave it half done until tomorrow. Nobody touches it.
- **When the design is right**, hold T at it, in either mode, and the whole building's drafts become plans at once (D155). A recruit answers ("Right, the hall, then"), and the tribe starts. Holding T at a plan nothing has been put into turns it back into a draft, and the tribe stops.
- **The keys follow R's pattern**: R taps for the next variant and holds for the wheel. T taps to switch modes and holds to turn a building between draft and plan. Which mode you are in shows on screen beside the hammer, and in the colour of what you lay.
- **A slip in Build mode costs nothing.** Recruits take a moment to come, since they walk to the stores first. Undo (Y) within its minute takes the plan back, and anything carried for it returns to the stores. Moving or redrawing a plan nothing has been put into is allowed in either mode; the tribe follows the change.
- **You never have to use Plan mode.** Playing alone, or without recruits, building is exactly as it is today.
- **Then the tribe carries.** Recruits with nothing else to do bring what a plan needs from the stores and pile it beside the build: logs (D140), stone, beams. The prompt on a part says so: "Log: 3 of 12. 4 here, 5 on the way". You put the parts in without fetching anything.
- **Then, later, they build.** A recruit who is a skilled builder (D88) puts parts in from the pile in the physical order, floor first and roof last ([building.md](building.md#planned-then-constructed)). You can work beside them.
- **Several plans are worked in the order they were laid or made plans.** To have one done first, ask a recruit to work on it.
- **Among players, a draft is someone's idea and a plan is the tribe's.** Any member may turn their own drafts into plans; a leader may do it to anyone's (see [ranks](#tribes-and-outposts)).
- **What is needed counts plans, not drafts.** The outpost's list of what its plans still need leaves drafts out. Aiming at a draft still shows its own cost, so you can plan.

**Plan mode might become a building mode of its own** (the developers, 2026-10-12, an idea): rather than drafting through your character's eyes, a separate view for designing, such as from above, or any camera that is not your character's, as The Sims and city builders do. Nothing is chosen.

**Plan mode gets tools for drafts** (D170, from the developers): copying, and other features, which are "extremely nice", since all it does is lay drafts, which cost nothing and change nothing in the world. Proposed, after what building games offer:

- **Copy and paste** a building, or part of one: pick it up as drafts and lay it again elsewhere, turned as you like.
- **Mirror** a draft, so a wing can match the other.
- **Move a whole draft building** at once, or raise it a storey.
- **Save a design** to lay again at any outpost, as Valheim's and Satisfactory's blueprints are saved (from memory, unchecked); perhaps shared with the tribe.
- **Copy what is built**: pick up a finished building as drafts, to build another like it.
- **See its cost** for the whole design, and what the outpost's stores already hold toward it.

**Who sees drafts** (D170, Leaning, from the developers): drafts show only to someone building, with the hammer in hand, in either mode. They show to anyone building within the claim. Put the hammer away and the outpost looks as it is, without anyone's half-finished ideas over it. Plans the tribe is working on still show to everyone, since they are work under way.

### Also

- **Recruits never draw plans.** What the outpost looks like is yours.
- **When materials run out**, carrying waits, and a recruit says what is missing ("We're out of stone for the wall").
## Equipment

From the developers (D161): they take their gear on their own. A container is either **personal** or **the tribe's**, and that is enough.

- **They arrive with what they had**, and it is theirs.
- **They take what their work needs from the tribe's containers**: a woodcutter the best axe they can use, a guard armour and a weapon. They put it back when it is not needed. No one is equipped by hand, which is the chore players complain of in Kenshi and Soulmask.
- **A personal container is yours alone.** Recruits and other players leave it be, so your best sword and last good food stay yours. Proposed: the tribe's is the default, and you switch a container to personal when you open it. A recruit never takes what anyone wears or has on their action bar.
- **Hand it over yourself** if you want someone to have a thing. What you give a recruit is theirs to keep.
- **Gear wears** (D99), and they mend it themselves at the station that mends it, quicker if they know the craft.
- **What they wear shows** on them (D128), so a guard looks like a guard.
## Needs

From the developers (D169): **recruits are generally much like a player.** Same body, same needs:

| | For a recruit | Met by |
|---|---|---|
| Health | As yours. When it runs out, they die (see [When a recruit dies](#when-a-recruit-dies)) | Rest, food, healing |
| Stamina and mana | As yours (D61, D102), spent in fighting and abilities | Rest, food |
| Hunger and thirst | As yours: an empty gauge weakens them, then wears health away over days | Meals and drink from the stores; cooked food is better than foraged |
| Body temperature | As yours (D153). Out of comfort, they work slower; far out, it harms them | Shelter, fire, clothing |
| Sleep | They sleep at night (D169). Without a bed, their stamina stays very low; perhaps they sleep on the ground and get a little back | A bed of their own, indoors (D155) |

Proposed, for how:

- **Sleep is their day's shape, not a meter** (D169). Recruits work by day and sleep at night in their own bed, so an outpost is busy by day and quiet at night, with a watch kept by whoever guards it.
- **Beds are claimed on their own** (D169, from the developers), as work is ([Who does what](#who-does-what)): you build beds, and the outpost settles who sleeps where. Proposed:
  - A recruit without a bed takes a free one at their outpost, the nearest to their work. As in Enshrouded, a new bed is taken by someone who needs it.
  - A bed, once taken, is theirs. They keep it, and nobody is moved from one to another.
  - When there are fewer beds than people, those who came first keep theirs, and the newest go without until another is built.
  - A bed is freed when its sleeper dies or moves to another outpost.
  - A bed can be personal, as a container can ([Equipment](#equipment)), so the bed you sleep in stays yours. Others are the tribe's.
  - Asking a recruit to sleep somewhere else is the same word as asking them to take other work: rare, and never needed.
- **No bed, little stamina** (from the developers). A recruit without a bed barely rests, so their stamina stays very low, and they work very slowly, all the time, not only the next day. Potentially (Leaning), they sleep on the ground instead: badly rested, but getting back a little stamina, so they are slow rather than nearly stopped. Proposed: by a fire, indoors if they can, which is warmer (D153). Work draws on stamina as fighting does, so a bed is the first thing a new recruit needs. Proposed: they say so ("I've nowhere to sleep"), and the claim stone's roster shows who has no bed.
- **They look after themselves** from what the outpost has: they eat, drink, warm up and go to bed on their own. You provide the place, the food and the fire, not each person's care, as Dragon Quest Builders 2's rooms do.
- **Hunger and thirst are slow to kill, and loud before they do** (D169, and the same for players, D101). From the developers: the gauge itself is fine; once it is empty, health wears away slowly, over a few days, not fast, and meanwhile they are weaker. Proposed:
  - The outpost runs out of food. Recruits say so, and the roster says so.
  - Those who can hunt, fish or gather go out for food on their own, before their other work.
  - Hungry, with the gauge empty, their attributes fall and they work slower and recover less, as you would.
  - Health then wears away over a few days: three in-game days for hunger and two for thirst (D101), an hour and a half and an hour of play at half an hour to a day (D134).

  So a death from hunger means an outpost left without food for days, with everyone saying so.
- **Food has a quality, and good food saves time** (D169, from the developers): what recruits forage for themselves is poor food, and cooked food is better. It fills more hunger, and may give other boosts. So:
  - Foraged and raw food fills a little and soon wears off, so recruits living on it eat often and spend their days finding more.
  - Cooked meals fill more and last longer, so a recruit fed by a cook eats less often and works more.
  - Other boosts from good food, for players and recruits alike, are still to be chosen (D101).

  A cook is worth having: the outpost's work goes further, and nobody has to forage.
- **Cold and heat slow work.** A forge-hand in winter without a coat works slower, as the developers said; a warm workshop is worth building.
- **How someone is doing shows** in how they look and what they say (D93), and the roster gathers it ([Seeing what they do](#seeing-what-they-do)). No mood meter to tend.

**Ideas for later**, not chosen:

- **Comfort**: a better house, a table, a fire to sit by, decoration, making work better, as in Medieval Dynasty, Soulmask and Dragon Quest Builders 2.
- **Safety**: walls, a watch and light at night, with recruits frightened when monsters are near.
- **Company**: others to eat and talk with, a place to gather.
- **Liking their work**: doing the work they like making them better at it.
- **Leaving**: a recruit kept badly for long enough goes, as in Medieval Dynasty, saying why first.
## Growing

Recruits grow as you do (D123). Their work's skills rise by doing it, on the same rank ladder as yours ([progression.md](progression.md#skills-on-the-rank-ladder)). What they can make follows their rank: an iron-rank smith cannot work silver-rank metal.

Crossing to the next rank may need a key (D126): a book, a teacher or a feat. You, or another recruit who already stands at that rank, can be the teacher, so a master in the tribe raises the others.

Their skills, ranks and likings read on their own sheet, like yours (D93).

**No ageing, for now** (D161). Recruits do not grow older, and no one is born into the tribe. Families, where recruits pair up and have children who grow into it, as in Medieval Dynasty, are wishful thinking for later.

## Defence

From the developers (D90, Leaning): they help defend you if you are attacked near your claim, and in raids if those come later. And (D169): monsters likely will not form inside an outpost, but **danger needs to be close to home**. Part of having an outpost is protecting it, with walls, guards or whatever else. Proposed:

- **Monsters do not form inside an outpost, but they come to it.** They form in the wild around it, roam, hunt and spill out ([creatures.md](creatures.md)), and an outpost is in their way. Walls, gates, light and a watch are what keep them out.
- **When danger comes into the outpost, or you are attacked near it, everyone who can fight drops their work and fights.** The rest take shelter indoors. Afterwards, everyone goes back to what they were doing. This is the one switch every game in the research uses.
- **The watch is work like any other**: a guard or two who keep at it, by day and through the night, patrol the outpost, and ring a bell or shout when something comes.
- **How well they fight is their skill and rank**, as for you ([combat.md](combat.md)). A bronze-rank monster walks through iron-rank farmers.
- **Raids are not a concern for now** ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#questions-for-the-developers), question 2). When they come, they come to outposts. A raid should be something you can answer, not a summons that nags, as Fallout 4's settlement quests do.
## Coming with you

From the developers (D161): recruits can travel and fight beside you, as a future to-do. Nothing is designed for it yet. When it comes, the questions are how many can come, how you ask them, and how they keep up on big round realms ([player.md](player.md)).

## When a recruit dies

From the developers (D169): **a recruit dies when their health runs out**, and slowly of hunger and thirst ([Needs](#needs)). A way to revive them may come later, if it is wanted.

- **Dead is dead**, for now. Everything they had, worn and carried, drops where they fell (D84), to be taken back before it decays. Graves, or the tribe marking its dead, may come later.
- **Deaths come from danger close to home, and from leaving the outpost unprotected or unfed.** That is the game: an outpost is a place you keep.
- **Never from the system's own failings**, such as a pathing bug or a recruit stuck behind a door. The research found these the losses players resent most in Kenshi and Soulmask. A recruit should only die where a player, watching, would see why.
## How many

Proposed: as many as each outpost can house and feed ([people.md](people.md#recruits)). Since recruits come by rescue and by helping settlements (D90), the supply is a limit of its own, as in Enshrouded.

There will also be a cap per outpost, technical and mine to set, since every recruit costs the server something (D52).

## Seeing what they do

Proposed:

- **A roster, to read, not to run**: who lives at the outpost, what they are doing now, and their skills. Kept at the outpost's claim stone, which is also where a recruit is sent to another outpost ([Moving a recruit](#moving-a-recruit-to-another-outpost)).
- **Above all, why someone is not working**: "Bren is waiting: no charcoal in the stores." An idle worker with no visible reason is the failure every game in the research shares, and their guides are troubleshooting lists.
- **Said in the world first** (D93): Bren tells you the forge is out of charcoal when you pass. The roster only gathers what you could have heard.

## Tribes and outposts

From the developers (D166):

- **A tribe holds its claims, and each claim is an outpost of it.** All of a tribe's outposts are shared within it.
- **Everyone in the tribe is part of it, players as well as recruits**, since you can recruit other players too. Whoever is in it may build in its outposts, use their stores and stations, and ask their recruits for something ([building.md](building.md#claims)).
- **Recruits are not shared between outposts.** Each lives and works at one. Different outposts may want different people: a miner at the one by the ore, a smith at home.
- **A player is in one tribe at a time**, and **a player on their own is a tribe of one**, and its leader. Joining another tribe means leaving your own.
- **Two ranks, member and leader**, as tribes in Ark and clans in Conan Exiles have (from memory, unchecked):

  | | Member | Leader |
  |---|---|---|
  | Build, take down what they built, use the tribe's stores and stations, ask recruits for something | Yes | Yes |
  | Recruit people, and bring them home to any outpost | Yes | Yes |
  | Invite and remove players, and set their rank | No | Yes |
  | Place, grow and give up claims; take down anything | No | Yes |

Proposed:

- **Recruiting a player** is asking them to join, and their accepting. Unlike a recruit, a player can leave whenever they like.
- **A recruit belongs to the outpost you bring them home to**, and to the tribe, not to one player. One who joined because you rescued them stays if you leave. What you kept in personal containers leaves with you.

### Moving a recruit to another outpost

From the developers (D166, Leaning): likely done at a station. You take them with you, or let them walk on their own and risk death. Proposed:

- **The station is the claim stone, for now** (from the developers). Every outpost has one already ([building.md](building.md#claims)). It also shows who lives there and what they do, which is the roster ([Seeing what they do](#seeing-what-they-do)) as a thing in the world. A station of its own, such as a board, can come later.
- **At the claim stone, you pick a recruit and another outpost of the tribe**, and how they go:
  - **With you**: they follow you until you reach it, as a companion would (D161, later). Safer, but you must make the trip.
  - **On their own**: they pack up and set off on foot, by roads where there are any. There is no fast travel between claims (D72), so it takes the time it takes. They can meet what is on the way, and a weak recruit sent through dangerous country may not arrive.
- **They arrive and settle**, taking a bed and work there as any new recruit does.
## What it asks of the engine

Technical, mine:

- **Detail by distance.** With a player nearby, recruits walk, carry and work in full. With no one near, an outpost's work runs as a summary: so much made, eaten and mended an hour, as Medieval Dynasty's output appears in storage. A thousand players' tribes (D52) cannot all be walking.
- **One list of work per outpost.** Each keeps a list of what wants doing. Its recruits take from it, rather than each searching the world. The list changes only when something changes (D127).
- **Pathing in what players build** is the hardest part, and the top complaint in Palworld and Kenshi: storeys, stairs, doors and clutter. Our parts come from known kits on a grid (D48), so where a walker can go can be worked out from the grid as each part goes in, the same way buildings and rooms are (D155).
- **Kinds of work, likings, priorities and needs are data** (D136), so a realm's culture can bring recruits who like different work.

3. Answered: **starving takes three in-game days, thirst two** (D101).
