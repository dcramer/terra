# Your tribe: recruits who live and work with you

Part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89). How recruits are found, and the rest of the world's people, are in [people.md](people.md). This page covers what recruits do once they have joined you. Each item is as settled as the decision it cites; the rest is a proposal for the developers to react to. What other games do is in [research/recruits-at-the-base.md](../research/recruits-at-the-base.md).

"Tribe" is the developers' word, used here as a working name for the people who live in your claims.

## What the developers have said

From 2026-10-12 (D161):

- **The intent is Kenshi-based to some degree.** You recruit people, and they live in your tribe.
- **They take part in the work**: working crafting stations and helping you haul resources. Helping you build was named too, but whether and how they help build is still to be worked out.
- **It is not a colony sim.** You do not micromanage them; they are functional on their own. "A little automatically managed tribe simulator."
- **They have stats they level up, as you do** (D88, D123), so they get better at the jobs they work.
- **Likely, they claim a job on their own** (D162, Leaning), by being better at it than the others, or by something else.
- **They help defend you in some situations** (D90, Leaning): if you are attacked near your claim, or in raids, if raids come later.
- **Medieval Dynasty is a source of inspiration** alongside Kenshi.
- **To be worked out**: how they get equipment, whether and how they help build, and how they prioritise work.

## Based on

- **Kenshi**: people who join you, work, fight and grow by doing (D95).
- **Medieval Dynasty**: a village that grows around you. Villagers who get better at their trade, live in houses you build, and keep working while you are away.
- Two more, suggested by the research for the part neither of those has, work without managing:
  - **Palworld**: everyone at the base takes whatever work they are suited to, and drops it when the base is attacked.
  - **Dragon Quest Builders 2**: you say what you want by putting things in the world, such as a blueprint, a labelled field or a chest, and residents get on with it.

## Not a colony sim

The line between the two, proposed: **in a colony sim the player is the scheduler; in ours the player builds the place and the people fill it.**

| You do | You never do |
|---|---|
| Find and recruit people ([people.md](people.md#recruits)) | Set work priorities, schedules or zones |
| Build what the tribe needs: beds, stations, storage, fields | Assign each recruit to a job before they will work |
| Draw the plans for what is to be built (D152, D154) | Equip each recruit by hand |
| Stock the stores, and sometimes ask one person for something | Put the injured to bed, or remind anyone to eat |

**Building a forge is how you say you want smithing.** A recruit who can smith takes it. A field is how you say you want farming; a plan is how you say you want a wall. The world is the interface, as in Dragon Quest Builders 2.

## Who takes which work

From the developers (D162, Leaning): likely each recruit claims a job on their own, by being better at it than the others, or by something else. Proposed:

- **The claim's posts.** Every station is a post, and so is each kind of standing work the claim has: a field, a woodlot, a pen of animals, the stores, the watch. Posts are data, as stations are (D136).
- **The best fit takes each post**: the recruit with the highest rank in its skill. Then whoever **likes** the work best: each recruit has a liking or two, from what they did before and from their culture (D86). Bren was a smith before you rescued her, so she goes to the forge even when someone else is as good. Then whoever is nearest.
- **Posts are settled when something changes**: someone joins, leaves or falls; a post is built or taken down; a recruit ranks up. Not every moment. So people keep their jobs and get good at them. This avoids the churn of games where workers wander off to whatever is nearest.
- **Liking pulls toward specialising.** Skills grow by doing (D123), so whoever starts at a job gets better at it. Without a pull toward what each person likes, whoever took the nearest job at the start would be stuck in it. With it, a born smith drifts back to the forge when a second one is built.
- **Asking, the one override.** Talk to a recruit and ask them to take a post, such as "work the kiln". They keep it until you ask otherwise. It is a word in the world, not a screen of switches, and the only order you can give.
- **Without a post**, a recruit helps wherever help is wanted: hauling, building, fetching. More recruits than posts means a faster tribe, never an idle one.

## What comes first

Proposed: one order of priorities for everyone, the same in every claim, so the tribe does the obvious thing without being told. It is data, not anyone's setting.

1. **Danger**: fight it if they can, keep away from it if they cannot (see [Defence](#defence)).
2. **Their own needs**: eat, sleep, warm up, heal.
3. **Their post.**
4. **Help**: build from plans, haul to the stores, fetch what a post is missing.
5. **Their own time**: talk, sit by the fire, wander the claim, which is what makes the place look lived in.

A plan never pulls a smith off the forge. Building and hauling are done by those without a post, and by those whose post has nothing to do. That rule avoids Palworld's failure, where a new plan empties the mines.

## Hauling and the stores

Proposed:

- **A claim's stores are one store to its tribe.** A recruit who needs iron knows there is iron in the claim, and goes to whichever chest holds it. Nothing is ever "in the wrong chest", which is the most common complaint about Medieval Dynasty and Soulmask.
- **Hauling is still seen.** A recruit walks to the chest and carries the iron to the forge. A hunter carries the deer home. A log goes over a shoulder (D140). Realistic, so you see the tribe working, but never the reason work stops.
- **Output goes to the nearest store** with room, carried by whoever made it or by someone helping.
- **Your own things stay yours.** Proposed: a chest can be marked as yours, so the tribe never takes your best sword or your last good food.
- **Between claims**, recruits who carry goods from an outpost to home are for later, after the first claim works. The idea is roads, carts and caravans of your own ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#why-explore-and-why-settle-in-more-than-one-place)).

## Building

The developers named helping you build, then left whether and how open. Proposed:

- **You draw the plans** (D152, D154), and **the tribe puts the parts in**, taking the materials from the claim's stores (D141). This is how Dragon Quest Builders 2, Palworld and Sons of the Forest all do it. Construction already allows it ([construction.md](construction.md#planned-then-constructed)).
- **Recruits never draw plans.** What the claim looks like is yours.
- **Who builds**: those without a post, plus anyone whose skill is building and who takes the builder's post. A skilled builder puts parts in faster (D88), as you do.
- **Building with you**: work on a plan and the tribe joins in. Big work becomes a scene: logs carried in, walls going up, people at every corner.
- **When materials run out**, the build waits, and the claim's list of what its plans still need tells you what to fetch ([construction.md](construction.md#planned-then-constructed)).

## Equipment

To be worked out (Open). Proposed:

- **They arrive with what they had**, and it is theirs.
- **They take what their work needs from the stores**: a woodcutter the best axe they can use, a guard armour and a weapon. They put it back when it is not needed. No one is equipped by hand, which is the chore players complain of in Kenshi and Soulmask.
- **Hand it over yourself** if you want someone to have a thing. What you give a recruit is theirs to keep.
- **Gear wears** (D99), and they mend it themselves at the station that mends it, quicker if they know the craft.
- **What they wear shows** on them (D128), so a guard looks like a guard.

## Needs

Proposed, light, and never a death spiral:

- **A bed in a building**, indoors (D155). A new bed goes to the nearest recruit without one, as in Enshrouded.
- **Food from the stores**, better if someone cooks. **Warmth** (D153).
- **Comfort makes work better**: a good bed, a roof, a fire, good food, and the work they like. Without them, a recruit works slower and says so. Pushed too far, they leave. They never starve in front of you.
- **No mood meter to tend.** How someone is doing shows in how they look and what they say (D93).

## Growing

Recruits grow as you do (D123). Their work's skills rise by doing it, on the same rank ladder as yours ([essences.md](essences.md#skills-on-the-rank-ladder)). What they can make follows their rank: an iron-rank smith cannot work silver-rank metal.

Crossing to the next rank may need a key (D126): a book, a teacher or a feat. You, or another recruit who already stands at that rank, can be the teacher, so a master in the tribe raises the others.

Their skills, ranks and likings read on their own sheet, like yours (D93).

## Defence

From the developers (D90, Leaning): they help defend you if you are attacked near your claim, and in raids if those come later. Proposed:

- **When danger comes into the claim, or you are attacked near it, everyone who can fight drops their work and fights.** The rest take shelter indoors. Afterwards, everyone goes back to what they were doing. This is the one switch every game in the research uses.
- **The watch is a post**: a guard or two who keep at it, patrol the claim, and ring a bell or shout when something comes.
- **How well they fight is their skill and rank**, as for you ([combat.md](combat.md)). A bronze-rank monster walks through iron-rank farmers.
- **Raids are not a concern for now** ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md#questions-for-the-developers), question 2). When they come, they come to claims. A raid should be something you can answer, not a summons that nags, as Fallout 4's settlement quests do.
- **Coming with you**, a recruit travelling and fighting beside you away from home, is still open ([people.md](people.md#questions)).

## When a recruit falls

Open ([people.md](people.md#questions), question 2). Recommended: **downed before dead.** A downed recruit is carried home, or gets up and limps home, and mends in bed.

Losing one for good should only follow from something the player chose, never from the system's own failings, such as a pathing bug or workers left to wildlife. Players in Kenshi and Soulmask resent those losses most.

## How many

Proposed: as many as your claims can house and feed ([people.md](people.md#recruits)). Since recruits come by rescue and by helping settlements (D90), the supply is a limit of its own, as in Enshrouded.

There will also be a cap per claim, technical and mine to set, since every recruit costs the server something (D52).

## Seeing what they do

Proposed:

- **A roster, to read, not to run**: who is in the tribe, their post, what they are doing now, and their skills.
- **Above all, why someone is not working**: "Bren is waiting: no charcoal in the stores." An idle worker with no visible reason is the failure every game in the research shares, and their guides are troubleshooting lists.
- **Said in the world first** (D93): Bren tells you the forge is out of charcoal when you pass. The roster only gathers what you could have heard.

## Playing together

Open. Proposed: **a tribe belongs to a claim**, not to one player. Anyone the claim allows can ask its recruits for something, as groups share claims and recruits ([arc.md](arc.md#playing-together)).

## What it asks of the engine

Technical, mine:

- **Detail by distance.** With a player nearby, recruits walk, carry and work in full. With no one near, a claim's work runs as a summary: so much made, eaten and mended an hour, as Medieval Dynasty's output appears in storage. A thousand players' tribes (D52) cannot all be walking.
- **One work list per claim.** Each claim keeps a list of its posts and of what wants doing. Recruits take from it, rather than each searching the world. The list changes only when something changes (D127).
- **Pathing in what players build** is the hardest part, and the top complaint in Palworld and Kenshi: storeys, stairs, doors and clutter. Our parts come from known kits on a grid (D48), so where a walker can go can be worked out from the grid as each part goes in, the same way buildings and rooms are (D155).
- **Posts, likings, priorities and needs are data** (D136), so a realm's culture can bring recruits who like different work.

## Questions for the developers

1. **Posts, filled by skill and then liking, with asking as the only override**: is that the automatic job claiming you meant (D162)?
2. **Building**: you draw the plans and the tribe builds them from the stores. Is that the help you want?
3. **Equipment**: do recruits take what their work needs from the stores on their own, with a chest you can keep for yourself?
4. **One store per claim**: is it fine that recruits know what any chest in the claim holds, for the sake of no "wrong chest"?
5. **Needs**: as light as proposed (comfort makes work better; pushed too far, they leave), or more?
6. **Families**, as in Medieval Dynasty: do recruits pair up and have children who grow into the tribe, or is the tribe only those you find?
7. **Recruits who die**: downed before dead, as recommended? Lost for good ever?
8. **Coming with you**: can a recruit travel and fight beside you?
9. **Whose tribe**: does it belong to a claim, shared by everyone the claim allows?
