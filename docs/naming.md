# Names and lore: the rules for making more

Status: Open, a proposal for the developers (D158), 2026-10-12. They asked for "a thematic pattern that you can continue to generate names, lore and so on from": a documented set of rules, so that every name and every piece of lore, written by us or by the generator, comes out of one pattern and never out of *He Who Fights With Monsters*'s own words (D125). The fundamentals the names are for are in [lore.md](lore.md).

## Two layers

- **The shared tongue.** The words for how the universe works, the same in every realm: magic, ranks, essences, the gap between realms, gods as a kind of thing. Few, fixed, chosen by hand by the rules below. A player learns them once.
- **The tongues of peoples.** Every name a realm's people give: persons, places, gods, monsters, styles, things they make. Many, generated by rule from the realm's seed and its magic, never written one by one.

The two never mix. A realm's people call their god by their own name for it, while the shared tongue only says what kind of thing a god is.

## The shared tongue: magic is water

**The one metaphor.** Everything about magic is spoken of as water:
- it flows;
- it pools;
- it runs deep or shallow;
- it floods;
- it wells up;
- it is crossed.

The docs already talk this way: pooling places, surges, magic as the world's weather. Taking it as the rule makes every new term guessable, and it holds in every realm: a fire realm's magic still pools and floods, only it runs hot.

**The rules for a shared word:**
1. **It says what the thing does, as water would.** A crossing between realms is where the gap runs shallow, so it is a ford.
2. **Plain, old English:** short words of one or two syllables, which can be joined into compounds, as wellspring or deepcraft. No Latin or Greek coinages, and nothing that sounds like science.
3. **Never a term the books coined,** and never a near copy of one. Common words the books also use, such as essence, monster, magic and mana, are free to use. **One exception, by the developers' choice: the rank names are the books' own** (D159).
4. **Readable in play:** order and size are obvious without a manual. Rank names say at a glance which is bigger.
5. **One word, one meaning.** A word used for magic is not also a place or a season. The ranks are metals and a gem (D159), so a rank is always said with "rank" (an iron-rank monster, a bronze-rank portal), and the metal alone always means the material.

## The tongues of peoples

Every people gets its own sound, worked out from its realm's seed, so a death realm's people and a growth realm's never name things alike ([settings.md](settings.md#making-generated-realms-fit-the-lore), point 6).

**A tongue is a few numbers and lists:**
- **sounds:** the consonants and vowels it uses, and how often;
- **syllable shapes:** such as consonant and vowel, or consonant, vowel and consonant, and how many syllables a word has;
- **patterns:** how a person's name is made (a given name and a kin name, or a given name and where they come from), and how a god's or a place's is;
- **the words for a few things it names often:** water, hill, home, god, death, kin. Its names are built from these, so a people's names carry meaning even when players can't read them.

**The realm's magic picks the sound,** as a starting palette that the seed varies:

| Leaning of the realm's magic | Sound | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Growth, life | Soft and flowing: l, r, w, n, m; open vowels a, e, o; long words | Green and slow |
| Death, decay | Dark and hushed: s, th, v, m, hard g; vowels o, u; closing consonants | Cold stone |
| Fire, heat | Sharp and breathy: k, h, sh, r; vowel a; short words | A spark |
| Metal, machine | Clipped and hard: t, k, d, b; vowels i, a; stacked consonants | A hammer |
| Storm, air | Rushing: v, z, r, f; vowels e, i; doubled sounds | Wind |
| Crystal, light | Bright and thin: s, n, l, t; vowels i, e; vowel pairs | Glass |

Other leanings get their own rows as they are made: more data, not code.

**Two names for every place.**
- **The people's own name,** in their tongue.
- **A plain English name the players use:** what is there, said simply, after the land. Splitrock, the Black Ford, Hollow Hill, Three Wells.

The plain name is what directions are given in, since the game gives directions by landmarks, not by markers (D92), so it must say what you will see. It is made by rule from the place's features: a feature or colour plus a landform.

**Every name passes some checks** before it is used:
- it isn't on a list of the books' names and terms;
- it can be said aloud;
- it isn't a real word with a meaning we don't want;
- no two things in a realm share it.

## Lore by the same rules

What the generator writes, beyond names, follows one chain, so every realm's story is explained by [lore.md](lore.md) and none is a random pile:

1. **The cause:** the realm's magic, its leaning and how much there is.
2. **What it made:**
   - its land and climate;
   - what pools there and what forms;
   - how its people learned to use the water;
   - its gods, who are those of the realm who went deepest in its magic (D156).
3. **Its history:** a few events, by rule. A people rose, used the water their way, and something happened: overreach, war, a drought of magic, a portal opening.
4. **What is left:**
   - ruins;
   - pools where the water was overdrawn;
   - the people who survived;
   - what they believe about their gods and their past;
   - names on stones.

**Nothing is told as text.** All of it is left in places and people for players to read (D92).

**What a god is, in the generator:** a name in the people's tongue, a leaning drawn from the realm's magic, a shrine kind, and what the people believe it wants. Peoples of one realm may believe different things about the same tides.

## Open

1. **Is water the right metaphor?** The alternative: no metaphor, plain words only, with less flavour and less to generate from.
2. Answered: **the rank names** are the books' own: iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond (D159).
3. **Which working terms change in the docs** once agreed. Until then the docs keep the working terms, and this table is the map between them.
