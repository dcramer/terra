# D492: The sky light's diffuse light is its spherical harmonics, worked out on the CPU

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The painted sky (D190, D490) is projected, as it is painted, onto the nine real spherical harmonics of the first three bands (`skylight/harmonics.rs`, after Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan 2001), the sky above the horizon and the ground below apart; the environment map's diffuse light, a 16-texel cube of half floats, and the irradiance volume's lobes (`OpenSky`, now in the painting's frame with its turn) are read from them. Bevy's filter no longer convolves the diffuse map: our patch of `bevy_pbr` leaves a diffuse map that is not a storage texture as the app filled it. Bevy's GGX filter still makes the specular light from the painted cube. A test holds the harmonics against fine quadrature: 1.1% of the brightest side's light on average, 4.1% at worst (up and down by a set sun), within the paper's bound of 3% and 9%.

## Why

The sky now varies round (D490), so the lobes' old quadrature, once a height, no longer held, and a quadrature over every direction would cost milliseconds. Shipped engines give a sky's diffuse light as three bands of harmonics (Unreal's sky light, Unity's ambient probe), exact enough for any sky because the cosine passes almost nothing above them. Bevy's own irradiance pass was 2.28 of the filter's 2.6 ms a filtered frame on the integrated GPU (0.23 of 0.42 on the RTX 5090), from 1,024 random samples a texel with a fixed speckle of up to 7% sideways (skylight handoff, 2026-10-25); the harmonics cost the CPU a fraction of a millisecond and have no noise.
