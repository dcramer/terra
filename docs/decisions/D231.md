# D231: More shapes, and a cube's shape in five bits

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D199's cube layout and adds to D220's shapes, as [research/enshrouded-parity.md](../../research/enshrouded-parity.md) gap 4 proposes: a cube's shape is five bits (12 to 16; fill 17 to 20, built 21, the pending change 22 and 23, the part 24 to 31), taking the bit the pending change did not use, so 32 codes: whole, half, a stair and a slope each way, post, and now the upper half (11), a stair (12 to 15) and a slope (16 to 19) upside down, and outer (20 to 23) and inner (24 to 27) corner slopes, rising toward a side and the next side clockwise, the lower or the higher of the two ramps; an opening is 31. Snapshots are version 2; any other is refused (D353). Each shape has a top and an underside over each point (`Shape::top`, `Shape::bottom`): a walker stands on its top and passes under its underside. Support, cost, order and taking apart are a whole cube's, as D220's. A block kind cuts its surface down to a planed top and up to a planed underside (`surface`'s cut is a range, each square's diagonal chosen to follow a crease), draws a stair either way up as a stone a step, and dresses each open side's line, level or raking, with a coping above or a fascia below; a timber kind boards them (`layout::shapes`): a deck on a flat top, boards up each ramp cut at the crease, a boarded underside, flat, raking or stepped, and its open sides clad course by course as long as the shape is wide. The shapes are general cubes a roof kind can later be laid on (D226).

## Why

Mine, building gap 4 of the parity note on 2026-10-13: the bit was spare, so the shape grew without a palette per section; a range cut gives every planed shape from one mesher, and boxes of boards are how timber builders close a cut shape.
