# D658: Sky visibility is a clipmap round the camera, filled as the world streams

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Proposed in [sky-visibility.md](../sky-visibility.md), not built, and waiting on the developers' reading (D49). How much of the sky reaches a spot is held in two levels of cells round the camera (4 by 2.5 m to 128 m, 16 by 8 m to 512 m), each cell six shares of sky and how lit its ground is: visibility, lit in the shader by the sky as it is painted, as Unity's probe volumes' sky occlusion and Ghost of Tsushima's probes store it, not the light itself as D270's volume holds. Columns are worked out on the streaming pool from the woods' trees and the ground's distance field as they stream, and again only where a tree is felled, a season turns or ground is dug; the boxes move by whole cells, as DDGI's camera-locked grids do, so no cell's place changes. Among irradiance volumes the densest holding a fragment gives its light first, so a den's volume and a room's probe keep winning (D485). `play` has it first; `lush` moves onto it and D270's one volume over a known forest is deleted. Would amend D270, D273, D310, D336 and D485 as the doc lists; none is amended until a stage lands.

## Why

The lighting review of 2026-11-09 found the forest's sky volume, `OpenOverhead` and every rule reading it only in the `lush` experiment: "The game the developers play is not the one the light was tuned in." In play the woods hide no sky. Storing visibility rather than light means a sky that repaints all day never refills the volume, and an edit changes bytes; the costs in the doc are arithmetic, to be measured at its first stage.
