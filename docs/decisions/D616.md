# D616: A window past the preset's pixels is drawn at a budget and scaled up, temporally next

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The GPU's frame at the lake grows with the pixels drawn. On the worker's 4090 at the play route's view, about 3.6 ms of the frame is fixed and about 1.5 ms is spent per million pixels. The fixed part is the sun's shadows, the air's tables, clustering and the vertex-bound share of the prepass and opaque pass. The per-pixel part is the opaque pass's shading, the haze, clouds, SSAO, transmission, TAA, exposure and bloom. A window of 3816x1550 is 5.9 million pixels, nearly three times D25's 1080p, and the developers' RTX 3080 drew it in 41.6 ms (`logs/20261103-061224-play.log`). Shipped games hold a frame-time target at a large output in three steps, and we take them in that order:

1. **A pixel budget** (this step). The world is drawn at no more than `render.pixel_budget` million pixels (`terra_world::upscale::PIXEL_BUDGET`), keeping the screen's shape. 0, the default for now, means no limit. The picture is scaled up by the low preset's path (D239): drawn with its own TAA and contrast-adaptive sharpening, then stretched over the screen by a camera of its own, with the interface drawn at the screen's size. Unreal's `r.ScreenPercentage` with its spatial upscaler, FSR 1's place in a frame and consoles' fixed internal resolution under a 4K output all work this way. At 3816x1550 on the worker's 4090, route view, in one load (job 20261103-222058-e844-patch), the GPU's total was 12.47 ms native, 8.07 ms at 2.95 M (2671x1085) and 6.68 ms at 2.07 M, 1080p's pixels (2251x914). Shading passes fall about with the pixels: the opaque pass 6.95 -> 3.57, the haze 0.99 -> 0.36, SSAO 0.42 -> 0.16. Shadows hold at 0.51. The stretch softens fine detail, such as grass and the moss on a boulder, which step 2 gives back.
2. **Temporal upscaling**. The world is drawn small and accumulated at the screen's size by the temporal pass itself, as Unreal's TSR, AMD's FSR 2 and 3 and NVIDIA's DLSS do. FSR is under MIT and is the source to port (D196). Where a stretch softens the picture, these keep most of native's detail at half its pixels. Bevy 0.20's TAA resolves at the size it is drawn, so this needs a patched TAA (D184). It reads the main pass's smaller viewport (`MainPassResolutionOverride`, which Bevy's prepass, deferred, transmission and atmosphere passes already honour for DLSS) and writes its history at the screen's size, with the textures' mip bias lowered by log2 of the share. SSAO and our own passes before TAA must honour the viewport too. Bevy's own DLSS (the `dlss` feature) runs only on Vulkan and NVIDIA cards and needs NVIDIA's SDK, so it can be an option beside our upscaler, never the only path. It is built and off by default: `render.temporal_upscale` 1 turns it on (`terra_world::upscale::TEMPORAL`), resolving through the patched `taa_upscale.wesl`, FSR 2.2's upsample and accumulate. FSR 2's depth clip, locks, luma instability and reactive masks are not ported yet. So a clamped history keeps its weight, as Bevy's native TAA keeps it; FSR 2's cut to 0.1 without those protections let contact shadows' per-frame dither through as dots. Transmission and contact shadows also honour the drawn corner. At 3816x1550 from 2251x914, route view, the 4090's GPU total was 12.46 ms native, 6.65 ms spatial and 6.97 ms temporal (job 20261104-012152-9b2d-patch). The extra 0.32 ms is TAA, exposure and bloom at the screen's size. The rock's fine detail measures as native's: its crop's mean \|laplacian\| is 11.5 native, 3.2 spatial and 13.9 temporal. The lake's clouds are blockier than native's.
3. **Dynamic resolution**. The share follows the GPU's frame time within a range to hold the target, as Unreal's `r.DynamicRes.FrameTimeBudget` and console games do. This waits until a temporal upscaler hides the share changing.

Swapping a camera's `RenderTarget` back to a target that already exists does not resize it in Bevy 0.20: `camera_system` measures a target again only when the camera is added, its projection changes, or the target's window or image reports a change. So the switch back to full size marks the projection changed. Before this fix, a low-to-high switch, or a budget turned off, kept drawing at the scaled size.

Each preset's budget, and whether high has one by default, is for the developers to decide once they have seen it, since it changes how a window larger than the budget looks.

## Why

Goal 1(f): the developers' next lake run on the 3080 must log a median frame under 16.7 ms at the window they play at. D25 holds 13.3 ms at 1080p. Past 1080p's pixels the frame grows with the pixels drawn, and no cut to any one pass gives back a factor of two and a half.
