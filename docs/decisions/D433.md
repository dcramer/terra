# D433: The fire sheet measures the flame on the renderer's ID matte

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The fire sheet (D415, `tools/fire/sheet.ts`) measures a fire's colour, dim share, soot and structure only on the pixels the renderer drew as flame, as an offline render's ID or matte pass (an AOV) picks an object out of its beauty pass. The sandbox's `--flame-matte` makes Bevy's tonemapping pass return each pixel's flame share as its alpha, the same share `film.rs` (`AFTER_FLAMES`) tonemaps it by (the HDR alpha past 1 a flame's or a glow's mask draw writes, smoke over it having taken its share back), and saves it with each shot as `<prefix>-<frame>.matte.png`, white where a pixel is all flame (`fire::matte`). `measure()` takes the matte: a pixel is flame where it is at least half flame (`MATTE_MIN`) and the colour rule passes it, so the bands and the scale-dependent measures alike leave the lit surroundings out. The photographs have no matte and are measured by colour as before. Only with the option: at the Low preset the scaled picture's presenter draws the world as a blended sprite, which an alpha of nought would leave out.

## Why

The measure counted whatever had a flame's colour inside the generous `fire.seen` box, and the ground and stones a campfire lights have it: at dusk 6.1k of the crop's 18k "flame" pixels were the ground below the stones, at night 26k of 45k, and in round 25 the pink-lit stones counted as red (fire handoff, 2026-10-21). Colour cannot separate them (night ground 0.55-0.66 saturation, max 121-199 against the flame's 0.45-0.76, 120-254), nor connectivity (the ground joins the bed), so the campfire's dusk and night numbers had meant nothing for days, and a lit ground's yellow read as the flame's. The renderer already knows which pixels are flame; reading it is exact, costs nothing in a frame, and lands in the same frame as the picture, where a second masked frame (D429's way) would see a flame that has moved. Glowing coals and a burning prop's glow write the share too and count as fire, as the photographs' crops include the bed.

The campfire is shot twice (2026-10-24, by an agent, campfire view round): its first camera (16 degrees over the ring from 6.2 m) kept as `campfire`, so history compares, and RDR2's camp view as `campfire above` (frame 420), measured from RDR2's frame: the stone ring's ellipse 0.55 as deep as wide, so the eye 33 degrees over it and the camera pitched 26 down, the ring 15% of the picture's width, 4.8 m off at our 45-degree lens, about 8.5 flame heights. The low camera showed the stones' unlit outer faces, a black sky and a lit strip on the horizon the judges read as a light leak. From the matched view the blind panel scored main 3.44 and the 10-21 anchor 4.72 (RDR2 8.25), against 4.06 and 4.39 from the low one: the ranking holds, the gap is four times as wide, so a build is judged from both, and from the matched one first.

The `campfire above` row holds its soot to RDR2's floor, not the photographs' (2026-10-24, by an agent, campfire soot round). Its band was the campfire class's, set by nine photographs taken from beside the fire, where the logs stand dark against the flame (least 0.13, 0.11 with the tolerance). Measured by the sheet's own rule from the matched view (its window mapped onto RDR2's two camp frames by the panel's offsets, the colour rule, a flame matte of the fire's own pixels), RDR2 reads 0.00 and 0.00-0.01 whatever the matte's brightness cut: from above its flame stands on its lit bed and glowing logs, with no dark within reach. So a fire seen from there takes no floor (`floors` in `tools/fire/sheet.ts`), its ceiling and every other number still the class's; the photographs' floor still holds the low row. Our builds, by the same proxy matte, read near their true matte's (0.06-0.15 against 0.09-0.19), so the proxy does not hide RDR2's soot. The glows' second matte (`fire.masks`) was not needed and is not kept: on the campfire's low row it took the soot under the floor (0.06-0.07 against 0.12 by the matte alone).

The measures that depend on scale (soot, edge soot, pockets, octaves) are taken with the flame's box resampled to the area of a 256-px square, not to 256 px tall (2026-10-24, the coordinator's call, campfire soot-measure round; `FLAME_SIDE` in `tools/fire/measure.ts`). Soot is the dark band round the flame over its area; at a fixed height a wide, low flame had less border for its area by construction (a 3:1 flame turned on its side read a third the soot; a test now holds the two equal). By area the measure is the outline over the root of the area, the same for a shape and its turn; by the larger side a long flame would score its aspect times a square's either way. The nine photographs re-measured: least soot 0.130 to 0.143 (the dusk Easter fire, a wide one, now the least), so the low row's floor is 0.123, up from 0.110; the tall ones fell (sunset 0.74 to 0.45, Lag BaOmer 0.61 to 0.42). Our low view's flame box is near square (w/h 1.02-1.16), so our soot hardly moves: main's low dusk 0.117 under both, now under the floor and held so (`held.json` regenerated from main's run); the foot round's S1 0.098 old, 0.096 new, still under it, so it was not landed.

Each fire's row is the mean over five shots 0.5 s apart, 2 s from first to last (2026-10-24, the coordinator's call, fire round m6; `SHOTS`, `SPACING`, `sampled` in `tools/fire/sheet.ts`). A fire changes over a second or two (flicker, a tongue's life, a swell), so a number from one frame depends on the phase that frame catches: round m3's build M won both motion panels and failed the gate by 0.009 on one frame's night pale (5.423 against 5.414), its swell's phase. A photograph is one random instant too, so the class's band is a band of instants and a row estimates the fire's expected value; the references stay one sample each of a real fire. Every fire on the sheet moves, not only the campfire: on main's run the shots' spread (one standard deviation) is 0.6-0.9 points of campfire pale, 1.3-2.9 of its yellow, up to 7 of the house blaze's yellow and 17 of the breath's orange. Each shot is measured in the crop `fire.seen` tells on its own frame; a fire not seen on any shot is not seen. The campfire's last shot is frame 330 and the view above's first 420, the frames each was shot on alone, so history and the matched views compare. On main (4151fe44) the old single frames hold 10 of 15 rows in their class and the means 6: the fireball's noon pale (8.9), the breath's dusk orange (48.4) and night edge soot (23), the house blaze's night yellow (2.9) and the low campfire's dusk soot (0.101, already held out) fall out on the mean though the one frame held them in; `held.json` regenerated from that run.
