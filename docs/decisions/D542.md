# D542: Clips carried onto a body by each bone's turn from a retarget pose

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A body's bones point at their next joint, and the clips made on the mannequin are carried onto it by each bone's turn in the world from a retarget pose, not copied bone by bone. `convert_rig.py` turns each bone to point at its moved child, its roll kept nearest (the root keeps its axes). The retarget pose is the body's rest with each limb segment (upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin) turned the least way to point as the mannequin's does at rest, the foot turned only about the up axis (its pitch from ankle to ball is its shape), and every other bone carried by its parent: the pelvis, spine, neck, head, clavicles, fingers and toes keep the body's own lines. Every frame, each bone's turn in the world is the mannequin's, taken off the mannequin's rest and laid on the retarget pose (`retarget.py`), in the export and in every tool that plays the mannequin's clips on a body (`rig_check.py`, `rig_render.py`). `aim.py` keeps only the narrowing of a stride's footfalls.

## Why

The render audit (2026-10-26) found the old copy wrong on every clip and body: Tripo's joints sit off our bones' axes by up to 15.5 degrees (the base's forearms bent 15 degrees forward, thighs splayed 6, feet out 14), so a clip copied bone by bone landed the hands 5.5 cm and the ankles 9.5 cm off where it puts them, and a held or strapped item, hung on a bone's +Y, sat 15 degrees off the forearm. Only two clips were corrected, by `aim.py`'s per-clip aim.

This is how shipped retargeters do it: Unreal's IK Retargeter copies each chain's rotations as deltas between the two skeletons' retarget poses, the target's aligned to the source's by "auto align" on the mapped chains, its retarget root (the pelvis) keeping its own; Rokoko's and Auto-Rig Pro's Blender retargeters copy world rotations from a rest pose matched to the source's. Aligning the torso as well was rejected: the pelvis-to-spine line runs 14.5 degrees off the mannequin's inside an upright body, and turning it would tilt the hips' skin by as much; the same holds for the clavicles (41 degrees, a shoulder's shape). With the bones on their limbs, every tool that reads a bone's +Y as the limb (the strap, `hold.py`'s aims, the arms layer) is right as written.
