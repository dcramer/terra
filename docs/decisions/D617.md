# D617: The volumetric clouds keep a history of their own, marched at an offset inside the pixel a frame

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The march (`cloud_volume.wesl`, D515) sends one ray through each pixel of its half-size picture. A heap's face is hard: a ray is inside it or not. So every edge stair-stepped, at native resolution as much as under the temporal upscaler (D616 step 2), and the main pass's TAA could not smooth it, since its camera jitter moves a cloud pixel only a quarter of its width and its neighbourhood clamp keeps what it sees.

Shipped engines resolve their clouds before laying them over the scene. Horizon Zero Dawn's Nubis (Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015 and 2017) marches a part of the pixels a frame and reprojects the rest from a history, taking the clouds as at infinity. Unreal's volumetric cloud traces into a volumetric render target at a fraction of the view, at a sub-pixel offset a frame, and reconstructs it with its own history (`r.VolumetricRenderTarget`). We port that shape from their talks and docs (D196):

- **The march** sends its ray through one of 16 offsets inside its pixel a frame (Halton 2, 3), from the view's turn and projection alone, unjittered by the main pass's TAA (`sky/volume/render.rs`, `cloud_frame.wesl`).
- **The resolve** (`cloud_resolve.wesl`) is a compute pass of its own after the march. It finds where each pixel's way through the world was in last frame's picture, the clouds at infinity, so a turn moves them and a walk does not. It clamps the history found there to the current picture's 3x3 neighbourhood (Karis, SIGGRAPH 2014) and takes a tenth of the current frame. It writes the history, ping-ponged in the render world's texture cache, and the picture the veil lays over the scene. A new picture size or the first frame starts the history again.
- It is built and off by default: `sky.cloud.history` 1 turns it on (`terra_world::sky::volume::HISTORY`); 0 marches through each pixel's middle and keeps no frame. Off still takes the ray from the unjittered view, so the main pass's TAA jitter no longer moves it. The resolve's cost is its own span, "clouds_resolve".

At the lake shore's mid cloud crop (1050,330 650x280), native at 3816x1550, clouds and day held, frames 410/411 (play-3080 j11, job 20261104-035755-e6f8-patch), the crop's mean grey |diff| between the two frames falls from 0.29 to 0.08 with it on, at the edges from 0.13 to 0.02: the edges no longer flicker. On differs from off by 0.56, mostly at the edges, which are softer. Under the temporal upscaler, it is still 1.61 from native's, and its edges still visibly step. It stays off until it is scored against a supersampled reference and checked for ghosting on a turning camera. The first reference, a picture of the shown size averaged over 64 frames, drew no clouds: the march reads the depth at twice its pixel (`corner = id.xy * 2u`) whenever the depth is as large as the view.

## Why

The developers saw the lake's cloud edges step under temporal upscaling. Native steps too (play-3080 j10), so the cause is the march's single ray per pixel, not the upscaler. A softer face in the march would blur every heap's detail to hide the steps; a history averages the pixel's footprint and keeps the detail, and is how the engines we port from do it.
