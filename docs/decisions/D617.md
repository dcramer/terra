# D617: The volumetric clouds keep a history of their own, marched at an offset inside the pixel a frame

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The march (`cloud_volume.wesl`, D515) sends one ray through each pixel of its half-size picture. A heap's face is hard: a ray is inside it or not. So every edge stair-stepped, at native resolution as much as under the temporal upscaler (D616 step 2), and the main pass's TAA could not smooth it, since its camera jitter moves a cloud pixel only a quarter of its width and its neighbourhood clamp keeps what it sees.

Shipped engines resolve their clouds before laying them over the scene. Horizon Zero Dawn's Nubis (Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015 and 2017) marches a part of the pixels a frame and reprojects the rest from a history, taking the clouds as at infinity. Unreal's volumetric cloud traces into a volumetric render target at a fraction of the view, at a sub-pixel offset a frame, and reconstructs it with its own history (`r.VolumetricRenderTarget`). We port that shape from their talks and docs (D196):

- **The march** sends its ray through one of 16 offsets inside its pixel a frame (Halton 2, 3), from the view's turn and projection alone, unjittered by the main pass's TAA (`sky/volume/render.rs`, `cloud_frame.wesl`).
- **The resolve** (`cloud_resolve.wesl`) is a compute pass of its own after the march. It finds where each pixel's way through the world was in last frame's picture, the clouds at infinity, so a turn moves them and a walk does not. It clamps the history found there to the current picture's 3x3 neighbourhood (Karis, SIGGRAPH 2014) and takes a tenth of the current frame. It writes the history, ping-ponged in the render world's texture cache, and the picture the veil lays over the scene. A new picture size or the first frame starts the history again.
- **The history is at half the shown size, the march at half the drawn** (`sky::volume::marched_and_shown`). At native the two are one size and nothing changes. Under the temporal upscaler (D616) the march is smaller: each of its rays is a sample at its texel's middle plus the frame's offset, and a history pixel takes the ray nearest its middle only when the ray falls inside it, so over the offsets each pixel averages its own footprint. This is Unreal's mode 0, which traces at a quarter of the view and reconstructs at a half (`r.VolumetricRenderTarget.Mode`), and Nubis's sixteenth of the pixels a frame. The clouds keep native's grid at the march's cost.
- **The veil reads the picture where each fragment's jittered ray points** (`cloud_veil.wesl`). The picture is marched unjittered, and the main pass's TAA or upscaler takes every pixel it is given as a sample at the jittered place, as FSR 2's integration guide asks of everything drawn before it.
- It is on by default (`sky.cloud.history` 1, `terra_world::sky::volume::HISTORY`), under the temporal upscaler too. Off marches through each pixel's middle of the view's own projection, jittered by the main pass's TAA as before D617, at half the drawn size, and keeps no frame. The resolve's cost is its own span, "clouds_resolve".

At the lake shore's mid cloud crop (1050,330 650x280), native at 3816x1550, clouds and day held, frames 410/411 (play-3080 j11, job 20261104-035755-e6f8-patch), the crop's mean grey |diff| between the two frames falls from 0.29 to 0.08 with it on, at the edges from 0.13 to 0.02: the edges no longer flicker. On differs from off by 0.56, mostly at the edges, which are softer. Under the temporal upscaler, it is still 1.61 from native's, and its edges still visibly step.

Against a supersampled reference (the picture at the shown size, averaged unclamped over 64 frames, the depth read at its own pixel; play-3080 j12, job 20261104-042043-d533-patch), the crop's mean |diff| is 0.74 native with it off and 0.64 with it on, 13% closer; under the temporal upscaler 1.62 off and 1.87 on, 15% further. On a camera turning 40 degrees and strafing 20 m it lags off by at most 0.10 of a frame (a frame-behind ghost reads 1) and settles as fast, and the probe finds no cost (8.198 -> 8.207 ms, A/A 0.093). Hence on by default, and at first off under the upscaler.

Under the upscaler it is now on (temporal-default j1, job 20261105-063237-9a49-patch). Against a fresh reference, the lake crop at f102, with the heap's rock left out (its residual, about 6, is the upscaler's on terrain), scores:

| | native | temporal, before (history off) | history at half shown | and the veil at the jittered ray | history and march at half shown, veil as before |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3816x1550 | 0.85 | 1.41 | 1.30 | **1.07** | 1.23 |
| 1901x1550 | 0.85 | 0.94 | 0.89 | **0.82** | 0.83 |

The whole crop, rock included, scores 0.81 native, 1.63 before and 1.30 now at 3816, and 0.81, 1.16 and 1.05 at 1901. The target was 1.3x native: 1.10 for the clouds alone and 1.05 for the whole crop. The steps on the march's texel grid are gone (`~/.cache/terra-review/temporal-default-j1/`). Marching at the shown half (2.9x the pixels at 3816, 1.4x at 1901) gained less than reading the picture at the jittered ray did, so the march stays at half the drawn size.

## Why

The developers saw the lake's cloud edges step under temporal upscaling. Native steps too (play-3080 j10), so the cause is the march's single ray per pixel, not the upscaler. A softer face in the march would blur every heap's detail to hide the steps; a history averages the pixel's footprint and keeps the detail, and is how the engines we port from do it.
