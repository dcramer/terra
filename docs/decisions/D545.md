# D545: A shirt hangs over the trousers

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Design · By: Developers · Status: Decided

A shirt is made untucked: its hem hangs past the hips, and it is made over the trousers (`--over=trousers`), so in every outfit it hangs over the waistband like a tunic, and worn alone it covers the hips with no skin between its hem and the briefs. Every worn piece has one look and looks right worn alone, on every body and through every clip (D128, the gear check's 2%); nothing is made to be finished only by another piece over it. Layer order still says what is made over what (a smith's apron over the shirt, a skirt over a shift). Alternatives offered: the shirt hangs alone and tucks under trousers by a fixed layer order (Rust, Cyberpunk 2077, our shift under the skirt; recommended, with trousers rebuilt over the shirt and standing a few millimetres further out round the hips); the player chooses tucked or loose per item (Red Dead Redemption 2's wardrobe styles, two looks per shirt, deferred until several items want styles).

## Why

The smith's linen shirt was made tucked, its hem at the hip joint where the trousers cover it, so worn alone skin showed at the hip (2-5% of the skin it should cover in every clip, over the 2% bar), and a player can wear it alone. Kenshi, Enshrouded, Soulmask and Medieval Dynasty give each piece one look worn everywhere ([research](../../research/2026-10-27-layered-clothing.md)). The developers, 2026-10-27, asked whether a shirt should (1) hang alone and tuck under trousers, (2) always hang over the trousers, as Kenshi, Enshrouded and Medieval Dynasty do, or (3) let the player choose tucked or loose: "lets go with (2)". The cheapest of the three: only the shirt is rebuilt, with a longer hem, over the trousers; the smith loses his tucked look, which his apron mostly covers.
