# D53: Collision

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Every body is moved by one kinematic character controller ported from Jolt 5.6's `CharacterVirtual` (MIT; its notice is kept in the ported code). It lives in `terra_motion` with no Bevy in it, runs on the fixed tick (D138) and is deterministic. Each body is an upright capsule whose size, mass and movement numbers come from its kind's data. Its position is kept in f64 and a tick works in f32 offsets from it, as Jolt's large-world build does, so a world's radius out it moves as finely as at the centre (D482). The controller does collide-and-slide against contact planes with a small solver, and walks up stairs by one step rule for every source. The step moves the capsule at once, and only what is shown smooths it. The controller sticks to the floor going down, stops at a walkable slope limit and slows by a speed curve on slopes. Godot 4.7's constant floor speed, stop-on-slope and minimum wall-slide angle are its settings. Its ground states are grounded, steep, airborne and swimming. Gravity and up point toward the world's centre (D63).

The controller asks one query trait, with two questions: the contacts near a capsule, and the first contact of a swept capsule. The trait is implemented for:

- the voxel SDF, swept by advancing the capsule by distance over the field's steepest slope, so a wall cannot be tunnelled through at any speed;
- a base's cubes and doors;
- props' boxes;
- trees and rocks;
- other bodies' pushboxes.

Water is a medium the controller reads, never a wall (D74).

A tick steps every body against the others as they stood when the tick began (Jacobi), never against one already moved this tick, so its bodies may be stepped in any order and shared over any number of threads to the same bits; the steps run on the caller's threads through a parallel-for the mover names without Bevy (`Executor`): in the game Bevy's compute pool, which runs its systems too, so the tick adds no pool beside it; on the server, which runs no Bevy, and in the tests and benches, rayon's. A lane a thread, each its own memo of the ground, is given the same block of neighbouring IDs every tick, and once done takes from the back of the fullest other block (work stealing). The pushes' lists, when due, are made beside the steps from where the bodies stood as the tick began, and a push round after the first asks again only a pair that overlapped or had a body pushed since: the same pairs answer, so the same bits. Another body is never a step (Unreal's pawns, `CanCharacterStepUpOn` no by default): a move another body blocked tries a step up only toward a steep face beside it, a riser, never toward the walkable ground it also presses. A step meets another body only by its core (`core_m`, a thin capsule on its axis, kind data), so no body is stopped dead by another nor passes through one. Because each step meets the others where they stood as the tick began, two bodies each moving toward the other by more than its radius less its core in a tick (a man at over about 22 m/s each: a fall, a charge, a knockback) could each stop short of where the other was and cross; so after the steps a body whose step is longer than 0.75 of the least radius less core of the bodies (Jolt's `mLinearCastThreshold`) has its core cast along its step relative to each other body's (its motion less theirs, from where both began), and a pair whose cores would meet is put back along their steps to the moment they touch, each by a push swept against the world, its velocity into the other taken out. That is Jolt's continuous collision (`JobFindCCDContacts` casts a fast body along its motion less the other's; `JobResolveCCDContacts` takes it back to the time of impact), except that both bodies of a pair are put back, not the first alone, since either going on crosses the other; a pair neither of whose steps is that long cannot cross, so a walking crowd pays a length a body, and it runs on one thread after the steps, the same bits on any number of them. Then, overlapping pushboxes are pushed apart over a few rounds in ID order (D479): a pair of two soft kinds (people) eases apart by a share of its overlap a tick (its kinds' `soft_s`), each body's sum taken from where all stood as the tick began and shoved as its step begins, so on its lane (a push's ground sweeps on one thread cost the crowd 2 ms a tick); any other pair at once by mass. The player is included, and each push is swept against the world, what a wall stops of one body's share handed to the other. Bodies find each other through a broadphase, a hashed uniform grid of bodies by place (Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection 7.1; Jolt's own character-against-character check loops over every character and leaves a crowd's to the game): it changes no answer, only which bodies are asked. A body's tick gathers its neighbours once, and the pushes keep neighbour lists from tick to tick, made again once a body has moved half their margin (Verlet lists). No collision meshes are built for walking. The design is [controller.md](../controller.md).

The game's walker moves onto the controller (the old point walk deleted) when, with a fixed camera path replayed identically in both builds (`--glide` through `--vantage …@frame` keys, the same eye and aim every frame, so what is in view cannot differ), the frame time's 99th percentile, pooled over at least two matched rounds of five alternating pairs each (a warm-up pair discarded, a quiet machine), sits within the old walker's range; the walk's own measured cost (`movement::walk`'s p99) is no more than about 0.05 ms over the old; and every check it already passes still passes: `cargo xtask cabin` 20 of 20, `cargo xtask collide` 0 of 26, the scripted play run, the lake walk, `cargo xtask controller --bench` and `cargo xtask combat` with no row worse, and the terra_walker, terra_motion, terra_creatures and terra_game tests.

## Why

The developers asked for collision on everything (2026-10-10). Today's walker is a point snapped to the ground with head probes and a 0.3 m circle. It is not swept, it has two disagreeing step heights that pop, and water is a wall (against D74). Its pushes undo one another and other bodies never push it. There are no tests of its walk ([research/2026-10-24-character-controller.md](../../research/2026-10-24-character-controller.md)).

Jolt's controller is the most complete one under a licence we can port. It has contact planes from a solver rather than one sweep at a time, stair walking with a forward test, stick-to-floor, ground states, a settable up for round worlds, and determinism across machines. Rapier's controller has no planes and no ground states. Avian's and Tnua's controllers need Bevy, which the server cannot run (D28, D447).

We depart from Jolt in four places:

- **Queries.** Ours are asked of the SDF and our own shapes, because the ground is a field and not a mesh. Querying it directly is what D33 leaned toward, and the Godot demo did it.
- **Pushing.** Jolt treats another character as an obstacle that never gives way and ignores mass, and Unreal's characters block each other likewise (the Pawn channel; its push forces act on physics bodies only, and its crowds steer round each other, RVO or Detour), but D479 wants people to give way and a troll to shove a man aside. Ours is Detour's crowd separation (`dtCrowd::update`'s collision pass, zlib: each agent moves by a share of its overlaps, 0.7 a pass, four passes), soft for people by a time rather than a pass, by mass for the rest, behind a core that keeps the sweep's no-tunnelling.
- **Step smoothing.** It is left to presentation, as in Unreal.
- **Step height and edges** (stage 2, from Unreal's `CharacterMovementComponent`): a step up is refused when the floor it lands on is higher than the step height above the floor it left (`StepUp`), so the capsule's round bottom adds nothing to the step; and a body on an edge too steep to stand on, over walkable floor within the stick distance, stands (the perch). A sweep's hit is also kept as a contact for the rest of the tick, because a field answers only its nearest face and can hide one behind it, and a collision iteration the sweep stopped on a face its contacts lacked goes on to the next, which solves against that face (Jolt stops there, its contacts holding every face; a field's one contact blends a shallow crease's two). A march that steps inside from where it approached a face has gone in through it, whatever face is nearest inside (a riser beside a step's top is nearer than the top a graze step under it). Edges, as Unreal: the floor a body walks and is carried along is the one under its centre (`FindFloor`'s line trace), not the round of an edge its foot rests on (a field's normal by differences takes, along each axis, the side the field is straight along where the other rounds a convex edge within the step, the ENO stencil of Osher and Fedkiw's level sets, so the floor just past a step's edge faces up; at a crease, where the field bends concave or its centred blend falls short of unit length, each face's one-sided normal is its own contact and the blend none, as Jolt keeps each contact's normal and Unreal judges `IsWalkable` on the face hit, since a floor meeting a face just too steep blends into a walkable slope of half the angle), and only when its feet are on it (no further under them than the floor's slope puts it; a foot on an edge over a lower floor walks along the floor it last stood on, as Unreal's floor is the face touched, `ImpactNormal`); a step up is tried at a walkable edge too (`StepUp` at any blocking hit), by what the move lost; a walking body its move lifted over its floor's slope is put back on the floor; ground too steep to climb under the bottom sphere's centre stops a walking body's velocity into it with its face laid level (Jolt's `CancelVelocityTowardsSteepSlopes`, Unreal's walking along a face it cannot walk as along a wall), so a body pressed into it is still, but only where the step tried this tick refused it (no walkable ground within a step on: too high, no step, or steep wherever it came down), as Jolt cancels only for a slope it cannot walk and after its stair walk; a riser a step climbs, or a lump's steep foot that comes down on walkable ground within the step (Jolt's step that gains no height), keeps its velocity and is climbed; and on the ground no velocity is taken out by what it stands on. Where a step's whole rise puts the head into what overhangs the way (a doorway's lintel over its threshold) and the feet come down on the edge, the step is tried again risen only 5 cm over that edge (Jolt's `WalkStairs` and Unreal's `StepUp` rise the whole step and stop). A thing of flora taller than the step over its ground is never a step, unless the feet already stand more than a step over its ground (Unreal's `CanCharacterStepUpOn`): its contacts are walls, their normals laid level as Unreal judges a hit by the face struck (`ImpactNormal`), since a field's contact faces up round an edge and would lift the body onto it (`mover::footing`).


The switch's bar is a fixed eye because the earlier A/Bs compared different eye paths: the old walker stopped short of the route, then on a route both finished the switch's camera saw 9-11 fewer meshes on one leg, so their frame time differences measured what was in view, not the walker (2026-10-25, the coordinator).

The tick steps bodies Jacobi-wise rather than in ID order against those already moved (Gauss-Seidel, as before 2026-10-25, unit E2) because a crowd is one island: Jolt's islands split bodies that cannot touch in a step, but men a metre and a half apart all reach each other, so islands would leave a thousand of them on one thread. Unreal's Mass crowds work the same way, each processor's chunks in parallel against the last frame's neighbour grid; Jolt's own character-against-character check is "not thread safe, so make sure that only one CharacterVirtual is checking collision at a time" (CharacterVirtual.h), and PhysX's controllers are moved one after another. Measured on the course: every row the same as Gauss-Seidel's to its printed digits, the crowd's included, and its five reruns (again, bodies reversed, interleaved, on 5 and 16 threads) bit-identical. The pushes stay in ID order on one thread. The executor is the caller's (unit E3) because the game already runs a pool of a thread a core for its systems, and a second pool beside it would oversubscribe the cores; Unreal's `ParallelFor` and Bevy's `TaskPool::scope` hand a parallel-for the engine's own workers the same way. Measured on the bench (1,000 men at 30 km, 8 threads with the caller's, pinned to 8 cores, load 2-4): rayon's and Bevy's pools within a few percent of each other (Bevy's lanes start 0.17-0.25 ms late, rayon's 0.25), every course number bit for bit the same as before, the crowd's reruns on Bevy's pool too. The step-up rule came from the crowd bench: walking into one another, bodies tried a step up a tick against the floor they pressed, 59 of their 248 ground calls a tick; with it 3, every course row unchanged.
