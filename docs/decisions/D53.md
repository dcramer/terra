# D53: Collision

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Every body is moved by one kinematic character controller ported from Jolt 5.6's `CharacterVirtual` (MIT; its notice is kept in the ported code). It lives in `terra_motion` with no Bevy in it, runs on the fixed tick (D138) and is deterministic. Each body is an upright capsule whose size, mass and movement numbers come from its kind's data. The controller does collide-and-slide against contact planes with a small solver, and walks up stairs by one step rule for every source. The step moves the capsule at once, and only what is shown smooths it. The controller sticks to the floor going down, stops at a walkable slope limit and slows by a speed curve on slopes. Godot 4.7's constant floor speed, stop-on-slope and minimum wall-slide angle are its settings. Its ground states are grounded, steep, airborne and swimming. Gravity and up point toward the world's centre (D63).

The controller asks one query trait, with two questions: the contacts near a capsule, and the first contact of a swept capsule. The trait is implemented for:

- the voxel SDF, swept by advancing the capsule by distance over the field's steepest slope, so a wall cannot be tunnelled through at any speed;
- a base's cubes and doors;
- props' boxes;
- trees and rocks;
- other bodies' pushboxes.

Water is a medium the controller reads, never a wall (D74).

After every body has moved, overlapping pushboxes are separated by mass over a few rounds in ID order. The player is included, and each push is swept against the world. No collision meshes are built for walking. The design is [controller.md](../controller.md).

## Why

The developers asked for collision on everything (2026-10-10). Today's walker is a point snapped to the ground with head probes and a 0.3 m circle. It is not swept, it has two disagreeing step heights that pop, and water is a wall (against D74). Its pushes undo one another and other bodies never push it. There are no tests of its walk ([research/2026-10-24-character-controller.md](../../research/2026-10-24-character-controller.md)).

Jolt's controller is the most complete one under a licence we can port. It has contact planes from a solver rather than one sweep at a time, stair walking with a forward test, stick-to-floor, ground states, a settable up for round worlds, and determinism across machines. Rapier's controller has no planes and no ground states. Avian's and Tnua's controllers need Bevy, which the server cannot run (D28, D447).

We depart from Jolt in four places:

- **Queries.** Ours are asked of the SDF and our own shapes, because the ground is a field and not a mesh. Querying it directly is what D33 leaned toward, and the Godot demo did it.
- **Pushing.** Jolt treats another character as an obstacle that never gives way and ignores mass, but movement.md wants a troll to shove a man aside.
- **Step smoothing.** It is left to presentation, as in Unreal.
- **Step height and edges** (stage 2, from Unreal's `CharacterMovementComponent`): a step up is refused when the floor it lands on is higher than the step height above the floor it left (`StepUp`), so the capsule's round bottom adds nothing to the step; and a body on an edge too steep to stand on, over walkable floor within the stick distance, stands (the perch). A sweep's hit is also kept as a contact for the rest of the tick, because a field answers only its nearest face and can hide one behind it, and a collision iteration the sweep stopped on a face its contacts lacked goes on to the next, which solves against that face (Jolt stops there, its contacts holding every face; a field's one contact blends a shallow crease's two).
