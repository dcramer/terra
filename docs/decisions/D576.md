# D576: Every build logs what ran long in a freeze; a session's report is written by the game

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A frame recorder in `terra_world` keeps each frame's timed spans in fixed buffers a thread. In every build, marks between each world's top-level schedules (`MainScheduleOrder`, and the render thread's `RenderScheduleOrder`: a schedule of one system that reads the clock, before the first and after each) time the schedules and each world's update, without Bevy's `trace` (`terra_world::recorder::marks`). A build with the `hitch-log` feature (`bevy/trace`, what `cargo xtask profile` builds) times every system instead, through a `tracing` layer over Bevy's per-system spans, and our own spans round heavy work in either. Every frame of 100 ms or more (a freeze) gets one log line naming its world, its five longest schedules, systems or spans and the time outside anything timed; the minute's hitch warning names the longest in most hitches; the `Loaded in` line names the load's longest steps. With `--profile-report <file>` (`cargo xtask profile`, the launcher's `Profiling: report`) it also writes, every 10 s and when the game exits, a report of about a hundred lines (the load, the frame by schedule, the slowest frames' systems, or their schedules in a build without systems, the systems by 99th percentile), replacing the Chrome trace and `tools/sandbox/spans.ts`, deleted once the report agreed with them on one recorded walk (2026-10-29); `--profile-seconds=<s>` ends the session that long after the load. After Unreal's hitch log (`stat DumpHitches`) and CSV Profiler with PerfReportTool. [launcher.md](../launcher.md#the-hitch-log-in-every-build-d576).

## Why

Perf round 1 (2026-10-29) needed a trace build, a hand launch of the Windows build and a 17 GB Chrome trace grepped down before spans.ts could read it, to learn the first frame spent 5.1 s on mipmaps; and the developer's 1 to 3 s freezes in play never showed in the traced release run, so what froze has to be caught in the session that froze, in the build that was played. A report computed while the game runs writes no trace at all. The bar for every build is 1% of the median frame.

## Measured

Perf round 3 (2026-10-29), RTX 5090, `cargo xtask bench world town300`, the developer at the machine (processors 8 to 24% busy besides). Without Bevy's `trace`: `world` median 9.74 ms (runs 9.72 to 10.06), the game's work 8.1 ms; `town300` 8.51 ms. With `trace` compiled in and every system timed: `world` 11.29 ms (10.87 to 11.85); with system spans switched off and only schedules timed: `world` 11.46 ms (11.06 to 11.88), `town300` 8.97 ms (8.88 to 9.64). So Bevy's `trace` compiled in costs about 15% of `world`'s frame whatever is switched on, fifteen times the bar, and stays behind `hitch-log`.

Perf round 9 (2026-10-29), the marks. On the CPU, an app of Bevy's main schedules alone updated 200,000 times in release, three pairs: the eight marks cost 3.1 to 10.4 µs an update (an update 102 to 105 µs without them), about 0.1% of a 10 ms frame; the render world has three. On the GPU, `cargo xtask bench world town300 play-walk --runs 3` without and with them, release, RTX 5090: `world` 11.57 then 10.14 ms, `town300` 8.60 then 9.15 ms, `play-walk` 10.21 then 10.67 ms, loads 11.6 then 12.0 s; each pair moved with how busy the machine was besides (3 to 22%), both ways, so the benchmark cannot see the marks under its 5% spread, which the CPU measure puts at a fiftieth of that. In a release build with them, a play-walk load logged `freeze: frame 1 took 503 ms, held by the game world: Update schedule 184 ms, PreUpdate schedule 14 ms, …` and `frame 7 took 114 ms, held by the render world: Render schedule 105 ms`, where before they named nothing.
