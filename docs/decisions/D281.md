# D281: How far an edit lays a base again

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

An edit lays again only the sections its pieces can change (`sections::reach`, `Whole::lay_again`): those round the sections it marked, as far as any piece reaches from the cubes it is laid from (an opening's dress, a frame's member from its post: under a section); a framed building (D236), whose members run the length of a wall, again whole; where an edit empties or first fills a column, or changes what is highest built in it, the sections that see it along the floor too, with the rows of logs along from them, as a log looks out of the building beside its wall (D262). What it does not reach is shared with the laying before, not copied. Every layout's reach is bounded as it is built in reality, so an edit's is a few metres whatever the building's size: a course of logs is jointed at places fixed along its row, a log's length apart and staggered course to course by its seed, no joint within 30 cm of a stretch's end (`layout::runs`), each log's daub, plate and sill decided by the cubes it lies along, so an edit reaches a log's reach along a row (`runs::reach_cubes`, two sections at most) and the sections that far either way are laid again; a roof's long pieces (ridges, hips, valleys, eaves, verges, battens, rafters) are jointed where their line crosses a section's edge, but within two metres of their ends (`roofs::join`), and only a roof's squares near a layout's scope are worked; a block kind's dress is laid per cube edge, its mitres from its neighbours; flights and decks are found from the layout's scope, a deck whole from its cubes there, not from every cube of the base. The base being built is drawn near and a few metres off a part of a section at a time, eight cubes a side (`sections::CLUSTER`), each part known by a number for everything its surfaces are drawn from: the pieces reaching within three cubes of it and how far in each is, the cubes from three below it up over it and three round it, the ground under them and, for a roof's, the roofs round (`sections::cluster_keys`); a section is drawn again only in the parts whose number changed, the rest left on screen as they are, and a roof's pieces are kept masked while the roofs round are as they were. Other bases are merged a section at a time, fewer meshes. The rest are kept as laid, and a section is cut in an order of its pieces themselves, so it is the same whether its base was laid whole or in part. Each kit lays again only where it can change: every kit round the marks, only log kits along the rows from them, and a roof kit only if a cube that changed is within four of a roof's (`sections::roof_near_changes`); a roof is worked out once for every kit that asks for it in one laying (`roofs::remembered`), and a layout gathers only the cubes of the kinds it looks for (`Cubes::within_where`), never every cube of the base. How far it reaches is worked out by the worker that lays the base again, which sets those sections waiting to be cut and says which lay as they were, to be left as drawn; the main thread only marks what changed, and cuts none. A base's columns are kept up to date cube by cube, not counted from every cube each operation. Tested against laying whole after edits to every kit's buildings, long halls and a walled yard (`sections::tests`).

## Why

The second goals review (research/goals-review-2026-10-14.md, finding 4): one cube in the castle re-laid all 342 of its sections, 337 ms on a worker, and the main thread then cut every one of them (55 ms). Now 198 sections are reached, laid again in 270 ms, cut on the worker; laying all of a row's sections apart looked at most of the base again for each, so they were laid together. The lead on 2026-10-14: "Castle edits take 0.3–0.5 s because log rows and dress bands span the whole castle. Bound each layout's reach the way it's built in reality." Now 24 to 54 sections are reached and laid again in 40 to 100 ms, and the castle is laid whole in 0.4 s rather than 0.95, its rows no longer each taken whole. The lead on 2026-10-14: "Castle roof re-lays should stop processing the whole connected roof." Measured as CPU time on one thread, back to back on a machine at load 35 to 50: a castle edit laid again in 60 to 115 ms rather than 120 to 205; a timber layout had walked every cube of the castle three times for its halves and posts, and each roof kit and each log kit worked out the same whole roof again.
