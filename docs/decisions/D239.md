# D239: Quality presets, and what the integrated GPU must hold

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Three presets, low, medium and high (`terra_world::quality`), chosen when the game starts from the GPU, an integrated one low and any other high, as shipped games pick their first preset from the adapter; `--quality` or the options menu's Graphics (automatic or a preset) overrides it. High is the look as made. Medium: a 2048 shadow map in three cascades to 200 m, the haze in 24 steps, relief in 8 layers, details and grass at 0.8 and 0.75 of their reach and density, buildings' finest detail casting no shadow and their middle detail's cast by a copy only the sun sees. Low adds the world drawn at three fifths of the screen's width and height and scaled up by a camera of its own, on its own render layer and standing where the main one does, sharpened first with AMD's contrast-adaptive sharpening as FidelityFX's upscaler finishes (`upscale`); two cascades to 60 m with the hardware's 2×2 filtering; no shade in corners, contact shadows, haze shafts or bloom; flat relief; the ground drawn cheaper (D307); the air's tables at half resolution; detail at half its reach (`DetailScale`, D230) and half the grass. The integrated GPU's target is 30 frames a second at 1080p shown, at the low preset: a median under 33.3 ms.

## Why

D25 sets 60 fps on a mid-range discrete GPU and asks for the integrated GPU's frame times without saying what they must be; 30 fps at a scaled resolution or a low preset is the industry's low end, as consoles' performance modes and Steam Deck targets are. Measured on the Ryzen 9 9950X3D's two-unit Radeon at 1080p: the yard from 187 to 25 ms, the village from 288 to 34, just over, the world and the forest from 324 and 376 to 71 and 91, which still miss on their foliage; the high preset on the RTX 5090 is unchanged ([research/integrated-gpu.md](../../research/integrated-gpu.md)).
