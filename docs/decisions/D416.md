# D416: The ground shades and mirrors what the camera does not see from a horizon map of the land, not from what is drawn

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What lies off screen already casts: Bevy 0.20 culls a shadow caster against each cascade's own frustum (`update_directional_light_frusta`, `check_dir_light_mesh_visibility`), which has no plane on the sun's side (`ViewFrustum::from_clip_from_world` leaves the cascade's near half-space open) and draws what lies past it pancaked (`unclipped_depth`), and `terrain` chooses its chunks all round the viewer, never by the view; no terrain chunk is `NotShadowCaster` or ranged. What was missing lies past what the shadow map and the screen hold: the sun's cascades end at 300 m (`Quality::shadows`), so a mountain's evening shadow across a valley a kilometre off was not drawn at all, and the lake's reflection, a march through the screen's depth, falls back off screen to the skyline's flat colours (D322). The fix is the far shadow D322 named, as Unreal's far shadow cascades do for landscape past the dynamic shadow distance: a horizon map of the land (Max 1988; Timonen and Westerholm 2010). `terra_land::horizon` (no Bevy, D28) samples the ground's heights once onto a plane at the map's middle and marches each of 512 x 512 cells of 24 m along great circles out to 6 km in 16 ways, the planet's curve in the geometry, as four RGBA8 layers (4 MB). `terra_world::horizon` bakes it on the async pool, round the land's middle and again when the camera has moved a kilometre off; the sun's moving needs nothing. `assets/shaders/horizon.wesl` takes the sun's light off where it stands under that horizon, softened over the sun's half degree, faded in over the last cascade's last fifth; the ground, the far forest's cards and the lake read it, and the lake's off-screen fallback shades the skyline's slopes it mirrors by it. A reflection probe captured at the lake is the next step if that seam still shows.

Measured (2026-10-22, research/2026-10-22-far-terrain-shadows.md): the bake takes 6.7 s on one worker; against a 1 m ray march the map's horizon is off by 0.17 degrees at the 95th percentile. At the lake valley at 19:00 in June a debug tint shows the map agreeing with the slopes' own facing on the far peaks and casting shade past 300 m on the faces under them; the stills differ by 1 to 3.5 of 255 in mean luma, the far land being hazed. The bench's `world` scene ran 25.7 ms median against main's 26.8 on a busy machine (both upper bounds): no cost shows.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-18: "the terrain must be rendered even if its not fully visible because reflections/ray tracing get fucked, same with shadows". Read in Bevy 0.20's source, culling was not it: the casters behind and beside the camera are in the shadow map already; the shadows and reflections past it were never drawn. A fifth cascade is not possible (Bevy's `MAX_CASCADES_PER_LIGHT` is 4) and stretching the last over kilometres redraws every chunk's shadow each frame and coarsens the near ones; a horizon map costs a lookup a pixel and nothing a frame.
