# D222: How trees, bushes, small plants and rocks are made

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Trees, bushes, herbs and flowers are grown by rules, as SpeedTree makes them for shipped games (`terra_world::grown`): a kind in `flora.ron` gives its habit (spreading as an oak, conical as a pine, upright as a birch) or its shape, its bark (a material) and its leaves (a card), and a few numbers; each kind is grown as a few variants from the world's seed (six pines, four birches, six broadleaves, four bushes, three berry bushes, four of each herb, six flowers), each drawn at D203's and D221's levels of detail, one set of cards thinning, with its shadow stand-ins, its far crown as lumps whose normals lean out of the whole crown. Leaves, fronds and flowers are cards in one atlas, each cut to an octagon round what it covers (`textures.ts cards`, from Tripo pictures on a blue background, keyed out), with mipmaps that keep their coverage (`mipmaps`, any picture with transparency). Bark is a material (Poly Haven's pine and brown bark, a generated birch bark). Trees are grown at the mighty end of their kind's real heights (docs/art-style.md): pines 16 to 27 m, birches 13 to 20 m, broadleaves 11 to 19 m, their sprays larger to stay within the near tree's budget, wood a fifth of an item a metre, and great trees, which only iron fells (D96), from 22 m. Rocks are shapes from Poly Haven's CC0 scans, a piece of a set where it is one, cut to 350 to 550 triangles (`tools/props/scans.ts`), drawn in the flora materials, plain rock now one of them. A tree near the camera may have 8,000 triangles, its cards included (docs/art-style.md). Grown plants are drawn with the lush spike's foliage shaders (D214, D216, D218), and keep their own generator apart from the spike's, which has moved on since; the two are to be made one. The shade is lit by the sky as `daylight` says hour by hour: Bevy makes the environment light the shading reads once from the generated one and does not follow its brightness or turn, so `skylight` passes them on; until then the shade kept the first frame's light all day, and D216's brighter sky never reached it.

## Why

The developers asked for the test scene to meet the art guide (D14): the free low-poly models read as low-poly in real materials, pines as stacked cones and crowns as blobs. SpeedTree's way, branches and leaf cards, is what shipped games use, and the lush spike had shown it running; a generated tree from a model generator is a solid blob, and a hand-modelled one per variant would cost a model each. Cards keep the leaves' shape where triangles could not, and a grown kind is data (D136). Scans have real shape at no cost, and a rock's material is its kind's. Measured back to back with main on the RTX 5090: the world scene 5.7 to 6.7 ms, the lush forest 5.4 to 6.4 before and 5.3 after; on the integrated Radeon both stay where they were, about 200 ms, within the shared machine's noise.
