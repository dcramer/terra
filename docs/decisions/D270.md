# D270: Sky light under the canopy from a volume baked from the crowns

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Everything under and among a forest's crowns takes its diffuse sky light from a Bevy irradiance volume (`terra_world::sky_occlusion`) instead of the open sky's environment map: the layer above gives its crowns as ellipsoids, each 4 by 2.5 m cell is given how much of the sky each of its six sides sees through them, once, on another thread, and each time the sky is painted again the volume holds the open sky's light for what is seen, the leaves' green glow for what is hidden, and the ground's bounce, dimmed in deep shade. Far Cry's world ambient occlusion and baked irradiance volumes do the same. It replaces the ground's own darkening under the canopy (D216), and the eye adapts in deep shade as D286 says. Past the forest and above it the volume sees the open sky; Bevy drops the environment map's multiple-scattering term where a volume lights a surface, so ground there is some 15% dimmer and leaves as much brighter than the map gives, a seam left in the meadow 70 m past the edge. The sky's environment map is filtered by Bevy only for four frames after each painting, not every frame, which cost 1.6 ms of the GPU's time; the filtered light stays on the camera between.

## Why

Measured on the lush path at 13:00: a sun fleck was 1.5 times as bright as the shade beside it, and 10.7 times with the sky's light off; every surface under the canopy but the ground saw the whole open sky, and the ground 68% of it. With the volume the fleck is 3.5 times the shade at the new exposure; the review of 2026-10-14 found the light flat, with no leaf or fleck going bright.
