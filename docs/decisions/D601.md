# D601: Eight joint influences a vertex, read from glTF's JOINTS_1 and skinned in our patched Bevy

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A vertex may be skinned to up to eight joints, as glTF writes it (`JOINTS_0` and `WEIGHTS_0`, then `JOINTS_1` and `WEIGHTS_1`), and the game skins with all eight. A mesh of four keeps its vertex layout, its pipeline and its cost.

- **How shipped engines do it** (D196). Unreal's GPU skin vertex factory reads a second stream of four bone indices and four weights beside the first when a mesh has more than four influences, under its own shader permutation (`GPUSKIN_USE_EXTRA_INFLUENCES`); its importer keeps eight by default. Godot's glTF importer reads `JOINTS_1` and `WEIGHTS_1` together into `ARRAY_FLAG_USE_8_BONE_WEIGHTS` (`modules/gltf/gltf_document.cpp`), and its skeleton shader adds the second four only for such a mesh (`skeleton.glsl`, `skin_weight_offset == 4`). Ours is Unreal's shape: a second pair of vertex attributes and a shader definition only meshes with them get.
- **What we change in Bevy** (D184; extending cannot reach the loader's attribute table, the pipeline's vertex layout or the vertex structs). In `patches/`: `bevy_mesh` gains `Mesh::ATTRIBUTE_JOINT_INDEX_1` and `ATTRIBUTE_JOINT_WEIGHT_1`, and its influence iterator (skinned bounds) walks them; `bevy_gltf` maps `JOINTS_1` and `WEIGHTS_1` to them; `bevy_pbr`'s `setup_morph_and_skinning_defs` adds them at vertex locations 8 and 9 with `SKINNED_EIGHT` when a mesh has both, its forward and prepass vertex structs carry them, and its mesh and prepass shaders call `skin_model_eight` (a linear blend of the eight in Bevy's own `skinning.wesl`). Our weight-blended skinning (`assets/shaders/skinning.wesl`) has the same functions, dual quaternions over all eight, the share of dual quaternions carried across all eight weights (`terra_walker::skinning::give_shares`); `body_mask.wesl` calls them as Bevy's shaders do, and a garment's trimmed skin renumbers both sets.
- **Four costs what it did.** The pipeline is specialised on the mesh's vertex layout, so a mesh without the second pair gets the pipeline it had: the same attributes, the same shader, the same skinning function. Only an eight-influence mesh carries 24 bytes more a vertex and blends eight matrices.
- **Held by**: `terra_walker::skinning`'s `a_gltf_of_eight_influences_brings_all_eight_to_the_mesh` (a glTF of eight influences loaded through the game's loader; every joint and weight reaches the mesh) and `a_vertex_of_eight_carries_its_share_across_all_eight_weights`; `terra_world`'s shader test compiles both skinning shaders through the mesh and prepass shaders with `SKINNED_EIGHT`; `bevy_mesh`'s `influence_iterator_eight`. `tools/characters/intact.ts` holds the files, these the game.

## Why

D599 keeps Quaternius's weights as made, and the woman's Ranger body and legs weight a vertex to up to eight joints (1,351 of her body's 2,785 vertices carry weight past the fourth, up to 0.075; 16 of her legs' 685, up to 0.017); Bevy 0.20's loader read only `JOINTS_0`, so the game dropped up to 7.5% of a vertex's weight and renormalised the rest, a skin other than theirs. Cutting their weights to four in our tools would break D599's "weight for weight"; a second attribute pair is what Unreal and Godot do, and costs nothing on the meshes that do not need it.
