# D292: Leaf cards graded to living leaves, each leaf bent, worn and glossy as its own

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D269. For D291. A composed card's leaves (`textures.ts cards`, `leaves.ts`) are graded to the kind's measured summer albedo (`albedo`, sRGB), the scan scaled channel by channel in linear light so each scanned leaf keeps its own veins, blemishes and shading, those deepened (`contrast`), not desaturated toward a pastel by a saturation and lightness multiple; each leaf folds up along its midrib and curls over at its tip (`fold`, `curl`), so its halves and its tip catch the light apart; some yellow in patches between their veins toward tip and margin, some are bitten or holed by insects, each a hue of its own (`yellowed`, `holes`, `hues`), each by a roll of its own, so the leaves' places stay as they were. The normal map's alpha is each pixel's roughness: the scan's roughness map, veins rougher than the blade, scaled to the kind's gloss (`roughness`), undersides matte, twigs and berries their own, a picture card as rough as its `<card>.surface.json` says; terra_world's mipmaps take a linear picture's alpha as data, not as a cut-out's cover. `keep` scales a kept cell to a new atlas's cells. Recomposing the litter atlas with this leaves its colour the same, its normals within 0.25%. **The foliage atlas** is 4096 pixels, its cells 1024, so a leaf is twice the pixels long (an oak's 141, a hazel's 145, a bramble leaflet's 216); its broadleaf cards graded so, the fern, herbs, flowers and holly cut again from their 1024-pixel pictures, birch and pine scaled up. **Shading** (`foliage_fragment.wesl`): each leaf's roughness is the atlas's, widened where normals turn faster than a pixel holds (Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan's specular anti-aliasing, ported from Godot), its reflectance a leaf's (`LEAF_REFLECTANCE`, some 1.3% head on, since Bevy's grazing reflectance rises with it and higher turned leaves chalky against the sun); the leaves' relief all of the map's (`RELIEF` 1); a leaf turned from its crown's face darker in the sky's light too, so leaves read apart in shade; leaves reflect the sky only where they see it, as bark does (D289), and then 0.15 of it, as more turned leaves in shade slate-blue. The crowns' leaves are no longer greyed or darkened (`LEAF`), the conifers' tints taking what that did for them, and glow lit through an eighth more as their colour is darker; the middle layer is darkened but not greyed; the floor keeps both, for its pictures.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-14 (D291), and the review of the lush forest against Sons of the Forest (research/2026-10-14-sotf-forest-look.md, recommendation 6: oak about 20% darker and more olive, a sheen on upper faces). The composed cards read pale mint: leaf pixels' mean luma 0.43 to 0.45 (sRGB) and saturation 0.44, against the holly picture's 0.23 and 0.64; each leaf one flat plane in the normal map; one roughness for every leaf; an oak's leaf 70 pixels long. After, the atlas's leaf pixels luma 0.32 to 0.39, saturation 0.49 to 0.58, the oak branch's linear mean (0.083, 0.135, 0.038) from (0.117, 0.193, 0.061), which with the crowns' 15% darkening gone is the research's 20% darker and more olive. In the game, the vantage sheet's forest views at 13:00: mean 0.143 (0.146 before; Sons of the Forest 0.148), below 0.08 46% (45%), red over green 0.91 (0.92), blue over green 0.57 (0.62), the blue lost being the sky's reflection off leaves in shade; close crops' local contrast up 2 to 9%. An adversarial review against Sons of the Forest's frames scored the leaves 3/10 before and 4/10 after: veins, varied greens and leaves apart in shade gained; still short in light through each leaf, glints leaf by leaf, and crowns of small leaves on cards that smear edge-on. The walk bench on the RTX 3080 in the same hour: 11.68 ms median against 11.60, the opaque pass 3.53 ms against 3.41; the atlas and its normal map take some 134 MB more of the GPU's memory (179 MB with their mipmaps), the process some 80 MB more; `lodcheck` passes (worst 0.10%).
