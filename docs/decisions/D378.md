# D378: Wildlife by niche, found as players go

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A kind of creature that lives about the land says its niche as data (`creatures.ron`): the growing season's mean warmth it takes and the places it keeps to (woods, open ground, the shore, open water, ground trodden by people), each read from the world as it is (the land's seasons, standing trunks, water depth, the cover's trodden ground), and how many live to a hectare of it, in groups. The land is cells of 32 m, each given its animals from its own seed when a player comes within 110 m and let go past 150 m (`play::wildlife`). Behaviour stays a few states (D145), with a refuge by kind: running, up the nearest trunk until nobody is near, or a flight to somewhere of its niche; fliers flit between places and swimmers keep to the water. Each is drawn to a distance by its size (Bevy's visibility ranges). Their rigs and clips are made by rule from each model by two body plans, a four-legged runner and a bird, legs placed by two-bone inverse kinematics (`tools/creatures/animal.py`).

## Why

Shipped open worlds people their land this way: ambient wildlife spawned round the player by habitat (Red Dead Redemption 2, Far Cry), so a world for a thousand players holds animals only where someone is (D127, D145). A niche as data makes a new land's animals follow its climate (D313), with no list per map. No released tool animates an arbitrary animal's skeleton that we could ship (research/2026-10-14-animation.md); gaits worked out from a few numbers, as Spore and Overgrowth do, cost no animator and no credits, and check themselves (feet off their marks, skin under the ground).
