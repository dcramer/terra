# D644: Each layer speaks across the network in words of its own, told apart by their first byte

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What crosses the network above `terra_net` is each layer's own words in bytes it alone reads, as one of three kinds: a **part** of an entity (what is shown of a thing, sent as it changes: a body, what is worn, what is held, a creature), an **order** (a change to the world kept in order where it happened and told to those near, now and when they come: the ground, a felled tree, the base, a prop, a gate, a mark), or a **happening** (said and gone: the clock); a joined game **asks** the host for the changes it makes itself. A word's first byte says whose it is; a layer takes its own from the host's asks and the link's events and leaves the rest; the bytes are all listed in one test (`terra_game::play::net_words`), which fails on two alike. The host takes a guest's word for what the server does not yet own (what it wears and holds, its marks, its character), and checks what it can (reach, bounds, that bytes read as what they claim).

## Why

Layers do not know of those above them (D198), so `terra_walker`'s net cannot name a creature, a base or a mark; opaque words by layer let each crate cross "as its own" (netcode.md, How each crate crosses) without a shared type. One stream a direction, split by first byte, is how the ground's operations already crossed; thirteen words in four crates now do, and a collision would be silent (each layer would take the other's words and make nothing of them), hence the one test. Orders are for what a latecomer must find as the host has it; a happening is for what is stale at once (a clock said every two seconds), so the record of orders does not grow with time.
