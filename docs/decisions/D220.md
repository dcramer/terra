# D220: Cubes cut to shapes, posts, and timber masses

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

As step 5 of [building-plan.md](../building-plan.md) builds D193's shapes: a cube is whole, a half (its lower half), a stair (two steps, each half its height and depth, rising toward a side), a slope (a ramp up to a side) or a post (code 10 of its shape bits, five since D231): a post is a column's cube drawn as an upright as narrow as its kit dresses `Role::Post` (a member of its timber, or `Pillar(width)` of its stone), and holds as a whole cube; a walker meets it as a box 36 cm square (`walk::POST_M`), not its cube (2026-10-13). A walker stands on a shape's top at the point under them (`Shape::top`), what is under the middle of their feet out to 60% of their radius, so a 25 cm step is climbed as the body reaches it, as a capsule climbs; a wall is met where the walker crosses into the next column. Support, cost, order and taking apart are a whole cube's (D199): joined face to face, a shaped cube costing what a whole one of its kind does, its offcut lost in the cutting. A block kind draws halves and slopes as its surface cut down to their tops (`surface`'s cut), stairs as a stone a tread and posts as a drum a cube (`surface::slab`), and dresses their lines with bands of its profiles (`Piece::Band`): a nose on each tread, a coping round a half's open top and raking up a slope's open sides, a plinth round a pillar's base and a coping round its cap. A timber kind lays a column topped by a stair or a slope, whatever is set on it, as a step of its flights (`layout::stairs`), open stairs as a carpenter builds them: a thick tread on each step, or a ramp of boards up the fall with a cleat, housed between board stringers up both sides from the floor to the top step's back, posts under the stringers where their span needs one (D306), nothing under the treads (the cubes under them still walked as cubes); halves as decks half a cube high; posts as one upright on a pad of boards, under a bolster where something rests on them, under a deck's bearer where a deck does (D306). A solid mass of a timber kind (whole cubes of a wall in a block two by two on a layer) is a framed deck: its top boards on joists with a rim of beams stacked a course deep, its inside nothing, its sides walls; the top layer of a wall tile over a post is what the post carries, not a wall. Slope cubes are not roofs: D209's roofs are laid from roof sections over walls, and a slope is a ramp of its own kind's material, under or beside a roof as any cube is. The hammer lays them as parts (`build::plans`): a flight of stairs or a ramp up to the floor of the storey above, a post to it, each on a footing on the ground, a floor drawn alone, and one block of a shape against the face aimed at, joining the plan of its kind beside it; the wheel turns which way a flight or a cut block rises. Roofs take a kit's masks: the covering greened by moss and lichen toward its eaves (`moss`, `moss_tint`), greyed toward its ridge by `weather`, its eaves and verges worn lighter.

## Why

Mine, building step 5 on 2026-10-13: Keen's platform and slab drew as piles of log ends; shapes existed in the cube store and the operations but walked as whole cubes and drew as nothing of their own. A stone a tread and a drum a cube are how stone stairs and pillars are built; cutting the surface keeps halves and slopes one surface with what they meet.
