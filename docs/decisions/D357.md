# D357: Trailer films

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The trailer is one run through each place, the game's player (`--experiment play`) running a smoothed, nearly straight route with the game's own third-person camera following, filmed by `cargo xtask trailer` (`tools/sandbox/trailer.ts`) as Movie Render Queue renders a sequence: a scripted run stepped a sixtieth of a second a frame, warmed up where it starts, every second frame kept for 30 fps. A run's look is the sandbox's `--cine <shot.json>`: the exposure held from when the run starts, the clock held at the hour, motion blur at a 180° shutter, depth of field on the player if asked; the letterbox (2.39:1), crossfades and cards are ffmpeg's. `--cine` also takes a keyframed camera path (positions and look-at keys with times, Catmull-Rom over time, eased; orbit and follow keys; lens, focus and aperture per key), tested without the GPU and unused by the trailer. Sound is the game's own code-made sounds and music written to a WAV (`terra_sandbox --sound-bed`). `--picture WxH` sets an offscreen run's size. Amended 2026-10-16: a run may be a scene (`scene`, `tools/sandbox/trailer/scenes/<name>.ts`), a timeline of remote calls on frames and the camera's cuts, glides and orbits, written as a QA film's (`qa/script.ts`) and made by `--calls` in the same scripted run with the same look, the player hidden; the first a dragon burning the burg (`--cut tools/sandbox/trailer/dragon.json`). A shot's look may bias every level of detail further (`look.detail`), as Movie Render Queue's cinematic scalability raises view distance. A shot may cut within itself: a key with `cut` starts a take of its own, its path from it to the next cut, and on the frame it cuts the anti-aliasing's history is dropped and the frame drawn without motion blur, as a renderer's camera cut does (the settlement's time-lapse, D381).

## Why

The developers, 2026-10-15: "by the end of the day we will want to create a cinematic trailer, we need 4 of those (3 biomes + village) to be stunning", then "the videos should all be simple, like just a dude running through a biome and its stunning, and thats it, no need to change its direction/focus on guy, its just about the ecosystem". A held exposure, because the eye's adaptation pumps between shade and open; a held clock, because the sun's shadows move in steps; the sound made offline, because scripted runs are silent and step their clock.
