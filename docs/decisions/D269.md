# D269: Leaf cards composed from scanned leaves, with a normal map

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D222. A broadleaf card is composed, as studios build their SpeedTree and Unreal foliage atlases, from single leaves cut out of ambientCG's CC0 leaf scans (`textures.ts cards` with a `<card>.leaves.json` recipe, `tools/textures/leaves.ts`): a twig and its shoots, or a cane with leaf stalks of three or five leaflets, the leaves laid along them alternately at angles, sizes and turns of their own, foreshortened as they turn and tilt, some young and yellower toward the tips, some old and darker olive toward the base, a few turned to show a paler underside, drawn back to front with a soft shadow where one lies on another, darker toward their stalks. Beside the colour atlas the tool writes its normal map (`normal.png`, OpenGL's convention), each leaf's scanned relief turned and tilted as it is laid, flat for cards drawn from pictures. Hazel (LeafSet024), oak and the oak branch tip (LeafSet016), bilberry with its blue-black berries (LeafSet001) and bramble (LeafSet014) are made so; holly keeps its picture, its yellowed leaves toned to olive; cards touched by hand since (the spruce, D248) are kept as the atlas has them (`keep`). Oak's spring scan is shifted toward summer's deeper green (`hue`). **Shading:** the foliage shader leans the crown's bent normal by each leaf's own, from the atlas's normal map laid on the card's face (Schüler's cotangent frame from screen derivatives, since a card's tangents carry its drop-out, D223), 0.8 of the way (`relief`), so a crown still shades as one mass while its leaves catch the sun one by one, and glow through by their own tilt. A crown's or shrub's inside sees little of the sky: `Canopy::sky` falls off steeply inside its outermost leaves, measured by the crown's own envelope direction by direction (an egg of holly or a vase of hazel is deep in by its shape, not as a ball), the lush oaks' likewise (conifers as they were); the shader's darkening of the inside leaves the skin alone; a leaf in another crown's shadow passes on 0.06 of the sun (from 0.2, D264), so shadowed leaves read dark against those a fleck lights. The middle layer is shaded as a crown, its inside dark and its leaves lit through (0.8). Card to card a leaf is lighter or darker by a fifth, an eighth young and yellower, more than a quarter old and darker olive. **Shrubs:** the middle layer draws every card of every clump near, and the trees' middle level past 28 m. A young spruce's shoots hang from its branches and turn round them, two more cards a spray, rolled steeply, so a branch is a bottle-brush, not a plank; its needles a fresher green and its wood grey-brown, not orange.

## Why

A review against Sons of the Forest scored the lush forest's foliage 4.5/10: hazel a topiary of identical round leaves, holly speckled cream-yellow, cards soft. The generated pictures were sprays of identical, evenly lit leaves all facing up, with no normal map, so every card was lit as one flat surface; scans are measured leaves, and composing them is how the industry's atlases are made. Light and shade scored 4.5/10: a hazel lit the same at its core as its skin, no fall-off inside crowns. Measured in close crops at 15:00, both with D270's sky volume, luminance spread at the 90th over the 10th percentile: holly 2.1 to 4.4 before, 3.0 to 6.4 after; young spruce 2.2 to 3.1 before, 3.3 to 4.4 after; bramble 3.2 to 5.0 before, 3.1 to 6.9; an oak from below 4.2 to 4.8, its spread (SD) 0.26 to 0.27; hazel 3.1 to 5.4 before and 2.5 to 4.0 after, darker overall (a pale ball against dark before). Canopy from eye height 3.6% sky before, 4.1% after; the undergrowth hides 41% of the horizon band, 40% after. The walk bench on a shared RTX 3080, other agents' sandboxes on the GPU: in the quiet runs main 10.6 ms of GPU work a frame (13.6 ms median frame, 21.1 ms 99th percentile) and this 11.3 (14.5, 29.0); `lodcheck` passes view by view (worst 0.10%).
