# D473: Dungeons are made in stages: a mission graph, placed on 3 m cells and 4 m levels

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

A dungeon is a theme and a seed, made in stages, each its own module of the plain crate `terra_dungeon` (D28) and tested alone ([dungeons.md](../dungeons.md)):

- **The mission graph** first, as Dormans' mission grammars and Unexplored build them: a main path of five to seven rooms from the entrance to the goal, a locked gate into the guard room before the goal, a loop of one or two rooms off the main path that the key rooms hang from (so the loop must be walked), a shortcut from beside the gate back to the first passage that opens one way, a secret room, a rest room part way down, and treasure with what is left of the room budget. Danger rises with depth along the main path; a room's mobs come from the theme's bestiary and its loot from the theme's tables, by role and danger. A graph is solvable by construction and checked: the goal and every key are reached, and no key lies behind its own lock.
- **Placement** on a grid of cells 3 m square (six building cubes, so a cube of wall each side leaves corridors 2 m clear) and levels 4 m apart (a slab and 3.5 m clear). Rooms are rectangles by role set beside the room they hang from, a cell clear of everything, on the level their danger asks for and at most one from their parent's; corridors are routed by A* with a turn costing more than a step, and a straight stair (two cells a level, a well and a landing over them) where they change level. A placement that cannot route an edge starts again from a derived seed, up to 12 times. Every cell knows its space and each side: a wall, open into the same space, a door (by the graph's edge) or a stair's way.
- **The fit** (next): the generator's output is in its own frame; in the open world the sandbox digs one box a level, one a stair well and the entrance pit, and lays the cells' cubes as one base; a dungeon behind a door (D468) takes the same output without digging.

## Why

Graph first, then space, is how the procedural dungeons players rate best are made (Unexplored's cyclic generation, Dormans' mission and space grammars): the lock-and-key order and the loops are decided where they are easy to check, and placement only has to keep them. Cells of 3 m are the smallest that leave a 2 m corridor inside a wall of building's half-metre cubes (2 m cells left 1 m). Few, large dug boxes because terrain evaluates its edits by a linear scan per sample.
