# D403: A mountain's face is drawn in tiers, weathering and skirts, its gullies shaped by face

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D395 and D401. A far wall's face gets variety at several sizes, mostly in the ground shader (`ground.wesl`), since terraces cut into the mesh held water as ponds:
- **Tiers.** On rock and steep ground the rock is banded by height: major tiers about 100 m high split into beds of about 30 m, each with its own tone. The band height is warped by hundreds of metres over kilometres and tilted a few degrees. Bands are strong on some faces and faint on others. Some tier tops are ledges: broken runs, a hundred metres or so long, that face the sky and hold snow where it lies round them, or turf below about 2,000 m. The tiers fade by the height a pixel spans, not by its footprint, since a wall seen from below is foreshortened across.
- **Weathering.** Patches a few hundred metres across: iron-stained rust, dark lichen and wet rock, and pale fresh scars, with dark wet streaks down the middle set of gullies on some faces. A far wall is a weathered mid grey, darker than the steep layer's scan. The tint is applied after the layers, because the far mesh is too coarse to measure 40 degrees, so its walls are drawn as the steep layer more than as bare granite.
- **Skirts.** On the 20 to 33 degree ground at a wall's foot, the great couloirs' lines run on as pale scree, with alpine turf between them below the bare heights. Scree is drawn paler than the face above it.
- **Snow.** Past about 500 m, rock breaks through the snow in outcrops tens of metres across, more of them the steeper the ground, and the snow's edge is sharpened.
- **Rib sets.** The shader's three sets of far ribs are each strong on some faces and nearly gone from others, so one wall shows broad couloirs, the next close runnels, and a third a smooth slab.
- **Buttresses** (`Mountains::buttresses`, 1 for the starting realm). In the mesh, the two coarser gully sets are shaped by a face noise about 1.6 km across. A power on how far into a gully a point lies narrows the gullies to slots between broad buttresses on some faces and widens them between sharp arêtes on others. The power runs from 2^-1 to 2^1.6. The rule only deepens or shallows what the gullies cut, so `highest_m` holds. It is off round the pinned places and the ranges they keep (`Range::away_for_tops`), so all four pins resolve as before.

## Why

The developers, on the round 2 castle rows: "i like the mountains better. the face of the mountain needs a bit of variety too"; and before, "the mountain is still a bit unnatural, maybe we adversary review against some good mountain ranges out there realism important". The round 2 review (4/10) found every face "the same pale warm grey, combed top to bottom by parallel vertical flutes about the same width and depth". It found no cliff bands, no scree or talus at the foot, and snow like wax caps. Real ranges (refs/mountains, refs/mountains-wide) are built in tiers, have grey cones and green turf on their skirts, and their snow is broken by rock.

The pins (`fields --site`) are the same at all four places. Only the far skyline readings shift, the highest ground seen from the town going from 2,280 to 2,275 m, and the lake query looks at 2,204 lakes where it looked at 2,222. GPU peaks during the stills are 7,850, 7,816 and 7,878 MiB (valley, settlement and lake), against 8,285, 8,063 and 8,056 MiB in round 2; transient, as D401 noted.
