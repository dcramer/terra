# D553: The fires are drawn after the haze, fogged at their own depth

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Every fire card (flames, embers, jets, the smoke and soot; `terra_world::fire::fogged::AfterFog`) is taken out of Bevy's transparent pass once sorted and batched and drawn in a pass of its own after the volumetric fog (`fires_after_fog`, in Core3d after `bevy::pbr::volumetric_fog_pass`), in the order it was sorted. Each card's shader then takes the haze between the eye and itself by the fog's own law (`assets/shaders/fire_fog.wesl`: Beer's law through the haze's density picture in 4 steps, the sun's single scattering through the Henyey-Greenstein phase with one shadow tap at the ray's middle, the camera's ambient as Bevy lays it), from the haze's fog volume and the camera's fog as the fog pass is told them (`fogged::told`). The change to Bevy is one re-export in `patches/bevy_pbr/src/lib.rs`, marked `Terra's patch (D553)` (D184).

For now the torches' smoke is left out: it is drawn by a material of its own in Bevy's transparent pass and fogged by the fog pass as before (`fogged::SMOKES`, its `torches` flag; the shader's `kind.z`). Drawn after the fog, a torch's thin thread of puffs over the sky showed as a string of lit, rimmed ovals that the old fog had dimmed (fire round t14's panel: worse by one, three judges of three). They are the smoke's own, not the film camera's depth of field: filmed with no aperture, main's torch and the after-fog one show the same ovals (fire round t15). It moves in once the ovals are gone.

## Why

Bevy's volumetric fog runs after the whole main pass and marches each pixel to the depth buffer. Smoke writes no depth, so a puff 6 m off with the sky behind was fogged as if it stood at the sky (about a fifth of its light through at the horizon, the rest in-scatter): dimmed, washed pale, and changing look sharply at the horizon, the smoke's hard band and its detached look (fire round t12: the green-probe campfire's column ran 0x89 just above the horizon against 0xF2 just below it, even from top to bottom with the haze off). Moving the fog before the transparent pass would leave lakes, the sky, the planet and windows unhazed; the smoke writing depth would leave the sky behind a thin wisp unhazed, its card's outline showing. Drawing the fires after the fog and fogging them in their shaders is the step that touches only them.

The general fix, for later: a froxel volume of the haze's in-scatter and transmittance fit to the camera's frustum, sampled by every translucent at its own depth (Wronski 2014; Unreal's and HDRP's volumetric fog; Bevy's atmosphere's `aerial_view_lut`), so lakes, clouds and windows are hazed at their depth too, and the fires' pass can go.

## Costs

A fire's card is drawn after every other transparent whatever their depths, so a fire seen through a lake's water or a window is drawn over it. The fog's fire glows (D496) lie under the cards, not over them. The haze's in-scatter from point lights is left out of the cards' fog. The torches' smoke is drawn before their flames, whatever their depths, and its hearth cards twice (each material steps over the other's). Under 0.1 ms on crypt and crypt-hall (fire round t14's bench).
