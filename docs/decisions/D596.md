# D596: A world is made from its seed alone

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A world's seed, a `u64`, is the only input its generation takes. It is kept in its `world.ron` (`seed`), chosen when the world is made and never changed; a world kept from before worlds had seeds was made from seed 1 and reads as it. `--seed <n>` gives the seed of a run that keeps no world and of a world made (`--fresh`, a new `--world=`); a kept world's own seed always wins. The world's home realm takes the world's seed as its own (`WorldSpec::seed`, `planet::entering::first_world`); another realm's seed derives from it through `terra_gen::hash` (a portal's from the realm left and its place). Every system's random stream is its realm's seed derived for its purpose (`Seed::derive` by name, `at` by place, `index` by number), never a seed written into the code, the clock or the operating system's randomness.
- **Held by**: the world-seed lint in the push hook (`xtask/src/world_seed.rs`): in the crates' own code, outside tests, benches and programs, no `Seed(<number>)` and no system randomness, with a short list of a sound's dice, which are heard and not the world. And `terra_land::fingerprint`'s test: the same seed makes the same ground, stands, trees and weather bit for bit on other threads, and another seed makes a world that differs in every part.
- **Staged**: a new world is made from seed 1 until the title screen can cross to a world of another seed and a sweep of 100 seeds finds every world valid; then New world rolls one.

## Why

v0's second seam (D589, docs/v0-goals.md): "The planet is `Seed(1)` and no option changes it. A world's seed becomes the only input; each realm's seed derives from it (`terra_gen::hash`)." The home realm keeps the world's seed rather than one derived from it so that seed 1's world, on which every pinned demo place (D393), look baseline and scorecard stands, stays as it is; a derivation would have moved all of them for no gain. A lint rather than review because a seed written in is easy to add and invisible in a picture; the fingerprint because only a run shows what a lint cannot see (an unordered map's order, the clock, a stream shared between two parts).
