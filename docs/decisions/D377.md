# D377: Fire in what is built

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A cellular fire on a base's cubes, as Far Cry 2's and Breath of the Wild's are (`terra_build::fire`, engine-free, deterministic, stepped four times a second): a cube heating or burning is a cell with its heat and fuel; a burning cube heats the cubes round it, most above (flame rises), less beside, little below, more downwind, and fills the air within six cubes of its open faces with hot gas whose heat is shared over that air, so a shut room flashes over and the open sky carries it away; a cube catches past its kind's threshold, varied a little a cube so a front is ragged, burns faster among burning neighbours, smoulders with no open face, and its harm, its health over its fuel, is sent as `Op::Damage`, so what it held comes down by D305's rule and every player agrees; burning cubes that fall land as brands on what is under them. How a material burns is data, its kit's `burns` by its pattern unless its material says (catches, fuel, heat): logs, squared timber, a frame, shakes and thatch burn; stone, slate and earth do not; in a game's times (a shake roof in seconds, a log wall in half a minute, where real logs char 0.65 mm a minute and burn for hours). What a fire reaches is blackened (`Base::scorch`, not saved): its level rides on each corner's layer number in the texture arrays, charring what burns to cracked charcoal glowing in its cracks and sooting what does not. `terra_build::burg` is a castle built to burn: stone with timber where castles had it (hoardings, jettied timber storeys, floors on bond timbers through the tower walls, towers clear of the curtain but for the hoardings' bridge). Timber charred through 60% of its fuel under stone gives way under it. Drawn by `terra_world::fire` (D325's way), as shipped games draw fire (research/2026-10-16-real-time-fire-rendering.md): what burns is given as blazes, two metres' cubes a side each, taller where more burns round them, into one buffer the GPU reads, so all flames are one draw of three cards a blaze from a flipbook of three fires simulated as a fluid (`tools/textures/flames.ts`: stable fluids with buoyancy and vorticity confinement over a ragged fuel bed whose gas burns on through a body to about a third of the frame, cooling faster above it so its tips break off by three quarters, as a real flame's continuous and intermittent regions do; 64 looping frames; it prints how far up its fires reach), played at the rate a fire that size moves, its heat the glow of soot at its temperature and in its amount (950 to 1670 K: deep red, orange, a yellow heart), the amount taken before the shoulder as a camera takes light, so a thin, dim or faded flame is dull red and only a bright one yellow, and kept a stop over the tonemapper's knee, since eye adaptation darkens a sunlit frame after it is drawn, so its heart is never a dimmed yellow (olive) nor pink; all embers another (sparks stretched along their flight); all smoke a third (near-black cards greying as they climb, each lit as a ball, its sunward side lit and its underside dark and glowing from the flames under it), drawn before the flames, which show through it; heat haze over the flames, the opaque scene read a few pixels off; every card faded in where it meets what is behind it; a breath is a jet of cards streaming out in a cone that ends where it strikes, flaring there, drawn as a flamethrower's stream is in footage and in the games that draw one well (research/2026-10-18-dragon-breath.md): what reads is the contrast in it, a solid rod near-white over the first third of its length, yellow cores with orange and dull red rims as it rolls open (1060 to 1900 K by the flipbook's heat), black soot pockets in it, and each card covering as it glows (premultiplied over), so hot yellow never goes olive at half cover; and what it sheds is let go where the stream is and then moves on its own, so it stays where it was breathed as the dragon flies on (two rings the CPU fills and the GPU reads), while what is still in the stream when it stops burns out in a moment: black lit soot rolling off its outer two thirds and its end, rising, swelling and hanging some seconds, and burning drops falling from it as streaks; eight point lights at the largest gatherings of fire, flickering as a fire that size puffs (1.5/√D Hz), two with shadows; how many blazes are drawn by the preset (2,048 at high). Where fire is broad, three or more blaze cells beside each other on a level burn as one wide blaze, a sheet of fire, not one flame to a cell in rows. A fire kept where something stands (`fire::Kindled`: a built campfire, a staged one) is drawn as one more blaze, flames, embers, smoke and light, so a campfire burns with the same fire as a burning house rather than a light alone; it stands just where it is and as tall as it was made, not jittered as a grid's blazes are, and its flames are sized to clear its logs, which hide a shorter one whole.

## Why

The developers asked on 2026-10-15 for a castle "and have a dragon fucking setting it on fire and it going down in a large destruction", with fire that spreads and burns and burnt-through parts bringing down what they held. Cellular because every shipped fire that spreads through a world is (research/2026-10-15-dragons-and-fire.md); through `Op::Damage` so fire is one more harm, not a second way of destroying; hot gas because without it a fire crept sideways a cube in seven seconds and a tower took minutes to burn, where real rooms flash over.
