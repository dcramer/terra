# D377: Fire in what is built

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A cellular fire on a base's cubes, as Far Cry 2's and Breath of the Wild's are (`terra_build::fire`, engine-free, deterministic, stepped four times a second): a cube heating or burning is a cell with its heat and fuel; a burning cube heats the cubes round it, most above (flame rises), less beside, little below, more downwind, and fills the air within six cubes of its open faces with hot gas whose heat is shared over that air, so a shut room flashes over and the open sky carries it away; a cube catches past its kind's threshold, varied a little a cube so a front is ragged, burns faster among burning neighbours, smoulders with no open face, and its harm, its health over its fuel, is sent as `Op::Damage`, so what it held comes down by D305's rule and every player agrees; burning cubes that fall land as brands on what is under them. How a material burns is data, its kit's `burns` by its pattern unless its material says (catches, fuel, heat): logs, squared timber, a frame, shakes and thatch burn; stone, slate and earth do not; in a game's times (a shake roof in seconds, a log wall in half a minute, where real logs char 0.65 mm a minute and burn for hours). What a fire reaches is blackened (`Base::scorch`, not saved): its level rides on each corner's layer number in the texture arrays, charring what burns to cracked charcoal glowing in its cracks and sooting what does not. `terra_build::burg` is a castle built to burn: stone with timber where castles had it (hoardings, jettied timber storeys, floors on bond timbers through the tower walls, towers clear of the curtain but for the hoardings' bridge). Timber charred through 60% of its fuel under stone gives way under it. Drawn by `terra_world::fire` (D325's way), as shipped games draw fire (research/2026-10-16-real-time-fire-rendering.md): what burns is given as blazes, two metres' cubes a side each, taller where more burns round them, into one buffer the GPU reads, so all flames are one draw of three cards a blaze from a flipbook of a fire simulated as a fluid (`tools/textures/flames.ts`: stable fluids with buoyancy and vorticity confinement over a ragged fuel bed whose gas burns on through a body to about a third of the frame, cooling faster above it so its tips break off by three quarters, as a real flame's continuous and intermittent regions do; 96 looping frames of 160 by 320 cells, each texel its temperature, its soot (made where the body burns rich, burnt away where hot, carried off as black wisps where cooled) and its motion to the next frame, so the shader blends frames along the flow as motion-vector flipbooks are played; it prints how far up its fire reaches), played at the rate a fire that size moves, its heat the glow of soot at its temperature and in its amount (950 to 1670 K: deep red, orange, a yellow heart), the amount taken before the shoulder as a camera takes light, so a thin, dim or faded flame is dull red and only a bright one yellow, and kept under the tonemapper's knee as the screen shows it, by the eye's adaptation as Bevy's auto exposure last applied it (copied after tonemapping into a buffer the fire reads, a frame late, `terra_world::adaptation`, as Unreal hands its materials eye adaptation), so its heart is never a dimmed yellow (olive) nor pink, and its hottest cores lift past the tonemapper's peak and clip white as fire does on film; all embers another (sparks stretched along their flight); all smoke a third (near-black cards greying as they climb, each lit as a ball, its sunward side lit and its underside dark and glowing from the flames under it), drawn before the flames, which show through it; heat haze over the flames, the opaque scene read a few pixels off; every card faded in where it meets what is behind it; a breath is a jet of cards streaming out in a cone that ends where it strikes, flaring there, drawn as a flamethrower's stream is in footage and in the games that draw one well (research/2026-10-18-dragon-breath.md): what reads is the contrast in it, a solid rod near-white over the first third of its length, yellow cores with orange and dull red rims as it rolls open (1150 to 2000 K by the flipbook's heat), black soot pockets in it, and each card covering as it glows (premultiplied over), so hot yellow never goes olive at half cover and a dim rim never lays rust over the bright cores under it (the cards are drawn in the stream's order, not by depth); and what it sheds is let go where the stream is and then moves on its own, so it stays where it was breathed as the dragon flies on (two rings the CPU fills and the GPU reads), while what is still in the stream when it stops burns out in a moment: black lit soot rolling off its outer two thirds and its end, rising, swelling and hanging some seconds, and burning drops falling from it as streaks; twelve point lights, three along each breath and the rest at the largest gatherings of fire, reaching 22 m (a breath's 30), flickering as a fire that size puffs (1.5/√D Hz); flames and the breath coloured through a heat gradient on the screen whose stops are spread as real fire's colours are measured (research/2026-10-18-dragon-breath.md §4: a burning building's flame over half deep red-orange, a tenth yellow, white only in its hottest few in a hundred), and blended as they cover: a building's dim rims see-through, its soot dark where its gas has cooled; a breath solid, yellow and pale in its core, orange through its body, soot only on its outside puffs, never a yellow at part cover or under soot (olive); a gathering's light hung 3 m out from the middle of all that burns, on its walls' outsides; the sky drawn before every see-through thing, so its clouds never lie over smoke; how many blazes are drawn by the preset (2,048 at high). Where fire is broad, three or more blaze cells beside each other on a level burn as one wide blaze, a sheet of fire, not one flame to a cell in rows. A fire kept where something stands (`fire::Kindled`: a built campfire, a staged one) is drawn as one more blaze, flames, embers, smoke and light, so a campfire burns with the same fire as a burning house rather than a light alone; it stands just where it is and as tall as it was made, not jittered as a grid's blazes are, and its flames are sized to clear its logs, which hide a shorter one whole. Such a fire, a hearth (up to 16 drawn so), is drawn as a campfire is in dawn footage and in The Witcher 3, Red Dead Redemption 2 and Kingdom Come, not as a building's blaze: a bed of glowing coals among its logs and five tongues of their own heights, the middle one over the logs and four licking up their outsides, each faded only within 6 cm of what is behind it (faded over 35 cm, the logs left one thin tongue), solid and orange through their body with a white-yellow heart and thin only at their tips, the hottest thing in the picture (with a roof's thin rims they were see-through peach, dimmer than the troll they lit); its logs charred black (`tools/props/char.py`); ten or so sparks a second rising from its coals a metre or two, short orange streaks; a thin pale grey wisp of smoke of 24 puffs let go close together so they rise as one thread, showing only clear of its flames and gone four or five metres up (lit from under as a burning building's black smoke is, it was glowing peach balls; living nine seconds it climbed past eight metres and hung among the trunks as fog); and an orange light of its own in its flames just clear of its logs' ends (level with them, the ends were the brightest thing by it), reaching 14 m, so it lights the faces round it and a ring of ground, not the far shore. A kept fire clears the ground 0.8 m past half its flames' width as a footprint does, so grass keeps out of a campfire's ring (lake film r7: blades standing just behind its flames faded them see-through). Amended 2026-10-18 (lake film r8): the cleared ground reaches 1.6 m past half the flames' width, a stride past a big ring's stones, as a kept camp's ground is bare round its ring; at 0.8 m the leaning blades still reached the stones.

## Why

The developers asked on 2026-10-15 for a castle "and have a dragon fucking setting it on fire and it going down in a large destruction", with fire that spreads and burns and burnt-through parts bringing down what they held. Cellular because every shipped fire that spreads through a world is (research/2026-10-15-dragons-and-fire.md); through `Op::Damage` so fire is one more harm, not a second way of destroying; hot gas because without it a fire crept sideways a cube in seven seconds and a tower took minutes to burn, where real rooms flash over.
- **2026-10-18**: D377 amended (by an agent): flames and the breath are coloured through a heat gradient on the screen (dull red, orange, a saturated yellow with almost no blue, pale yellow, white only in the hottest cores) and blended as they cover, as Unreal's fire materials colour a flipbook, in place of soot's lift by brightness, which whitened a wall's flames into cut-outs and, added over a blue sky, took the breath to salmon pink (castle film r7, 12% yellow); twelve fire lights, three along each breath, reaching 22 m (a breath's 30), since at 55 m they tinted every sunlit wall.
- **2026-10-18**: D377 amended (by an agent, the developer: "dragons fire still sucks, like its all too cartoonish, look at some real materials"): a burning wall's flames are frames of real fire footage (Wikimedia Commons "File:Fire.ogv", public domain), one filmed fire to a blaze, a third of a grid's blazes only embers and smoke, a dim texel's green held under its red, so a wall is separate fires and not a lime-flecked hedge; a breath is a raymarched volume, opaque in its yellow core (18 per metre) and thinner at its orange rims (5), its colours under the tonemapper's knee, ending in a lumpy fireball held off the wall with a brown-black soot cap (castle film r11-r12).
