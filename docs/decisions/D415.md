# D415: One Fire module, built by the rules shipped games follow

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Every fire in the game, from a candle to a burning castle, a dragon's breath and an explosion, is drawn by one module, `terra_world::fire`, from a preset of data per kind (`assets/data/fire.ron`, D136). Its rules are those of [docs/fire.md](../fire.md), each taken from shipped games ([research/2026-10-19-how-games-draw-fire.md](../../research/2026-10-19-how-games-draw-fire.md)).

**Flame and smoke**
- A fire is layers on their own clocks: fuel glow, body, licks, rod, smoke, sparks, flare, haze, light, fog and ground.
- Flipbooks hold heat, cover, soot and distortion as separate channels, with six-way smoke maps and motion vectors. They are baked by our own pipeline from Blender's Mantaflow sim rendered in Cycles. No paid tool or library is used.
- Heat is coloured through one ramp for the whole game, with a premultiply column, and drawn premultiplied.

**Exposure and tonemapping**
- A fire's luminance is physical, scaled toward the eye's adaptation by one exposure weight per realm. Lights stay physical.
- The camera's tonemapper is chosen by a measured bake-off of Bevy's curves and a Hable curve ported from Godot. Fire is exposed and graded with the world; only a hearth's or torch's flame takes a film's shoulder of its own, per channel, past the white, and skips the post-tonemap saturation, as Frostbite and The Witcher 3 treat fire (`fire::film`: the flames write a mask in the HDR alpha, the tonemapper mixes Hable per channel into KhronosPbrNeutral by it). The glowing parts of what burns (a campfire's coals, its logs' cracks and hot feet, `fire::glow`) write their glow's share of each pixel to the same mask, and the masked share is taken from before the sections' grade, so the night's fading of the dim never turns a dull coal's red pink; what the fire lights is graded with the world. This keeps D370's neutral grade (white balance and tint untouched): only the saturation applied after the curve is skipped on the flame. The breath and blazes stay on Neutral until measured.
- A fire light's in-scatter in the camera's haze is taken at 0.15 (`scatter::LOCAL_SCATTER`), so its glow stays under the tonemapper's white.
- What shows through a flame (a pixel with any flame share) is tonemapped as a firelit surface is, by the hue-keeping shoulder, under the flame's own film: through the camera's own, the bright logs and coals behind a thin flame read salmon, the campfire's "magenta card" (campfire roll round, 2026-10-23).

**Light and smoke lighting**
- Fire lights are clustered, flicker from one signal per fire that also drives the embers' and char's glow, and jitter in position. A campfire's painted `Fire` part, its coals and burning faces cut from its logs, glows with them; only an unpainted `Fire` flame gives way to the drawn one.
- They cast shadows near the camera, are brighter by night than by day (a blaze's and a jet's; a kept fire's by 3 stops under a half-strength moon (fire round 12: at 2.2 its ground had no firelit pool; at 3.2 under a quarter moon its stones went salmon past the shoulder), a blaze's by 2.5, its flame still the brightest thing by it), fall off as a disc light (a kept fire's as wide as its flames, its light in two shadowed lights: a key low in the pit and a brighter one at its flames' top, for the faces sat round it (`HEARTH_TOP`, lake round 19); fire round 19: three times as wide, its fall-off stayed level to some 4 m and the ground had no pool) as wide as their flames (`falloff`), and a kept fire's scatters in a small fog volume of its own (a blaze's stays out of the fog: in the haze it lit a ball of air brighter than the house).
- Smoke is six-way lit, backlit, lit from inside, dark, and shadowing. Each smoke card plays a baked one-shot puff (`campfire-smoke`, `house-smoke`) whose six-way maps come from three Cycles renders a frame, the six lights coloured three to a render. It is lit by its own light loop, not as a surface: the sun through the atmosphere and its shadows, every clustered fire light held at its radius, and the open sky from six sides.

**Composites and budget**
- A breath, an explosion and a building fire are composites, never one volume or one card.
- A cinematic quality preset lets a film spend more through the same data.

**The check**
- Every preset's stills are measured against photographs of real fire, by colour band, opacity, soot and detail, and a change that touches fire fails when one falls outside its class.
- The fire sheet's gate (`cargo xtask fire --gate`) runs as a continuous check on main (`cargo xtask continuous`, D37, D398): on the GPU machine, whenever main moves over what it reads (`FIRE` in `xtask/src/scope.rs`), reporting a regression with the commit range it came in. No hook runs it and nothing waits on it before landing. Until a stage brings a fire into its class, the gate fails only where a fire is worse than what is out today (`tools/fire/held.json`), so the gate stands from stage 0 while today's fires fail, and what each stage fixes stays fixed.

It supersedes D377's way of drawing fire as each stage lands; D377's cellular fire stays the truth of what burns.

## Why

The developer, 2026-10-18, after twelve rounds of tuning scored 6-7 of 10: "idk how to tell you how to do fire, i gave you so much feedback and it all looks like fucking joke all the time", and "BIG theme here is whether we can capture the rules in some static systems/modules that we could reuse so we don't have to always fix those problems". Then: "We are going to focus on CRUSHING the fire module, we wanna do it properly, well, and in the way that would make an AAA developer proud. we do it properly, and once, every time this entire simulation needs fire ever again, we have it properly done!"

On tools: "we are in the era of LLMs, and i do want to be able to beat it without having to pay for fire library." On films: "for cinematic shot, we may wanna cook that scene differently where the distances are a bit better, we can burn the budget there a little bit."

Four surveys agree on the causes (The Witcher 3, ARK and Unreal, the AAA talks, the survival games):
- emission and opacity were keyed from one luma channel;
- KhronosPbrNeutral desaturates bright orange to peach, where Hable's per-channel curve turns it yellow;
- the fire was fully exempt from exposure;
- the lights had no shadows and were not in the fog;
- the smoke was unlit;
- the breath was one smooth volume.

Each is a rule here, so it is fixed once for every fire, and the check catches it before a person must.

The baker is Mantaflow, not a sim of our own, because Cycles also renders the self-shadowed six-way maps from the same voxels. A sim of our own would need a volume renderer as well.

The gate runs continuously on main, not in a hook or before landing. The developer, 2026-10-18, on the rule that every change to fire ran it before landing: "why did you decide that every commit must do a fire check? this should be a check for continuous main checks, not for precommit hook, that would be too expensive. if there is some guidance that suggests that, it should be reworked and mention that precommit hooks are mainly for simple quick tests/formatting/linters and etc. and that tests like these are to be ran on main via continuous tests". CI has no GPU (D37), so the GPU machine runs it.

The baker as built (stage 3): three passes a frame, rendered as ray-marched emission-and-absorption volumes on the CPU (heat as the flame's emission-weighted mean, motion from the sim's velocity in m/s, cover and soot from optical depths), so each is an exact integral, never a sampled guess, and a bake takes no GPU memory. Flipbooks are PNG with a JSON sidecar until GPU compression (D188), about 4.3 MB a set; far sets are mips. Six-way smoke is stage 4's.

The engine draws the baked sets (stage 3, 2026-10-19): the campfire, tongue and torch sets as layers of one array texture, premultiplied, their heat coloured through the ramp, blended frame to frame along their motion where it brings frames nearer. The ramp is the blackbody as a photograph records it (each channel 1 - e^(-film x light)), since the curve stays KhronosPbrNeutral, which keeps hue: the raw blackbody drew the sets salmon and 90-99% red. A card stands by its set's `reach`, so a fire's `tall` is how high its flames lick.

The flames' own shoulder (2026-10-19, by an agent, after the salmon trace): a hot campfire texel has no blue until KhronosPbrNeutral, which mixes everything past its white toward white alike (sRGB 254,233,208, B/R 0.64), and `post_saturation` 0.82 then floors blue at 18% of brightness; night photographs of campfires go yellow at their brightest (254,233,141, B/R 0.03-0.15). Film clips each channel on its own, so red, then green, then blue run out: orange, yellow, white. Tonemapping the whole camera with Hable would change every scene's look (the developers' call), so only the flames take it, by a mask; exposure and the grade stay the world's, so Battlefield 3's "wrong color" particles, lit or exposed apart, do not return. The pink ball over the logs was the haze's in-scatter of the hearth's light, not the flames and not the fire's own fog volume: an A/B with the fire's volumes off kept it, with the light out of the fog it went.

The decision is Leaning until stage 1's bake-off and stage 3's first baked sets are measured.
