# D430: A film's breath is marched as a volume, play's stays cards

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The cinematic tier of D428 draws a dragon's breath as one raymarched volume (`terra_world::fire::volume`, `fire.wesl`'s `breath_gas` and `breath`), in place of its cards; play, at every other preset, keeps the cards. A film asks for it by the ultra preset (`Quality::breath_marched`, a row of the preset's table) or by its shot's look (`marched_breath` in a `--cine` shot), so it is data, not code that names a film (D249). The volume is the spike of research/2026-10-19-raymarched-breath.md made a part of the fire's plugin: density, temperature and soot fields (Houdini's and EmberGen's pyro split) for a widening, wandering stream and a lumpy fireball rolling up off the face it strikes; marched front to back in up to 96 steps through the jet's hull, ended at the depth prepass, emitting through the fire's ramp and absorbing by Beer-Lambert; its hull drawn a second time into the picture's alpha with the flame's share, so the tonemapper takes it through the film as a hearth's (D415). Over the spike: the stream wider by its reach's end (to 1.6 times its spread, not 1.2) and the fireball from 1.5 m (was 0.8), with the gas no thinner; no gas behind the struck face, so the ball flattens on it; its soot lit by the sun through four shadow taps toward it, plus a fifth of that unshadowed for the sky (albedo 0.12; at 0.32 the crown stood pale as a cloud at noon); and its own light, srgb(1, 0.78, 0.3) at 1.4e6 lm a light where the cards' is srgb(1, 0.74, 0.32) at 2e6, so the wall round the larger ball stays orange-yellow and under the tonemapper's shoulder. Its sprites are hidden but the soot stain on the face. In a blind A/B against the cards, fresh reviewer, the references: the volume 5/10 and the cards 2.5/10, the volume better in every row the breath burns in at 90 to 92%. Its cost on the RTX 3080 at 1920x1080 (`--bench` offscreen, dusk, 240 frames, ultra against high): seen head-on, the fireball filling most of the frame, the transparent pass 17.1 ms against the cards' 2.1 and the GPU frame 29.8 ms against 11.2 (ultra's finer haze, D428, 2.9 ms of that); from the side the run gave no pass times and the GPU frame read 3.9 ms both ways. That is seven times the spike's 2.2 ms, from a fireball some three times the screen area and the shadow taps; it held a film's 33 ms with little to spare. Its value noise is now read from a tiling 32-cell volume texture (`volume::noise`), one fetch where the shader hashed eight corners, as Nubis and Unreal's volumes read theirs; the shadow taps and the film mask's second march read a coarse gas (no warp, no finest octaves), the mask in steps twice as long, and the sun is found at every other sooty step: head-on the transparent pass is 4.6 ms (from 17.1) and the GPU frame 16.9 ms (from 29.9), some 2.5 ms over the cards. Once off, its fireball burns out to soot that rises, spreads and thins over 5.5 s, where nothing was left but the stain. The level line at the wall's top seen head-on is the wall's lit top edge showing through the ball's holes, not the yard's other fires (it stays with them left out). A blind A/B of this with a hotter jet and a solid ball against main's volume: 3.5/10 against 4.5, the hotter gas salmon through the camera's curve, so only the cost and the smoke were kept; the smoke won its row. Not measured: the integrated GPU.

## Why

The developer: "for cinematic shot ... we can burn the budget there a little bit", and "we do it properly, and once". The volume's first blind review scored it 4.5/10 against the cards' 2/10 for about 2 ms more seen head-on, which fits a film's 33 ms frame and not play's 0.3 ms for a fire (docs/fire.md, "LOD and budgets"). Unreal draws its Heterogeneous Volumes and Niagara's gas the same way, for cinematics. Whether the volume also replaces play's cards is the developers' to decide, so play keeps them.
