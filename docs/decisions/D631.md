# D631: The figure's transitions follow shipped engines' methods, measured on the animation course

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

How the figure goes from one clip to the next is measured by numbers alone on the animation course (`terra_walker` `figure::course`: the man's figure posed headless with no GPU by the game's own clips, blends, foot lock, pose layers and lift, moved as the mover moves a man through every transition it makes) and held by a scorecard, `cargo xtask anim` (`tools/anim`, D452's ratchet, its rows ranked by how much a player notices them, then by how many bars out). Each fault is fixed by the method shipped engines use for it, ported (D196), in this order of need:

- **Sync markers** between stride loops (Unreal's sync groups; ours `figure::synced`, `Markers::synced`) and **stride warping** (Unreal's Stride Warping; ours `pace::gait`): kept as they are.
- **Inertialization** in place of the linear crossfade (Bollo, "Inertialization: High-Performance Animation Transitions in Gears of War", GDC 2018; Unreal's Inertialization node; Holden's dead blending, 2023, its simpler form): the new clip plays at once and what the old pose and its velocity were decays into it, so a transition adds no change of velocity the clips lack (the `pop.*` rows).
- **Distance matching** for starts and stops (Lyra, UE5's Animation Locomotion Library): a stop clip played by the distance left to the body's stop, a start's by the distance gone, from the baked distance curves (`Markers::distance`, `terra_motion::distance`); a stop on the planted foot (Paragon's left and right stops) (`glide.stop`, `start.root`).
- **Orientation warping** for turns (Unreal's Orientation Warping): the lower body turned toward the travel, the upper body kept (`pop.turn`, `glide.turn`, once a turn's rows fail).
- Not now: **motion matching** (UE5's Pose Search, Ubisoft's Clavet GDC 2016): it needs a large database of motion we do not have; the methods above work on the clips we do.

Built (anim-blend j3 to j9): inertialization at acts' starts and ends and at every change but a stride loop into a stride loop, the offsets refit a frame on so the pose keeps its velocity; the course on the world, so its foot lock works, and a lock's step landing headed as over its landing (D455). The distance-matched stop is built (anim-stop j1), at D633's 17 m/s² brake; distance-matched starts and orientation warping are not built yet. Where each row stands: `cargo xtask anim` and the `anim` skill's task list.

## Why

The developers want the figure to look like a shipped game's ("Do it as shipped games do", AGENTS.md); the course's first numbers (anim-blend j1) put a roll's first frame at 13.6 times the clips' own roughest change of velocity, a jump's take-off at 7.5 and a sprint's stop at 3.7, all at the frame a 110-180 ms linear crossfade starts, and a stop's legs striding 2.7 m/s faster than the body for the fade. These are the faults the named methods were made for, each in a shipped engine with its talk or docs; measured headless they are judged in seconds without a GPU or an eye. Motion matching would replace the clips' state machine whole, and needs captured motion the game does not have.
