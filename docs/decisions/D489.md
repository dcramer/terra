# D489: The sky light's repaint is worked out once a height

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The painted sky the shade is lit by (D190, D392) is the same all round its up, so its repaint works out the sky's light once for each height a direction can have, not once a direction (`skylight::Colours::at_height`): the irradiance lobes once a row of their 48 heights, the 32-pixel cube once for each of the heights its six faces share. The painting is byte for byte what working out each direction gives, and a test holds it so; a repaint takes 0.8 ms of the main thread where it took 7.3 to 7.7 (medians a run, the controller's walk route, 36 repaints a run in both builds, release on Windows), about once a second while walking, and stills before and after differ no more than two stills of one build. A sky that is not the same all round, a glow round the sun's side, makes the painting a task off the main thread (Bevy's `AsyncComputeTaskPool`) rather than undoing this.

## Why

Walking repainted the sky about once a second (the eye's up turns on the planet, so the sun's height and the sky's colours move a shade), and every repaint took 7 ms of the game's frame: the slowest game frames in every A/B of the controller's switch (D53) were these, and `frametimes.ts` had to count them apart. Nearly all of it was ten thousand directions each moving its colour onto the daylight locus by a 24-step search (D392), though at most a few hundred heights differ. Shipped engines spread a sky's update over frames, Godot's incremental sky processing filtering one roughness layer a frame and Unreal's real-time sky capture time-slicing its faces and filtering, because what they update is a full capture filtered on the GPU that cannot be made smaller; ours is a CPU painting with a symmetry that makes it twelve times cheaper and exact, with nothing to blend between frames. Bevy's filter of the painted map, on the GPU for four frames after a repaint (`FILTER_FRAMES`), is unchanged.
