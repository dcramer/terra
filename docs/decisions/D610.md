# D610: The play scenes are benched at each landing, a failure bisected unattended, and an A/B reads main's side from it

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The perf row ([tools/perf](../../tools/perf/README.md)) benches lake-play, forest-run and town-dusk, one run each, once at each landing batch on main that touches what they read (`tools/perf/row.json`): the landings that come while it runs are taken together at the next tip, as a busy Buildbot builder collapses its pending requests. It runs in the render worker's daemon, the one always-on GPU box, as a bench job of its own queue, so D319 (a bench has the box alone) and D445's planner order it, with a rest of 20 minutes after each of its benches so it never holds the box with benches back to back. Each scene's median, p99, hitches, load and card are kept by commit (`~/terra-render/perf/history.csv`). A metric past its noise band (the window of the 12 jobs before it, one point a job, its runs' median, so a retrigger's five runs of one hour do not narrow it below the day's spread: the window's median, and the wider of a floor and three robust deviations) is retriggered at the same commit with five runs, and when that holds its range's good end is benched beside it with five, as Pinpoint measures both sides of a change; an alert needs the two ends to differ past the metric's floor and by Pinpoint's rank test (Mann-Whitney U at its low threshold, p ≤ 0.01), so a level shift of the box alone, both ends moved together, is no alert and re-bases the band; ends past the floor but not sure are benched once more, then let go. An alert names the scene, the metric, before and after (its ends' centres) and the range; a regression is bisected by halving the range's commits touching the row, each step as many runs as an end's and judged against its good neighbour (the newest good measured), bad when its offset from it is nearer the ends' difference than nothing; a neighbour measured over an hour before is benched again beside the step first, and a bad end left as the first bad is benched again beside its neighbour, as Pinpoint compares adjacent changes whose attempts it runs together on one bot (job_state.py), so the box's level drifting over a bisect's hours names no commit; at most five steps (the benches again outside them), and the alert names the first bad commit, what is left, or no first bad when even the bad end measured like its neighbour. An alert is closed by hand as invalid (not the code: the box's level, noise) or accepted (a real change kept on purpose), with its reason (`perf.ts alert <id> --invalid|--accept`), as Perfherder's sheriffs triage one; either drops its bisect and re-bases its series' band at the level since its bad end, so the level it saw raises nothing again. A scene the bench took on a busy machine (D185, its row's `busy` column) is recorded but judged nowhere, so it raises no alert, as Perfherder ignores an infra-flagged run. An A/B reads main's side from the history at its base (`node tools/perf/perf.ts ab`), naming the commits between and whether they touch what the row reads, and benches only its own; its variants go through the probe (D609). As Chromium's perf bots chart every change and Pinpoint bisects a regression, Mozilla's Perfherder alerts on a sustained change past a threshold from its back window and its sheriffs retrigger a suspect to confirm it, and Unreal's Gauntlet runs a fixed set per changelist. [research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md](../../research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md#4-proposals-ranked).

## Why

On 2026-11-02 town-dusk regressed at 14:44 and was seen only at ~18:12, by a job benching four scenes for another reason; its last +11% was bisected by hand over 41 commits and never confirmed. The two perf rows are lake-only and rest 6 and 12 hours. Meanwhile every job benched both sides of its own A/B, so benches held the worker's queue: three back to back starved it for 1.5 hours, j9's three hours went mostly to the queue, j13's bench was cancelled while waiting.

Built in the worker rather than as a row of `tools/continuous/checks.json`: under the embargo no machine runs continuous with a GPU, and the worker already owns the queue and the bench's turn on the card; the daemon reads main by `git ls-remote` of the public repository and the landing's LFS objects are on the box from its push check. Castle is left out of the row for now: three scenes keep a landing's bench to a few minutes. One run a landing is noisy for hitches, so a single point never alerts: the retrigger is the second run, as a Perfherder sheriff's. Nor do runs at the bad end alone: on 2026-11-03 lake-play's median rose 9.30 → 9.68 ms over 316f1d5e3..4688fd3d7, held on its retrigger and was bisected to a refactor of xtask's bench bookkeeping, but five runs at each end gave 9.71 and 9.63 ms: the box's own level had drifted about 4% over the day, inside a ±3% band, which only a comparison of both ends measured in the same hour tells from a change, as Pinpoint compares both sides with repeats and a rank test rather than against history. The developers: "these should all be based on what are the best practices on the gaming industry, not what i want".
