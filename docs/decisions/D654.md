# D654: Nothing is guessed: a system starts from sources read, and a check from a known-good reference

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Developers · Status: Decided

Nothing we build, decide or gate on rests on a guess. Before a system is built or rebuilt, its design doc carries a **Prior art** section (or links the research note that is one): at least two shipped implementations of it (an engine's source or docs, a paper, a talk, a game's own write-up), read in this job and linked; the architecture they share, in the industry's terms; which one we port (D196) and where we depart, with the reason. Every claim about how others do it carries its grade: **[P]** primary, read now (the maker's source, docs, paper or talk), **[S]** secondary, **[C]** community, **[M]** from memory. An [M] claim is a lead to look up, never a ground: no architecture, decision or gate stands on one. Where no source is found, the note says so and the work is a spike answering one question, or a question for the desk, never an invention written up as the practice.

The same in code and in debugging. An API is read from the pinned source. A constant says where it comes from: a source, a measurement, or a derivation; one tuned until a check passed, or to cancel another system, is a fault to raise, not to land. A bug's cause is shown by a measurement that would read otherwise were the cause another, before the fix; a fix landed on a cause not shown says so in its commit.

A check must be right, not only awake. D632 proves a check sees its fault; this adds that what it passes is good. Its bar comes from a reference, not from what our build happens to read: the prior art's own number, an answer worked out another way, or a reference measured as data (D311). A test beside it runs a **known-good** case through the same measure and shows it passes, as the planted fault fails. Before it gates, its first passing result is looked at once against the reference; a result that passes its numbers and is plainly wrong is the check's fault, fixed by adding the measure that sees it, never by tuning the system until the number agrees. How: [development.md](../development.md#building-a-system-without-guessing).

## Why

The developers, 2026-11-09: "when we implement systems, we should look up architecture, how others do it, ideally prior art. we then need to focus on programatic verifiable solutions, but we have to be careful so we dont create something thats clearly wrong as well. mostly we should avoid guessing".

D196 allows prior art and AGENTS.md asked for it, but nothing said it must be read rather than recalled: 27 of 155 research notes say "from memory" somewhere, and the lighting review of the same day gave the industry's practice "from memory, said; nothing measured". That review also found what guessing leaves behind: thirteen constants that each cancel another system, and looks held at one hour only. Five notes had already graded their sources ([P], [S], [C], [M]; *read*, *recalled*, *measured*); this makes their habit the rule. D632 came from a check that passed while measuring nothing; the other half is a check that measures something and passes the wrong thing, which a known-good reference and one look catch.
