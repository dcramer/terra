# D359: Canopy shade dappled, pines' stubs snapped, a thicker middle layer under the pines

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D289, D309 and D318. A crown's solid shadow stand-in casts a crown's shade, not a disc: alpha-tested in the shadow pass, its shade broken into gaps 0.45 m across by noise in the world and thinning to nothing toward its rim as the sun sees it (its offsets from its lumps' middles carried in its vertex colour, `dapple` in `foliage_prepass.wesl`), part shade a different share of shadow texels each frame for the shadow filter and TAA to average, as Unreal shadows a masked material's dithered opacity. A Scots pine's bole stubs mostly a hand or two long, a few to half a metre, tapering to a broken point and drooping. A pine's long limbs carry pads nearer the bole as well, its pads' shoots some level or hanging so their edges are a ragged fringe, and its forks drawn as deep as their limb so no pad floats. Under a pine stand's needles the understorey's hazel, coppice, holly and young oaks thin to under half, not to none; a third of the pines ivied and half mossy at the foot. Climbing ivy's leaves a hand across or less, many more of them, its vines leafier (cards of a third of a metre read as a column of stickers up the bole); the floor's fern and herb cards reflect a third as much as a leaf (0.1), since a frond drawn as one flat card mirrored the haze round a low sun whole and stood pale grey-white. **The middle layer brought up to the crowns' standard** (the developers: the bushes and small trees "have flat leaves problem", "the same problem we have addressed for big trees"): its hazel, oak scrub, saplings, bramble and holly tinted two thirds as bright as the crowns' oak leaves (0.6, against a third, set when the cards were pale, D268; as bright as the crowns', the bushes in the trailer's low sun glowed lime from rim to core, 0.47 to 0.54 grey against Sons of the Forest's undergrowth's 0.27), neither greyed nor darkened, lit through two thirds as a crown and darker inside (`THICKET`); an oak scrub's and a sapling's leaves held in flat sprays along their shoots as a hazel's are (`flat_sprays`), not in clumps of cards round each spray, which drew them as domes of leaves with no gap, bilberry shaded as they are rather than greyed as the floor's pictures; near a shrub's or sapling's cards each folded along its stalk by 0.3 to 0.6 radians and drooping toward its tip (`folded_card`, eight triangles to six), and within 14 m any card shaded by its own face 0.6 of the way from its crown's normal (`NEAR_FACE`), as SpeedTree and Unreal fold near cards and blend their normals, so a bush a stride off is no longer planes of leaves of one tone; rendered grey, its every leaf had been lit alike. The trailer's run measured against Sons of the Forest: lime top 1.1% to 2.0% (2.0%), red over green 1.18 to 1.14 (1.10), local contrast 0.20 to 0.21, trunks in the middle band 83% to 69% (49%); the place's looks check's leaf rules (D368) hold, flat foliage 28% (at most 43%), leaf colour spread 0.080 (at least 0.073), leaves catching light 20% (at least 3.7%). **Holly and fern composed from scans** as the other leaves are (D269, D292), no longer flat-normal pictures: holly from ambientCG's LeafSet022, dark and glossy (albedo 0.17, 0.28, 0.12; roughness 0.3), its margins cut back between four spines a side (`spines`, a share of the leaf's width); fern a frond (`frond`) of LeafSet013's leaflets as pinnae in pairs along one rachis, corner to corner as its picture lay, lobed by eleven cuts a side. `cargo xtask foliage` (D368): holly's relief 0% to 95% and tilt leaf to leaf 0° to 36°, fern's relief 0% to 97%, its pinnae 79 to 178 texels long and living green 27% to 93%.

## Why

The forest's adversarial review (6/10, 2026-10-15): the canopy's shade lay on the floor as round black discs, the pines' crowns read as broccoli with floating pads, their stubs as pencils pushed into the bole, every bole a clean pole, and the middle layer hid 19.8% of the eye-height band against a wild wood's 30 to 60%.
