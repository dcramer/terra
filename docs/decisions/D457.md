# D457: The character controller is held by a scorecard of criteria and a ratchet, measured on a plain-Rust course

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What the controller must get right is data, `tools/controller/criteria.json`: a row for each of docs/controller.md's production criteria, split where one bar covers several numbers, in D452's shape (what a player notices and how much, the bar, the source, the check, cheap or heavy, measurable or what it needs). `cargo xtask controller` measures every cheap one on the plain-Rust course, `terra_motion::course` (feature `course`): analytic signed-distance fixtures (steps 0.1-0.8 m, slopes 0-70 deg, walls with a gap, a 2 m tunnel, a stair, a ledge, a pool, a round world, a crowd) a mover is driven through by scripted intents, its answers known from the geometry, the kind's numbers or a law, every case on every core (D406). The mover is driven through one trait, `course::Mover`, so stage 2's controller replaces today's walker without the course changing; today's walk is driven through an adapter among `terra_walker`'s tests, the same `movement::step` the game's walk takes, and seeded the baseline. Which test drives the mover is the criteria file's `course`.

The ratchet is combat's (D452), shared in `tools/scorecard`: a criterion's number is its miss, the baseline `tools/controller/baseline.tsv` holds each, a change may leave one below its bar but never make it worse unless it raises it with `--raise "<why>"`, and nightly alone lowers it (D414), carried onto a main that moved only if nothing `CONTROLLER` in `xtask/src/scope.rs` names changed. It is a stage of `cargo xtask check --all`. The in-game filmed course (stage 1b) and the mover's bench are heavy rows, not measurable yet, each saying what it needs. The failing criteria, ordered by `noticed`, are the task list in the `controller` skill.

## Why

The controller workstream follows combat's "QA first" (D452): the criteria, a scorecard and a ratchet exist, seeded from today's controller, before the new mover is built, so the mover's every stage is measured against where the walker stands and nothing it gets right slips back. A plain-Rust course measures in seconds without a GPU; analytic fixtures make every expected answer known without the mover. Sharing the ratchet with combat keeps one definition of a miss, a hold and a lowering.
