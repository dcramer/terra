# D428: An ultra preset for films, spent on distance

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A fourth quality preset, ultra (`terra_world::quality`), for demo films and cinematics: asked for only, by `--quality ultra` (the film tool passes it through after `--`) or the options menu, never chosen for a GPU, so high stays the look as made and the default. Like D239's presets it is numbers in the preset's table and nothing else. It spends the GPU on what a town seen from the air shows far off: the sun's shadow map at 4096 in four cascades from 32 m to 1,200 m (high: 2048, 24 m to 300 m); every level of detail half as far again (`detail_reach` 1.5, capped by the 10 GB card: at 2.0 the settlement film's pull-back over its 253 buildings held 8.6 GiB of it, too near the card's edge with anything else on it; a shot's own `detail` bias that also raises it does not stack on it but takes the larger, as Unreal's view distance is set by whichever asks further, while a bias or preset under 1 still multiplies, `cine::biased_reach`: multiplied, the settlement film's 1.35 made 2.7 and ran the 10 GB card out of memory as its camera pulled back over the whole town), so buildings' finer shapes, trees, plants, grass and the far buildings' stand-ins give way twice as far off; the ground's octree splitting at 1.5 times its distances (`ground_reach`), so ridges and banks far off keep their shape; the haze marched in 96 steps (high 48); and the air's sky table and aerial-perspective table twice as fine, with twice the steps. It cuts what only shows close up, which stays as high has it: relief layers, texture size, detail textures, sharpening, fire, smoke, drifting leaves, the lights that cast shadows and the building system's debris budgets. `cargo xtask bench --quality ultra` measures it, recorded as `<scene>@ultra`, and holds it to no budget. Its cost on the developers' RTX 3080 at 1920x1080, `town300-fly` (three hundred buildings flown over), three quiet runs each: a median of 15.7 ms at ultra against 9.8 ms at high, 64 frames a second against 102; the triangles drawn 2.75 million against 1.37, the opaque pass 6.0 ms against 4.9, the haze 4.7 against 2.4, the air's tables 1.0 against 0.3, and the render world waiting 8.0 ms for the GPU against 2.5, most of it the larger and longer shadow maps, which the passes' times miss (D185). A first run lays its far sections at the finer detail for minutes before the view is whole, so a film warms up longer (its first `--bench` run stuck at 300 s).

## Why

The developer on the settlement film, 2026-10-19: "i think this could look better in either dawn or dusk, and we should make them 'ultra' settings, strain that gpu for it, cut where you can to get even more impressive effect on high distances". Shipped games' top presets work so: Unreal's scalability groups have a level above high (`sg.*` 3, Epic) and a cinematic one (4) that raise view distance (`r.ViewDistanceScale`), cascade resolution and distance (`r.Shadow.MaxCSMResolution`, `r.Shadow.DistanceScale`) and foliage and landscape LOD; The Witcher 3's Ultra+ raises foliage and detail distance. The high preset's shadows stopped at 300 m, so a town seen whole from 300 m up had none on its far half, and its detail gave way at the distances a person on the ground needs.
