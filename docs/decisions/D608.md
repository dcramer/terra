# D608: Work after the load runs in priority classes under a per-frame budget

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What streams after the loading screen lifts goes through one scheduler, `terra_world::streaming::Streaming`, on D603's pool: a task is asked with its class and place and started in its turn, the classes in order (near ground, far woods, far ground, flora, anything else) and nearest the camera first within each, two a pool thread at a time, every class with work and nothing under way given one first so none starves (`streaming::order::next`); a result enters the world while the frame's main-thread milliseconds and upload bytes are under the budget (`assets/data/perf/budgets.ron`'s `streaming`: 2 ms and 8 MiB a frame, 50 ms and 64 MiB behind the loading screen), the first of each class a frame and the near ground always let in and counted (`streaming::order::Meter`). The far ground and the woods' far cards (laid in rings of equal area, a task each, then merged) are on it; flora and the rest have their class for when they move onto it. It says each class's tasks, seconds and pool thread-seconds when it drains (`Streamed FarGround: …`) and when the start's far view is all drawn (`Far view in N s`), which the bench says. As shipped engines do: Unreal caps async loading's game-thread time a frame (`s.AsyncLoadingTimeLimit`, `s.PriorityAsyncLoadingExtraTime` for high priority, one unified budget larger behind a loading screen) and keeps texture streaming to a pool of bytes; Unity caps the main thread's integration (`Application.backgroundLoadingPriority`, 2 to 50 ms); Frostbite and Decima order streaming requests by priority, then distance; Godot keeps low-priority tasks to a share of its `WorkerThreadPool` but always at least one thread (`low_priority_thread_ratio`). [research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md](../../research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md#4-proposals-ranked).

## Why

On 2026-11-02 D603's streaming ran 110 to 260 s beside play with nothing holding it to a share: the far cards' one task waited behind 1473 chunk tasks on the FIFO pool (259 s), town-dusk's game is likely ~2 ms higher while it runs (perf-regress j2, unconfirmed), and a far chunk costs about 0.29 thread-seconds, five times a near one, which no line showed. Fed nearest first a few at a time, with the cards in rings, the lake's whole far view came in 106 s, not 259 (perf-regress j2); load-time j5 held the far ground's entry to 8 MiB a frame. The developers: "these should all be based on what are the best practices on the gaming industry, not what i want" (2026-11-03), so the classes, the floor and the budget are the engines' above. Built and benched (2026-11-03, fef3ecdfc, RTX 4090 worker, job 20261103-094027-53e9): lake-play 9.37 ms median, 11.16 ms 99th, 0 hitches, loaded in 43.3 s; forest-run 7.88 / 9.96 ms, 0 hitches; town-dusk 9.41 / 11.89 ms, 0 hitches; all hold D25; the scripted far view all drawn as the loading screen lifted.
