# D402: Staged figures, told over the remote

Area: Lore, magic, combat and growth · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A film's set piece on the ground is played by staged figures (`play::staged`): a model stood on the ground (`Planet::ground_at`), shaped by a template, dressed and holding looks on its sockets if asked, that does what a script tells it over the remote, as an actor does its blocking and as fliers are told theirs (D382). `staged.spawn` stands one (its model, template, clips file, what it wears and holds, where, facing what, how big, how fast it turns); `staged.play` blends it into a named clip, looping, or once and held on its last pose; `staged.move` walks or slides it on the ground to a point at a speed, facing a point or another figure, a stride clip's playback following its pace (`crate::pace`) so its feet do not slide; `staged.face` turns it to a point or a figure; `staged.impact` raises what a heavy blow raises where it lands, dust and stones in the ground's colour on ground (on the forest's soil also dead leaves of the litter, fluttering down, and on any ground a shove through the plants round it, a body that parts them (D271) swelling and falling back a few times, so bushes, ferns and grass shudder) and a splash with rings over water. A clips file other than the model plays on it when its bones are the model's (by glTF's target paths); otherwise the clips file's own body is used. Every name, of a model, an item or a clip, is in the calls, never in code. The first is the troll fight on the lake shore (docs/troll-fight.md); when creatures think, their minds tell figures the same things.

Amended 2026-10-17 for the fight's second round, after the developer called the first "tons of delays between moves, no fluidity, no juiciness": `staged.play` may start a clip so far into it (`from_s`), past a slow start; `staged.rate` runs a figure's own time, its clip and its going, at a rate, so a scene holds a wind-up's top, speeds a strike and freezes both fighters in a hit-stop on contact (research/melee-feel.md); and `staged.bend` (or `staged.spawn`'s `bend`) turns named bones about the body's axes over every clip, blended in, as a retarget pose fits clips made on a person to a heavier body (`play::staged::pose`): the troll's arms out of its belly, the man's shield out of his chest. The proper home of a body's retarget pose is its export (the clips carried onto it), which a later round may move it to.

Amended 2026-10-17 for the third round, after the review found the man's blows swung at air from 3 to 4 m: the clips carry their steps and staggers in the hips (the troll's reel 2 m), which `play::staged` leaves in the clip rather than taking as root motion, so the scene does the root motion's bookkeeping: it knows each clip's measured hips travel, places a blow by where the target's body is on the frame it lands, and moves a figure to where its hips were while the next clip blends in. A camera turned more than 30 degrees in one frame is a cut as well as one moved 5 m (`cine`), so a reverse angle a few metres away drops the anti-aliasing history and the blur rather than smearing a whip-pan.


Amended 2026-10-22 (lake rounds 24-25, a troll's toes in its campfire, then its shins over the stones): a fire a script set down (`burns`) is moved clear of the legs of a staged figure at rest by it, measured from its own skeleton (`calf_*`, `foot_*`, `ball_*`, `ball_leaf_*`: the shin from knee to ankle, the foot and the toes to their tip, as thick as 0.18 of the shin is long), so any kind and size of body and any pose, the shins leaning over the feet among them: no part of them nearer its middle than the larger of its footprint (its model's bounds as drawn, the stone ring at the scale it was set at) and its heat's reach (its flames' half width and the distance its radiated heat falls to the sun's 1 kW/m², a campfire's 5 kW radiated), moved straight away from the sitter by the least that clears every part (`play::staged::fireside`). It waits for its model to be drawn, so it moves once. The fire gives way, not the sitter, who is on a seat of its own.
## Why

The developers on 2026-10-17: "have our guy fight it for some time ... our guy has to have a sword/shield and move sideways and have attack animations and it needs to be engaging." A demo needs fighters that hit their marks on the frame, which no system of the game does yet (combat.md is to be built on); this is the least that films one, data-driven so the next scene is a script, not code.
