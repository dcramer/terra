# D402: Staged figures, told over the remote

Area: Lore, magic, combat and growth · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A film's set piece on the ground is played by staged figures (`play::staged`): a model stood on the ground (`Planet::ground_at`), shaped by a template, dressed and holding looks on its sockets if asked, that does what a script tells it over the remote, as an actor does its blocking and as fliers are told theirs (D382). `staged.spawn` stands one (its model, template, clips file, what it wears and holds, where, facing what, how big, how fast it turns); `staged.play` blends it into a named clip, looping, or once and held on its last pose; `staged.move` walks or slides it on the ground to a point at a speed, facing a point or another figure, a stride clip's playback following its pace (`crate::pace`) so its feet do not slide; `staged.face` turns it to a point or a figure; `staged.impact` raises what a heavy blow raises where it lands, dust and stones in the ground's colour on ground and a splash with rings over water. A clips file other than the model plays on it when its bones are the model's (by glTF's target paths); otherwise the clips file's own body is used. Every name, of a model, an item or a clip, is in the calls, never in code. The first is the troll fight on the lake shore (docs/troll-fight.md); when creatures think, their minds tell figures the same things.

Amended 2026-10-17 for the fight's second round, after the developer called the first "tons of delays between moves, no fluidity, no juiciness": `staged.play` may start a clip so far into it (`from_s`), past a slow start; `staged.rate` runs a figure's own time, its clip and its going, at a rate, so a scene holds a wind-up's top, speeds a strike and freezes both fighters in a hit-stop on contact (research/melee-feel.md); and `staged.bend` (or `staged.spawn`'s `bend`) turns named bones about the body's axes over every clip, blended in, as a retarget pose fits clips made on a person to a heavier body (`play::staged::pose`): the troll's arms out of its belly, the man's shield out of his chest. The proper home of a body's retarget pose is its export (the clips carried onto it), which a later round may move it to.

Amended 2026-10-17 for the third round, after the review found the man's blows swung at air from 3 to 4 m: the clips carry their steps and staggers in the hips (the troll's reel 2 m), which `play::staged` leaves in the clip rather than taking as root motion, so the scene does the root motion's bookkeeping: it knows each clip's measured hips travel, places a blow by where the target's body is on the frame it lands, and moves a figure to where its hips were while the next clip blends in. A camera turned more than 30 degrees in one frame is a cut as well as one moved 5 m (`cine`), so a reverse angle a few metres away drops the anti-aliasing history and the blur rather than smearing a whip-pan.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-17: "have our guy fight it for some time ... our guy has to have a sword/shield and move sideways and have attack animations and it needs to be engaging." A demo needs fighters that hit their marks on the frame, which no system of the game does yet (combat.md is to be built on); this is the least that films one, data-driven so the next scene is a script, not code.
