# D540: The clouds move by the world's clock, drawn from a weather map of the climate

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The sky's clouds are a function of the climate and the world's clock alone (D52, D315), never a machine's run time (`terra_world::sky::clouds`). **Shapes**: the cloud noise lies in the start's flat frame (the air's frame, D334), on a layer 1.5 km over the eye, and drifts on the wind aloft for the world's seconds of play (the clock in days times `DAY_SECONDS`). The wind aloft is the climate's prevailing wind at its mean speed, grown by Hellmann's seventh-root law (about twice the ground's at 1.5 km) and veered 20 degrees (the Ekman spiral; to the left in the south); a deck's scud rides the ground's bearing at 300 m. The CPU works the drift out in f64 as an offset for each octave of each noise field, wrapped where the shader's lattice repeats (256 cells), so it stays exact on a world decades old. **Weather map**, as Horizon's Nubis has one: an Rgba8 texture of 128 x 128 texels of 500 m (64 km) in the start's frame, its middle on a 4 km grid round the eye. r is the climate's cover, g how far what falls thickens cloud (`weather::thickening`), b the cloud's kind in Nubis's convention (1 cumulus, 0.5 stratocumulus, 0 stratus deck, from `weather::deck_cover`), a the snow share. It is built for each world-hour step of the clock, one step ahead on another thread, and drawn blended from one step to the next, so it changes as the climate does, never every frame (D127). The 2D layer takes its cover, deck and rain from it (D518), and stage 2's volumes read the same map.

## Why

docs/sky.md: "The same sky for everyone (D52, D312). Clouds, their motion and lightning are functions of the seed and the world's clock". The clouds moved by Bevy's `globals.time`, which counts from each machine's launch and its loading, so two players saw different skies. Nubis's weather map is what every shipped volumetric system reads (research/2026-10-26-volumetric-skies.md), so the 2D layer and stage 2's volumes can share one map, and the low preset's 2D fallback can agree with the high preset's volumes (D515). The wind aloft is the climate's steady mean, not its hour-by-hour turning. A wind that varies cannot be integrated as a pure function of the clock without summing from day 0, and Nubis and Flight Simulator also move clouds by one wind per weather. A map is about 13 ms over every core in the test profile, so it is built off the main thread. 500 m texels resolve the climate's fronts (3 km, eddies about 1.3 km), and the climate has nothing finer to give.
