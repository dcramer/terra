# D214: Temporal anti-aliasing, eye adaptation and bark that reads

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The camera resolves with Bevy's temporal anti-aliasing instead of SMAA, as every game with dense foliage does, with `ShadowFilteringMethod::Temporal`; every wind shader gives last frame's position from last frame's time (`globals.time - globals.delta_time`), so swaying leaves and blades do not smear. Foliage draws its crossfades between levels of detail with its own fragment shaders, adapted from Bevy's (MIT or Apache-2.0), dithering in Jimenez's interleaved gradient noise shifted every frame, as Unreal dithers its foliage, since Bevy's fixed 4×4 pattern cannot be resolved by TAA and shows as a crawling screen door. The eye adapts (Bevy's `AutoExposure`) round D212's exposure for the hour, at most two stops brighter in deep shade and one darker in glare, by a compensation curve flat over that band. Bark is a scanned furrowed bark (Poly Haven's Bark Willow 02, CC0) with its normal, occlusion and roughness maps, at sRGB 112 or so, the middle of real bark's range; the trunks' triangles were wound inward, so the inside of the far wall was drawn, lit from behind, and every trunk was black.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-13, after a video of the walk: "The bark is very dark, the things pop in and out, it doesn't yet feel like AAA game". What shipped games do and what Bevy 0.20 has for it is [research/aaa-forest-look.md](../../research/aaa-forest-look.md). The walk bench on the RTX 3080 went from 6.2 to 7.1 ms, within budget; recorded.
