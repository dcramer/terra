# D324: Town life in code

Area: People and the tribe · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A person's day is a schedule of activities by role in time windows, as Skyrim's packages are, each done at smart objects that advertise what can be done there, where to stand and which clip plays (The Sims; The Witcher 3's pooled spots), so a new activity is data. People walk a settlement's own path graph, from its layout (streets, plot paths, doors), by A*, with ORCA avoidance ported from RVO2 (Apache-2.0); a navmesh comes with collision (D53). They are simulated by distance as creatures are (D145); beyond it, where one is and what they do is a function of the clock and their schedule, and nothing ticks. They are drawn in tiers: full within 25 m; reduced to 80 m (animation at a lower rate, interpolated, fewer joints, the outfit merged); far to 200 m, instanced and posed from vertex animation textures.

## Why

Shipped towns run so: Assassin's Creed Unity carries 10,000 people on 40 full AIs and 120 high-detail models, and Kingdom Come: Deliverance II keeps 2,400 NPCs' routines through AI level of detail. No navigation crate supports Bevy 0.20, and the layout already knows every street; a graph is cheap on a server (D52). Our crowd measured bound by the CPU's animation and transforms ([research](../../research/bevy-character-capabilities.md)), so the tiers save CPU first. See [demos.md](../demos.md#3-a-living-town).
