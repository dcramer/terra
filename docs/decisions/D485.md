# D485: A reflection probe smaller than every irradiance volume holding a spot gives its diffuse light

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Per fragment, the smallest light probe holding it gives its diffuse indirect light: a reflection probe (a room's, D320) inside a larger irradiance volume (the forest's sky occlusion) wins over the volume; a volume smaller than every reflection probe there still wins, and outside every reflection probe nothing changes. A fragment no irradiance volume holds is lit by the environment map, as with no volume in the picture (`irradiance_volume_light_held`; Bevy 0.20 left it without diffuse light whenever any volume was in the view's frustum, so facing the den's volume darkened the whole land, 2026-11-09; held by `tools/sandbox/turnlight.ts`). Every 3D view holds a list of irradiance volumes, empty or not, so the volume path is one permutation fixed for the view (`LightProbeComponent::HELD_BY_EVERY_VIEW`; Bevy 0.20 set the view's `IRRADIANCE_VOLUME` key and layout bit only while a volume was in its frustum, so the first turn that lost the den's volume drew two wholly black frames while every pipeline was made again, 2026-11-10; held by `tools/sandbox/turnflash.ts`). A volume whose box takes in open air holds the open sky's whole light there, so what stands in the open is lit the same in the box and out of it (the den's, `den_light::Whose`: its cells with nothing overhead and the rock under them alone; a roof's rock the open sky's on the sides facing up). A volume says how much of the sky arrives, the environment map what the sky's light is: a volume's cell is an ambient cube and the map the sky's harmonics, which agree only for a normal along an axis (a trunk read 1.05 of itself in the den's box, by direction), so the volume's light is scaled by the map's toward the normal over what an open cell holds toward it (`environment_map.wesl`'s `sky_light_over_its_cube`, each colour apart, held within a half and two; seven reads of the 8-texel diffuse cube a fragment), as Unreal's sky light is read from its own harmonics with the volumetric lightmap's occlusion and bent normal, and Far Cry 4's sky harmonics are multiplied by a baked sky occlusion; held by `tools/sandbox/volumelight.ts`. Where a volume holds a fragment, the view's environment map's share of its reflection is occluded by the volume's light against the open sky's (`sky_reflection_seen`, D336); a room's reflection probe's own map is not. A probe is a unit cube in its own space, so how small it is is the determinant of its world-to-probe transform. The change is a patch of `bevy_pbr` 0.20.0 (`patches/bevy_pbr`, `patches/README.md`, as D184 allows): `irradiance_volume::smaller_reflection_probe_holds` and the volume step of `pbr_functions.wesl`'s `apply_pbr_lighting`, marked `Terra's patch (D485)`. A view holds up to 64 light probes of each kind (`MAX_VIEW_LIGHT_PROBE_SLOTS`, the uniform and `mesh_view_types.wesl`), sharing Bevy's 8 texture slots: a probe whose cubemap would be a ninth texture is skipped, and closed rooms share one cube pair, so a dungeon's rooms past the 8 nearest keep their own light. The air round the camera is lit by the sky as far as its room lets it in (`terra_world::scene::IndoorSky`, 1 outdoors, set by `terra_bases::light` from the room boxes round the eye and eased over a probe's fade): the haze's sky light and the distance fog's colours are dimmed by it. A shader that reads a volume itself asks whether one holds the fragment (`irradiance_volume_light_held`'s weight), never whether `IRRADIANCE_VOLUME` is defined: a leaf's light from above is the environment map's toward up outside every volume and the volume's, scaled as the lit pass scales it, inside one; a trunk on snow is spared its own sky's dimming only inside one; shaded snow's tint takes the same scale.

## Why

Bevy 0.20 takes diffuse light from an irradiance volume first and from reflection probes only where no volume is in view, so once the lush forest's sky volume was in view every closed room under it was lit by the sky: a dungeon's torch-lit corridor measured 0.168 mean brightness at noon and 0.150 at night, its room probe (dimmed to what the room's openings let in, D486) unused. The light probes live in Bevy's clustered lighting, with nothing of ours beside the shader's choice to extend. Shipped engines let the smaller, more local probe win: Unreal's reflection captures (smaller ones override larger ones, its Reflection Environment documentation) and Godot 4's ReflectionProbe, whose ambient replaces the scene's inside its box (`ambient_mode`). Blending by the probe's falloff weight, as Unreal blends captures, is left until a room's edge shows a seam.

## Measured

`node tools/sandbox/indoorlight.ts` on the crypt (seed 7), noon against midnight, mean brightness: corridor 0.168/0.132 (21% apart) before, 0.149/0.134 (10%) after; room 0.038/0.007 before, 0.032/0.006 after; the forest view 0.286/0.008 before, 0.294/0.007 after (the same to the eye). Not yet enough: Bevy keeps only the 8 visible reflection probes nearest the camera (`MAX_VIEW_LIGHT_PROBES`) and the crypt has 168 room boxes, so what lies past them still takes the sky volume; next, more probe slots sharing the 8 textures.

Round 5: 64 probe slots moved the numbers little (corridor 0.147/0.133, room 0.029/0.003). The forest's haze and fog in a closed room were a flat green veil over the corridor at noon, gone at night; dimmed by `IndoorSky`, the corridor's darkest twentieth at noon fell from 0.054 to 0.023 and the veil is gone (corridor 0.096/0.127, room 0.029/0.003). Still failing, for two reasons left: the room view stands at the stair's foot, whose room the sky reaches down the stair (`sky_seen` casts against the ground, not the dungeon's own cubes, so a stair's roof does not shade it); and doorway jambs lie in no room's box (an opening is part of its wall, D155) and take the sky's volume, green by day. Opening the eye eight stops indoors rather than five changed nothing, so the corridor's noon-night gap is not the exposure's band.
