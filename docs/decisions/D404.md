# D404: A face's gullies come in several sizes, its tiers stand in the light, and talus aprons lie under its walls

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D403 and D395. A far wall's face varies in its gullies' sizes and its tiers' light, not only in its tones:
- **Gully depths** (`Mountains::gully_depths`, 1 for the starting realm). Each gully between two ribs is cut to its own depth, from a quarter of the deepest up, most of them shallow and a few deep, in the mesh (`Range::stripes_by`) and in the ground shader's far ribs (`fall_stripes`, from a third up). It is whole at the ribs, so the ground never jumps.
- **Wide gullies** (`Mountains::wide_gullies`, 1). On some faces, about 1.3 km across, the coarsest set's gullies are blended with a second set 2.2 times as far apart and 1.5 times as deep, from cells of their own, and the middle set is fainter there. The blend runs over a narrow span of the face noise. A spacing scaled smoothly across a face would sweep the stripes' phase, kilometres from the centre, into a moiré.
- **Slabs** (`Mountains::slabs`, 1). On some faces, about 1.9 km across, the two coarser sets are cut to a tenth and a half, a broad unbroken sheet of rock between fluted faces.
- **Tiers in the light.** Cliff tiers of different heights, about 95 m on average: the riser's normal is turned toward the level and drawn darker, and the bench on top, a fifth to a third of the tier, turned up. The beds dip 4 to 9 degrees, the dip turning over kilometres. Only some tiers stand out, in runs some hundreds of metres long, broken where a great couloir cuts through. Snow and turf lie on the benches in patches. They fade by the height a pixel spans, from 18 to 40 m. D403's ledge, fading by 3 to 9 m, was all but gone at 3 to 5 km.
- **Talus aprons** (`Mountains::aprons_m`, 80 m). The band at and below a wall's foot, from 0.28 to 0.64 of the climb from the valley to the crest, is raised by up to 80 m. There the gullies are filled to a fifth of their depth, and the ground between the coarsest set's lines is lowered by up to 40 m, so the lines stand as cones. The land lays scree where the apron's slope and the cliff above it say (D322). The gullies only cut down from the ridged height, and nothing on an apron rises near a crest, so `highest_m` holds.
- **Snow.** Far snow's outcrop breaks are fewer from 2,000 to 2,500 m and where the snow lies deeper, so the highest ground keeps the most. On steep ground the vertices' snow lies from 0.12 of the way down into a gully's bed and deep from 0.4 (terrain.rs `SNOW_BED`). It was 0.25 and 0.6, but most beds are now shallower.

All of it is off round the pinned places and the ranges they keep (`Range::away_for_tops`), and the pins test sets each to 0.

## Why

The developers, on the round 2 castle rows: "i like the mountains better. the face of the mountain needs a bit of variety too". The round 3 review (3.5/10) found every face "combed top to bottom by evenly spaced parallel grooves of the same width". It found the bands "thin dashed scratches" that cast no shadow, no scree or talus at the walls' feet, and a snowline running backwards. The grooves were the stripes' own comb: each cell's two or three gullies cut alike. The bands faded out by 9 m a pixel, about 3 km off. A CPU panorama from the town showed the walls 60 to 70 degrees from crest to foot, with 3 to 6% of the seen ground in the 20 to 33 degree band a talus makes.

The pins (`fields --site`) resolve to the same places at all four, the lake at the same level, area and depth. The far readings shift: the highest ground seen from the town and the settlement is 2,277 m (it was 2,275). The town's skyline stands over 2 degrees on 69% of the ways round (it was 62%). The lake query looks at 2,398 lakes (it was 2,204), since the aprons close more hollows.

Reviewed against Alps, Dolomites, Tatras and Rockies photographs (castle valley, settlement and lake stills): 3.5/10 before and after. The one-size comb is gone, but the variety reads as soft folds and thin pale stripes, not rock: the gullies' bottoms are round, the benches have no shadowed riser under them, no talus shows, and the far snow caps look rounder.
