# D552: One command fits a new held or worn item, and its look records its source's hash

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A new held or worn item goes from its data to a checked look by one command, `node tools/characters/fit.ts <item>` ([attachment.md](../attachment.md#fitting-a-new-item)): read its data and kind; for a kind fitted by a detector with no turn given, measure the source with `glb.ts` and write the turn and grip into its data; make the look with its kind's maker, for a worn piece `fit_gear.py` and `carry_gear.py` for that item alone, not the whole body build; check it with `attach_contract.ts`; draw one workbench sheet and paint its icon again only when the look's bytes changed. Every number it finds goes into the item's data, so the data stays the one source and a refit needs no person. Each look's extras carry `source_sha`, the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its source model's bytes, which the check compares with the source as committed, so a source changed under a look not made again is found, as Unity re-imports on a hash of an asset's inputs and Unreal keys derived data by them. The controls audit builds the command; the check verifies `source_sha` wherever a look gives it, and requires it once the fitter writes it for every look. The check also measures each look against its data as it stands (a `length` fit's length and grip, a `hung` fit's `out`, a `belt` fit's height and hanging point, the `swing`) and against its item's `hold` in `items.ron` (its class one of its kind's `classes`, its length within 3%).

## Why

The attachment review (2026-10-27) measured what one new item costs: about nine steps by hand across four tools (data, `fit_held.py` or `fit_gear.py`, the check, a workbench still, the icon), with a turn found by eye from renders and a worn piece's morphs made only by the whole `build_bodies.sh`. A Blender fit takes about 5 s and is byte-for-byte repeatable, so the cost is the person's steps and the full rebuild, not the run. Tried against 41 broken copies of committed looks, the check caught 23; with the measures against the data and the hold it catches 38, and the three it misses are a tool with no hold named the wrong kind of in-hand item, and a `points` fit made from a source since changed, which only a hash of the source catches. Shipped pipelines detect stale derived assets by hashing their inputs (Unity's importer dependency hash, [importer consistency](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/assets-and-media/import-assets/import-analyze/importer-consistency); Unreal's derived-data keys) and validate each asset class on save (Unreal's [Data Validation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/data-validation-in-unreal-engine)).
