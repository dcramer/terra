# D611: Characters are composed per slot at run time from a wardrobe catalogue

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A character on Quaternius's bodies (D599) is built from a wardrobe catalogue, data the game reads, and composed by the game per slot when it appears and whenever what it wears changes. Nothing is pre-joined: a body, a piece and a look are rows, so a new piece or look is a row and a file, never code (D136).

- **How shipped systems do it** (D196). Unreal's Mutable composes a figure from a Customizable Object's child objects, chosen one a group by parameters, its Populations drawing crowds by weighted characteristics and tags ([Mutable guides, UE 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/mutable-development-guides-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.7), [Populations](https://github.com/anticto/Mutable-Documentation/wiki/Populations-(v5.5))). Synty Sidekick picks a part a slot on one skeleton ([changelog](https://syntystore.com/pages/sidekicks-changelog)). WoW's character model shows one geoset a region, the item naming which ([warcraft.wiki.gg](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Character_model)). Ours is Sidekick's shape (a skinned mesh a slot bound to the body's skeleton, as our worn gear already is) with WoW's rule of one geoset a region; Mutable's merged mesh and texture packing for crowds is later, when draws cost (research note, "What they share").
- **The catalogue**, `assets/models/character/wardrobe.json`: **bodies** (the body's file: his skeleton, sockets and clips; his head piece; a bare piece for every slot, D600; his default look), **pieces** (the source file, theirs or a cut of theirs; the body it was made on; its slot; the item it is the look of; its layer; the regions of the body it stands for and so hides; its colourways; its tags), **looks** (a piece a slot, a colourway, hair). The starting kit (D602) is the look `ranger`. **Pairings**: every two worn pieces of a body's different slots, measured through the clips and written by `tools/characters/pairings.ts`, never by hand (apart, theirs, pass or refused). Their own outfits poke too (40 to 77 mm in the roll and crouch), so a mix of two outfits is judged against the worse of the two outfits' own pairings of those slots: refused when it pokes past 5 mm in a clip where theirs stay within it, opens more edge than theirs, or pokes more at rest. Dye masks and populations are fields left for the jobs that build them.
- **The rule**, one place (`terra_walker::wardrobe::compose`): each slot (body, arms, legs, feet) shows the look's piece when its item is worn, else another of the body's pieces for a worn item in that slot, the first the matrix does not refuse with a piece already shown, else the body's bare piece (a refused pairing is never composed, and no look may wear one); the head piece shows unless a shown piece holds the head's region (their UpperBody, the bare arms, has the head too). Every region of the body is then drawn exactly once. Until populations come, a character with no equipment of its own (the town's people, the sparring partner) wears its look whole, as they looked before; the player's follows what its equipment holds.
- **The game's files**, one build step a body (`build_bodies.sh`, `wardrobe:<body>`): the body's file with no mesh (`models/character/male.glb`, the fight's clips sampled on his skeleton, sockets and lift read off his default look), and each piece as a glTF of its own beside its shared textures (`models/character/male/<piece>.gltf`), so one texture loads once however many pieces and people use it.
- **Held by**: `node tools/characters/wardrobe.ts` (the catalogue whole, every pairing of worn pieces measured, every game file there, each piece theirs untouched or an exact cut by `intact.ts`, each piece's bind poses those of the body's skeleton, and every look in every state of undress composed by the game's rule with every slot filled or bare and nothing drawn twice); `terra_walker::wardrobe`'s tests.

## Why

The developer, 2026-11-02, approving the pipeline [the research](../../research/2026-11-02-character-pipeline.md) proposed: "Alright we need to test this out and ensure we have a clean pipeline. We should do what the best games and engines do but in a way that makes it possible for you (our agents) to succeed". The research found the gap was a catalogue, not tools: the man in the game was one pre-joined Ranger file, so equipping drew nothing on him and every man in town was the same Ranger. Rows an agent adds and a check proves are what lets agents add bodies, pieces and looks without hand-joined files.

## Log

- **2026-11-03**: D611 decided (by the agent, char-pipeline j1): the wardrobe catalogue as data and the game's per-slot composition; the pre-joined `male-ranger.glb` goes.
- **2026-11-03**: D611 amended (by the agent, char-pipeline j2): the pairing matrix measured by `pairings.ts` and written into the catalogue, a mix judged against its two outfits' own pairings; `compose` never composes a refused pairing (the next candidate for the slot, else bare) and no look may wear one. Why: their own outfits poke 40 to 77 mm in the roll and crouch, so no absolute bar passes them, and the brief asked that the game and later populations never compose a pairing that pokes.
- **2026-11-05**: D611 amended (by the agent, char-pipeline j3): optional slots (`optional`: headwear, hair, beard), which may be empty and have no bare piece, composed after the four in order: the look's piece when worn (hair and beards, the person's own with no item, always), else another worn, never one whose region a shown piece holds (a hood holds the hair, the armet the hair and beard), else nothing; the matrix measures hair too and skips two pieces holding one region; `outfit` may name two outfits (the Knight's arms, legs and feet are the Knight Cloth's). Why: the hoods, helmets and hairstyles of their packs have no bare piece to fall back on, and a hood over hair would draw the hair through it. The wardrobe check also opens with the guard on their parts (vendored, private, reshape; D599).
