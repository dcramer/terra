# D515: The ground's edits are found by a hashed grid; the operation list stays the truth

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

A planet's edits (digs, fills, dug boxes and the bridges between them) stay the ordered list of operations D21 saves and syncs; beside it `terra_voxel` keeps an index by place (`planet/index.rs`): a uniform grid of 4 m cells hashed by cell (`terra_gen::hash::QuickMap`), each edit numbered in the order it was laid and kept in every cell its reach (its size and the 2 m margin) overlaps, those over more than 512 cells in a list looked at every time. A sample of the ground lays over the generated field only the edits in its own cell, in order, and the fills that could still lower it there: a fill only lowers the ground, so one whose sphere is no nearer the point than the most the ground can be there (the field raised by the digs that reach it) is left out. The answer is bit for bit that of laying every edit (a test over random overlapping digs, fills and boxes compares them). Adding an edit touches only its cells; a bridge taken away is crossed out; nothing is rebuilt.

## Why

Every sample scanned every edit (about 7 ns each, handoff `controller-stage2.md` Unit F2), and the mover asks the ground a few hundred times a body a tick: on the 2 km planet with 10,000 edits a body cost 4.8 ms a tick against 13 µs undug (`cost.edits`). A uniform hashed grid is the textbook broadphase for many small objects of like size (Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection 7.1; Godot 3's `BroadPhase2DHashGrid`, MIT, keeps large objects apart the same way), and the order kept is Voxel Tools' modifier stack's (MIT): modifiers laid in stack order, culled by bounds. Cached baked bricks (Voxel Tools, Dreams) are the next step if dense diggings make the edits within a cell many; the operation list remains what is saved either way (D21).
