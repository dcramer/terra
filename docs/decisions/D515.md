# D515: Clouds are volumes drawn from the climate's weather map, ported from Bevy's atmosphere clouds

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Clouds are drawn as every shipped real-time cloud system draws them (Horizon's Nubis, Frostbite, Unreal, RDR2): a shell of air ray-marched through a weather map times a height profile per cloud kind, eroded by 3D noise, lit by Beer's law, a forward-scattering phase and multiple scattering, at reduced resolution rebuilt by temporal reprojection, with a cloud shadow map that shadows the ground and the haze, and the sky light following the cover (from the CIE's clear sky toward its overcast). The weather map is the climate's cloud, kind and precipitation out to the horizon (D518), the base from temperature and humidity, moved by the climate's wind at cloud height and by the world's clock, never a machine's run time, so every client draws the same sky (D52, D315). Clouds stand at their true heights over the ground but spread in the air's frame (D212, D334). The eye goes inside and above cloud from the first volumetric stage (D519), as in Horizon Forbidden West (Nubis Evolved, 2022; Nubis Cubed, 2023) and Microsoft Flight Simulator: the march starts at the eye rather than at the shell, steps shorten and the high-frequency detail sharpens near the eye, empty air is leapt on the coarse distance field, cloud round the eye is fog lit as the cloud is and blended with the haze, and the world's objects inside cloud are fogged by the depth buffer. A heightened storm (D522) is data on the cumulonimbus kind's density, depth and base and on the storm cells' strike rate, never a change to the lighting. The code is ported in parts from the open Nubis-based clouds on Bevy's atmosphere (Máté Homolya's `volumetric-clouds-nubis` branch, under Bevy's MIT or Apache-2.0) into terra_world, with no patch to Bevy: a half-resolution compute march per view after the depth prepass and before the main pass, as Bevy's SSAO runs (`sky/volume.rs`, `sky/volume/render.rs`, `cloud_volume.wesl`, GPU time span "clouds"), laid over the scene by a veil; its march, its coarse distance field and its time-sliced cloud shadow map are ported, not the branch whole, which costs too much inside cloud (stage 0); the march is cut where it costs (fewer, longer steps inside cloud and far from the eye as Nubis Evolved, the cloud round the eye as fog), from Nubis's talks where the branch does not fit, upstream's when it lands. Budget: 1.5 ms at the high preset on D25's target, 2 ms on the integrated GPU at the low preset, inside cloud as well as under it; the low preset otherwise keeps a 2D layer on the same weather map, with fog standing in for the inside of a cloud. See [sky.md](../sky.md).

## Why

The developer, 2026-10-26: "we'd like to have things like volumetric clouds and realism for weather, but in general to have it look 'real'"; "clouds wouldn't be in the skybox though they'd be in.. like in the sky itself". The method is the industry's, unchanged in its bones since Horizon Zero Dawn (about 2 ms of a PS4); the Bevy branch is that method on the atmosphere we already draw, with a path-traced reference, RDR2's time-sliced shadow map and Nubis Cubed's distance field, so porting it beats writing our own (D196). It is unmerged and on a newer Bevy, so it is measured before anything builds on it ([research](../../research/2026-10-26-volumetric-skies.md)).

Revisited 2026-10-26 after the developers answered D516 to D522: none changes the method. D519 (flown through from the start) moves the camera inside and above cloud into the first volumetric stage, which costs most in every engine (close detail, cloud round the world's objects, fog inside cloud); Forbidden West and Flight Simulator show it fits a frame, but whether the Bevy branch handles the eye inside its layer is unknown. D522 is data on the method. Still Leaning: the developers have read sky.md, but the code's source is untested and D519 raised what it must do; it is Decided when stage 0's spike measures the branch below, inside and above cloud within the budget.

Revisited 2026-10-26 after stage 0's spike ([research](../../research/2026-10-26-volumetric-clouds-spike.md)): the branch looks photographic, but on the RTX 5090 its clouds cost 1.1 ms under cumulus and about 2 ms in and above the layer, over the 3080's whole 1.5 ms on a card some 2 to 2.5 times faster; only the overcast deck (0.75 ms) is near it. Most of it is the march, whose step budget rises elevenfold for a ray inside the layer; it already traces a sixteenth of the pixels a frame. So its parts are ported and the in-cloud march cut, rather than the branch taken whole. Still Leaning: the cut is untested; it is Decided when stage 2 measures it within the budget on both GPUs, under, inside and above cloud.

Revisited 2026-10-27 after stage 2's first measurement: the layer is built in terra_world, not in patches (the text above says so now), because a compute pass between Bevy's prepass and main pass needs nothing Bevy does not already expose, and a patch would have to be carried through every Bevy upgrade (D184). On the RTX 5090 at the high preset, 1920x1080, noon, the "clouds" span costs 0.43 ms under fair-weather cumulus and stratocumulus and 0.63 ms with the eye inside cloud (0.01 ms with the march returning at once; the GPU's frame 2.38 to 2.80 ms and 0.79 to 1.44 ms), against the branch's 1.1 and about 2 ms. At stage 0's ratio of 2 to 2.5 between the cards, that is about 0.9 to 1.1 ms and 1.3 to 1.6 ms on the 3080, inside the 1.5 ms under cloud and at its edge inside; the low preset keeps the 2D layer, so the integrated GPU pays nothing for it. Still Leaning: the 3080 and the eye above cloud are unmeasured, and the eye inside cloud is at the budget's edge before the checkerboard and reprojection (a quarter of the pixels a frame) are ported.
