# D539: A dungeon's rooms are dressed as room types, each a hero prop, wall props, floor clutter and a story vignette

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Design · By: — · Status: Open

Proposal ([dungeons.md, room types](../dungeons.md#room-types-and-their-dressing-d539-open-a-proposal-for-the-developers)): each graph role maps to a room type with a story (antechamber, ossuary passage, robbed tomb, guard post, ossuary, treasury, chapel, the lord's chamber, a delvers' camp), and each type is dressed as level artists set dressing out: one focal hero prop on the wall that faces the way in (or the room's feature, which is its hero), wall props in rhythm along its bare walls, floor clutter in its corners, and a vignette, a few props laid together that tell what happened there. The types, their sets and the rules are theme data (`dressing` in a theme's `.ron`), the placement code (`stamp/furnish.rs`) knows no room type by name.

## Why

The developer, on the review desk (2026-10-26): "the newest is good, i want the actual dungeon to make more sense, be rich, use more things, like chests, more interesting shit, generally speaking". Shipped crypts make sense by environmental storytelling (Smith and Worch, GDC 2010, "What Happened Here?"): a room's purpose and what happened in it read from its props, a hero prop to look at first and clutter to find. Skyrim's Nordic ruins (Burgess and Purkeypile, GDC 2013) lay that clutter over a modular kit, as ours is. Which room types the crypt has, and what each holds, is design: the developers' call; this round builds the mechanism and a first set, as the developer asked for more things and chests.
