# D606: One table owns the game's threads and each kind of work's share of the cores

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Every thread the game starts takes its count from one table in `terra_gen::par` (Bevy-free, the lowest layer that must know it, D198): the main and render threads keep their cores, foreground compute takes the rest, background work (the far view's streaming, D603, the far cards, building's layouts) at most a quarter of the cores, IO a small fixed count. `terra_world` sets Bevy's `TaskPoolOptions` from it and builds the streaming and building pools from it; `par::map`, terrain's first view and every `std::thread::scope` site in the game take their thread count from it, and a thread of a background pool works its items alone (`keep_to_this_thread`, as now). A test sums the pools against the cores, and the one-definition lint fails `available_parallelism` outside the table (tests' sweeps, D406, keep every core). Godot does the same with `threading/worker_pool/low_priority_thread_ratio` (0.3), the share of its pool background tasks may hold; Unreal ranks foreground and background tasks on one set of workers (`ETaskPriority`). [research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md](../../research/2026-11-03-performance-systems.md#4-proposals-ranked).

## Why

On 2026-11-02 D603's streaming pool took 14 of the bench's 16 cores and the far cards' task fanned out over all of them through `par::map`: lake-play's main thread went from 6.5 to 15-22 ms, 755-800 hitches, five hours on main and three jobs to find (perf-regress j1); building's pool was made earlier for the same want ("Bevy's pools have none, so building has a pool of its own"). Each pool sizes itself as if it were alone, some fifty threads on sixteen cores if all ran at once. Leaning until a job builds it and lake-play, town-dusk and forest-run hold D25 on it.
