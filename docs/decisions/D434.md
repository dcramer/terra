# D434: A far wall keeps a third of its own light before it gets more detail

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Work on far and middle-distance mountains (1 to 5 km) is measured before it is reviewed. The measure is the rock's local contrast and edge density in fixed crops of the lake's massif and the valley's walls: high-pass std over the mean, and the share of pixels off a 1.5 px blur by 6% of the mean. The changed pixels are counted too.

Two rules follow:
- **The air first.** The ground shader's air (aerial perspective, foot mist, then Bevy's distance fog) must leave a wall's foot (within 80 m of the eye's height) at least a third of its own light at 2 to 3 km by day (at dusk the developer keeps the haze, round 26), before any more surface detail is built for those views. A test on a CPU twin of the shader's `through` holds it, as D432's dusk sky has one.
- **Then the folds.** The next step is the shape's occlusion: how open a far vertex's ground is within 40 and 120 m, against its own plane, dimming its sky light and a share of its rock's colour, with snow left out. It follows Battlefield 3's occlusion in the far terrain's colour and Gaea's Occlusion mask. It is kept as a patch in the mountains r23 folder until then.
- **The sun's shade goes with the sun.** A far wall's darkenings that stand in for faces turned from the sun (the far wall's and far dim's, the aspect's shade tint) fade with the direct light's strength, as the far snow's do: at dusk the snow went undimmed while the rock kept them, near-black under snow about 22 times as bright in linear light, and read as unlit (mountains rounds 4 to 6).

## Why

Five shape rounds (D426, rounds 18 to 22) gained tenths in review, and the last changed under 0.4% of the pixels. This round built the fold occlusion and rendered it twice against main ad930678 (the [far mountain detail note](../../research/2026-10-21-far-mountain-detail.md)).

The walls' sky was dimmed by about a quarter on average, yet only 0.04 to 0.2% of the pixels changed by more than 3%. The lake's low rock lost contrast (hp 0.053 to 0.038 at 15 s), because the folds hold the snow and dimming them dimmed it.

The shader's own constants say why. `AERIAL_M` 2400 m and the foot mist's 900 m under 80 m over the eye leave a wall's foot:
- 13% of its own light at the lake's 3 km at dusk;
- 7% at the valley's 2 km by day.

Real clear air leaves 70 to 85% at that range. So the shape and shading work of six rounds was hidden by the haze, not lost in the mesh. Any change to the surface shows at a tenth of its size until the air is thinned.

## Round 24

The candidate air is a clear day's: aerial perspective over 9 km (Koschmieder visibility 35 km) and the foot mist over 4 km (11 km inside it). It leaves a wall's foot 60% of its light at the lake and 53% at the valley, and a CPU twin of the shader's `air_through` holds the rule. A blind A/B review preferred it in all four views: the lake from 4.5 to 5.0, the valley from 4.6 to 5.8 and the settlement from 4.7 to 5.8. The rock's local contrast rose 1.5 to 2.3 times. It changes how every far view looks, so it waits for the developers on their desk, as branch `mountains-r24-thin-air` with patches in the mountains r24 folder.

The folds, with their snow left its sky, changed 0.03 to 0.06% of the pixels under main's air, and the blind judge saw no difference in any view. They are not landed alone; they ride with the air.

## Round 26: clear by day, hazy at dusk

The developer chose the thinner air by day and the thicker haze at dusk (2026-10-22): "on the lake run the better ones are the left ones, hazier ones, on the settlement one, i think the thinner is better." So the air follows the direct sun's brightness at the eye (`day`, the shader's `DIRECT_FAINT` smoothstep, which falls as the sun sinks and is the moon's after sunset). The aerial perspective's depth blends from 2400 m at dusk (`AERIAL_DUSK_M`) to 9000 m by day, and the foot mist's from 900 m (`FOOT_MIST_DEPTH_DUSK_M`) to 4000 m. At dusk the air is main's before round 24; by day it is round 24's clear air. The CPU twin tests both ends: by day a wall's foot keeps a third of its light at 2 to 3 km; at dusk at most a sixth at 3 km and three-tenths at 2 km; and the air clears steadily as the sun brightens. The far wall's fold occlusion (round 23) rides with it. A dawn scene (the castle at 06:06 at the equinox) may still be hazy while its sun is low.

## Round 30: the low sun

At a low but bright sun the air before a far range is brighter and takes a little of the haze's hue (`LOW_SUN`, `LOW_SUN_AIR_LIFT`, `LOW_SUN_HUE` in ground.wesl): haze depth barely moved the settlement's far wall (r29), its brightness at the hour does. The day and dusk depths above stand.
