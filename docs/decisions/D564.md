# D564: A tool carried by the rule tips at the wrist, little in the fist, its forearm kept down

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Superseded for the one-handed tools (2026-10-30, D584's holding as an overlay from a clip): the hammer, the axes, the pickaxes, the knife and the arming sword are carried in the `Carry_Tool` pose (their `carry_hold`, D560's carry poses), their arm leaning with the chest, not tipped by the rule, so nothing here moves them. What follows holds only for the looks still carried by the rule, the spear and the bow, and is deleted with `carry_hand` once a long-tool carry pose takes them.

A look in hand carried by D546's rule takes no more than `OBLIQUE` (15) degrees of its tilt in the fist, turned about the socket's X as before; the hand turns the rest at the wrist about the same axis (its ulnar deviation), so the look points as the rule says while its handle crosses the palm nearly square. While the look is carried, the elbow opens in its own bend's plane until the forearm is at least `FOREARM_DOWN` (45) degrees below level, never past 10 degrees short of straight, so the tilt, which is laid on from the hand, still points the working end forward and down when a jog or sprint swings the forearm up. Each eases in with the tilt (`Held::carried`). It runs after the clips, the holds and the fit and before the fists and the arms kept off the hips (`terra_walker::carry_hand::tip_carried`, a pose layer, `pose.rs`); its twin `tools/characters/carry_hand.ts` lays the same over the carried clips in `moves.ts --carry`. Measured by the move check's "fist" row: `fist_wrap_deg.<look>` (grip.ts `wrapOf`, bar 80), `carried_rise_deg.<look>`; the criteria `held.wrap.axe_carried`, `held.fist.axe_carried`, `held.carry.axe_down`.

## Why

The developer's still of the axe carried at a jog (2026-10-28): the handle lay under bent fingers, not in a closed fist, and the axe came up level, pointing ahead. The fist row passed (0.6 cm) because it measured how near the bones come, not how far round: the rule's 45 degrees, turned all in the fist about the palm's middle, ran the handle slantwise from under the index's tip to under the heel of the hand, the index 25 degrees round it, the ring 46. A real power grip holds a handle only a little slantwise and points a tool with the wrist, whose ulnar deviation reaches some 45 degrees (Kapandji, "The Physiology of the Joints", vol. 1). Why 15: the slant takes the handle away from the little finger's knuckle, which must bend further to reach it, 86 degrees square, 106 to 109 at 15, past its 115 at 19 to 20 on the male, heavy, strong and thin hands, where it missed and curled into the palm (21 degrees round); the base's and the female's smaller hands still reached (105, 108). Less in the fist is more at the wrist, which swings the fist back: at 10 a carried pickaxe went 1.3 cm into a heavy body's thigh at the jog (1 cm at 12, clear by 0.2 at 15, by 3.5 with all 45 in the fist). At 15 every finger of every template wraps 95 degrees or more through every carried clip.

The rule tips the look from the hand, so it points down only while the forearm hangs: the jog's forearm runs from 83 below level to 23 above, and the handle rose with it, to +37 degrees at 0.53 s. No wrist fixes a level forearm (a fist-held handle cannot point down from one), so the forearm is kept down. Shipped games carry a one-handed tool on its own arm overlay with the swing damped (ALS's overlay layers and their arm curves; docs only); opening the elbow in its bend's plane keeps the clip's shoulder swing. Before, with all of the tilt in the fist and the forearm left: the axe's highest +37 degrees at the jog, +40 at the sprint, +55 in the talking idle's gestures. After: the jog's highest 29 degrees below level, the walk's 27 below, the sprint's 14 below, the talking idle 1 above, a jump's start 7 above as both arms swing up; the spear, carried at 32 degrees, from +66 to +14.
