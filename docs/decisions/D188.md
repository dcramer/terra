# D188: Texture budgets for characters and gear

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Source art keeps Tripo's maps as made (4096: colour, normal, roughness and metal). The game's files take each kind of asset at its budget: a body's maps at 2048, a worn item's at 1024, scaled when the build writes them (`--textures=` in `build_bodies.sh`). Garments cut from the body bake the dressed model's normals and roughness as well as its colour.

The GPU gets cooked textures: an offline bake, `cargo xtask cook` (`terra_bake`), turns every model's pictures and the ground's, flora's, foliage's, litter's and barks' materials and the fire's flipbooks into block-compressed KTX2 with their mipmaps, kept in the user's cache (`~/.cache/terra/cooked`) under the XXH3 of the source's bytes and the bake's version (`terra_core::cooked`), never in the repository. By kind: colour BC7 in sRGB, a normal's or a motion's two components BC5 (the shaders make z from x and y), other data BC7, a map of one channel BC4; colour cut out by its alpha keeps the game's coverage-keeping mip chain (`terra_core::cooked::mips`), so leaves do not thin. A side not a whole number of 4-texel blocks is resampled to the nearest that is. The fire's flipbooks are cooked without mips, the shaders sampling an atlas's top level. A prop in `builds.ron` whose largest side needs fewer than 1024 texels at a metre from a 1080p, 55° camera has its maps capped at 1024. The game reads a cooked copy where one is there for those very bytes and the GPU samples BC, as sRGB only where it is colour (Bevy's KTX2 reader takes that from its settings, not the file); otherwise the source, as before, so a fresh checkout runs without the bake. `cargo xtask play` (and `yard`) runs the bake first and, under WSL, copies what is cooked to the Windows build's own cache, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\terra\cooked`, a file only where it is missing: nothing ran the bake before, so the developer's game drew every picture uncooked, mipmapped on the CPU at load (debug build, the developer's save: load 29.5 to 25.1 s, the GPU's peak 4,107 to 1,856 MiB). The encoder is `ctt` (MIT, Apache, zlib): bc7enc-rdo's bc7e for BC7 and Intel's ISPC compressor for BC4 and BC5, the best open encoders, behind one maintained library that writes KTX2 with zstd.

## Why

Realistic characters (D186) get their look from the normal and roughness maps, not the triangles ([research/realistic-building-textures.md](../../research/realistic-building-textures.md)), so the maps are kept. At 4096 one body's three maps take about 250 MB of GPU memory uncompressed, and the body file grew from 7 to 13 MB. Shipped games keep source art large and set budgets per kind of asset.

The cook is how shipped engines do it: Unreal cooks textures offline into the platform's block formats and keeps them in its derived data cache under a key of the source and the settings; Unity and Godot import into the same kind of cache. "Cut Terra's texture memory ... as shipped engines do." Measured (`terra_world::gpu_memory`, the settlement run to frame 700): textures were 3,485 MiB, 3,124 of them uncompressed RGBA8, of 8,640 MiB the allocator reserved. The bake: 563 pictures in 225 files, 3,701 MiB as RGBA8 with mips to 925 MiB, 20 s on 10 cores, 1 s when nothing changed. The same run cooked (to frame 700, the same script): textures 1,580 MiB at the peak against 3,482, 1,902 MiB less, the RGBA8 of them 3,124 to 554 (1,021 now block-compressed); allocated 7,274 to 5,373 MiB, reserved 8,640 to 6,848 MiB, the card's peak 9,537 to 7,729 MiB of 10,240. The lush forest's path at eye height looks the same cooked and not (ground, bark, leaves and petals); the fire sheet holds as it did (9 to 10 fires' numbers worse than held, 11 uncooked), its campfire's yellow 19% to 12% because the ground in its crop is brighter, the ground's mips now averaged in linear light, not because of the flame maps (read as made, they measure the same as BC7).
