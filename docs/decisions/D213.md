# D213: How bases are drawn

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

As step 3 of [building-plan.md](../building-plan.md#drawing) builds it: a base is an entity and each section of its cubes, 16 a side, a child entity; every piece belongs to the section of the cube its middle is in, worked out in ticks, and a section holds exactly what the base laid whole has there, cut from it (D238). A change sets waiting the sections it marks and those its pieces reach (`sections::reach`, D281); each is laid and merged on building's own threads, as many as Bevy's async compute pool has, so an edit never queues behind the world's streaming there, nearest the camera first, a section already out, or back and not yet shown, sent again only once it is shown (as a voxel game remeshes a chunk edited while it meshes once that mesh is in) and its change held until it is back, its parts drawn again after an edit side by side on a few spare threads, a part keyed by the pieces drawn in it, those shaping what is within a cube of it (a cube shapes only the faces of the cubes beside it, a lintel's bed the stone it is set in) and the rain over its own columns, so a step built puts less on the GPU again; the base it is laid from copied for the workers in a few thousand steps, its sections', columns' and parts' cubes shared until changed; a change's meshes are put on the GPU a budget of corners a frame, past it by one mesh at most (D541), and the change swapped in once all of it is there, as a streaming world uploads a chunk before it shows it; and a section left as it is if it lays the same (`Laid::key`). A base nobody is building is drawn a detail at a time (`draw::detail`): each of a group's details (near, middle, far, hull) laid alone when the camera comes within where it is needed or will be a second ahead of its way, sent out within 1.5 workers' worth of each detail's measured cost (urgent ones first, those for later only while a whole one is afforded), put on the GPU 40,000 corners a frame, and held beside the others, each one's range stretched over any missing so they hand over by the same crossfade; a house walked past is laid once at each detail, and walked back past, not at all. Built pieces merge into a mesh per section, kind and material, long members and block surfaces sharing their corners; a block surface is worked out a cube past its section's edge, so sections meet with no seam. A plan's pieces are ghosts, a unit box or eight-sided prism each stretched over the piece, merged on the worker into a mesh for each of two ghost materials, drawn as a blueprint (each face's edges as lines, `shaders/ghost.wesl`), to 90 m; the next part is drawn as it will be, lit. Every piece as made to 25 m; to 60 m a textured mid detail, members of eight sides and stone in four steps; past 60 m one mesh a section, coarser members and stone, roofs a plain covering, its textures and their occlusion baked into its corners' colours; past 180 m coarser again, to 600 m; `VisibilityRange` fades each into the next. Finer detail is made only near the camera (full within 32 m, textured within 70 m) and dropped as it leaves; at the high preset, where the full detail casts its own shadows, a section within 14 m of the camera (kept to 17 m) is laid with the full detail alone, its middle detail made when the camera leaves, before it is seen (`Reach::Close`). A room closed and covered holds a light probe of the sky dimmed to 60% (D155; the camera is exposed for daylight, so less reads as black), seen to 60 m, every room closed sharing one reflection. The loading screen waits for the buildings in the world to be drawn. Measured by `cargo xtask bench village` against `village-bare`.

## Why

Mine, building step 3 on 2026-10-13: a cabin's redraw took 20 ms on the main thread and the whole kit sheet could not be laid at once; whole-base relayout and an entity per piece had to go. Laying by building rather than only the marked sections, with sections that lay the same left alone, is exact for what reaches across a building (a run of logs' joins, a roof's kind from its walls, a deck's boards) and still merges again only what changed. Bevy batches entities sharing a mesh and material, so a ghost per piece costs a draw per material, not per piece. Texture arrays were not needed: a section near is ten or so draws, far off one.
