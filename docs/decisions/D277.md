# D277: Wall or floor, read from the cubes

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Whether a cube is in a wall is read from the cubes round it, not from storeys of six (`Base::in_wall`): a cube not of a roof is in a wall when a whole cube, a post or an opening, not of a roof, is over it or under it; one with nothing under it is a floor where a cube of floor, with nothing over it or under it, is beside it or corner to corner with it, as a floor runs on under the foot of a wall standing on it; one alone in its column is a wall's first course where it rests on the ground and is in no slab two by two on its layer, and a walkway in the air (2026-10-15); a stair or slope set on a floor leaves it a floor. Which way a wall runs is read the same way (`Base::runs_along`). The wall tiles the base kept are gone: a storey is only the quick tools' default height, the room and wall tools reading a column of six cubes as a tile (`Base::filling`, worked out when asked).

## Why

Mine, from the goals review's finding 5 on 2026-10-14, as the lead asked: walls built by hand off a storey's layer, or storeys over a roof (D226), were read by the storey they fell in. The rule keeps what every kit draws as it was (the layout tests are unchanged), and reads a wall from layer 3 to 8 as one wall; a timber walkway a cube wide is a deck at any layer, not a log wall at one and a deck at a storey's floor (the second review's finding 6), and the plain mesh's colours read rooms by the cube. Wattle's frame is modules since D258, but `layout::frames` still puts its rails and girts at multiples of a storey (`STOREY_CUBES`), until that rule reads the cubes too.
