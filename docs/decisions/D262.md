# D262: What makes a generated log read as real

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Within D200, a log's face is one material made of two: bark lying in patches over bare wood weathered silver, as a log keeps its bark in places (`textures.ts blend`: Poly Haven's `pine_bark` at half its size, faded, over `rough_wood`, two thirds bark, in soft-edged patches a sixth of the way round and two thirds of a metre along, ragged by the bark's height, the bark proud of the wood with a dark lip), in a texture a metre round by two along, stretched along the log by the kit's `metres`; the bark lost only where a log is exposed (`--exposed`, `timber.exposed`): the texture laid once round each lying log from underneath, its top and its face to the weather (a base's fixed weather way) bare and silvered, a little of its lee face, none underneath, and the log paler toward its ends; its sawn ends real scans (ambientCG's TreeEnd002 to 005, CC0), cut out by their opacity, drawn out round, a little greyed and opened by five radial checks each (`textures.ts ends --checks`), darkened and warmed by the kit's `end_tint`, four in an atlas a log's end picks one of by its seed (a texture's `cells`); a wall's log shaded toward its grooves and lighter on top, and mottled along its length, in its corners' colours (`timber.groove`, `variation.mottle`); a knot or two a log where branches were trimmed, a low swell dark at its heart (`timber.knots`); and the daub mud, in the groove's shade, not a pale strip. Generated logs stay: the fault was the material and the shading, not the shape. Compared with a parametric log module made by a script in Blender, not a modelled log, laid on the same walls as the kit's module (`art/kits/logs`, a `log` module kind in `tools/kits`: round, uneven, lumpy, with branch stubs, D258's `timber.module`), the generated logs read better: the module's copies show a seam each metre, its texture mapped flat from three sides breaks along the log, and its ends' grain is off centre and one picture; the module stays in the kit's pieces, off. This was not the comparison D251 asks for: that is with a whole modelled log, which is still to be made and shown (D200 stays to be compared). Amended by D290 (2026-10-14): a log's face is the modelled Tripo log's bark baked round it (`pine_log`, `tools/kits/bake.py round`) (amended by D362, 2026-10-15: baked at 2,048 texels, its rows read between the bake's rather than the nearest, and the light the Tripo texture was painted in, six times darker underneath than on top, taken out round it), worn point by point to wood weathered silver (`log_wood`, the texture's `worn`, blended through the bark's low places): toward its sawn ends, on its top, and on its face out of the building it walls, the base's fixed weather way only for a wall that walls none. Which side is out is the side not within the building (`layout::enclosed`): something built over the place and something standing each way along the floor, read by column, so a window, a door or a hole knocked through a wall closes it in as the wall round it does and no log turns when one is cut (amended 2026-10-15 by the agent, for the lead: "a window or door shouldn't change which side of a wall is outside"; rooms opened at a hole and turned some 170 logs, 15 to 20 ms on one thread). Rooms stay for light and shelter (D155, D261); `log_bark` retired. Amended 2026-10-15 by the agent (D362): the face is pine bark scanned at 4K (Poly Haven's `pine_bark`, at half its size) lying in patches over bare wood (`log_bark`, `textures.ts blend`, its occlusion from its height), sharper than the Tripo bake, which is retired; the wear to silvered wood is as before. And, for the developers ("id like them more varied and we want to focus on a degree of realism per our art style"): each log its own age by its seed, from fresh brown to grey silver, its colour taken that far from the kit's warm tint and more of its bark lost where the weather gets at it; dark stains in patches along it; lichen on its weather face and top; moss in blotches up its foot; the upper courses greyed; the daub a little lighter or darker course by course (`timber.variation`: `age`, `stain`, `lichen`; `masks.moss` on timber), all pattern data. Amended 2026-10-15 by the agent, for the developers ("you can adjust the look. i dont want them to be literally grey but yeah they're a bit warm"): the wood less warm and still wood, measured against a grey reference (D370): pine logs tinted (0.98, 0.94, 0.88) from (1.0, 0.92, 0.8) and their sawn ends (0.56, 0.52, 0.47), hewn timber (0.98, 0.92, 0.83) from (1.0, 0.86, 0.7), shakes (0.96, 0.96, 0.95) from (1.0, 0.97, 0.93), so a log aged grey is its scans' own grey-brown and a fresh or sheltered one still browner; fieldstone tinted evenly (0.91), keeping its scan's own colour, where it was warmed; slate's blue-grey kept over its neutral scan.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-13: "tbh the log textures still dont look great.. kind of reminds me a bit of our old shitty logs before we added the realism". Beside photographs of real cabins the logs were too dark, one fine brown noise with no medium shapes, striped by bright daub, and capped with one clean picture; real cabin logs are silvered wood with bark left in patches, dark grooves, and greyed, checked ends, which is also how Enshrouded's rough wood reads.
