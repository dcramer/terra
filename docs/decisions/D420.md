# D420: GPU memory in pages, made once, uploaded over frames

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What the GPU holds is made at its size once and in bounded pieces, as shipped engines keep it (`shell`, `mipmaps`, `quality`):

- **Mesh slabs are pages of at most 64 MiB.** A slab starts at 4 MiB and doubles to 64; a full 64 MiB slab is never grown, another is begun beside it, as engines keep fixed-size page pools. A growth copies and batches again at most 32 MiB of meshes, a vertex layout holds at most one part-empty page, and a small layout no 64 MiB one. Replaces D283's 64 MiB start doubling to 512.
- **A model's or material's texture is made once, with its mipmaps.** `mipmaps` runs after the asset events in the frame an image arrives and changes it unannounced, so the GPU first sees it mipped; it was made without mips, then replaced the next frame, the first copy's memory left as a hole wgpu never compacts.
- **Texture uploads are spread, 128 MiB a frame** (Bevy's `RenderAssetBytesPerFrame`; Godot's staging pool is 128 MiB), not every texture of a place staged on its first frame. An update to a drawn texture is double-buffered (`uploads::swap`): it waits in its own queue, first in line, the copy on the GPU drawn until the new one is prepared, as Godot's and Unreal's texture streaming keep the resident copy bound until the new one lands. Meshes are not held back: Bevy's mesh allocator writes every extracted mesh into its slab before the cap is looked at, so holding the mesh back saved no upload and only left it undrawn.
- **The sun's shadow cascades are 2048² at High**, Unreal's default (`r.Shadow.MaxCSMResolution`): 64 MiB for the four, not 256.

## Why

"Cut Terra's mesh and allocator GPU memory." `gpu_memory` (D71) on the settlement r14 run to frame 700 (~/.cache/terra-review/vram/REPORT.md) held 8,640 MiB, of it 3,264 in mesh slabs holding 1,611 of meshes, 1,366 of holes, the shadow map 256 and 3,278 of upload staging on the first frame.

After, the same run (~/.cache/terra-review/vram/meshes/a2): 7,360 MiB reserved (-1,280), the process 7,304 on the card by nvidia-smi (8,804 before); mesh slabs 2,337 in 44 (3,264 in 18) holding 1,731 of meshes, slack 606 (1,650); holes 1,184 (1,366); the shadow map 64; the first frame's staging 598 (3,278). The run's frames over 27 ms were as before (45 reports, against 46). `cargo xtask bench town300`, the walk D283 was chosen on, on the 3080 at b27a59cc and after, both on a busy machine: median 10.31 to 8.69 ms, 99th percentile 184 to 203 (both far over budget, and as noisy), hitches 150 to 72, its meshes' slabs 1,280 to 745 MiB. The lush looks check holds the same 11 of 18 at 2048 as at 4096, its hard shade edges 40.1% against 40.7%.

The cap as first set left a re-made thing undrawn: Bevy 0.20's `prepare_assets` drops an updated asset's GPU copy before the new one passes the cap (`render_asset.rs`, "we remove previous here"), so the settlement's ground, re-made as the time-lapse wears it, went undrawn and the lake's water showed at frames 125 and 135 (with the cap lifted, ground throughout). Bevy's mesh allocator writes every extracted mesh into its slab before `prepare_assets::<RenderMesh>`, whatever the cap, and frees the old one, so the cap on meshes held back only the draw. D184 says extend first: `uploads` is two pairs of our own systems beside Bevy's `prepare_assets`, no patch to carry: updates of drawn textures taken into a queue of their own, prepared after the new ones (one a frame whatever the cap, as Bevy uploads one new one, so neither starves) and swapped in on completion; and the cap set aside while meshes are prepared. After (~/.cache/terra-review/final/settlement16/d), the ground shows at frames 100, 119, 125, 135, 150, 180, 300 and 480, the coarse tiles at 150 gone too; tests against Bevy's own `prepare_assets` with a stand-in asset hold it without a GPU.

`gpu_memory` now puts each mesh to who drew it in the frame after the peak's meshes were counted, not at the report: of the 601 MiB "drawn by nothing" before, most was grass and building sections let go between the peak and the run's end; what is left (about 210 MiB, building sections in the texture arrays and model parts) is labelled by its vertex layout.
