# D574: The climate's fronts are tens of kilometres across

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The start's climate (`assets/data/climate.ron`) makes its weather's fronts 30 km across, their eddies about 13 km, so a front's cloud shield, a deck or a clearing, spans tens of kilometres as it does in a weather model, and the sky's 500 m weather map (D540) changes little from square to square. A test holds it on a demo's 30 km world: neighbouring squares' cover differs by about 0.01 on average and the cover is half as alike 11 km off (`the_maps_cover_changes_over_a_fronts_width_not_from_square_to_square`). How long a spell lasts at a place is unchanged (`spell_days`). Every place's weather at an hour changes with it, so a demo shot on a date is shot on the day of the clock whose weather the demo's design asks for (`tools/sandbox/places.ts`): the tundra on 3 October of the clock's fifth year at 11 h, snow lying in the lees only; the lake keeps the developers' 9 June, still clear at its site.

## Why

At 3 km the cover jumped from 0 to 1 between squares 500 m apart (0.11 on average, 0.88 at the 99th percentile, half as alike 1.5 km off): noise at the map's own scale. The camera's weather, one point of the climate, said full overcast and snow falling while the cloud volume, which blends the map, drew a clear navy sky with cumulus over the tundra (tundra rounds 4 to 6), and the snowfall's haze turned the range beside the plateau into a flat white wedge against it. Real fronts' cloud shields are hundreds of kilometres; on the demos' worlds, 30 km in radius, tens of kilometres keep several fronts in a 64 km map.

On 8 October of the first year, the tundra's old date, the wider fronts bring a snowfall on the 5th and 6th that buries the plateau (26 cm open, 59 cm in the lees) where docs/demos.md §2 asks for the first snow lying in the lees; the first year has no day in early October with snow in the lees alone. A search of eight years (tundra round 7) found the fifth's 3 October: open ground bare, 9.7 cm in the lees, the fells 150 to 300 m up white, 1.9 °C and clear at 11 h. On the tundra the change took the fresh judges from 1.97 to 3.93 (p 0.010) and its looks table from 8 to 14 of 18 (tundra round 8). The lake's 9 June stayed clear (cloud 0.00 at every frame, both builds), and its reshoot (tundra round 9) held 8 of 18 against main's 9: blue/green went 0.995 to 1.029 against a bound of 0.998, the only row lost; the lake-range table held 2 of 2, far under sky 0.175 to 0.069 against 0.064; the forest's 13 and the town's 10 held. That loss is accepted: main held the row only because its cloud volume drew a grey overcast deck over a third of the lake's views, a deck its own weather (cloud 0.00) says is not there, which is the inconsistency this change fixes; under the fronts the sky is what the weather says, clear blue with a few cumulus, and the water mirrors it bluer. The row's target is itself not a clear sky: mountain-lake-day's eleven Far Cry 5 frames measure a pale sky (mean 0.73, saturation 0.18, blue/green 1.05, Morskie Oko's photographs 0.76, 0.11 and 1.06), a hazy or partly cloudy one rather than a clear deep blue, so a truly clear 9 June is held to a cloudier sky than it has (the frames themselves were cleared on 2026-10-28; the band numbers are in the reference's json).
