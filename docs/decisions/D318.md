# D318: The lush forest a tall Scots pine stand, each pine's crown pads of needle tufts

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D223, D235, D247, D273, D276, D289 and D309, for D301 (research/2026-10-14-scots-pine-crowns.md). **The stand** (`species_at`, `KINDS`): Scots pines nearly everywhere, Norway spruces in its damper hollows, oaks in the odd patch where a broad field is lowest (`OAKS_BELOW`, `SPRUCES_OVER`), about 82%, 8% and 10% of its 2,760 grown trees; pines 24 to 34 m in twelve variants (four more), their live crowns their top quarter to third (from 0.64 to 0.76 of their height, a broad one 0.7 to 0.8), standing 0.62 of `SPACING_M` apart before their knots (from 0.75); spruces 22 to 32 m, their crowns from 0.3 to 0.44 of their height, dead twigs only above 0.4 of that, bare bole below; so few oaks that their young sub-canopy (D247) is nearly gone, and broadleaf saplings a third as likely on needles. The far forest the same mix and heights, its spruce a cone from a third of its height, undergrowth at every foot. **A pine's crown** (`pine_crown`, `pad`): not whorls of four to six spokes at a height but 14 to 23 crooked limbs in loose whorls of two or three round the bole, low ones long and drooping (leaving the bole near level, sagging toward their ends), high ones climbing, each forking toward its end; each end, fork and a point every 0.7 m (`PAD_STEP_M`) along a limb's outer 60 to 70%, on alternate sides, carries a pad, eight to eleven short shoots crowded about it, fanned and most near level so a pad is a flat plate and the pads along a limb overlap into one bough, a tuft at each tip, a mass and a far lump of its own, lit from its own middle, its inner side and underside dark; the shoots inside their tufts not drawn. **Its tufts** are a card drawn needle by needle (`scots.leaves.json`, `textures.ts cards` `needles` with `stalk`, `spread`, `shoot`, `lean`, `per`), a short stalk with five needle brushes fanned from its top on bare orange twigs, their tips near one line, needles 9 to 12% of it long, so each brush stays its own spike with sky between at 40 m, about 24 px (twelve shoots of bold needles merged into one lobed blob there, a broadleaf's leaf); three cards a tuft, each 0.68 to 0.84 of its half size, turned a third of a turn apart round its shoot, as SpeedTree's needle clusters are, two cards a tuft far off as for every tree (one, grown about twice, was a clump-sized blob) (the dropping encodes how many stay, D223); a conifer's crown drops to those it keeps by 70 m, not 110 m (`NEEDLES_DROP_M`), as it is high over its bole and never seen near. **Light:** conifers' needles lit through at a third of broadleaves' (`NEEDLES`); a pine's crown hides 0.78 of the sky over it, as its pads measure (72 to 85% projected from below), not 0.62; a pine's low bole grey-brown (0.55, 0.7, 0.8), not neutral grey. Tried and dropped: four to seven long shoots fanned flat a pad read as palmate leaves; feather-shaped cards of a long stalk and side shoots, and a tuft card of fine needles, outlined as notched or rounded leaves from 15 m.

## Why

The developers chose the stand (D301): "its pretty large looming thing, i do enjoy that". Review of D309: "the pine crowns are still broadleaf-looking clumps on spoke whorls". Measured at 13:00 on the vantage views against Sons of the Forest's day frames: trunks across the middle 54% (SotF 49%, the oak wood 13%), lime at the top 2.1% (2.0%, was 7.4%), mean 0.181 (0.148, was 0.120), below 0.08 30% (46%, was 54%); sky seen looking up from eye height 13.6% (the oak wood 2.8%), under D245's 15%. On the walk bench it costs 0.2 ms more on the GPU than the oak wood (10.1 against 9.9 ms), all volumetric fog along the longer sightlines; a conifer's cards drawn whole to 110 m cost half a millisecond more for no difference measured, so they drop out by 70 m. The hitches first seen with it were the machine's (research note). Looking up into the crowns (the looks review, 2026-10-25), with pads overlapping along each limb, loose whorls and drooping low limbs, the sky inside the crowns' outline looking up at noon fell from 11.4% to 6.8% (dusk 12.4% to 7.4%, winter 16.4% to 11.3%), straight up 85% to 89% covered, for 13% fewer triangles on a near pine (14,942 to 13,047); blind-judged no different (8 judges each: noon 5.56 to 5.63, dusk 4.78 to 4.85, winter 4.96 to 4.90), at the same frame time.
