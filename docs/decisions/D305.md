# D305: Loss and repair: damage, collapse, fragments and mending

Area: Building · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

As `terra_build::loss` holds them (step 6 of [building-plan.md](../building-plan.md), D193). A cube's damage is kept beside the cubes, as what it changes to is (its 32 bits are full, D231); its health is its kind's, `toughness` in its pattern's file unless its material says otherwise (D290: block 600, coursed 400, frame 300, roof 200). Harm is an operation (`Op::Damage`) with an inverse, never refused for what it brings down, as is the ground giving way (`Op::Subside`); what then hangs from nothing is found by a flood from the change on placing's rule of joined (D199), lowest first, so a hit costs what it reaches. What comes down is gone from the base, and with it the plans that hung from it alone (a ghost hangs from nothing no more than a cube does), and given back as nothing while building takes no real resources (D193 as amended); the act says it, as it was, for the game to break into fragments (`terra_build_mesh::fragments`). How a kind breaks is data, `toughness.breaks` by its pattern unless its material says: stone into chunky irregular blocks, logs into lengths and splinters, wattle, plaster and roofs into small bits, earth crumbling, each a table of pieces, sizes, throw, spin and dust. A cube destroyed bursts at once, its pieces thrown out from where it was hit, tumbling; a cube left hanging falls whole a little after what gave way under it, the higher the later, and breaks where it lands, so a collapse comes down progressively. Fragments move on a fixed step of our own, no physics engine: gravity, bounces with friction off the terrain and the base's cubes by their shapes, rolling off ledges, glancing off walls; they touch no one. They lie still, then shrink away. A puff of dust in the material's colour where they broke and where big pieces land, and the view shaken near a big collapse, through one shared effect. Drawn instanced, a bevelled box in three shapes a kind in the texture arrays (D290); capped by the preset (2,000 at high, 1,400 medium, 700 low, with fewer pieces a cube at lower presets and past 60 m), the oldest fading first past it. A damaged cube is mended before anything more goes in or comes out, so taking apart's full refund (D193) is no way round a repair, which costs its kind's repair share of what went in, a quarter of that for each quarter of health lost. Damage is drawn by darkening and flecking the colours of a cube's surfaces by the quarters it has lost, every material alike. Until attacks (D90), damage comes from the remote and a debug key. Amended by D290: near and a few metres off, a damaged cube's textured surfaces reveal a shared damage mask in the texture arrays by the health it has lost, whatever the material: hairline cracks at a quarter, wider cracks and chips at a half, flakes gone toward the end, dark in the crack, rough, the mask's normal on its walls; at the low preset, far off, and for a pack's own pieces, darkened by what is lost as before. A repaired cube draws clean. Amended 2026-10-15 (by the agent, for the developers' "look sick"): damage breaks a material by its pattern's structure, as each pattern's file says (`damage`): a block wall at its joints, then its stones' edges, then whole stones, showing its core; timber along its grain, gouged, its ends checked; a covering a shingle at a time; the stones and shingles a texture set's cells from its height map; damage spreading a little round the cubes hit. Amended again 2026-10-15 (by the agent, for the lead's "stone reads jaggy and noisy" and "logs are still faint"): damage has depth. A stone wall's surface is pushed back over each stone gone and the mortar round it, and its points beside a hole are put on the hole's outline, as a contour is marched over a grid finer than its steps on the faces a stone has gone from alone; the outline is read between texels from a distance field kept with the cells (as decals and fonts are drawn), so a hole follows its stones' edges however near, not the grid's or the texels' steps. What a point has lost is each cube round it by its loss and how near its middle the point is, smooth from cube to cube, and more in a wall's weaker patches, so a battered wall gives way in clumps and a stone gives way whole by what was lost at its middle, the same on the CPU and the GPU. A log or beam harmed past half loses chunks out of its outline, so deep as its pattern says (`Grain` `chunks`), the fresh wood bared, and its sawn ends splinter back; its splits widen to a hand's width with torn pale lips. A covering's shingles gone show its battens and rafters. Amended again the same day (by the agent, for the lead's "logs read as melted wax" and "keep stones rigid"): each stone is judged once, by what was lost at its middle, which every texel of it finds the same (the cells keep the way to it in whole texels), and the surface marks its sunk points (their wear), which the shader draws as the hollow, so a stone is whole and flat to its face or gone, the same on screen as in the geometry; a log's chunks are cut by flat faces, an axe's V across the grain or an impact's one face slanting in along it, shaded flat with hard edges, the wood under them bared and pale, the rest of the log keeping its round; its splits are straight cracks down the grain; its sawn ends splinter into long thin spikes along it. Fragments stay cubes, as the developers asked ("explode into voxels"): a timber one is a piece of its log, its ends across the grain the sawn end with its rings, its sides bark, where it was split fresh wood, turned along the wall it fell from; a block of cubes bursts into its cubes and their pieces where it first lands; a piece on a slope steeper than about 20 degrees, a roof's, slides down it and off its eave. A stone's hole is deeper than a stone is thick (16 cm), its floor the wall's core in shadow, near black at its back, its sides plain mortar; a big fallen piece lies 8 to 10 s before it fades, a small one 2 to 3, within the fragments' cap. A texture set not yet resident is drawn plain, never in another slot's pictures, which had shown the damage mask's as black cracks on undamaged daub. What hangs comes down by rules of support (`fragments::collapse`, amended 2026-10-18; research/2026-10-18-how-structures-collapse.md): its blocks fall together from the bottom up, the cracks running up it 0.012 s a cube; a fall 8 cubes tall and 120 or more tips only when what gave way under it lies to one side of its foot (30% of its half depth) and it is 1.5 times taller than deep that way, hinged on that edge, and cracks off within its material's angle (stone 0.12 rad, timber 0.35, a third further for the stub under the third of its height where a falling body bends hardest), each block flung on and turning as the whole was and as its crack threw it; otherwise it crumbles where it stood, its shell peeling outward, faster the higher; a big fall's blocks are of its material's size (stone two cubes a side at least, timber, plaster and earth one), bigger by the budget; its dust is lit and normal-mapped. Amended again 2026-10-17 (by the agent, for the developer's "hit the castle" film, whose keep vanished and towers sank as grids of cubes): what hangs comes down in chunks of uneven size, the box round it cut across its longest side at an uneven place again and again (a k-d split, standing in for the pre-fractured Voronoi cells of Red Faction: Guerrilla and Frostbite) until each is between its least and twice that, two to four cubes a side at least by the fall's size, each split into the cubes of it that touch; a chunk landing hard cracks the same way into a few smaller chunks and a spray of pieces, down to stones, never into its cubes in a grid. Past the cap, fragments fade by worth, not age: the small and those lying still first, the big and falling last, as Frostbite's debris budget does, since a burning castle's many small falls had faded a keep's chunks as it fell. A big fall's dust comes up where and when its chunks land, a billow as big as each, lasting seconds, rolling on the way it went and up, lumpy and darker in its core, in the stone's dust colour (block's now a grey-brown), rather than a cloud over the whole fall the moment it gave way. A chunk landing within half a billow's width of one raised in the last 1.5 s joins it rather than raising its own (an emitter per area, as shipped destruction's dust is), since a billow for every chunk had spent the billows' budget on the first tower's two-cube chunks (963 of 1,335 refused), leaving the keep and the last tower none.

## Why

Mine, building step 6 on 2026-10-14, the developers having decided collapse into debris (D193) and said of the remaining work "Like get it done". Valheim's and Rust's structures take damage, collapse when unsupported and are repaired by the hammer for materials; 7 Days to Die's collapse drops rubble. Property tests over random operations hold it: nothing floats after any hit or subsidence, what is in, came out and was lost is what went in, and undo is exact (`ops/tests.rs`, `loss/tests.rs`, `tests/rules.rs`); live, a log wall's first course knocked out brings the wall down in fragments that are gone eight seconds on (`tools/sandbox/rules.ts`). Debris piles to gather were built first and replaced the same day, at the developers' word (D193 as amended). A castle tower's top cut off (`build.damage` with `build: "castle"`, Windows GPU): 2,208 cubes fall into 256 piles, worked out in 10 to 13 ms on the main thread; the piles made on a worker and on screen in 120 to 200 ms; the sections in 220 ms with frames at their usual pace, 0.65 to 1.2 s while another run loaded the machine. Damage drawn as vertex colour reads as a dark scorch on a block surface, whose triangles are few where it is flat (D284); cracks would need the material's shader.
