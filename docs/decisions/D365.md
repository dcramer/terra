# D365: Townsfolk dressed by role, sex and seed

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Serves D350's varied people, women among them. A town's people wear real garments made on the base by the character pipeline (D128): the woman's shift, bodice, skirt and apron, coif and shoes from one dressed model of the female (`art/gear/dressed-female-kirtle/`), the skirt and aprons hung from the body (`hung_garment.py`: rings round the body and what it is worn over, the pelvis's weight falling to the thighs toward the hem, as games without cloth simulation skin a long skirt), and the smith's shirt, bib apron, cap and boots from one of the base (`dressed-male-smith/`), all built by `build_bodies.sh` with the rest. A role's look (`townsfolk.ron`) says what its men wear and, apart, what its women wear (`women`, on the `female` template), each garment by a `chance` and one of a `group` (a shirt or a coat), dyed one of its colours by the seed; shoppers carry a market basket (`models/held/basket.glb`, hung from the hand by its handle, `fit_held.py`). The skirts and aprons clip in a crouch and a roll, which no townsfolk plays.

## Why

The town review found everyone alike, bald, barefoot, in four tunic colours, and no women (round 1, 4/10); Kingdom Come's townswomen read at once by coif, apron and skirt. Six pieces from two Tripo models dress every role; by chance, group and dye a few hundred looks a player can tell apart (D182).
