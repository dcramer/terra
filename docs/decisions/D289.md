# D289: Trees that hold up from below, and a pine stand that reads as pines

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D235, D247, D264, D267, D268 and D271. **Branches** are drawn as the curve through their grown points (centripetal Catmull-Rom, two rings between points on a near great limb, one on a near branch), as SpeedTree draws a branch as a spline, not straight runs with elbows; every great limb bows a little under its weight, most near its foot, a low one more, and turns up toward the light at its end; limbs, branches and conifer boughs narrow to 12 to 16% of their foot's width, then run on to a fine point nine of their own widths and at least 12 cm long, rather than a stubby cone. **Bark's reflections** are dimmed by how much of the sky the point sees, the sky occlusion volume's light (D270) against the open sky's, as Frostbite takes specular occlusion from ambient occlusion and Unreal's sky light occlusion dims reflections too: the environment map's specular is the open sky's, so limbs among the leaves shone slate-blue from below. **What a trunk's relief is read from** leaves out faces turned down: a closed model's floor made a veteran oak's solid a ring round a hollow, which a vantage camera stood in and pictured the trunk's inside as "sails". **Bark at a third-person lens** dithers out within 0.45 to 0.8 m of it, as the lens slides round trunks (D285). **Young spruces** stand in tiers a year's growth apart, each whorl its own vigour, a fifth of the boughs missing, drooping more and turning up at their tips, needles on their outer two thirds; the middle layer's young spruces keep their needles off the inner third of each branch, lose a fifth of their branches and hang a curtain on every other spray. **Snags** carry dead branches, not truncated planks: out from where each grew, a quarter to two thirds of its length, bowed down, crooked, narrowing, six-sided, snapped in a splintered point. **Pine stands** stay open: only one young conifer place in four under pines is planted (its room kept, by where it stands, so no other tree moves), and the middle layer's young spruces come up under spruces, a fifth as often under pines.

## Why

Review #5 against Sons of the Forest (trees 6/10): from below the veteran oaks "fall apart" into single-sided sails, limbs end in blunt blue-lit caps, branches are dead-straight pick-up sticks, slate-blue in the look-up; young spruces "uniform, bumpy, broccoli-like masses"; the snag's branches "straight, plank-like"; the Scots pine stand "reads mostly as spruce". Measured: the sails were the inside of a veteran's trunk, the eye 1.6 m from its foot inside its bole, its solid a ring of nine pieces round a floor at the ground; the blue was specular (taking it out took a limb from sRGB (12, 22, 21) to (13, 19, 4)), and only once the sky had been painted again: the first painting of a run gives bark no specular at all, so a run's first view and its later ones differ. After: slate-blue share of the twelve look-ups 2.4% to 0.6% (the worst 10.1% to 1.9%); a test young spruce's clumps near its trunk 22% to under 6%. Wood triangles near: a tall oak 17,000 to 26,000, a spruce 13,000 to 21,000, the level beyond a quarter more; walk bench 14.6 ms median against main's 14.5 in the same hour (main pass 6.4 against 5.9 ms on the GPU); lodcheck's forest views worst 0.11%; collide passes 26 walks.
