# D384: Ambient encounters staged to be seen

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A meeting ahead of a player on the move (D378) is staged where it shows: every 3 to 6 s, 28 to 44 m ahead and mostly within a few metres of their way, at the best of a few spots tried, weighed by being in view from the camera (no trunk, rock, log, crown or ground between), the light (the canopy's shade), bare or short ground and nearness to the way; in a group of the larger sort its kind keeps; or, half the time, a crossing: a group already put up, from 44-54 m ahead and 16-24 m aside, running or flying across the way, timed by its speed and the player's to cross 10 m ahead of them; a few met standing, the way the player faces; within a budget of its own, so the cells round a player, full, never leave nothing to meet. What follows is the animals' own: a runner that notices someone coming within two and a half times its flight distance stops and watches them (a hare sitting tight to 5 m); a shy animal startled by someone coming on breaks across their way and on ahead (four times in ten, with 6 m of lead, across to the other side), a climber to the trunk that way, a flier into the air that way, onto a crown or, in a town, a roof's ridge (`Perches`, the town's ridges), where town birds are also put out sitting in rows. Each animal is a little bigger or smaller than another of its kind and keeps its own beat at rest; a seasonal coat (`coat` in `creatures.ron`, `play::coats`) follows the date and the climate, brown in summer and white from mid-October to the end of November and back from April into May, earlier and further on colder land, each on its own days. A film measures it: twice a second of a shot, the animals drawn and not hidden (by what the world knows stands between: trunks, rocks, logs, crowns, the ground, the player, grass over most of an animal), each by its skeleton's span on the picture (`play::sighted`), summed per film by the trailer.

## Why

The review of round 1 of the biomes' life (2026-10-16) found the animals met ahead 3 to 12 px for a moment and half hidden in cover: "the systems work; the staging doesn't". Red Dead Redemption 2 and Far Cry stage ambient wildlife near, in the open, crossing the player's line of sight or running alongside, in groups, with time in frame; prey breaks at an angle to what comes at it. Real mountain hares and arctic foxes moult by the day's length, their timing and how white they go by the climate. A measure of what a film sees replaces squinting at frames (D69).
