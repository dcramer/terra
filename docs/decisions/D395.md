# D395: Far walls' gullies run long, their snow lies in the mesh's own gullies, and pinned places keep their ground

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D394 and D322. A range's gullies are laid with each cell of their stripes spanning 2.5 gullies (`Mountains::gully_stretch`), so a gully runs on down a wall for a few times its own width before the next cell's take over, rather than breaking off within its width. The vertices' snow on steep ground lies in the gullies' beds (`Rise::bed`, `GeneratedPlanet::gully_bed`, terrain.rs `lying`), so it follows the relief the mesh draws; the ground shader draws a set of ribs only past where the far mesh is too coarse to hold it (its cells about a 190th of the distance), and lays the snow in its own couloirs only there. The granite's dark streaks are laid down the fall cell by cell (`fall_stripes`). A range's crests are cut into cols and notches between the summits left standing, up to 380 m deep about 1.4 km apart, up its walls only (`Mountains::summits_m`), so no valley floor or lake moves and nothing rises past `highest_m`, whose rise would shift the climate's tables and move the tundra. The steep ground's snow is broken by patches some hundreds of metres across, the far ribs come in patches, and the granite is darker and varies over 600 m and 200 m. The places pinned in `tools/sandbox/places.ts` (D393), read from that file, keep the ground they were pinned on: within 700 m of each the front rules and the longer gullies are off, whole from 1.8 km, and the cols within 2.5 km, whole from 4 km, since they reshape the skyline over a place (`Range::with_kept`); all four pins resolve to the same places, the lake at the same level, area and shore walk. The front rules stay at 0 in the starting realm. Peaks at 0.35 keep every pin, but they change only a range's outer front, not the wall behind the castle, whose crest lies within the range (0.9% of its field map changed), and they lowered the dome over the lake. Spurs dam valleys kilometres below the lake, which floods it into a larger one, so no fade round the pin can keep it.

## Why

The developers, watching the castle-and-dragon film: "the mountain is tooo regular in the back, it needs to be fixed up". After D394 the wall's snow cap was an egg-crate of even round bumps capped with snow (review, 3.5/10). A vantage still with the shader's couloirs painted showed why: its snow followed the shader's own set of couloirs, 150 m apart, while the mesh drew the ground's own gullies, 140 m apart, from other cells; the two patterns, each breaking off within a gully's width, made round bumps, and the snow lay on them wherever the shader's set happened to fall. The dark bands across the mid-wall were the granite's streaks, a noise of `dot(world, up)`, the distance from the planet's centre: a noise of height alone.

Castle valley and lake stills before and after, reviewed against Tatra and Alps photographs: 3.5/10 before, 4.5/10 with the long gullies and the snow in their beds, 5/10 with the cols. The egg-crate and the bands are gone; left are the snow's tongues hanging evenly as a comb under a solid band, a crest with few sharp summits, an even fluting of ribs with no buttresses, cliff bands or scree, a pale flat grey, and the wall's foot lost in haze ([research](../../research/2026-10-16-far-mountains.md)).
