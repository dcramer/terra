# D395: Far walls' gullies run long, their snow lies in the mesh's own gullies, and pinned places keep their ground

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D394 and D322. A range's gullies are laid with each cell of their stripes spanning 2.5 gullies (`Mountains::gully_stretch`), so a gully runs on down a wall for a few times its own width before the next cell's take over, rather than breaking off within its width. The vertices' snow on steep ground lies in the gullies' beds (`Rise::bed`, `GeneratedPlanet::gully_bed`, terrain.rs `lying`), so it follows the relief the mesh draws; the ground shader draws a set of ribs only past where the far mesh is too coarse to hold it (its cells about a 190th of the distance), and lays the snow in its own couloirs only there. The granite's dark streaks are laid down the fall cell by cell (`fall_stripes`). The places pinned in `tools/sandbox/places.ts` (D393), read from that file, keep the ground they were pinned on: within 700 m of each the front rules and the longer gullies are off, whole from 1.8 km (`Range::with_kept`); all four pins resolve to the same places, the lake at the same level, area and shore walk. The front rules stay at 0 in the starting realm. Peaks at 0.35 keep every pin, but they change only a range's outer front, not the wall behind the castle, whose crest lies within the range (0.9% of its field map changed), and they lowered the dome over the lake. Spurs dam valleys kilometres below the lake, which floods it into a larger one, so no fade round the pin can keep it.

## Why

The developers, watching the castle-and-dragon film: "the mountain is tooo regular in the back, it needs to be fixed up". After D394 the wall's snow cap was an egg-crate of even round bumps capped with snow (review, 3.5/10). A vantage still with the shader's couloirs painted showed why: its snow followed the shader's own set of couloirs, 150 m apart, while the mesh drew the ground's own gullies, 140 m apart, from other cells; the two patterns, each breaking off within a gully's width, made round bumps, and the snow lay on them wherever the shader's set happened to fall. The dark bands across the mid-wall were the granite's streaks, a noise of `dot(world, up)`, the distance from the planet's centre: a noise of height alone.

Castle valley and lake stills before and after, reviewed against Tatra and Alps photographs: 3.5/10 before, 4.5/10 after. The egg-crate and the bands are gone; left are the snow's tongues hanging evenly as a comb, a crest with no peaks or cols, and an even corrugation of ribs down the wall ([research](../../research/2026-10-16-far-mountains.md)).
