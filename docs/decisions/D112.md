# D112: How body templates are made

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

One base body, made once and checked: one mesh (our own base male, made by Tripo) in our T-pose, our skeleton, skin weights made for it, skin patches and socket rules. A body is a few parameters on it, as modern games do: proportions as bone scales, build as a few soft morphs, the head as blends of head shapes, and a painted, tinted skin. The creator's sliders (D111) are those numbers, clamped; a template is a preset of them; an NPC is a template with offsets from its seed. A template from a design gets estimated parameters and a skin Tripo paints on the base's layout. The morphs themselves are made once each from designs of the base person (heavier, thinner, stronger, a woman), the base's mesh registered onto each. Hair is a separate part bound to the head like gear. See [characters.md](../characters.md).

## Why

Separate models each need their own rig, and gear made and checked against each, so gear costs items times bodies. Shipped games keep one skeleton per family and put shape mostly in the bones, with a few morphs copied onto every garment (Red Dead Redemption 2, Starfield, Final Fantasy XIV, Bannerlord; [modern-character-variety.md](../../research/modern-character-variety.md)). Fitting a shared mesh freely to each generated body copied its creases, broke faces and failed the rig checks (2026-10-12). Untested: whether a few parameters reach our designs, which the spike answers.
