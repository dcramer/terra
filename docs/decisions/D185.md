# D185: How performance is measured

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

`cargo xtask bench` runs named scenes (`yard`, `world`, `forest`, `forest-walk`, `village` and `village-bare` (fifty buildings on the yard, and the same view bare, D213), building at scale (`town300` walked down a street, `town300-rise`, `town300-fly` and `town300-bare`, three hundred buildings; `castle` and `castle-field`, one base of 140,000 cubes; `far`, three hundred bases out to 2 km; on the yard at a 30 km radius, cameras gliding between `--vantage`s with `--glide`), and `walk`, 40 seconds through the lush forest on a scripted `--route` with the world and walker alone, looking up into the canopy once halfway, the heaviest view), each a scripted run at 17:00, built for release and drawn offscreen at 1920x1080 with one frame in flight: 240 frames to settle, then each scene's own count timed (600, or 2,400 for `walk`), three runs, reported as the middle run's median and 99th percentile and the worst frame of any. Each scene must hold to D25's frame budget on the machine it runs on, vsync off and no frame-rate limit: its 99th percentile under it, at most 1% of frames over, no hitch (a frame over twice it), and the game's own work no more than 15% slower in its last third than its first; the task fails if one misses. The GPU's time is the passes that ran in the frame last timed, so a pass that ran once is not counted ever after; each run also says how long the render world worked on a frame and then waited for the GPU, which shows what the passes' times miss, the sun's shadow maps among them. Results are kept in [research/benchmarks.csv](../../research/benchmarks.csv); a median more than 10% slower than the last recorded on the same GPU, size and build is a regression to look into. It measures on a quiet machine, or says it could not: before each run it waits, at most 90 s and five minutes over the whole task, for the processors under 35% busy and the GPU under 15% (under WSL as Windows counts them, which includes WSL's own machine; `xtask/src/load.rs`), samples the load while the run goes, and calls a run busy if it started busy or everything but the run itself was over 30% busy on average while it ran (or 70% at once), or every hitch it had (settling or playing) ended in a collapse of the machine's CPU clock, from 0.1 s before its fall to 1 s after, the sandbox stamping each hitch with the epoch time (on Linux, the mean clock of the fastest processors it may run on read every 20 ms, of those whose `scaling_cur_freq` is fresh, their APERF/MPERF clock, rather than an idle one's last P-state, a whole 100 MHz step; a collapse a sudden fall under 40% of the run's median: the render worker's i9-14900K falls from 5 GHz to about 1 GHz now and then under load and its frames slow for ~600 ms, its power management and not the game, as Chromium's perf bots flag frequency throttling and Google Benchmark warns of CPU scaling; the run's line says so, and, where the CPU package's temperature can be read (coretemp's hwmon sensor, whose `_crit` is TjMax, or the `x86_pkg_temp` zone), its hottest in the 2 s before each collapse, saying `thermal` and to check the box's cooling when it was within 5 °C of TjMax, as the worker's was before every collapse of forest-hitch j1); it makes up to as many runs again, while the machine is quiet, to stand in for busy ones, takes its result from the quiet runs when at least half are, and says so when they are not. Each scene's line gives the runs' medians' range and a verdict, `holds` or `MISSED`, against D25's budget or, on an integrated GPU (which a run now says, with its preset), D239's median under 33.3 ms, and says when runs disagree; a median is `SLOWER` than the last recorded past 10% or the runs' own spread, whichever is more. The QA traces (D308) do the same (`tools/sandbox/load.ts`). On macOS a scripted run keeps itself out of App Nap, which slowed hidden runs threefold after 40 s. Benchmarks stream the ground as play does. Scenes stay short: a benchmark is run often. See [development.md](../development.md#measuring-performance).

## Why

Asked for by the developers on 2026-10-12: "some sort of performance testing should be well defined for us". Numbers taken before then moved with things that are not the game: on macOS a covered window, or a screen asleep or locked, draws nothing, and a Retina screen draws at 3200x1800; a debug build adds CPU time; a shell's background jobs ran at lower priority; and scripted runs waited for each chunk of ground every 4 m, a stall play never has. Offscreen, the same scene repeats within a few percent on the same machine, and runs with nobody at it. On 2026-10-16, with several agents building at once, agents reran benchmarks by hand and argued over whether a change or the load moved a number ([building workflow retro](../../research/building-workflow-retro-2026-10-16.md), item 6).
