# D546: The attachment contract: how a held or worn look goes on a body

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Every look the game puts on a character is of a kind its data names (`"kind"`: `hafted`, `blade`, `polearm`, `bow`, `shield`, `hung`, `belt`, `pack`, `garment`, the last also any skinned look with no data), each kind with a life-size range and a rule for where its grip falls along its handle (`tools/characters/attach_kinds.json`), and goes on in one of five mounts, each with a fixed origin, axes, facing, size reference and glTF extras, and is made to them once, so the game puts it on any body from its file alone ([attachment.md](../attachment.md)). Axes are glTF's (D303). Six sockets, `socket.hand.R`/`.L`, `socket.head`, `socket.back`, `socket.belt.R`/`.L`, have the same names and axes on every body, placed by `export_character.py` from its bones and skin; the hands' +X out of the back of the right hand (the left's palm), +Y along a held handle toward the thumb's side, +Z toward the wrist; the rest face as the body does. **In hand**: the grip (the handle's middle where the fist closes) at the origin, +Y toward the working end, its edges or face in the Y-Z plane and a one-sided head toward -Z, its real length; carried, it tips by a rule, not a number per item: its working end 45° forward and down, its end no lower than 0.55 m under the grip (`terra_walker::character::carry_rule`), a `carry_tilt` in its data winning over it. **Hung from the hand**: the grip under its handle's top at the origin, hanging down -Z, its long way along +Y, `"hold": "hung"`. **Strapped**: the grip at the origin, its board across Z with its front toward -Z, `strap`. **Rigid on a socket**: saved in its socket's space, its origin where it hangs from, `socket`, `morphs`, `swing`. **Skinned**: the base's joints and morph targets, in its T-pose, `covers`. The numbers a look is made from are data beside its art (`art/held/<item>.json`, `art/gear/<item>/fit.json`), read only by the tool that makes it. `tools/characters/attach_contract.ts` checks every committed look and the base's sockets in `cargo xtask check`, with today's known faults listed with why in `attach_faults.json`, which only shrinks. Extends D303 to characters and is how D128's "made once in a socket's space; held things once to the hand socket's axes" is held.

## Why

The rules lived in characters.md's prose, which no longer matched what is built: it called caps and packs rigid (both are skinned), placed a helmet's crown, a pack's panel and a quiver's loop at origins nothing uses, claimed no numbers per item (each has a turn, size and grip, now data), gave socket axes only in a code comment, and put a held thing's edge toward +Z. Measured 2026-10-27, a point 0.8 m up the right hand socket moves mostly toward -Z (away from the wrist) through the fight's clips (`Keyed_*`, `Open_*`, -0.87 to -0.90 of its motion at speed) and `Hammer_Loop` (-0.77), so a one-sided head leads there; the sword's edges serve both ways, and the Y-Z plane is the one the left hand's mirrored socket leaves alone. Shipped engines hold held and worn meshes to the same kind of convention: a weapon's grip at its origin on a named bone socket (Unreal's sockets, Godot's BoneAttachment3D, the Creation Engine's `WEAPON` node), armour skinned to the shared skeleton, app data in glTF extras. An audit found eight looks fitted by length lying 90 degrees off, six off their grip by 2.5 to 4.7 cm, and the basket without a marker of its kind; they are listed faults, the turn held for the developers' eye. The kind was then inferred from the maker's fit (a strap, `"fit": "hung"`), which told an axe from a sword from a spear not at all, so no rule could be set per kind; named in the data, each kind is solved once, and a look of the wrong size or gripped at the wrong place along its handle is caught by its kind's range (a knife left at its source's scale, a bow held near a limb's tip).
