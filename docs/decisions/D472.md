# D472: Visual A/B judging scores one build per judge, against fixed anchors

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

A blind visual comparison by subagent judges (a fire round, a look round) runs through `tools/review/judge.ts`: each fresh judge sees the fixed anchors (a shipped game's frames at a given score, say RDR2's at 9, and a fixed low one) and then ONE build's views, and scores each 0-10 with one decimal. Several fresh judges a build; a build's score is its judges' mean; two builds are compared by Welch's t (95% interval, a permutation p beside it). No judge ever sees two candidates, so position cannot favour one. Side-by-side scoring (two strips in one panel, A then B) and forced choice are not used for a verdict. A panel says before it is scored how many judges a build it has (the tool reports the least difference it can show and the judges a 0.25 difference needs); an A/A pair (one build entered twice) in the same panel is cheap and shows the panel's noise.

A strip panel (8 frames 0.25 s apart) cannot see motion, so it cannot pass or fail a lever whose aim is motion. Such a change's gate is the motion measure's targets (`tools/fire/motion.ts` against the filmed real campfires), the fire sheet passing twice, and a panel on campfire AND torch showing it is not worse (its interval not wholly below -0.1).

## Why

In fire round g3 the strip shown second scored higher in 14 of 16 judge-hours, always by 0.5, and in g2 in 8 of 12: the order moved scores three times as much as the builds did. Measured on the tool (2026-10-25, ~/.cache/terra-review/review/panels/):

- **Forced choice is position.** Side by side in one image, an A/A pair (identical images) was chosen on the right by 5 of 6 judges; main against M6 was chosen on the left by 7 of 8 (14 of 16 views). The side bias is 80-90% and flips with the layout, so it leaves almost nothing for the builds, and the image is half as sharp per strip.
- **Absolute scoring has no position and a small spread.** Judge spread (sd of a judge's mean) 0.15-0.25, 0.36-0.48 with one outlier judge in 8. A/A (main entered twice, 8 judges each): +0.11 [-0.30, +0.51], no difference.
- **Its scale is compressed.** Differences that within-judge ranking panels put at 1.2-1.3 points come out at about 0.25 here: the history panel's e9e5b733 against d0cc4051 (1.25 there) +0.28 [+0.00, +0.56], p 0.048 at 6 judges a build; the 10-21 anchor e04d47af against main's matched view +0.23 [-0.08, +0.55], p 0.13 at 6 (not shown; 16 a build needed). So 0.25 here is a large difference, and a 0.25 bar needs about 16 judges a build at sd 0.24 (23 at 0.30); a panel's own spread sets its count.
- **Corrected verdicts.** g3's M6 against main (16 main judges, 8 M6): +0.29 [+0.08, +0.50], p 0.009 (permutation 0.04): M6 better, where the side-by-side panels had it 0.125 behind. g2's M3 against main: +0.14 [-0.08, +0.36], p 0.20: no difference shown. M6 against M3: +0.15, p 0.09.
- **Strips are blind to motion** (fire round g17, panels/g17, pre-registered): one real campfire clip (Claytor Lake, CC BY 2.0) made into strips at its own speed and slowed to a third (the same 8 frames' span covering a third of the motion), 16 judges each: 8.70 against 8.64, +0.06 [-0.08, +0.20]. Real fire with three times the motion scored the same, as L3b (all four motion targets met) tied main, +0.04 [-0.06, +0.14].

Fresh judges are independent samples of one model, so a judge's offset (one scoring 0.5 lower throughout) is noise across judges rather than cancelled within one, which is why it needs more judges than a within-judge ranking; a ranking of many shuffled builds by one judge is more sensitive but is the position-biased design again when there are two.
