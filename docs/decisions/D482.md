# D482: The mover keeps bodies in f64 and asks in f32 offsets

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The mover (D53) keeps each body's position in f64 and works a tick in f32 offsets from it, as Jolt does for large worlds (`JPH_DOUBLE_PRECISION`, Jolt's Docs/BigWorlds.md): a capsule carries its frame's origin in f64 (`Capsule.origin`) and its points as f32 offsets from it; contacts live in the asking body's frame, and other bodies' capsules are put into that frame; a push is summed in f64. A source is asked at a point in f64, the origin plus the offset: the ground's field takes a `DVec3` (`Sdf<F: Fn(DVec3) -> f32>`), an obstacle answers `Shaped::distance_at(DVec3)` (a turned box exactly, others rounded to f32), and the planet's ground takes the length and the radius's subtraction in f64 (`terra_voxel::Field::distance_near`, `Planet::distance_in(DVec3)`); its land's height is still asked in f32 along the point's direction. The field's gradient takes its differences in the offset, so its noise threshold (`kink`) is the offset's, not the world point's. A body's f32 `feet` is kept for what shows it. The course frames a fixture on a world of any radius in f64 (`Frame`, `Course::on`).

## Why

Controller stage 2, unit B (2026-10-25): the lake world is 30 km across, where an f32 point is only about 2 mm fine, above the mover's 1 mm tolerance and close to its 1 cm gradient step, so contacts flickered and the sweep stepped on noise. One man walked level ground at jog for 4 s on an exact sphere: with the f32 field `|p| - R` at 2 km, 140 field calls a tick and no tick airborne; at 30 km, 528 calls a tick and 95 of 256 ticks airborne. The same field worked out in f64 from the f32 point: 146 calls, none airborne, the feet ±1.75 mm; f64 positions too: 147 calls, ±0 mm. So the field's own f32 arithmetic is most of the cost and f32 positions the rest. With these frames the mover's release bench reads at 30 km 162 calls and 13.2 µs a tick (443 and 41.9 µs before), against 153 and 11.0 µs at 2 km. Jolt's way keeps every solver and shape in f32, so only the positions and the first subtraction pay for f64. A reviewer approved the approach; the floor normal at a convex edge that exact positions exposed was fixed first (D53, unit B1).
