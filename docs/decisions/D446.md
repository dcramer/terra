# D446: The dusk lake film faces the set sun's side across the water

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Design · By: Developers · Status: Decided

The lake's dusk film looks across the water toward the side of the sky where the sun has set, so the warm afterglow (D432) stands over the far water and the water mirrors it. The water stays ruffled, as it is. The camp, the troll by its campfire, moves to the shore that looks that way. The site itself does not move.

## Why

The developer answered desk item q-lake-evening on 2026-10-24: "Face the sunset only": move the camp or turn the scene to look across the water at the afterglow, and leave the water ruffled rather than calm it. The film had run along the lake's south shore (its start's terms) toward -X, facing directly away from the set sun (D432: the sun 1.6 degrees under at 21:45 on 07-20, bearing +X turned 10 degrees toward +Z, about 298 degrees). That framing showed the earth's shadow and the Belt of Venus, never the warm band.

Where it can face, measured on the CPU (a throwaway skyline probe in the field maps' binary, 2026-10-24): from every point on the shore the range stands 19 to 22 degrees over the horizon toward the set sun itself. The warm band of D432 lies under about 17 degrees, so it cannot be seen right over the sun from anywhere on this lake. The lowest skyline on the sun's side is 30 to 50 degrees left of the sun's bearing (toward the start's -Z), at 7 to 10 degrees over 600 to 750 m of water, from the lake's west corner (about -370,-80 m east and south of the start). The film is framed from there: a slow drift past the troll's camp, looking along -35 degrees (east toward north in the start's terms), with the set sun's bearing just inside the frame's right edge. The camp is a one-off scene (D249, `scenes/lakesunset.ts`, kept with the round's files, not committed).

To get the warm band itself low over open water, the film needs another lake site or a lower ridge west of this one. Both change the land, and that is the developers' call.
