# D309: Crowns that read from below, a Scots pine's stubbed bole, bark with depth and things growing on it

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Amends D264, D276, D289 and D294. **Oaks** carry their twigs evenly along each branch's outer four fifths, each over 1.3 m long leafy along its length as well as at its end, at least 2.5 cm thick at their foot, and a card's stalk is within a third of a clump of its twig (it was up to half a clump off), so from below a crown is leaves between its branches, not clumps on hairs; six or seven cards a clump. **Scots pines** are 0.7 to 1.15 m through at the foot for 26 m (girth 1.3 to 2.2% of the height in radius, from 1.6 to 2.6%), their crowns round off rather than coming to a spruce's point, their boles carry 12 to 20 dead stubs, closer toward the crown, more than half snapped short, under four to nine dead branches, dead wood silver-grey and lighter than the bark round it; their low bark tinted grey-brown, the plates orange higher up. Tried and dropped: a tuft card drawn needle by needle on shoots off the branches' outer half; from 15 m its brushes read as lobed broadleaf leaves on bare spokes, worse than the spray cards (review, 2026-10-14). **Bark**: a grown trunk's bark blends its two lookups as D294 meant (the blend's result had been dropped, so every trunk wore one repeated, unflattened picture); a bark's picture is larger on a stouter trunk (its metres times its radius over 0.5 m, 0.8 to 1.5 times), a pine's scan laid over 3 m rather than 2; furrows relief-mapped from the scans' depth drawn out to its full range (`trunk_depth.jpg`), 8 to 9 cm deep for oaks and pines; micro-shadowing in the sun as HDRP does it (the relief-mapped normal against the occlusion's aperture, on the sun's share of the light), and the furrows half as much again occluded from the sky in the shade; a veteran's triplanar bark not relief-mapped, as its seven reads a pixel, each with a parallax search, made a veteran filling the view 16 to 17 ms a frame. **The near wood's finest limbs**, twigs and stubs, are a level of their own, drawn with the near level but casting no shadow, as a shadow cannot dither. A crown's shadow stand-in cards are 0.7 of what the clump's cards cover together, not 0.8, as its clumps overlap. **Overgrowth** is each kind's data (`lush/overgrowth.rs`): ivy, the floor's ivy cards laid on the bark from a mat at the foot up three to five vines on one side, densest low, half its leaves standing off the bark, on 35% of oaks and 20% of pines (none on spruces, whose skirts leave it no light), two variants a kind; a band of moss round the foot, all round and thickest on the damp side, its top given out in tongues up the furrows, on 40% of oaks, 30% of pines and 35% of spruces, 1 to 3 m high, carried in the trunk's mesh tag.
