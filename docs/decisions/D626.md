# D626: The clouds are lit by octaves of multiple scattering and shaped as domed towers, measured against photographs

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Under D516 (the sky photographic) and D515 (the volumetric clouds), the march (`cloud_volume.wesl`) draws a fair-weather heap as measured against nine photographs of cumulus (research/2026-11-05-cloud-look.md, the pictures in `~/.cache/terra-review/refs/clouds/`):

- **Multiple scattering by octaves**: Hillaire 2016's (after Wrenninge 2013), S = Σ bⁱ p(θ, g cⁱ) e^(−aⁱ τ), four octaves with a = 0.25 and b = c = 0.5 (a ≤ b keeps energy, by Hillaire's condition), gain 1.5 (`sky.cloud.octaves`, `terra_world::sky::volume::OCTAVES`). Under them, the diffusion's deep light, 0.75 · (1/4π) / (1 + ¾ (1 − g) τ), which exponential octaves lose. Nubis 2017's in-scattering probability darkens the edges and the bases. The f / (1 − f) series the bevy branch had stays behind `sky.cloud.octaves` 0.
- **The sky's light gathered three times** the three directions `march` samples, so the shade takes the sky's blue.
- **The water doubled** (the extinction scale 2) and **the light march's first three taps through the eroded edges**, so billows shade one another.
- **Domed towers on flat bases**: the shape noise changes 3 times slower up a heap than across it, and a heap's coverage narrows toward its own top by 1 − 0.7 (lift / top)², with `sky.cloud.heaped_cover` 0.7 giving back the cover the narrowing takes.
- **The detail** erodes to 0.75 of the body and repeats twice over its tile (finest cells 23 m).

Over the lake (views v06 and v09 at 13 h and 17 h, 1901x1550, temporal on; cloud luma p5/p50/p90, the shade's blue as tint):

The cloud steps against a supersampled reference with this look hold D617's bar: 1.04 at 3816x1550 and 0.89 at 1901x1550. It is not the finished look: an adversarial judge scored it 4.5 out of 10, a clear gain on the flat white, and named what is left (the research note).

It costs nothing measurable. lake-play at 1901x1550 with the defaults, 3 runs each, measured GPU medians of 7.96 ms with it, 7.97 ms with main's clouds, then 7.95 ms with it again; the clouds pass took 0.10 to 0.12 ms in all three (job 20261105-192323-0fe0-patch). Main's own runs were over the 7.91 ms budget on the worker that day.

## Why

The developer, on the review desk (r-cloud-history): "Those clouds look unrealistic, let's fix that". Measured, the clouds were one flat white: half of every heap at 0.96, in the tonemapper's shoulder. The cause was the f / (1 − f) gain on the column's whole depth, 10 to 100 times, so a sunlit face sent up to four times what a white surface under the same sun would. The shade carried none of the sky's blue (tint 0.05 against 0.11), and the heaps were dumplings and mushroom caps on stalks.

The method is the industry's (D196): Hillaire's octaves are Frostbite's and Unreal's, the in-scattering probability and the coverage remaps are Nubis's (Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015 and 2017), and the deep light is the two-stream diffusion's. What was tried and dropped is in the research note: a height-field dome after Nubis Cubed, which threw away the coverage cut; the detail distorted by a noise field after Nubis 2015's curl, and sharper billows, which changed nothing or ate the cover; halving the single scattering, which left p90 where it was.

The fine detail is still under the photographs' (0.007 against 0.013), but the photographs measure 0.008 when scaled to half their size and back. The march draws at half the drawn size, and its history at half the shown size (D617), so the cauliflower's finest billows are below what the picture holds. That gap is the march's resolution, not the noise's.
