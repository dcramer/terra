# D379: Ambient life round the eye

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

What lives in the air and on the water at a few metres is drawn round the camera, not simulated: each kind of drift (dust motes, midges, falling leaves, down, far flocks) one mesh of cards moved on the GPU from each card's luck and the time, wrapped in a box that follows the eye and is moved on by whole boxes so its cards stay put in the world, lit by the sun and its shadow and glowing toward it, so motes and midges show only in the shafts; each kind is data (`assets/data/drift.ron`: its look, motion, box, and how much of it is seen in woods, in the open and by water, by the season's and the air's warmth and the hour), its material touched only when that or the wind changes (`terra_world::drift`). Fish rise on water over 0.6 m deep near the eye, most at dawn and dusk: a ring is slopes added to the lake's own waves in its shader, eight at a time (`rises`), and one rise in six a small fish leaping (`terra_world::rises`). What drifts near the ground (motes, midges, leaves, down) fades out as the eye rises above its box over the ground, gone by twice its height and a few metres, so a crane or a bird's-eye shot has no motes beside its lens; flocks fly over any eye.

## Why

The developers asked for biomes "more livlier with more life" (2026-10-15). Shipped open worlds add this life round the camera rather than as actors (Red Dead Redemption 2's and The Witcher 3's insects, motes and rings on still water; Niagara's camera-following fields): thousands of cards cost one draw each kind, and nothing of it needs agreeing between players. A ring drawn on the water's own normal takes its reflection as a real ring does, where a decal over the water could not bend what it mirrors.
