# D629: The iso experiment's camera follows the hero as an ARPG's does, or stays the spike's fixed Baldur's Gate II view

Area: The game as a whole · Owner: Design · By: — · Status: Open

Two cameras for the 2.5D isometric experiment: an ARPG's follow camera (perspective, steep, a narrow field of view, 15 to 25 m off, locked on the hero and zoomed by context, never free-rotated in combat, as Diablo IV, Path of Exile 2, Last Epoch and V Rising do), or the spike's fixed view (one orthographic camera 42 degrees down and turned 28, never rotated, scrolled over the scene with a party selected and paused, as the Infinity Engine's, [research/2026-11-05-isometric-party-spike.md](../../research/2026-11-05-isometric-party-spike.md)). Recommended by the agent: the follow camera. Open.

Settled within it, by the developers (2026-11-07): the hero's size on screen stays as the loop's camera shoots it, about a tenth of the frame's height with about 18 by 12 m of ground in view. Which camera, follow or fixed, is still open.

## Why

The developer first asked for "a realistic game that would be 2.5d isometric topdown in the style of like baldurs gate 2", and the spike found the fixed view, selection rings, portraits and pause read as BG2 at once. The art pipeline since (D628) is shaped as an ARPG's: one hero with a run and an attack played in real time, its reference frames and budgets from ARPGs' closest camera ([research/2026-11-05-iso-arpg-art-pipeline.md](../../research/2026-11-05-iso-arpg-art-pipeline.md)). A follow camera keeps the hero at one size on screen (0.07 to 0.20 of the frame), so every asset is judged at one camera as Diablo IV checks its; it shows the animation the pipeline makes, which a party scrolled at a distance does not; and perspective gives the depth an orthographic view flattens. The fixed view suits a party played with pause, which is a different game; which game the experiment is, is the developers' call. Asked on the review desk whether the hero's size on screen was right, the developer answered "Keep it" (2026-11-07).
