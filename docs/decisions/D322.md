# D322: Mountains, lakes and water

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The world's ground gains a mountain term set by a theme's numbers (relief, ridge sharpness, erosion): ridged fBm damped by its own slope (Quilez's derivative noise), and gullies cut per point along the downhill gradient (Runevision's erosion filter, worked from its write-up, its code being MPL-2.0); bare rock above 40°, scree at 24 to 38° with a cliff within 45 m above, gravel in the gullies' beds. The upper slopes stand as walls (a theme's `walls`, 0 to 1: above half the climb from valley to crest the climb bends convex, steep from the wall's foot and easing to the crest, the valleys and their lakes below as they were), and the gullies run down them as couloirs (`couloirs_m` deeper, only where the ground falls past about 31°, so no couloir cuts a pit). On such a world chunks of 256 m and coarser split within three of their sizes rather than two, so far walls are meshed finer (4,544 chunks in the lake's first view against 2,569); past that, where the mesh is still tens of metres a cell, rock, steep ground and snow carry the ribs in a detail normal down the fall line, darker in the gullies, with the snow lying in the gullies and off the ribs. Lakes are the generated ground's depressions filled to their spill points by Priority-Flood on a coarse grid, before edits, answered through `terra_core::Water` (D45). Water is drawn as one layer after the opaque pass, a medium that absorbs by depth read from the depth prepass (Beer's law, ported from boujie's MIT water shader for Godot, D196), with foam at the shore from depth, a wet band on the ground above it and waves by the climate's wind: 28 trains of plane waves summed as GPU Gems' "Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models" sums them, each running at its own speed by water's dispersion (gravity's and surface tension's), a swell always running and ripples as steep as Cox and Munk's slopes for the wind in the gusts' cat's-paws, filtered by the pixel's footprint with what a pixel cannot hold widening the sun's glint (Bruneton's ocean lighting), the waterline rising and falling with them so it laps at the shore, and each train running one fixed way, the wind strengthening those near its own, so a turn of the wind never swings their phases; the sea's are the same (`waves.wesl`), laid out from a frame travelling with the camera, its phases carried in double precision, as Bruneton's planet ocean does; for now it mirrors the skyline round the lake as the land's rules make it (forest, rock and snow by height, hazed), taken once at the start, and over it what stands on screen along the reflected ray, marched through the depth prepass and read from the opaque scene Bevy copies for transmissive materials (`ScreenSpaceTransmission` on the camera), as Godot's and Unreal's screen-space reflections march, a ray leaving the screen's top over what is drawn at its edge taking that edge's colour (the shore's trees overhead, not the sky); still water mirrors between cat's-paws the wind ruffles. Pools under 1,000 m² are not drawn. The mountain lake query keeps, of the lakes that fit, the one its peaks loom over steepest. Far peaks are the octree's own coarse chunks, shaded past the last cascade by a horizon map baked with each. Amended by D394 (2026-10-17): the far ribs laid cell by cell, the ridges' warp shifted by whole cells, the couloirs varied by cell and face. Amended by D416 (2026-10-18): the far shade's horizon map is the land's round the camera, baked in `terra_land`, not each chunk's.

## Why

Mountains need a kilometre of relief before the lapse rate makes a tree line (the world's is 150 m, about 1 °C), and erosion has to work per point because the world is generated where you stand. Priority-Flood is the standard way to fill a height field's basins and gives engine.md's basin filled to its own level in one pass. Bevy's screen-space reflections need the deferred renderer and ours is forward, and a still mountain lake reads by what it reflects: a march of our own in the water's shader costs nothing where there is no water, where a planar reflection draws the scene again. The reviews' first gap was rounded felt-like peaks: couloirs 40 m deep made the walls ribbed in a CPU picture of the land, and the walls stood the massif over the lake rather than behind forested hills, the lake, the tundra and the town found where they were; ungated couloirs dug 1,100 pits that filled as puddles. `bevy_water` does not support Bevy 0.20. Measured on the first steps, and changed with a reason if they fail. See [demos.md](../demos.md#1-a-mountain-lake).
