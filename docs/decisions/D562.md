# D562: The hands close on what they hold: a finger-only grip layer, closed by touch at run time

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

Whenever a hand socket holds a look, that hand's fingers take a grip pose laid over whatever clip plays: a finger-only layer, one per grip type, chosen by the look's mount (docs/attachment.md): in hand a **power** grip (fingers and opposed thumb round the handle), hung (`"hold": "hung"`) a **hook** grip (the fingers alone), strapped none (the shield's arm is `strap`'s). The pose is solved, not keyed: each finger joint, knuckle first, closes from the rest pose about the hand's own flexion axis (the socket's +Y, however a tipped handle crosses it) until its bone, padded by a finger's half-thickness, touches the handle's surface; the thumb's metacarpal swings round the handle no more than it must. The handle is read from the look's mesh (its section at the grip, y = 0, the points within 5 cm of its line: their middle and median radius), and a power grip's look is seated with its handle's line on the palm, so a thin handle sits in the fist rather than where a sword's would. It runs after the clips and before the arms are kept off the hips (`terra_walker::grip`), solved again only when the look turns in the hand; its twin `tools/characters/grip.ts` lays the same grip over the clips in `held.ts` and `moves.ts` (the row "fist", `fist_cm.<look>`, the combat criteria `held.fist.*`, bar 1 cm).

## Why

The developer: "hes not gripping items". Shipped games lay a hand pose per weapon grip on the finger chains as an additive or override layer (Lyra's and ALS's hand poses, docs only); ours are generated from Kimodo and Uthana, which hold no prop, so a keyed pose per item would be one more asset per item and per body (D127, D136). Closing each link until contact is GraspIt!'s auto-grasp (Miller and Allen, "GraspIt!", IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine 2004): a new item needs no pose, only its mesh, and every body template closes on it alike. The fixed flexion axis keeps a tipped handle (a carried tool, D546's rule) from bending the fingers sideways; the seat on the palm is where a power grip holds a handle in a real hand. Measured on every template through every move and carried clip (grip jobs 1 and 2): the sword 0.8 cm, axes 0.8, torch 0.65, pickaxes 0.8 from the handle's surface; the knife and hammer 1.2 on the thin template, whose ring and little fingers cannot cup a handle under 2 cm without palm bones.
