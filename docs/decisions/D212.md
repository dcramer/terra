# D212: How the air, the exposure and plants' materials are drawn

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

The sky's colour, the sun's colour through the air and the haze over distance come from Bevy's physical atmosphere (Hillaire 2020, as Unreal's sky atmosphere), with Earth's air as Hillaire gives it and a little more dust, centred below the camera and seen with every distance through it 18 times the world's, since the stand-in planet is 2 km round (`terra_world::air`). Our sky dome draws the clouds, lit by the sun through the air, the stars and the moon over it, and the dusk's and night's own colours once the sun is down, as the atmosphere goes dark at sunset. The camera is exposed for sunlit ground, EV 10.8 with a low sun and 11.8 with a high one, opening to 9 at night through the dusk, keyed by the sun's height with the rest of the light (`daylight`); AgX tone mapping; screen-space ambient occlusion over 1.2 m, with D204's contact shadows; a faint bloom (0.03, since an energy-conserving bloom blends that much of a wide blur into every pixel and at 0.1 washed the middle distance flat). The sun's light is given as it is above the air and the atmosphere dims and reddens it: the light's colour is the same at every height of the sun (`daylight::keys`), since Bevy's atmosphere multiplies the directional light by its transmittance on every surface (`pbr_lighting.wesl`'s `directional_light`) and its sky tables read the light's colour, our sky dome's clouds and the sky light take `air::sun_through`, and the volume clouds take it on the CPU (`through_the_air`), against a sun overhead, so a high sun lights them as they were tuned and a low one reddens them (the whole column's transmittance turned the lake's clouds at 13 h flat and yellow: far contrast 0.21 to 0.10). The low keys keep the brightness on the ground their old warm colours gave, so the dusk's exposure holds; the black body's warmth they had stays only on the haze's glow toward the sun, which nothing else reddens. The air scatters the sun above the air, not the held one: our patch of Bevy's atmosphere (`AtmosphereSettings::sun_gain`, patches/README.md) scales what it scatters into the sky, the haze and the air cube by the unheld sun over the held one (`daylight::keys::air_sun_gain`, about 1.5 at 12 degrees), eased in from 4 to 12 degrees so the sunset's and the night's air are unchanged; the lit ground keeps the held sun. Trees, bushes and rocks are drawn in real materials, a texture stack of bark, leaves and lichen-covered rock (`materials/flora`) named per part in `flora.ron`, projected triplanar as the ground is, tinted by the kind's colour, with the free models' flat-shaded normals smoothed across curves and split at creases.

## Why

The developers: "the graphics/lighting still dont look like our art goals fwiw (closer to realism)" and "you can just fix the core textures if you need to (and the lighting) to make our test scene match up" (2026-10-13). The scene was over-exposed and washed out, ACES turned greens saturated and flat, the sky was a painted wash with distance fog of one colour, and plants were flat colours. A physical atmosphere is how shipped engines draw sky and aerial perspective, and Bevy ships one; a fixed exposure keyed by the time of day is how games with a day cycle hold a look, cheaper and steadier than eye adaptation; AgX is the film-like response Blender moved to from Filmic. Triplanar materials texture models with no UVs without remodelling them.

The light's colour as above the air (2026-10-28, sky job 3): the settlement r21 keys warmed it under 20 degrees toward a black body's, and since everything it reaches already went through the air's transmittance, the light was reddened twice; the tundra's sky at a 10-degree sun was brown (top fifth sRGB 70, 69, 67) and blue with the light white (81, 106, 142). The air's transmittance alone gives a 12-degree sun green at 0.83 of red, about 4,500 K.

The air's sun unheld (2026-10-29, tundra rounds 11 to 13): `held` dims the low sun on the ground, which takes the painted sky light on top, but Bevy's atmosphere draws its sky in proportion to the same dimmed sun, so at the tundra's 11 h (a 12-degree sun) the shaded snow stood brighter than the open sky over it. Scattering the unheld sun, on the worker (A/B 20261029-113213-2784-patch against main 799be03cf): the tundra's sky brightness 0.383 to 0.465 (the photographs' 0.777), frame 0.321 to 0.355 (0.487), far under sky 0.062 to 0.165 (0.452), still 14 of 18 holding; the lake-range at 8 h top band sRGB 130, 144, 168 to 144, 163, 193; the lake at 13 and 17 h, the forest at 13 h, the town at 13 and 18.7 h move under 2 sRGB and hold the same rows. The fire sheet's gate passed twice and film.ts's motion gate passed (20261029-113214-ace5-patch, 20261029-150558-e5a0-patch). Four fresh judges a build scored the tundra at 11 h 3.85 on main and 3.88 with it, no difference shown.
