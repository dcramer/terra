# D418: The weather at the eye drives the sky, the light, the air and what falls

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

One `Weather` resource holds the climate's cloud, what is falling and how much of it is snow, and the wind where the camera is, eased over seconds (`terra_world::weather`), and every drawn part reads it as everything reads the one wind (D255). Cloud dims the sun's beam and keeps the sky's light by Kasten and Czeplak's fit (a full deck leaves a quarter of a clear day's light, none of it direct; rain thickens it); the eye takes back most of the stops lost (0.8 of them), as a camera's meter does; a deck covers the sky as one grey layer graded as the CIE's standard overcast sky, three times as bright overhead as at the horizon and on average as bright as the light it gives the ground (the 2D deck, `overcast.wesl`; the cloud volume's not yet), and the haze takes its colour at the horizon, where it meets it; what falls shortens visibility as a METAR grades it, snow far more than rain. Rain and snow are drift kinds (D379) brought by the weather, thinned by their cards with the rate. Named weathers (`assets/data/weather.ron`) are queries only: `--weather <name>` moves a run's clock on from its date to the first day whose hour has that weather at the start, so the ground, the snow lying and the light all agree with what falls. See [weather.md](../weather.md).

## Why

The developer, 2026-10-18: "spin up a new session working explicitly on weather and season conditions (i.e. storms, and winter)". The climate already made the weather, but nothing drawn followed it: a full deck lit the forest with a clear day's sun. Reading the quantities, not a weather flag, keeps climate.md's rule ("fundamentals not hardcoded simplistic concepts"); finding a moment rather than forcing one keeps every machine's climate the same (D312).

The deck was drawn flat at the light it sheds and the haze under it at that brightness, about six times white on screen under snowfall, so the far land stood in pure white haze (tundra round 4); the overcast sky the ground is lit by (`skylight::overcast_seen`) is a third as bright at the horizon as overhead, and the 2D deck and the haze take it (tundra rounds 5 to 7). The cloud volume's deck lit so brightened the town's clear sky past its bound, as the volume map draws deck squares there (round 6), so it waits on the volume drawing a deck only under one.
