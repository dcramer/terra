# D381: A settlement's time-lapse, built by the building system's own steps

Area: Tools and workflow · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

`--timelapse <file>[@frame]` with the town experiment lays the town's streets and plots and puts nothing up. From its frame each building goes up from its plan by the steps the hammer takes (`terra_build::construct`, whose candidates, `Base::next_steps`, the player's hammer shares): the lowest first, a log course across its wall, a stone a block at a time beside the last, a roof a layer and a stage at a time; paced by how much of it is built, eased. Its builders work at its sites while it rises (`ThingKind::Site`, `Life::set_open`, a `Build` activity tried before the rest while one is open). A house's hearth, household, yard and animals, fences, torch, doors and shutters come once it is up, the square's things as the settlement reaches them. The sun runs through the day while the people keep the day's own pace. What happens when is data (`assets/data/timelapse/*.ron`); the camera is a `--cine` shot (`tools/sandbox/timelapse/`), and the ground cover's floor map follows a camera high over the ground, a texel twice as wide and its middle ahead along the way it looks, so a bird's-eye view sees its ground painted. `--site settlement` is the town's place with only its plots cleared of the forest; its meadow a mix of taller drifts, greener and drier patches and shrubs toward the forest's edge, its plots trodden as they go up, its streets trodden out as it grows and its square laid when its market comes. The settlement's site is by water: of the town search's best places, the first whose drawn water (its lakes on the world's own 10 m grid, as `round::Round` finds them, and the sea) has a shore 95 to 450 m from its middle and none within its reach (`MarketTown::water_m`, `Land::lake_beside`); its start away from the lake, and its forest cleared down to the shore. A clearing's edge grows back young (`Clearing::grown`): its trees stand at under half the forest's height where it begins, full grown 30 m into the forest. A cover may sow its grass (`CoverSource::sows`): the ground then takes the cover's grass outright, over the land's bare patches, where otherwise it takes the lower of the land's and the cover's; only a settlement rising's meadow sows. Its meadow is where its clearings keep less than 0.6 of the forest (`MEADOW_KEPT`), the forest round it the world's own cover, its needles, litter and moss; the clearing is mown (`flora::MowAround`), its trees, bushes, logs and stones taken away and its flowers, herbs and ferns left, and the flora grows round the settlement as round the start. A plot is pegged out as its builders are called, its stacked logs and tools beside it; a plot and the square are trodden over three of the film's seconds, the treading first and the grass going after it (`treading`), so no frame jumps from meadow to earth. A building a storey tall and more is scaffolded once its walls pass a builder's reach (`play::scaffold`): standards, ledgers, boards and a guard rail round it, a lift for each its courses reach, struck a lift at a time when it is done, a lift every 1.2 of the film's seconds; two in three of its builders at work stand on its top lift's boards facing its wall (`scaffold::Decks`), the rest at the logs. A lowland lake, as the settlement's on the valley floor, is drawn as one: olive-brown water stained by its woods, dark a metre or two down, over a silt bed, its water reaching its true shoreline, its shallows however shallow being the lake's (`lakes::fill`), its meadow down to its water over a few centimetres of wet mud at the line, darker than the meadow, wet and so darkened only over those few centimetres (ground.wesl `wet_band`; a hand or two of height was a sky-mirroring slate ring tens of metres wide on a flat bank) (`shore::grass`, from 2 cm; a mountain lake's bank bare to 1.6 m) and one even bed of silt under all of it, brown-green and darker going down (ground.wesl's soft shore: a margin 3 to 30 cm over the water was a mud flat tens of metres wide on a flat bank with the meadow's bumps standing out of it, and the meadow's own ground showed through a metre down as olive patches), its water looked for three of the 10 m cells round (`shore::REACH_CELLS`) so a flat bank's bare and wet ground never ends in the cells' squares ("textures of the beach are kinda chonky"), rather than a mountain lake's clear green over a band of scoured granite (`lakes::LOWLAND_*`, ground.wesl `lowland_at`, from 250 to 500 m over the sea, as its shore stones), the shingle the land lays round every lake giving out a few metres over a lowland one's water, and rank grass and sedge thick and tall along its waterline in clumps where the meadow meets it. Seen from high, the ground's map is painted ahead of the camera, its texels doubling with each doubling of the camera's height to 2 m, so a town 400 m off has its streets and square (`cover::floor`). The film is one continuous shot (`tools/sandbox/timelapse/settlement.json`): close over the first house as its walls go up, then pulling back ever faster and rising, from 13 to some 60 m over the ground and 650 m out, while it turns 110 degrees about the town, past the lake and the mountain to its north, its pitch flattening early from 45 degrees to 5, and from seven seconds its look rising to the skyline, so the mountains come into view by twelve seconds and stand whole under the sky at the end ("camera sort of flatter of flattening to shift to mountains earlier"), so it ends east of the town looking west over it to the forested ranges under the sky, the sun behind the camera (looking north the near mountain's bare slabs fill the frame, and south is flat); drawn with the detail the game has, 1.35 times as far (the shot's `detail`, a cinematic's level-of-detail bias), the houses past their far detail their hulls with their windows dark (D278), as the developers asked ("keep panning out (and faster to fit time budget) to show the environment? mountains forests etc."); at 2.2 the woods' reach ran a 10 GB GPU out of memory. A time-lapse may show its buildings alone (`people: false`): its people, their animals and the land's wild animals unseen while the town's life runs on, as the developers asked of the film ("doesnt need npcs tbh, just like starts with one building and it grows into settlement as the camera pans out with slight rotation").

The settlement film's town is eighty to ninety houses on streets 1.6 times a market town's, walled from 10.5 s, the castle from 12 s so it stands finished before the end, toward sunset (15.5 to 17.0 h: as measured in the game there, the sun sinking from about 29 to 13 degrees up in the west, behind the camera as it starts looking east and beside it on the left as it ends looking north, the late orbit swinging the castle toward the middle as it is finished and the last three seconds pushing in on it; at 6.0 to 6.35 h it stood due east, ahead of the camera, from 17.1 h the western forest's shadow covered the town, and run on to 17.4 h it reached the castle as it was finished); its place (`--site settlement.41,2.51`) is by a lake of at least 40,000 m² whose shore is 230 to 420 m from its middle, its meadow cleared 210 m round.

## Why

The developers asked for a film "where it has like a settlement built by us (there is a whole building system) where you have a bird eyes view thing where it shows you constructing your settlement". City-builders and survival builders (Manor Lords, Foundation, Valheim, Kingdom Come's Pribyslavitz) show a settlement growing from plots and frames, never finished houses faded in; and the bench and QA can use a scripted time-lapse of construction, as D71 asks of a tool. For the far film the developer asked: "settlement can be bigger since we are moving far far away, you can even imagine it finishing off with like castle and walls, lake must be bigger too. and camera sort of flatter of flattening to shift to mountains earlier".
