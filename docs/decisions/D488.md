# D488: A room shut off from the sky is seen as by night, with a dim neutral fill

Area: Engine and code · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Where the air round the eye takes under a tenth or so of the sky's light (`IndoorSky`, D485, D486; `terra_world::indoor::shut`, wholly at 0.05 and not at all from 0.35, so a house's room at 0.6 is left alone), three things follow, eased in and out over 1.5 s (`indoor::ShutOff`: eased only as the air is, over its last third, the fires stepped 3 stops in a quarter of a second at a doorway, dungeon light round 2): the eye's key is the room's own, EV 10.8, whatever the hour (`indoor::key`, in `daylight::open_exposure`), with the eye adaptation's indoor range on top (D240); the fires burn as by night (`indoor::fires_night`, in the fire lights and their glows), nothing outshining them; and a dim fill lights everything in view, 140 cd/m² of a cool grey (`indoor::FILL`, 0.78/0.84/0.95, a camera `AmbientLight`, taken away outside; on stone some 3.2 stops under a mid-grey), the stone's own shade between the torches, which the room probes do not carry; the torches light in their own yellower colour (`fire::TORCH_LIGHT`, about 2,700 K), so the warm is near the fire and the cool between, as torchlit interiors are graded. The smoke over a fire takes the sky's light only as far as it reaches the air round the camera (`fire::smoke::sky`).

## Why

Round 8's three dungeon films were judged to have a red-orange haze over every interior, long near-black stretches and grey searchlight cones over the goal's fire bowls. Measured, the haze was not in the air: with the fire lights out of the volumetric fog, the fog off, bloom off, the distance fog's colour at 0, or the smoke off, the keep's goal still measured R/G 4.5 and R/B 11.5. It was the torch light itself under the day's exposure: a dungeon at noon was exposed for sunlit ground (EV 11.8) with its fires at their day light, so all that read was the torches' saturated orange (`HEARTH_LIGHT`, linear R/G 4.7) on the walls nearest them and black between. The cones were the smoke lit by the whole noon sky underground.

Shipped games light interiors so: Bethesda's interior cells have no sky and an ambient of their own; Unreal's take a low fill and set the eye by the eye adaptation's range, not the sky's exposure. A fill and a fixed indoor key were both open; both are needed, the key so noon and midnight read alike underground, the fill so no stretch is black (an exposure floor alone lifts nothing where no light falls).
