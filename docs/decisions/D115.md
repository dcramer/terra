# D115: How characters are made and rigged

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

A character's base body is drawn as a design sheet in a T-pose, made into a textured model on Tripo, and rigged with Tripo's current (V3) auto-rig in its Unreal skeleton, both on the Studio subscription. Our skeleton is the one Quaternius's animation libraries use (Universal Animation Library 1 and 2, bought, current versions), which has Unreal's bone names too; the clips the game plays are cut from them into `models/character/mannequin.glb`. A script moves our joints to Tripo's, keeps our bones' directions, keeps Tripo's weights by name, and adds Tripo's four twist bones a side. The clips put a limb's whole roll in the forearm and shin; as each clip is sampled for a body, that roll moves to the hand and foot and the twist bone takes half (Unreal's convention), and bodies and gear are skinned weight-blended: dual quaternions but round the hips (`models/character/skinning.json`). A clip check passes every clip first; a rig checker passes the body: same skeleton and rest pose as ours, at most four bones a vertex, nothing driven by a distant bone, left and right alike, seams that stay closed, and through our animations no worse than 3.5 times the mannequin. Each character is exported with our animations re-sampled on its own skeleton, and looked at in the sandbox's workbench before use.

## Why

The developers asked for a pipeline that lets non-artists make good characters with Tripo doing the hard parts. Tried on 2026-10-11: generating in an A-pose and moving the body into our T-pose crushed the shoulders in every animation; Blender's own weights and the mannequin's copied weights both did worse than Tripo's converted rig, which matched the mannequin in everyday moves. Still to do: strip the clips' unchanging joint translations, since Bevy applies them and would undo our moved joints.
