# D544: The world's heights: how high one goes and where the clouds stand

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Design · By: Developers · Status: Decided

Heights are Earth's up to 3 km over the sea, and above 3 km each metre of ours stands for 2.5 real ones: `ours = 3 km + (real − 3 km) / 2.5`. Up to 3 km, about the highest crests, a height is the real one: the start's mountains stand at the Tatras' own height (D322, D425) and the climate cools 6.5 °C a kilometre, so fog, valley cloud, stratus, stratocumulus, cumulus bases by Espy's rule, the freezing level and the tree and snow lines stand where Earth's air puts them. Above it: Earth's death zone (8 km) from 5 km, just over the highest summits (horns about 3.7 km); the tropopause (11 km) at 6.2 km, which is also the ceiling, thin air no one lives in, where lift fades with it, never a wall; cumulus congestus tops at 4.2 km (6); altocumulus and altostratus 2 to 4.6 km (2 to 7); nimbostratus tops 3 to 3.8 km (3 to 5); cirrus 3.8 to 7 km (5 to 13); the cumulonimbus anvil at the tropopause, 6.2 km, its tops to 8.2 km (16), higher where a storm is heightened (D522); the Armstrong limit (about 18 km) at 9 km, beyond reach. Winds above 3 km are the real height's divided by 2.5, so high cloud crosses the sky at Earth's angular speed. A player reaches the summits on foot, builds as high as building's rules hold up with no cap of its own, and later flies to the ceiling. The air at an eye is the air of the real height its height stands for (11 km's deep blue at the ceiling, never black), not its height times the air's horizontal scale (amends D334). Cloud shells are spheres round the air's Earth-sized centre under the eye, not the 30 km planet's, so layers reach the air's horizon as on Earth. The arithmetic, Earth beside ours: [sky.md](../sky.md#heights). Alternatives offered: halved above 3 km (death zone 5.5 km, ceiling 7 km); a quarter above 3 km (death zone 4.25 km, ceiling 5 km, some cirrus under the tallest horns); Earth's heights everywhere (ceiling 11 km); one factor for everything, mountains included (undoing D425 and D322's ranges); shells on the planet's own curvature (each layer a ceiling closing under 10 km off).

## Why

The developer, 2026-10-27: "we probably also need to define how high you can go, where cloud layers should be etc" and "it should be realistic but scaled down". The world's radius is 1/212 of Earth's (D13) while its ground stands at real heights, so the sky cannot take one factor from the radius: Earth's 1/212 would put cirrus at 50 m. The first proposal halved heights above 3 km (ceiling 7 km); the developer: "might still be a LITTLE high for how big our world would be.. like where would the atmosphere edge be (aka where you'd start dying)". Against the real references (altitude sickness from about 3 km, the death zone from 8 km, the tropopause at 11 km, the Armstrong limit about 18 km), 2.5 to one puts the death zone just over the highest summits and the ceiling at 6.2 km, while high cloud still stands well over the crests (cirrus mostly 5 to 6.2 km, about twice a crest's height, storm tops nearly three times it); a quarter would put cirrus under the horns. The developer, 2026-10-27: "ok im fine with this decision for now": decided for now, to revisit when flight or the sky shows otherwise. Sky stage 2's round 3 found a black, orbit-like sky at 1.5 km: on the 2 km stand-in the eye stood 27 km up the air (D334's 18 times) over a 2 km ball, which the vertical scale and judging on the 30 km world both answer.
