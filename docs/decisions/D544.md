# D544: The world's heights: how high one goes and where the clouds stand

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Design · By: — · Status: Open

Recommended: heights are Earth's up to 3 km over the sea and halved above it. Up to 3 km, about the highest crests, a height is the real one: the start's mountains already stand at the Tatras' own height (D322, D425) and the climate cools 6.5 °C a kilometre, so fog, valley cloud, stratus, stratocumulus, cumulus bases by Espy's rule, the freezing level and the tree and snow lines stand where Earth's air puts them. Above 3 km a metre stands for two (`3 km + (real − 3 km) / 2`): the tropopause at 7 km (Earth's 11), altocumulus and altostratus 2 to 5 km (2 to 7), cirrus 4 to 8 km (5 to 13), nimbostratus tops 3 to 4 km, congestus to 4.5 km, the cumulonimbus anvil at 7 km and its tops to 9.5 km, higher where a storm is heightened (D522); winds there are the real height's halved, so high cloud crosses the sky at Earth's angular speed. A player reaches the summits on foot (about 2.8 km), builds as high as building's rules hold up with no cap of its own, and later flies to a ceiling at 7 km, where lift fails with the thinning air, never a wall. The air at an eye is the air of the real height its height stands for (11 km's deep blue at the ceiling, never black), not its height times the air's horizontal scale (amends D334 once answered). Cloud shells are spheres round the air's Earth-sized centre under the eye, not the 30 km planet's, so layers reach the air's horizon as on Earth. Alternatives: Earth's heights everywhere (scale 1: tropopause and ceiling 11 km, cirrus 5 to 13 km, the planet's horizon 43° down from the ceiling); a stronger squeeze (a third above 3 km: tropopause 5.7 km, cirrus 3.7 to 6.3 km, barely over the horns); one factor for everything, mountains included (a half: crests 1.4 km and the lapse rate doubled, undoing D425 and D322's ranges); shells on the planet's own curvature (each layer a ceiling closing under 10 km off). See [sky.md](../sky.md#heights).

## Why

The developer, 2026-10-27: "we probably also need to define how high you can go, where cloud layers should be etc" and "it should be realistic but scaled down". The world's radius is 1/212 of Earth's (D13) while its ground stands at real heights, so the sky cannot take one factor from the radius: Earth's 1/212 would put cirrus at 50 m. What a player reads as real is the ground's air at its true heights, where peaks meet cloud, and the high air standing well over it, which halving above the crests keeps (cirrus mostly twice a crest's height, storm tops over three times it) while the ceiling and the march stay within 7 to 9.5 km. Sky stage 2's round 3 found a black, orbit-like sky at 1.5 km: on the 2 km stand-in the eye stood 27 km up the air (D334's 18 times) over a 2 km ball, which the vertical scale and judging on the 30 km world both answer.
