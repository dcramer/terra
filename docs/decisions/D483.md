# D483: A theme names its own sockets, and a socket few modules take is an adjacency rule

Area: Worlds and realms · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Leaning

Amends D474 (the dressing in [dungeons.md](../dungeons.md) follows it). Besides the built-in `open`, `wall`, `door`, `stair`, `rubble` and `water`, a theme's modules name sockets of their own, up to 32 in all ([dungeons.md](../dungeons.md)). Every socket but wall, door and stair is interior: an open side must share one with the module across. A socket only a few modules take is how a theme writes structure:

- **Pillars in rows**: a pillar takes `aisle|wall` along its row, so a row runs from wall to wall (or to a feature).
- **Runs along a wall**: loculi take `alcove` beside them and end at a corner, a door or an end piece.
- **Rhythm**: two beats (`ra`, `rb`), a sconce or relief then a plain bay, alternate along every decorated wall.
- **Collapses** spill `rubble` into both cells beside them.
- **Water** meets dry floor only at a `shore`, steps down from the dry side; pools take no stair, so a flooded gallery has a shore before its stair.
- **Runners and rails** (`runner`, `rail`) run unbroken from a door, stair or feature to another, or to an end piece or buffer.

`any` is every socket but water and rubble: the held features (dais, altar, fire) take it, so a run may end against them. A module is under water when a side takes water and none takes a dry floor. The tests check that every open side's two modules share a socket and no water ends without a shore, over 1,000 seeds a theme.

## Why

The built-in sockets barely constrained the solve (every dressing took one try): a plain floor fitted beside anything, so pillars, alcoves and decoration fell as scattered noise. Tiled WFC gets its structure from sockets that only some tiles take (Townscaper's and Bad North's edge labels, Gumin's simple tiled model); naming them in the theme keeps a theme's rules data (D136). Arc consistency resolves these rules without contradiction (still one try, 100% without the fallback), so the rules cost nothing in solve rate.
