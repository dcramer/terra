# D432: The dusk sky: an amber afterglow toward the set sun, the earth's shadow and the Belt of Venus opposite

Area: Art, characters and sound · Owner: Tech · By: Agent · Status: Decided

After sunset the sky low toward the set sun glows amber, yellow-orange near the horizon (about sRGB 255,180,100) and redder as the sun sinks, in an arch that spans the sun's side of the sky (half its strength 60 degrees off the sun's bearing, none past 90). Opposite it the earth's shadow lies blue-grey on the horizon with the pink Belt of Venus over it, 4 to 20 degrees up. One function draws it for the sky, the water's mirror and the far land's haze (`assets/shaders/dusk.wesl`, `dusk_sky`). Its CPU twin, `terra_world::sky::dusk`, is tested without a GPU: low toward the sun the sky is orange and warmer than anything opposite, and the shadow is bluer than the belt over it. A second test holds the two copies to the same numbers in the same order.

## Why

The developers on 2026-10-21 wanted "the evening look, a realistic horizon". Lake review r19 listed "a flat blue-hour sky with no warm band" second among its faults. Both causes were found without a render:

- **The band was absent.** The band toward the sun took the horizon's own colour, and daylight's horizon key goes mauve as the sun sinks. With the sun 2 degrees under, the sky 3 degrees up toward it came out sRGB hue 8, salmon-mauve. The test fails on that rule.
- **It was off camera.** At the lake film's 21:45 on 07-20 (declination 20.4 degrees), the sun stands 1.6 degrees under the lake's horizon. Its bearing in the lake start's terms is local (0.98, 0.18) in x and z: +X, 10 degrees toward +Z, about 298 degrees, west-northwest. That was read from `SkyPath`, `Frame::turned_for` and `Start::by_lake`. The film ran from 250,11 toward -X, so its camera faced directly away from the sun.

Real dusk, as photographed: the afterglow and twilight arch are warm toward the sun, and the earth's shadow and the Belt of Venus lie opposite it.
