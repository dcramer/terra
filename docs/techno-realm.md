# A realm of machines

Part of the game's design ([gameplay.md](gameplay.md), D89), as a proposal: how a realm can feel technologically advanced without guns, without science fiction, and without being better than magic. Open, for the developers to react to; nothing here is built or decided. It is one setting worked through under the general frame in [settings.md](settings.md), where every realm's theme fills the same roles.

## What the developers have said

From 2026-10-11: no machine guns; technologically advanced, but on theme with magic, and not superior. Its makers could be gone or still at work. Standing: realms may differ in setting (D86); one art style (D14); no science fiction (D89).

## Based on

| Reference | What to take |
|---|---|
| *He Who Fights With Monsters* | Force without magic does not scale against rank, and magic items are already technology made of magic ([research/hwfwm-magic.md](../research/hwfwm-magic.md)). |
| Eberron (Dungeons & Dragons) | Magic as industry: trains on lines of lightning, airships driven by bound elementals, built soldiers. Machines that are plainly magic. |
| *Arcane* | Hextech: engineering shaped around a magic crystal. Precise, powerful, dangerous, never replacing the people who use it. |
| Kenshi, Soulmask, Subnautica | An advanced people who are gone, known from their ruins, their guardians and what still runs. Technology as something you dig up and learn, not something you are behind. |

## The idea in one line

Proposed: **a fallen civilisation of magitech, whose machines run on magic, cannot grow, and are best at work that repeats.** Its devices are tools for holding ground; your own power is still what gets you anywhere new.

## The rules

**1. Everything runs on magic.** A device is powered by a **core**: the crystallised magic monsters leave, of the monster's rank (D165, [magic.md](magic.md#raising-powers)). There is no other power. Monsters, pooling places and machines become one economy.

**2. A machine is as strong as its core, and stays so.** A bronze-rank turret is a bronze-rank turret for as long as it stands. It does not learn, rank up or improve by use. You do (D88, D164, [progression.md](progression.md)). So machines are strong early and in numbers, and are outgrown by a person who keeps climbing; they never stand in for that climb.

| | Machines | Your own skills and essences |
|---|---|---|
| Grows | No: as strong as its core's rank | Yes, by use and through ranks |
| Works without you | Yes, while powered | No |
| Needs | Cores, repair (D99), and a claim to stand in (D94) | You |
| Good at | Work that repeats: making, carrying, guarding, lighting | Fighting, exploring, anything new |

**3. What machines are for: holding and running a place.**

- **Stations that work on their own** (D109), faster or for longer: a core-fired smelter that runs all night.
- **Infrastructure in a claim**: lifts, pumps, lamps, doors that open for you, carts on rails, chutes between stations.
- **Wards and sentries**: a turret that fires bolts of its core's element, a ward that keeps small things out. Built for defending outposts while you are away, which the developers raised earlier.
- **Constructs**: built workers and guards. They do simple work tirelessly; they do not trade, teach, talk or run a station that needs skill, so rescued people (D90) stay the heart of a hold.

**4. What they are not.** No guns, no lasers, no vehicles that outrun everything, no machine that out-fights a person of its rank in their hands. A hand-held device is at most a charged tool: a bolt-thrower with a few shots from its core, slower than a bow in skilled hands, useful to someone without the skill.

**5. Its makers, gone or still at work.** The developers said on 2026-10-11 it could be either. If they are gone, what is left:

- **Ruins** where the old works drew magic in, so magic pools there now and monsters form among the machines ([creatures.md](creatures.md)).
- **Guardians**: constructs still keeping their posts, at the rank of their cores, as dangerous as monsters and worth more.
- **Survivors or descendants**, perhaps: a culture living among the ruins, who keep some of the old knowledge and might teach it or join you (D86, D90).
- **Learning by finding** (D68): you learn a device by salvaging one, or from people who know it, not from a research tree.

That keeps the realm from feeling ahead of the others: its machines are the past, and something went wrong with them. If the makers are still at work, a living culture of tinkers, the same rules hold: their machines run on cores and are capped by them, and what they teach is the realm's speciality ([settings.md](settings.md)). A realm could have both, the living among the ruins of the old.

**6. Each realm has a speciality.** If realms teach different things (D86), each should be needed and none should be the top. Proposed for machines: holding a place, through automation, defence and logistics. The other settings' specialities are in [settings.md](settings.md#four-settings-from-the-earlier-mood-board). A hold that draws on several is stronger than one that uses one, which is a reason to go everywhere (D91) and to trade.

**7. The look.** One art style (D14). Brass, bronze, carved stone and crystal, not chrome or plastic; machines that look built by hand and fitted together; runes and the glow of the magic showing where the power runs. Its cultures differ in models and details, as every realm's do.

## How it fits the rest

| System | What changes |
|---|---|
| Crafting ([crafting.md](crafting.md)) | New stations and recipes from the realm; cores as an ingredient; the smelter and campfire's way of working on their own (D109) is where machines start. |
| Building ([construction.md](construction.md)) | Machines are stations and props, single models placed from the catalogue like any other (D108). Rails, chutes and lifts are parts of a build. |
| Combat ([combat.md](combat.md)) | Unchanged at its centre: melee, a bow, abilities. Turrets and constructs fight beside you at their cores' rank. |
| People ([people.md](people.md)) | Recruits stay worth more than constructs: they do skilled work, teach, and talk. |
| Your gear | Machines you carry work in every realm, as all gear does; machines you build stay in the claim where they stand. |

## Risks

- **Automation crowding out play.** Factory games show how far automation pulls. Keeping machines in claims, on cores you must hunt for, and at their cores' rank, should keep them a help and not the game.
- **Constructs crowding out recruits.** If built workers do everything, rescuing people stops mattering. They should be limited to simple work.
- **Looking like science fiction.** The developers ruled that out. The look and the rule that everything runs on magic are what keep it on theme.

## Questions for the developers

1. Answered: **gone or still at work** could be either (2026-10-11).
2. Answered: **cores come from monsters** (D165). Open: whether they can also be charged where magic pools.
3. **Constructs:** in, and if so, what may they do?
4. **Hand-held devices:** none at all, or charged tools like the bolt-thrower?
5. Superseded: there is no single long arc to tie into (D172). What happened to the makers is the realm's generated history ([settings.md](settings.md#making-generated-realms-fit-the-lore)).
6. **Is "machines teach holding a place" the right speciality**, and do the other realms' specialities sound right?
