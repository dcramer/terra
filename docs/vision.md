# Vision

## Pitch

Players land on a dead planet and terraform it into a living one, then have to live with what they grew.

Early on the planet kills you by being dead: no air, no water, cold, radiation. Later it kills you by being alive, and the life that hunts you is a consequence of how you terraformed.

## Pillars

Draft. A feature that serves none of these is cut.

1. **The planet is the main character.** One real, round, persistent world that visibly changes because of you, from orbit down to the ground.
2. **Dead to alive.** Hostility hands off from the environment to the ecosystem. What you grow decides what hunts you.
3. **Automate survival.** Every chore starts manual and ends as infrastructure.
4. **Local action, global consequence.** You change a place; places add up to a planet.
5. **Seamless, and together.** No loading screens, surface to space. Played through a server, with friends first (D51); how many players a world holds is open (D52).

## What we take from each inspiration

| Game | We take | We leave |
|---|---|---|
| Planet Crafter | Phased terraforming; the world visibly transforming | Handcrafted map; numbers that only go up; life as decoration |
| No Man's Sky | Seamless planet to space; the art benchmark; procedural everything | Infinite universe; shallow planets |
| Minecraft | Dig and build anywhere | Blocky cubes; flat world |
| Vintage Story | Survival systems with some depth | The punishing difficulty |
| Space Engineers | Real planets in a real solar system; voxel terrain with separately built structures | Engineering-grade physics |
| Green Hell | The environment as the enemy | The intensity |
| SimEarth | A coupled planetary simulation | Abstract god-game view |
| Spore | Life arriving in stages, simple to complex | Creature editor |
| Factorio | Automation as the answer to threats; your own output provokes the enemy | 2D factory focus |

Astroneer is deliberately not on this list (D15). It shows the tech is achievable by a small team; its gameplay and art are not models for us.

## Tone

Slightly harder than Planet Crafter, well short of Vintage Story. Threats are real but are solved by building, not by reflexes. No fail state.

## Non-goals

- PvP.
- Photorealism.
- Handcrafted maps.
- A creature editor.
- A second planet before one planet is a complete game.

## Constraints

- Two developers.
- Every asset is free, bought or LLM-generated, and code is LLM-written. This pushes us to procedural content and a code-first stack.
