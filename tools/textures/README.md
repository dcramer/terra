# Textures

Makes the game's materials (D14, D191): the tileable surfaces a model or the ground is drawn with, as `assets/materials/<name>/`, each a colour map, a normal map (OpenGL's convention, as Bevy reads it), an ARM map (occlusion, roughness and metal in red, green and blue) and, for parallax, a height map. Where every material came from is in [assets/materials/README.md](../../assets/materials/README.md), which each step keeps up to date.

```bash
node tools/textures/textures.ts fetch pine_bark                         # a Poly Haven material, CC0, at 2K
node tools/textures/textures.ts fetch ambientcg:Ground104 --name packed_earth   # an ambientCG one, CC0
node tools/textures/textures.ts material shakes art/textures/shakes.png --tile y   # from a generated image
node tools/textures/textures.ts stack ground brown_mud_02 leafy_grass rock_face_03 sand_01 leaves_forest_ground forest_leaves_04 roots moss_004 --height
node tools/textures/textures.ts cards foliage oak=art/textures/cards/oak.leaves.json birch=keep … holly=art/textures/cards/holly.png
node tools/textures/textures.ts cards litter oakfall=art/textures/litter/oakfall.leaves.json … ivydark=art/textures/litter/ivydark.leaves.json --size 2048 --columns 4
```

- **fetch** downloads a [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com) material by its id. Libraries come first: their maps are measured, not guessed. The material's size in metres is printed; a kit's file says it. `ambientcg:<Id>` fetches from [ambientCG](https://ambientcg.com), also CC0, where Poly Haven has nothing as good (earth packed in lifts, for one): its maps come in a zip, unpacked in a folder of its own outside the repository, and its ARM map is put together from its occlusion and roughness; ambientCG gives no size, so it is judged by eye.
- **material** makes one from a single image no library has, such as a Tripo image of a roof of shakes: it crops the image to the length at which it repeats itself, so rows tile without a seam, and works out a normal map and a height from its light and dark (weaker than a scanned material's). `--strength` sets how deep the relief reads, `--roughness` its roughness.
- **cards** makes the atlas grown plants' leaves, fronds and flowers are drawn from (D222), `assets/materials/<name>/cards.png`, and its normal map, `normal.png`, giving each card the octagon round its leaves that it is cut to and its average colour, written to `assets/data/<name>.ron`. A card is made one of three ways. From a **recipe** (`<card>.leaves.json` in `art/textures/cards`, D269), as studios build SpeedTree atlases: single leaves cut from an ambientCG leaf scan (CC0, downloaded once into the system's temporary folder) are laid along a twig and its shoots, as leaflets in threes and fives along a cane, or (`litter`, the forest floor's atlas, recipes in `art/textures/litter`, D275) fallen in a loose heap with needles, twigs and cones among them, each at an angle, size and turn of its own, foreshortened as it turns, some young and yellower, some old and darker, a few showing their paler underside, a soft shadow where one lies on another; the normal map carries each leaf's scanned relief turned and tilted with it (`leaves.ts`). From a **picture** drawn on a flat blue background (a Tripo image, prompt beside it), keyed out, its edges unmixed from the blue, flat in the normal map. Or `keep`: its cell as the atlas has it now, for one touched by hand since. Give every card each time, in the order wanted: the atlas is made whole.
- **blend** makes a log's face from two materials already fetched, one lying over the other in patches, as bark stays on a weathered log in places: `blend log_bark rough_wood=0.5 pine_bark=1.0 --cover 0.5 --saturation 0.7 --lighten 1.3`, each at its size in metres, in a texture `--around` metres round the log by `--along` metres along it (1 by 2), squeezed square for the kit's `metres` to stretch back. Where the bark lies is tileable noise drawn out along the log, its edge ragged by the bark's height; the bark stands proud of the wood in the height and normal maps with a dark lip where it ends.
- **ends** makes a log's sawn ends from ambientCG's scanned tree ends (CC0), downloaded by id (`ends log_ends TreeEnd002 TreeEnd003 TreeEnd004 TreeEnd005`): each cut out by its opacity and drawn out round so its rim falls where a log's cap maps its edge, greyed by `--weather`, laid in an atlas whose `cells` the kit names.
- **stack** puts several materials' colour and normal maps one above the next, 1024 pixels each, for a surface that blends them, such as the ground, which loads them as a texture array. Each layer's occlusion is multiplied into its colour. With `--height`, each layer's height, its broad swells taken out, is in its normal map's blue, which a shader that works z out from x and y can blend the layers by (the ground, D275). The stacked materials' own folders are not needed after; the stack's credit names them.

It needs ImageMagick (`convert`), and `unzip` for ambientCG. The tests are `node --test tools/textures/textures.test.ts`.
