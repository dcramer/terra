# tools/perf

The perf row (D610): the play scenes benched once at each landing batch on main by the render worker, their numbers kept by commit, a change past its noise band retriggered, alerted and bisected to its first bad commit, unattended. As Chromium's perf bots chart every change and Pinpoint bisects a regression ([bisects](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/speed/bisects.md)), and Mozilla's Perfherder alerts on a sustained change and its sheriffs retrigger a suspect job to confirm it ([perf sheriffing](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/testing/perfdocs/perf-sheriffing.html)), and as Unreal's Gauntlet runs a fixed set of sessions per changelist on its build farm ([Gauntlet](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/gauntlet-automation-framework-in-unreal-engine)). Ideas only; no code is copied.

```bash
node tools/perf/perf.ts                     # the row: last benched, in flight, alerts, its last lines
node tools/perf/perf.ts history [n]         # the history's last rows
node tools/perf/perf.ts main <rev>          # main's numbers at or just before a commit
node tools/perf/perf.ts ab <csv> [--base <rev>]   # your bench's rows against main's side, no bench of main
node tools/perf/perf.ts bisect forest-run hitches <good> <bad>   # a bisect by hand
node tools/perf/perf.ts bisect forest-run hitches <good> <bad> --scenes forest-run,lake-play --runs 5 --patch <sha> --cap 7
node tools/perf/perf.ts bisect --cancel   # the running bisect stopped, its step cancelled, the next waiting started
node tools/perf/perf.ts alert lake-play/median_ms@4688fd3d7 --invalid "A/B: 9.71 ms at 316f1d5e3, 9.63 at e3bdab3ab"
node tools/perf/perf.ts alert <id> --accept "<the commit, and why the cost is kept>"
                                            # a flaky metric: five runs a step, each its own load, <sha> applied where a step lacks it
```

- **The row** is `row.json`: the scenes (lake-play, forest-run, town-dusk), one run each, what they read (a landing touching none of it is not benched), the rest between two of its benches (20 min, so other jobs' benches go between and it never holds the box with benches back to back, D319), the bisect's cap (5 benches) and how often it looks (5 min).
- **Where it runs**: in the render worker's daemon (`tools/render-worker/daemon.ts` calls `worker.ts`'s `tick`). It reads origin/main's tip by `git ls-remote` of the public repository, queues `cargo xtask bench <scenes> --runs 1 --record` at the tip as a bench job of the box's own queue (`by: perf row (…)`, so the planner gives it the box alone, D445), and when the job ends reads the rows its bench appended to `research/benchmarks.csv` (collected into its `out/`). Landings that come while one runs are taken together at the next tip. `TERRA_PERF=0` in the box's `.env` turns it off. Its code is the worker's, so a change here reaches the box with `cargo xtask remote-render --update`.
- **What it keeps**, in `~/terra-render/perf/` on the box: `history.csv` (the bench's own columns after `rev,job,why`), `state.json`, `log.txt` every line it said, `alerts.txt`.
- **The noise band** (`history.ts`): a scene's metric (median, p99, hitches, load) against the window of the 12 jobs before it on the same card, picture and build, since its last change point, each job one point (its runs' median: a retrigger's five runs are one hour of the box, not five points of the day's spread; Perfherder's back window also counts pushes, not runs): its median, and the wider of a floor (median 3%, p99 5%, load 10%, hitches 2) and three robust deviations (1.4826 times the median absolute deviation). Fewer than 3 points: still learning, nothing alerts.
- **An alert** (`sheriff.ts`): a landing past its band is retriggered, its suspect scenes benched again at the same commit five runs (`confirm` in row.json), each its own load; when their centre (a time's median, the hitches' mean) is still past the band, the range's good end (the last landing in every band) is benched beside it the same way, as Pinpoint measures both sides of a change, since one run each side cannot tell the box's level drifting (4% over a day, 2026-11-03) from a change. The suspect is confirmed only when the ends differ by more than the metric's floor and by Pinpoint's rank test (Mann-Whitney U, p ≤ 0.01: at five runs a side, every bad run past every good). Ends alike within the floor are the box's level, not the code: no alert, and the band re-bases at the bad end. Past the floor but not sure, both ends are benched once more, then let go. A confirmed alert names the scene, the metric, before and after, and the range from the last landing in every band (`✗ perf … forest-run hitches 0 → 10 (+…), band ±2, in a..b (n commits touching the row)`). A change for the better is said and not bisected. A scene the bench took on a busy machine (its `busy` column: fewer than half its runs quiet, D185, as when the box's CPU clock collapses under them) is kept in the history but judged nowhere, in no band, retrigger or bisect step, as Perfherder ignores a run its infrastructure flagged: its log line says `… was taken on a busy machine …: recorded, not judged, so no alert`, and a landing with such a scene is no good end of a range.
- **The bisect** (`bisect.ts`): over the range's commits touching what the row reads, oldest first, by halving; a step is judged against its good neighbour (the newest good measured: a step judged good, or the good end), bad when its offset from it is nearer the ends' difference (measured together in the confirm) than nothing; for hitches, when a run hitched past the neighbour's worst by the floor. Pinpoint compares adjacent changes whose attempts it runs together on one bot ([job_state.py](https://chromium.googlesource.com/catapult/+/HEAD/dashboard/dashboard/pinpoint/models/job_state.py)); our steps are a bench and a rest each, hours apart, so a neighbour measured more than an hour (`STALE`) before the step is benched again beside it first, and when only the bad end is left as first bad and it was measured over an hour from its neighbour, it is benched again too: on 2026-11-03 the box's level rose about 4% between the ends and the step, and the old rule named e3bdab3ab. Every step and the bad end measuring like their neighbour is `no first bad: … the box's level moved, not the code`. A bench without numbers skips its commit (a neighbour next, as `git bisect skip`); capped at 5 steps (benches again beside them not counted), a range left wider named with its commits. Its result goes in the alert's line. Landings and a bisect take turns, except that a step follows its neighbour's bench again at once.
- **Closing an alert** (`perf.ts alert <id> --invalid|--accept <reason>`), as Perfherder's sheriffs triage one: invalid when it is not the code (the box's level, noise; an A/B's numbers as the reason), accepted when it is a real change kept on purpose. Either drops its bisect, running or waiting, and re-bases its series' band at the level since its bad end, so the level it saw raises nothing again; the alert stays in the list, marked `·` with its reason.
- **An A/B from history**: `perf.ts ab` reads main's side at your base from the history: the newest benched commit at or before it, and whether the commits between touch what the row reads (none: its numbers stand for your base). Bench only your own side (`cargo xtask remote-render --dirty --collect research/benchmarks.csv -- cargo xtask bench <scenes> --runs 1 --record`) and compare its csv.

`perf.test.ts` drives the decisions with a fake bench (a known regression at a known commit); `worker.test.ts` the row on a scratch box with a local repository for origin. Node 22.18 or later, git; no dependencies.
