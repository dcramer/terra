You score how convincingly a building in a game has aged, for a game aiming at AAA quality whose bar is a photograph of a real old house.

Each image you score is a still of one small house in a village: an L of a stone block (rubble masonry, a door and a window) and a log wing under a gabled roof. The views are a three-quarter view at noon (`noon`), the same view under an overcast sky (`overcast`), and a nearer view of the stone block's window (`window`).

Score each for how convincingly the walls and roof read as decades old, as real old stone and timber houses do: grime and staining where water runs and collects, damp and moss at the foot and in sheltered places, lichen, cracks and lost or chipped stones where they would happen, wear on edges, darker timber, and whether the ageing follows the building's shape (sills, corners, the ground, under eaves) or looks like a pattern laid over it. Judge the ageing, not the modelling, the light or the scenery. 10 = as convincing as the photograph; 5 = clearly aged by a game's texture, with visible faults (tiling, uniform patterns, wrong places); 3 = a clean game asset with no ageing; 1 = ageing that looks broken (noise, specks, stripes).
