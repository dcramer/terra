# Items: kinds and parts of assembled items

The checks of D565's assembled items ([docs/assembled-items.md](../../docs/assembled-items.md), D566's convention). Node 22.18+, no dependencies. Each prints a line a stage; a failure names the file, the field and the fix (D388, D556).

| Command | What it checks |
|---|---|
| `node tools/items/part.ts <part>` | One part, `assets/data/parts/<part>.json` (`part.schema.json`), against its type in `types.json`: its points (each its template's, on the line facing +Y for a join along a handle, a full point with its roll and on the surface, the origin where the type says), no node scaled, its openings measured round the line within 1 mm of their declared sizes (wider is a gap round the handle, narrower buries it; a tang's flat across Z and X), its triangles and textures within budget, its materials named by role, its `volume_m3` (a working part's), `centre_m` and `bounds_m` as measured on its closed model; its size for each kind it is made for (a head's mass in the kind's envelope, a handle's length and grip); and every pairing its kinds allow with the parts that exist (a handle's top not in the head's top face, a trim against its head, a boss or rim seated on its board): no gap, nothing buried, no two faces in one plane. A part made by code is checked from its numbers |
| `node tools/items/part.ts --write <part>` | First measures a modelled part's `volume_m3` (a working part's), `centre_m` and `bounds_m` on its model and writes them into its data, then checks it: nobody types them. Its model must be a closed surface facing out (`mesh.ts`'s `solid`, the divergence theorem), or it is refused, not measured |
| `node tools/items/kind.ts [kind…]` | Every kind in `assets/data/kinds/` (or those named) against `kind.schema.json`; that the file is named for its kind, every number has its source beside it, every slot fits part types that exist in `assets/data/parts/types.json` and are made as it says, and the reference's inertia and envelope follow D459's rule from its own numbers |

Making a modelled part (D565 step 4, [research](../../research/2026-10-28-assembled-parts-art.md) sections 1 and 4): a kind's parts are drawn together on one concept sheet from `art/style/reference.png` (`art/parts/<kind>/<sheet>.png` with its prompt); `node tools/items/cutout.ts <sheet.png> <part>...` cuts it into each part's `cutout.png`, in reading order; Tripo models each cut-out (`art/parts/<kind>/<part>/model.glb`); then `tools/blender/run.sh tools/items/prep.py art/parts/<kind>/<part> assets/models/parts/<part>.glb OUT.json` welds it closed, turns and sizes it to its frame (D566) by its `prep.json`, cuts the eye where the hand marked it, and the seat flat under it, and writes the points it finds from that cut, with renders of the part before and after beside OUT.json. A trim on the line (a collar) is turned instead (`lathe.py`): Tripo models a tube with its own hole, open and uncuttable, so prep turns a closed tube on its outer profile, the bore exact and the wall real, and bakes the model's look onto it (Cycles, selected to active).

`schema.ts` is the JSON Schema (2020-12) validator they share: only the keywords our schemas use, and it refuses a schema with any other, so nothing passes by a keyword it ignored. Written rather than taken from npm (ajv) because tools carry no dependencies; ajv 8 and it agree on the eleven kinds.

The kinds a part fits and the joins are the assembler's, never worked out here: `part.ts` asks `cargo run -p terra_items --bin assemble -- kinds <type> [<kind>...]` which kinds take its type and which it is made for (`terra_items::assembly::kinds_of`), and asks `cargo run -p terra_items --bin assemble -- joins <part> --parts -` (the parts it checks against on stdin) how each pair sits, and measures them there (`terra_items::assembly::meet`, the one placing rule). A working part gives its `volume_m3` and is weighed in each material of `assets/data/parts/materials.json`; a trim gives its `mass_kg`. An assembled item's model, for the held-look checks, is `cargo run -p terra_bake --bin item -- <kind> <part>... --out FILE.glb` (`item.test.ts` reads one as `attach_contract.ts` and `grip.ts` read a held look).

`mesh.ts` holds the measures on a part's triangles (slices, rays from the line, distances, inside, shared planes); `fixtures.ts` the made-up parts the tests use (a box head with a round eye, a collar, a code-made haft), built from numbers so the answer is known without the checker.

The tests (`*.test.ts`) run in `cargo xtask check`'s tools stage and hold the real kinds to the check.
