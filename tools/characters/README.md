# Characters

The asset pipeline for bodies and what they wear and hold (D115, D128, D316): Blender scripts that turn the source art in [art/](../../art/README.md) into the files the game loads in [assets/](../../assets). The step by step, with what to do when a check fails, is the `character` skill (`.agents/skills/character/SKILL.md`).

Run everything from the repository root. Blender scripts run headless through [tools/blender/run.sh](../blender/README.md); paths given to them are relative to the root. What a script writes and we do not keep goes to `tools/characters/out/`, which is ignored.

Everything the game loads is rebuilt from `art/` by one command, free (the playbook's section 7):

```bash
tools/characters/build_bodies.sh
```

It writes `assets/models/character/base.glb` (the base body with every morph), `assets/models/character/skins/`, and `assets/models/gear/<item>.glb` (each worn look, made once, with the same morphs). Everything between, such as the base's own export `out/base-male-game.glb` and the gear as made on it `out/gear/base-male/`, stays in `tools/characters/out/`.

## Made clips

Where Quaternius's clips read as a cartoon on our bodies, the walk and jog first (research/2026-10-14-animation.md), we make our own with NVIDIA's [Kimodo](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo), a motion model trained on 700 hours of capture, run on this machine. Its weights are under NVIDIA's Open Model License, which allows commercial use and claims no ownership of what it makes. Each clip's prompt, speed (0 to stand), length, seed and number of takes (`samples`, 3 unless it says) are in `art/animation/made.json`, with `"still": true` for one that stays where it stands, such as the idle, which loops between its two most alike frames rather than a stride; its kept take, a BVH on Kimodo's skeleton, in `art/animation/made/`. `made_clips.py` puts them into the game's clips as part of `build_bodies.sh`; no one needs Kimodo to build.

What people do where they stand is made the same way (D347): a clip of work or talk is `"still"`, with `"loop"` (seconds, least and most) for how long its loop may be, and `"busy": true` for one that works (a hammer, an axe), looped in its busiest stretch, since a take that stops and stands is stillest and would otherwise loop where it does nothing. A still clip plays at the take's own pace, Kimodo's 30 frames a second keyed on the scene's 24; a stride keeps a key a frame, the game setting its pace from its feet. Kimodo follows a short plain prompt, as its own examples are ("A person repeatedly hammers something..."), better than a long one naming every limb. A clip in `clips.txt` that no library has must be one of these; `clips.py` leaves it to `made_clips.py`, and with no library here the build keeps the library's clips already in the committed mannequin.

A stride's entry may also carry what the take alone gets wrong (D360). `"step"` (metres) sets how far apart the feet land, and has the stride carried onto each body by where the mannequin's limbs point (`aim.py`). `"arms"` lays a runner's arms over the take, its legs and spine untouched (`made_clips.py`'s `swing_arms`): the upper arms' swing mapped onto `swing` (degrees forward and back of the trunk), both timed by the left arm; the elbow bent from `elbow[0]` behind to `elbow[1]` in front; the forearm across the body at most `across` degrees; the wrist within `wrist` of the forearm's line; and the hand rolled within `palm` of thumb-side-up. `"fist"` closes the fingers that share of the way into the library's clenched fist, since Kimodo leaves them straight.

`"way"` (degrees from where the body faces, 90 to its right, -90 to its left) lays a stride's path that way with Kimodo's heading held ahead, so a strafe steps sideways facing forward; `made_clips.py` turns it by its travel less `way` and prints the way the take really went (`WAY`). `"once": true` makes a one-shot (a blow, a block, a dodge): its take is held to no path, kept from start to end or over `"span"` (seconds in the take), played at the take's pace, in place, and not closed into a loop; its `ONCE` line says when each hand is raised highest and moves fastest, when the faster hand's blow lands (it first slows below 30% of its fastest), and how far the body travels, which a scene moves it by. A set of clips for something other than the game's bodies (a creature, a demo) has its own list, given to both tools as `--list FILE`: `art/animation/sets/fight.json`, its kept takes in `art/animation/sets/fight/`, made onto the mannequin into a copy named for it (`made_clips.py tools/characters/out/fight-mannequin.glb --list=art/animation/sets/fight.json`) that carries the game's clips and the set's, for `export_character.py --clips=`, so the body plays both; the game's list and mannequin are left alone.

An entry with `"source": "uthana"` is made by Uthana's text-to-motion 3.0 on our body instead (D532, `tools/uthana`): `prompt`, `seconds` (4 to 10, whole, $0.10 each), and `"looped"` (seconds) for a clip Uthana closes into a loop itself, which `made_clips.py` keeps whole. Its takes are GLBs on our skeleton (the kept one `art/animation/made/<clip>.glb`, in place of a BVH), lifted onto our ground and refused unless they stand as the same motion does on Uthana's Manny (D533, `tools/uthana/carry.ts`); a take paid for is never made again (`--samples N` makes more; a refused one is downloaded again, free), and what each cost is printed and kept in `takes.json` beside them. Every take, either source, is measured for its loop seam (deg, and cm: hands, feet and head at the end from the start, relative to the hips, before the easing), how deep the arms go into the body on every template (`skin.ts` `armsInBody`, the move check's hands, bar 2 cm), and for a clip that stays where it stands, how far each foot wanders and how it stands (`stance.ts`: the knees' median bend, the hips' height and the head's forward lean against the body's rest), ✗ beside each over the idle's bar (`stance.ts` `BAR`). An entry may name Uthana's `"model"` (`"2"`, text-to-motion 2.0 at $0.03 a second, each take seeded from `seed` on, with `"foot_ik"`); 3.0 unless it says. A still entry may also ask for `"planted": true`, its hips kept over where its forefeet stood rather than sliding with them (Uthana's idle slid 7 cm), and `"stand": true`, the stand pass (`stand.py`, tested by `test_stand.py`): its hips raised toward the body's rest by straightening each knee to a share of its bend, so a step stays a share of the take's, and its head drawn back over them, its feet where they stood.

A start or a stop is a one-shot held to a speed profile: `"speeds"` (seconds, m/s keys, straight between) lays its path straight ahead with its heading held (`motion.ts` `profile`), so Kimodo brakes or picks up as asked, and `"travels": true` keeps its hips' travel (its root motion) rather than taking it off, for the game to take off by the clip's distance curve as Lyra's distance matching does (`Jog_Stop`, `Jog_Start`; controller criterion 21d).

A set's entry may instead be made from another clip with parts of the body held as a third clip holds them (D454): `"of"` the clip it is made from, `"hold"` a list of parts, each `{"clip", "bone", "turn"}`: that bone and everything below it held at the clip's first pose for the whole clip, in their own frames, with `"turn"` laying a bone's turn about the body's up axis on it (degrees, + to the left seen from above). It is a layered blend per bone baked at export (`hold.py`, by `export_character.py --list` after aim and twist), so the game and `moves.ts` play it as any other clip; it needs no take. The fight's roll is `Fight_Roll`, the library's `Roll` with both arms held as `Keyed_Guard` holds them, each turned 90° out at the shoulder, so the strapped shield and the sword stay beside the body through the tuck; the walker's `Roll` is left as it was. A drawn body walks in the library's `Walk_Loop` with both arms held as the set's guard (`Low_Guard`) holds them, laid at run time by a bone mask (`terra_walker::layer`, D458), not baked; `moves.ts` measures it as `Walk_Loop+Low_Guard` (`glb.ts`'s `layered`, the same mask). A hold may also take `"in"` and `"out"` (clip seconds, `[from, to]`): the part plays the `of` clip until `in`, eases to the held pose over it by a smoothstep weight (each bone slerped in its own frame, Unreal's Layered Blend per Bone driven by a curve), and back over `out`. An `out` before the `in` holds the part at both ends and plays the `of` clip between. The guard rests the shield low at the hip (D531): `Low_Guard` is `Keyed_Guard` with the shield arm held low at the left side (hold.py `aim` and `roll`), and the blows hold the same arm throughout (`Open_Slash`, `Open_Backhand`, `Open_Heavy`, from `Keyed_*`), so the sword never crosses its own shield (D454); `Low_Strafe` lays it over the strafes with the forearm a little further out, clear of the stepping thigh. `Guard_Board` holds `Fight_Guard`'s shield arm as the guard's. Each solves its arm to its board's place against the chest (a hold's `"shield"`, D554): the guard, the blows and `Fight_Guard` to `Low_Guard`'s, the strafes to `Low_Strafe`'s. The block plays `Clear_Block`, `Keyed_Block` with the sword arm eased out of the raised board's way (in 0-0.25, out 0.95-1.35 s), the blade leaning out at the right side behind the board, and the shield arm eased up from the low rest into the raise (out 0-0.2 s) and back to it (in 0.95-1.35 s). The `of` clip's blow spec holds the made clip too (`moves.ts`). An entry with `"over"` (a list of clips) in place of `"of"` lays its holds over each of them in place, after the made clips, held throughout: `Strap_Grip` holds the left hand on the strapped heater's hand strap (the fist turned about 100° from the guard's, the back of the hand to the board, bent 20° toward the little finger and 20° back, the twist bone half of it) in every clip a fighter plays, as the straps fix it to the forearm (D459). `Jump_Loop` is held so too, so the walker's fall shows the left fist closed.

To make one, Kimodo is set up once, outside the repository, in `~/.cache/terra-kimodo` (or `KIMODO_HOME`): a Python 3.12 environment with PyTorch for CUDA and Kimodo installed from its repository (`uv venv --python 3.12 venv`, `uv pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu128`, `uv pip install -e kimodo`), and its text encoder in `text_encoders/`: Meta's Llama 3 8B Instruct at `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct` (Meta's gated copy, or NousResearch's, which its licence lets be shared), and McGill's two LLM2Vec adapters at `McGill-NLP/<name>` with their `base_model_name_or_path` pointed at that folder. The encoder runs on the processor, about 16 GB of memory; the motion model takes about 3 GB of the graphics card, so ask the other sessions for the card first. When CUDA is down (any CUDA call segfaults under WSL after the Windows driver updates, until `wsl --shutdown`), `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""` runs the motion model on the processor: eight takes in about four minutes.

node tools/characters/motion.ts Sprint_Loop            # three takes, each measured

tools/characters/build_bodies.sh                       # into the game
