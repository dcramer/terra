# Creatures

Blender scripts that make the game's animals from their source art in `art/creatures/`, for animals whose bodies Tripo's auto-rig does not serve (docs/creatures.md, docs/demos.md).

- `animal.py` makes an animal from `art/creatures/<name>/`: Tripo's export (`model.glb`) welded and cut to its budget, turned to face +Z in glTF at life size with its feet at the origin, skinned on a skeleton of our own and given its clips, by one of two body plans, a four-legged runner (`quadruped`) or a bird (`bird`), the way Spore and Overgrowth animate many bodies with one set of rules (research/2026-10-14-animation.md). `creature.json` says where each bone's head and tail are, in the model's units once turned (a bone ending `.L` is mirrored), which bones make each leg, and each clip's kind and numbers: `idle`, `gait` (walk, trot, gallop, half-bound, bound or hop: by when each foot lands and its time down, or by a gait of `GAITS` and the speed it goes, its stride and cadence then a real animal's at that speed, cited there), `graze`, `sit`, `fly`, `swim` (and dabble), `death`. A runner's `haunch` keeps the rump's skin with the pelvis and spreads the weights over the hip, so a thigh swung forward does not crease it. Legs are placed by two-bone inverse kinematics worked out in the script, so a planted foot stays where it was put; locomotion is in place, and the script prints each clip's pace (`PACES`) for `assets/data/creatures.ron`. A kind made from another's model textured again (`like`) takes that one's skeleton and clips. `tools/blender/run.sh tools/creatures/animal.py red_squirrel` writes `assets/models/creatures/<file>.glb`.
- `hen.py` makes `assets/models/creatures/Chicken.glb` from Tripo's export in `art/creatures/chicken/model.glb`: real size, feet at the origin, facing +Z as `Deer.glb` does, re-skinned on a skeleton of our own (Tripo's avian rig weighted the feet to the tail), with `Idle`, `Walk` (in place, 0.375 m/s) and `Peck` keyed through leg IK and baked; written before `animal.py`, which would make it today.
- `sheet.py` renders a preview sheet of a model in the game's light, to check against docs/art-style.md: a row standing (beside the figure for scale, then front, side, three-quarter and behind), then a row for each clip, its moments from the side. `--cpu` renders on the processor with Cycles.
- `dragon.py` makes `assets/models/creatures/Dragon.glb` from Tripo's unrigged export in `art/creatures/dragon/model.glb`: 13 m nose to tail, front +Z, its origin the body's centre of mass since it flies; the mouth Tripo modelled shut cut open along the lips, lined and given teeth; skinned by bone heat on a skeleton of our own (spine, six neck bones, head and jaw, ten tail bones, hind legs, and each wing an arm with a thumb and four two-bone fingers), with a `Mouth` empty on the head for the breath, and `Fly`, `Glide`, `Hover`, `Breathe`, `FlyBreathe` and `Roar` keyed as loops at 30 fps, in place (the game moves and banks it). No clip library has wings, so the clips are made here.
- `dragon_sheet.py` renders the dragon's contact sheets: its rest pose beside the figure, and each clip at four moments from the front, the side and the three-quarter, with its head close and the mouth's socket marked. `cycles` renders on the CPU, so it needs no turn of the GPU.

Run them through `tools/blender/run.sh`, and anything that renders under the GPU's turn: `flock --shared ~/.cache/terra/gpu.lock tools/blender/run.sh tools/creatures/sheet.py assets/models/creatures/Squirrel.glb sheet.png`. What they write for looking at goes under `shots/` or `tools/creatures/out/`, which are not committed.
