# Kits

Makes a building kit's dressed pieces (D215, [docs/building-kits.md](../../docs/building-kits.md)): the sections a layout sweeps along each exposed edge by its role (a coping, a plinth, a sill, a lintel) and the modules laid along a roof's or a frame's lines, from their source under `art/kits/<kit>/`, in Blender. Corner stones (quoins, jamb stones) are not made here: the game cuts them from each block material's own wall, from numbers in its pattern's file (`dress: roles: Arris: Corners(...)`), so no material wears another's (D215 as amended).

```bash
tools/blender/run.sh tools/kits/pieces.py art/kits/block/pieces.json assets/data/kits/pieces/block.ron
```

The source, `pieces.json`, says each piece in numbers a person can read and change:

- **Profiles**: a polyline of points (out, up) in metres from the edge it is swept along, out from the cube's face and up from the edge's line, nothing past an eighth of a cube out (D192); `round` names the points Blender's vertex bevel rounds, by `radius`. The two ends of the line lie inside the wall: the layout closes and mitres them.
- **Modules** (D258): a length of mesh along +X, `length` long, Y up from what it sits on and Z across: a section of a `kind` swept along it and shaped as it goes. `timber` is squared timber or a board between `y` and `z`, its corners rounded by `round` and wandering, its faces scalloped by the `adze` every `period`; `pole` a round pole of `radius`; `mound` a capping crowned across `half` either side and `height` over `base`, its edge cut in `points` every `period`; `bundle` an oval about `y`, `z` of `radii`, `pinch`ed every `period`; `log` a log in its bark of `radius`, `uneven` and `lumps`y, `flat`ter where it bears, with `stubs` (fewest, most a copy) of branches sawn off `stub_length` proud and `stub_radius` round on its two faces. `rods` lays thin rods along it (a ridge's liggers); `parts` joins several sections in one module; `joint` presses a gap at each end, as between ridge stones; `open` leaves its ends open so copies run on as one member. `cap` (`flat`, or a `dome` drawn in over `reach` to a `pivot`) closes a line where it stops; `blend` is how far either side of a crease a draped module turns from one slope to the other. Each of its `variants` is written near and at a far budget (`far`, a fraction).

- **Modelled modules** (D251, D258): a module with a `model`, a model kept as source art (a Tripo model of a concept, accepted as a shared reference piece (D258, D290), `art/kits/<kit>/<piece>/model.glb`), is read in place of numbers: laid along +X by its longest side (`lies`: `board`, `log`, or `capping` and `channel`, which are unfolded flat so the game can drape them over any angle a crease makes), made to its contract (D290, D303): its real size (`dims`: along, up and across, metres) with its origin at its `anchor` (where in that box, from its low corner), the point the game seats its line on (a roof's deck or its covering, `roofing::on_covering`), and cut to its `budget`. One model is one size wherever it is used, which the kit checker holds; the pieces file states each module's `dims`, `model` and its `start` and `end` sockets, and the checker holds each modelled piece to its size from its origin. A pattern's pieces (`art/kits/roof`, the roof pattern's boards and valley) are shared by its materials and drawn in its atlas as `Trim`; a material's own (`own` in its pack: thatch's ridge, shakes' and slate's ridge caps) override them by name and are drawn in its atlas as `Piece`. The modelled modules have their own textures baked into one atlas a pieces file, 2,048 square, `atlas` under `assets/` (`color.jpg`, with a flat `normal.jpg` and a plain `arm.jpg`); each corner keeps its place in it. This is the path the developers asked for in D251; the numbered kinds above are the script-made pieces it replaces.

`pieces.py` writes the game's file, `assets/data/kits/pieces/<kit>.ron` (`terra_build::dress::Pieces`), which the kit names (`dress: (pieces: Some(…))`) and which is committed with it, as the props' models are. A new look is a source here and a kit file; no code. `cargo xtask kits` checks that every role a kit dresses has its piece and that each keeps to its budget.

## A new material in one command (D290)

Which materials the game has is the developers' choice (D236): make one only when they ask. The names below are examples.

```bash
cargo xtask pack river_cobble --from polyhaven:river_small_rocks --title "River cobble" --pattern block
cargo xtask pack cut_sandstone --from ambientcg:Bricks076C --metres 2 --pattern block
cargo xtask pack wattle_panels --from image:art/textures/wattle.png --pattern frame
cargo xtask pack split_shakes --from image:art/textures/shakes.png --metres 1.5 --pattern roof
cargo xtask pack thick_logs --from model:art/kits/logs/log/model.glb --round 0.3,2 --metres 2 --pattern coursed
```

Every pattern works from one command. A material is made for one face, and laid over a reference pack for the rest its pattern needs (`under`): a stone (`block`) needs nothing more; a wood (`coursed`) is laid over the pine logs' pack, its daub, sawn ends and modelled log; a roof covering (`roof`) over the shakes', its battens, rafters and ridge. `--under <pack>` lays it over another, `--under none` over nothing. A run that fails anywhere, the source, the set's check or the kit checker, leaves nothing behind: the files it made are taken away and those it changed (the credits) put back as they were, so a failure never breaks a later check.

`pack.ts` makes a construction material from a source: a CC0 scan (`polyhaven:`, `ambientcg:`, through `tools/textures`), a generated picture (`image:`), a hand-made reference model baked from above onto a square of the material (`model:`, `bake.py` in Blender: colour, normal, occlusion and roughness, height), or, for a member such as a log, baked round it (`--round diameter,length`: laid along the way its points spread most at the size the kit lays it, baked onto a cylinder inside it, once round by `--metres` along, cross-faded along so it tiles, its taper taken out of its height, its darkest side, the model's underside, turned underneath, and the light the model was painted in taken out of its colour), or a variant of a texture set already made (`pack:`, its colour multiplied by `--tint`, its size the set's own). It fits every map to the texture budget (2,048 texels, D362), checks the set (its maps there, its colour not black), credits it in `assets/materials/README.md`, writes its pack (`assets/data/packs/<name>.ron`: its texture sets for its pattern's slots, its size in metres, its parallax depth, its mean colours for far off) and its material file (`assets/data/kits/<name>.ron`: the next free id, its pattern, pack, cost, weight and roof, as its pattern's defaults unless told), and runs the kit checker. The hammer's picker and the kit sheet read every material file, so nothing else is needed; tune it by editing either file while the game runs. `node --test tools/kits/pack.test.ts` tests it.

**Checked as it is added (D349).** The kit checker refuses what would make a material look wrong only later, in the game (`terra_build::packs`, [construction-002](../../docs/proposals/construction-002.md#what-a-pack-is-checked-for-d349)): a height map that is weak, inverted, noise or not its colour map's; a texture that does not tile; a size far from its pattern's (stones, flags, logs, courses); relief or parallax past what its pattern draws; a role its pattern asks every pack for left to a default; glossy or metal maps, or a shine; a field its pattern never reads, named with the file that sets it. Each problem says how to put it right: `--invert-height` flips a height map whose joints are bright, `--normalise-height` stretches a weak one, `--metres` and `--rows` set its size. Whether a material needs a pack or a new pattern is construction-002's [table](../../docs/proposals/construction-002.md#what-each-pattern-can-and-cannot-express).

**Its preview sheet (D349).** `node tools/kits/preview.ts <name>` pictures a material on every role and shape of its pattern at 10 m and 2 m, at noon and in the evening, in `shots/preview/<name>.jpg`, for a developer to look over once: the kit sheet lays its preview alone (`--sheet-rows "<name>: preview"`, west of the start, so its views are fixed). `cargo xtask pack` makes it where a GPU draws the game (`--preview` makes it anyway) and prints the command elsewhere, as under WSL, whose Linux build draws on the CPU (a few minutes an hour of light). `node --test tools/kits/preview.test.ts` tests it.
